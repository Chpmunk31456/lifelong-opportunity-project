## United States occupation mapping

Use **SOC/O*NET 43-9041.00 — Insurance Claims and Policy Processing Clerks** as the primary occupational match. O*NET describes the occupation as processing new policies, changes to existing policies, and claims forms; obtaining information to verify accuracy and completeness; and updating policy/company records. O*NET was updated in 2026.

- O*NET OnLine: https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-9041.00
- U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, May 2025 national OEWS table: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ocwage.t01.htm
- BLS insurance-industry profile: https://www.bls.gov/iag/tgs/iag524.htm

### Official U.S. income evidence

BLS May 2025 national OEWS data report approximately **214,260 workers**, a **$25.44 mean hourly wage**, a **$52,920 mean annual wage**, and a **$23.67 median hourly wage** for Insurance Claims and Policy Processing Clerks. These are survey estimates for the BLS occupation, not guaranteed pay for an individual job.

Within the insurance-carriers-and-related-activities industry, BLS reports 2025 wages of approximately **$23.97 median hourly / $49,860 median annual** and **$25.92 mean hourly / $53,920 mean annual** for this occupation. Industry figures and national all-industry figures must not be mixed or presented as the same measure.

### Current non-government market estimate

Keep current market estimates clearly labeled as **non-government estimates** and separate from BLS data. ZipRecruiter reported, as of **July 21, 2026**, an estimated U.S. average of **$46,461 per year ($22.34/hour)** for the title **Insurance Claims Processor**, with a reported majority range of approximately **$38,000–$53,000**. Its methodology uses employer postings and third-party data, so this is a market indicator rather than an official occupational wage statistic.

- ZipRecruiter: https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Salaries/Insurance-Claims-Processor-Salary

## Canada mapping

Use **NOC 14201 — Banking, insurance and other financial clerks** as the closest broad Canadian occupational group, with the OaSIS insurance-clerk profile **14201.02** for duties such as processing enrolments, cancellations, claims transactions, policy changes and premium payments; reviewing applications and coverage; maintaining claims/insurance records; and answering insurance-product questions.

- NOC 2021 profile: https://noc.esdc.gc.ca/Structure/NOCProfile?GocTemplateCulture=en-CA&code=14201&version=2021.0
- OaSIS 2025 insurance-clerk profile: https://noc.esdc.gc.ca/OASIS/OASISOccProfile?code=14201.02&version=2025.0

Do not imply that U.S. SOC and Canadian NOC classifications are legally or statistically interchangeable. Candidates should verify province-specific employer requirements and any licensing rules attached to duties beyond clerical processing.

## United States funding and low-cost training controls

### Federal student aid

The **2026–27 FAFSA** can provide access to federal grants, work-study, and federal student loans for eligible college, career-school, or trade-school programs. Eligibility is program- and student-specific; never imply that a particular course is automatically aid-eligible.

- Federal Student Aid: https://studentaid.gov/articles/fafsa-student-steps/

### WIOA / American Job Centers

WIOA programs provide eligible job seekers with career and training services, including classroom and work-based learning, through the American Job Center network. Funding and eligible training-provider rules vary by state/local workforce area.

- U.S. Department of Labor WIOA programs: https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/wioa/programs
- Adult and Dislocated Worker Program: https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/workforce-investment/adult

### Employer educational assistance

IRS Publication 15-B for **2026** states that employer educational-assistance programs can exclude up to **$5,250** of qualifying benefits from an employee’s income and that the student-loan-payment exclusion under such programs was permanently extended for payments after 2025. Employer programs are optional and plan-specific.

- IRS Publication 15-B (2026): https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p15b.pdf

### Registered Apprenticeship

Registered Apprenticeship is a paid work-and-learning model, but applicants must verify whether relevant insurance/financial-services programs are actually available in their location. Do not imply that a claims-processing apprenticeship exists everywhere.

- Apprenticeship.gov: https://www.apprenticeship.gov/
- Apprenticeship Job Finder: https://www.apprenticeship.gov/apprenticeship-job-finder

## Latin America coverage rule

Outside Colombia, describe insurance-processing pathways cautiously because job titles, licensing boundaries, education systems, and compensation differ by country. Direct readers to the national insurance regulator, public employment service, accredited education/training providers, and employer career pages in the country where they intend to work. Do not convert U.S. wage data into a purported Latin American salary benchmark.

## Editorial controls for the v2 master

1. Distinguish **claims/policy processing clerical work** from licensed sales, underwriting, and claims-adjuster/examiner authority.
2. State that licensing requirements depend on jurisdiction and assigned duties; tell readers to verify locally before paying for a credential.
3. Label every income figure by **country, source type, occupation/title, measurement (mean/median/range), and data date**.
4. Keep official statistics separate from private job-market estimates and single-vacancy examples.
5. Prioritize free or employer-funded learning before paid credentials.
6. Include accessibility, privacy, secure handling of personal/financial/health information, and human review of consequential AI-assisted decisions.
7. Do not claim independent human review, accreditation, endorsement, certification, employment outcomes, or guaranteed funding unless separately documented.

## Review cadence

Revalidate wages, training availability, funding, licensing boundaries, apprenticeship availability, and links **at least annually and before material career or spending decisions**. Private salary estimates and live vacancies should be treated as especially time-sensitive.
