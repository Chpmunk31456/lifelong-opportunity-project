## Required v2 substantive corrections and additions

### 1. Occupational scope and boundaries

The v2 English master must anchor the U.S. occupation to **SOC/O*NET 43-9041.00 — Insurance Claims and Policy Processing Clerks** and explain that the guide concerns clerical/processing work. It must not imply authority to perform licensed insurance sales, underwriting, or claims-adjuster/examiner functions where those duties require separate authorization.

For Canada, use **NOC 14201 — Banking, insurance and other financial clerks** with the OaSIS insurance-clerk profile **14201.02** as the closest broad mapping. Do not imply U.S. SOC and Canadian NOC classifications are legally or statistically interchangeable.

### 2. Income evidence

Replace or qualify any unsupported or stale salary statements. The v2 master must keep different evidence classes visibly separate:

- **Official U.S. occupational statistics:** BLS May 2025 national OEWS data for Insurance Claims and Policy Processing Clerks, including the measurement type and date.
- **Insurance-industry occupational data:** BLS insurance-carriers-and-related-activities figures, clearly distinguished from the national all-industry estimates.
- **Current non-government estimate:** the July 21, 2026 ZipRecruiter Insurance Claims Processor estimate, explicitly labeled non-government and title-specific.
- **Colombia vacancy evidence:** if used, the recent Bogotá Servicio Público de Empleo vacancy is a single-job example rather than a national compensation benchmark.

No U.S. wage figure should be converted into or presented as a Latin American salary benchmark.

### 3. Education, funding, and free-first pathways

The v2 master must explicitly direct readers to verify eligibility before spending money and prioritize free, public, or employer-funded routes. Include, where applicable:

- 2026–27 FAFSA access to federal grants, work-study, and federal student loans for eligible programs and students;
- WIOA/American Job Center career and training services, with local eligibility and provider rules;
- employer educational-assistance programs, noting the 2026 IRS exclusion of up to **$5,250** for qualifying benefits and that employer participation is optional and plan-specific;
- Registered Apprenticeship as a paid work-and-learning model while making clear that insurance-processing opportunities are location- and employer-dependent; and
- free or low-cost foundational learning before paid credentials.

### 4. Colombia and broader Latin America

Add a Colombia pathway using current official/public resources, including:

- SENA **Técnico en Atención Comercial y Operación en Seguros**;
- SENA **Tecnología en Gestión Administrativa y Comercial de Seguros y Riesgos**;
- SENA enrolment/search resources; and
- Servicio Público de Empleo.

For Latin America outside Colombia, avoid one-size-fits-all credential or licensing statements. Direct readers to the relevant national insurance regulator, public employment service, recognized training providers, and employer career pages in the country where they intend to work.

### 5. Licensing and spending safeguards

State that licensing requirements depend on jurisdiction and assigned duties. Readers should verify requirements with the relevant regulator and employer before paying for a course, examination, membership, or credential. Do not describe optional credentials as mandatory unless the applicable jurisdiction or employer actually requires them.

### 6. Accessibility, privacy, security, and AI

Retain and strengthen the legacy accessibility, privacy, cybersecurity, ethical-AI, fraud, and worker-protection material. The v2 master must make clear that:

- insurance work can involve sensitive personal, financial, health, and claims information;
- workplace systems and employer-approved processes should be used for protected information;
- public AI tools should not receive confidential claimant, policyholder, or employer data unless explicitly authorized and appropriately protected;
- AI can assist with drafting, summarization, organization, and practice, but consequential decisions require appropriate human review; and
- AI output must not be treated as authoritative evidence of coverage, liability, eligibility, fraud, or claim disposition.

### 7. Accessibility and human readability controls

The rewritten English master should use plain language, descriptive headings, short paragraphs, meaningful link text, consistent list structure, and explanations for unavoidable insurance terminology. Avoid dense jargon, unexplained acronyms, decorative symbols that impair rendering, and language that implies guaranteed outcomes.

### 8. Publication and review claims

Do not state or imply independent human certification, accreditation, linguistic validation, regulator endorsement, employment outcomes, guaranteed funding, or guaranteed income unless separately documented. AI-assisted research/editing must remain disclosed consistently with project policy.
