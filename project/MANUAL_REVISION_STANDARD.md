# Manual Revision Standard

**Scope:** Lifelong Opportunity Guides 00-100  
**Revision branch:** `revision/guide-00-100-2026`

This standard applies before writing, revising, translating, or publishing any guide in the collection.

## 1. Required workflow for every guide

1. Review the current English source, guide README, metadata, and existing PDF.
2. Verify spelling, grammar, punctuation, terminology, structure, links, filenames, version numbers, and visible characters.
3. Revise the English edition for plain language, natural human readability, factual accuracy, accessibility, and occupation-specific usefulness.
4. Add the required funding, training, apprenticeship, and compensation sections.
5. Verify licensing, certification, accreditation, safety, privacy, legal, and jurisdictional boundaries.
6. Produce neutral Latin American Spanish (`es-419`) and Brazilian Portuguese (`pt-BR`) editions from the approved English source.
7. Compare all three editions for headings, tables, links, images, code blocks, citations, warnings, and required sections.
8. Render and visually inspect every DOCX and PDF page before publication.
9. Update README files, metadata, checksums, version identifiers, and quality records.
10. Do not mark a guide complete until all required checks pass.

## 2. Funding, free/low-cost training, and apprenticeship locator

Every occupational guide must include a clearly labeled section covering:

- United States
- Canada
- Latin America
- Colombia as a detailed example when the pathway is relevant

Use current official sources whenever available. Supplement official sources with credible nonprofit, employer, union, foundation, college, and recognized industry resources when they provide a legitimate pathway.

Distinguish clearly between:

- Free training
- Subsidized or low-cost training
- Scholarships
- Grants or forgivable assistance
- Employer-paid education
- Paid apprenticeships
- Paid traineeships or co-ops
- Unpaid internships
- Repayable loans

Loans are not the first recommendation. Exhaust grants, public funding, employer support, paid work-based learning, scholarships, and affordable alternatives before borrowing.

Each listed resource should identify:

- Country and region
- Provider
- Type of support
- Whether it is free, paid, subsidized, forgivable, or repayable
- Important eligibility limits
- Application or availability status
- Last verified date
- Official or primary link

Do not guarantee funding, admission, licensing, employment, or completion.

## 3. Compensation research standard

Every guide must explain possible income using more than one source.

### Source hierarchy

1. Official government wage or labor-market data
2. Current employer job postings with disclosed compensation
3. Union or collective-agreement wage schedules when relevant
4. Credible non-government estimates, including Glassdoor, Indeed Salaries, PayScale, Salary.com, Talent.com, and LinkedIn salary information when available

Crowdsourced and commercial salary sources are supplementary estimates, not authoritative facts.

Every compensation section should identify:

- Country, state/province/department, and city or region when available
- Date checked
- Hourly, monthly, annual, commission, contract, or self-employment basis
- Entry, typical, and experienced ranges when supported
- Base pay versus total compensation
- Overtime, shift premiums, tips, commissions, incentives, and bonuses
- Employee wages versus contractor billing or business revenue
- Gross income versus take-home pay when the distinction matters
- Sample-size or methodology limitations when the source provides them

Do not present compensation as guaranteed, expected, or transferable across locations.

## 4. Regional and language standard

Each guide must include role-search terminology in:

- English
- Neutral Latin American Spanish
- Brazilian Portuguese

Do not assume that a title, credential, license, apprenticeship model, scope of practice, or salary transfers between countries.

Translations must:

- Preserve meaning, headings, tables, links, warnings, and document structure
- Use professional `es-419` and natural professional `pt-BR`
- Preserve product names, framework names, standards identifiers, citations, commands, code, URLs, and file paths unless translation is explicitly required
- Maintain a project terminology glossary
- Flag ambiguous legal, regulatory, or occupational terminology
- Be labeled accurately as machine-assisted and editorially reviewed unless an independent qualified translator has certified the edition

## 5. Human editorial standard

Every guide must receive a separate editorial pass for:

- Spelling, grammar, punctuation, and consistency
- Natural sentence rhythm and varied paragraph length
- Concrete, occupation-specific examples
- Plain language where jargon is unnecessary
- Removal of generic, inflated, promotional, or repetitive wording
- Reduction of repeated boilerplate when Guide 00 already explains the project-wide rule
- Limited and purposeful use of headings, tables, bold text, em dashes, and formulaic three-part lists
- Named sources instead of vague phrases such as “experts say”
- No invented citations, unsupported claims, placeholders, or broken markup
- A voice consistent with Al explaining the subject carefully to a real learner

This review is intended to improve quality and authenticity, not to evade automated detection systems.

## 6. Required occupational safeguards

Each guide must verify, where relevant:

- Licensing and scope-of-practice limits
- Accreditation and credential recognition
- Health and safety requirements
- Privacy and data protection
- Background checks, immigration, language, age, or residency limits
- Tools, uniforms, software, transportation, insurance, examination, renewal, and membership costs
- Shift work, travel, seasonal work, physical demands, emotional labor, and on-call requirements
- Employee, contractor, commission, franchise, and self-employment distinctions
- Automation and AI-related changes to entry-level work
- Scam, predatory-program, wage-theft, and misleading job-advertisement risks

## 7. Standard action plan

Use a consistent 30/60/90-day structure:

- **Days 1-30:** investigate the occupation, verify local requirements, review real job postings, and test free or low-cost learning.
- **Days 31-60:** build evidence through coursework, supervised practice, projects, volunteering, or work-based learning.
- **Days 61-90:** refine the résumé and portfolio, apply selectively, prepare for interviews, and review results.

## 8. Publication QA gate

A guide is not complete until all three language editions pass:

- Factual and source verification
- Spelling and grammar review
- Human-readability review
- Funding and apprenticeship coverage
- Compensation triangulation
- Licensing and jurisdictional safeguards
- Translation completeness and terminology consistency
- Accessibility review
- Broken-link and filename review
- DOCX render inspection
- Searchable PDF render inspection
- Metadata, version, and checksum reconciliation

## 9. Status language

Use clear status labels:

- Draft
- Machine-assisted translation draft
- Editorially reviewed
- Structurally verified
- Publication candidate
- Published
- Independently human-reviewed or certified only when that review actually occurred

## 10. No silent assumptions

When a fact cannot be verified, say so. Preserve completed work, identify the missing evidence, explain why it matters, and record the safest next step. Never invent details to make a guide appear complete.
