## Occupational mapping

The direct U.S. occupational reference is **Loan Interviewers and Clerks, O*NET/SOC 43-4131.00**. ## U.S. mortgage-processing licensing boundary

The title `loan processor` does not itself determine licensing status. Current CFPB Regulation H / S.A.F.E. Act rules distinguish between clerical/support work performed under actual supervision and activities that require mortgage-loan-originator licensing.

CFPB defines clerical/support duties for residential mortgage processing to include receiving, collecting, distributing and analyzing information and communicating with consumers to obtain processing information, so long as the communication does **not** include offering or negotiating mortgage rates/terms or counseling consumers about those terms.

CFPB §1008.103 and Appendix C explain that an independent contractor performing residential-mortgage loan-processing or underwriting clerical/support duties is generally within the state licensing requirement, while a state is not required to impose S.A.F.E. Act licensing on certain employees who perform only clerical/support duties under the direction, supervision and instruction of a properly licensed/registered or otherwise excluded person. The supervision must be real, not merely nominal.

Sources:
- https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1008/23/
- https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1008/103/
- https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1008/c/

## Controlled role boundary

This guide covers administrative loan-processing and loan-clerk work. It does not authorize the reader to:

- take residential mortgage applications or offer/negotiate mortgage terms where those activities trigger loan-originator rules;
- hold themselves out as a licensed mortgage loan originator without the required registration/license;
- approve or deny credit independently unless the actual role and employer grant that authority;
- alter or fabricate borrower information, underwriting findings, appraisals, income, assets, liabilities, collateral, rates, terms, disclosures or approvals;
- provide legal, tax, financial, appraisal or underwriting advice outside role authority; or
- assume that employee, contractor, bank, credit-union, mortgage-broker, consumer-loan and commercial-loan processing roles share the same regulatory rules.

Readers must verify the exact duties, employment/contractor structure, product, employer, NMLS/state requirements and other applicable rules before paying for licensing or credential preparation.

## Core control themes for the revised guide

- document completeness and source verification;
- identity and confidential-data protection;
- clear distinction between receiving information and making/communicating a credit decision;
- fair and consistent treatment under approved policy;
- no manipulation of borrower data or exceptions to influence an outcome;
- escalation of suspected fraud, altered documents, identity discrepancies and conflicts without unsupported accusations;
- audit-ready notes and evidence provenance;
- responsible AI limited to employer-approved, low-risk assistance with consequential decisions remaining subject to authorized human review; and
- accessibility and plain-language communication without changing approved disclosures or terms.
