## Scope and title control

The legacy guide title combines two related but non-identical ideas: a **Recreation Worker** and an **Activities Coordinator**. Current official evidence supports using U.S. SOC/O*NET **39-9032.00 — Recreation Workers** as the primary occupational benchmark for workers who organize, conduct, and promote group recreation activities. Coordinator titles may carry additional scheduling, records, volunteer, event, purchasing, or program-administration responsibilities, so the revised guide must compare actual duties and authority rather than assume every coordinator is a manager.

The guide must not represent a general recreation or activities coordinator as a recreational therapist, nurse, social worker, teacher, childcare professional, lifeguard, exercise physiologist, athletic trainer, licensed coach, or facility manager unless the person actually holds the required credential and assigned authority.

## United States — official occupation, wages, entry, and outlook

### O*NET / BLS current benchmark

O*NET OnLine identifies **39-9032.00 — Recreation Workers** and currently displays Bureau of Labor Statistics **2025 wage data** and 2024–2034 projections:

- median hourly wage: **USD $17.58**;
- median annual wage: **USD $36,560**;
- 2024 employment: approximately **327,700**;
- projected employment growth, 2024–2034: **3% to 4%**;
- projected annual openings: approximately **68,100**.

O*NET's current industry distribution shows substantial employment in government and health care/social assistance, with additional employment in arts, entertainment and recreation; other services; and educational services.

### BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook

The current BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook states that recreation workers typically need at least a **high school diploma or equivalent**, although requirements vary by job, and usually receive **short-term on-the-job training** after hire. BLS reports a May 2024 median annual wage of **$35,380** and projects **4% growth from 2024 to 2034**, with about **68,100 openings per year**.

The difference between O*NET's $36,560 figure and the OOH's $35,380 figure is a reference-period difference, not a contradiction: O*NET is displaying BLS **2025 wage data**, while the OOH page currently reports the **May 2024** median. The revised guide should label the year beside each figure and use the newer O*NET/BLS 2025 wage benchmark when presenting the most current official wage number.

BLS also notes that some recreation jobs may qualify with high school education and short employer training, while employers for positions such as coordinating activities in a residential-care facility may prefer a bachelor's degree in recreation and fitness or a related field. Some employers require certifications such as CPR or first aid. These are employer/job-specific requirements, not universal credentials for the occupation.

BLS reports that recreation workers may work in recreation centers, parks, summer camps and other settings; many jobs can be part-time, irregular or seasonal. The occupation also has meaningful injury exposure, supporting explicit safety, emergency-plan, equipment, environmental, and escalation boundaries in the revised guide.

## Canada — official comparison

Government of Canada Job Bank places **Program leaders and instructors in recreation, sport and fitness** in **NOC 54100**. Job Bank describes these workers as leading and instructing individuals or groups in recreational, sports, fitness or athletic programs, in settings that can include community centres, recreation facilities, health-care facilities, retirement homes, government, resorts, correctional institutions and private businesses.

For the current recreation-program-leader profile, Job Bank states that the occupation usually requires a **secondary school diploma or several weeks of on-the-job training**. Job Bank also states that job prospects vary by province or territory.

Current national Job Bank wage data, updated **November 19, 2025** using the **2023–2024 Labour Force Survey** reference period, report:

- low: **CAD $15.44/hour**;
- median: **CAD $19.00/hour**;
- high: **CAD $31.00/hour**.

NOC 54100 is a useful broad comparison for recreation-program leadership/instruction. It must not be used to claim that every Activities Coordinator title maps exactly to NOC 54100; actual duties, supervision, health-care setting, management authority, and provincial/employer requirements must be checked.

## Colombia — current public training pathways

### SENA Betowa — complementary recreation leadership training

SENA Betowa currently lists **Liderazgo en la actividad deportiva y la recreación**, a **40-hour**, in-person, complementary special course. The current record shows a minimum age of 14 and a stated competency related to directing human talent according to organizational needs. Current 2026 offerings have appeared in locations including Cundinamarca, Bogotá and Chocó, demonstrating an active public-training pathway. Availability and admission remain subject to SENA schedules, capacity and selection procedures.

This 40-hour course is a useful skill-development option; it is not a universal occupational license and must not be represented as equivalent to a technical or technological degree.

### Bogotá SCRD–SENA pathway

A current 2026 agreement between Bogotá's Secretaría de Cultura, Recreación y Deporte and SENA includes **Tecnólogo en Actividad Física**. The official call describes learning related to healthy lifestyles, physical-activity programs, recreation, group activities, community or educational settings, wellness, and safe exercise according to age and general condition. The listed 2026 cohort is a substantially longer formal training pathway than the 40-hour complementary course.

A separate official SCRD program page lists the activity-physical technologist pathway at **3,520 hours**. The current 2026 call should be used for active-program context, while the 3,520-hour page demonstrates the scale of this longer-form training option. Applicants should verify the currently offered curriculum, duration, intake dates and eligibility directly before enrolling.

SENA also reported in May 2026 on learners in **Técnico en Ejecución de Programas Deportivos**, including exposure to recreation models, injury-prevention topics, functional play and sports-sector institutions. This provides additional evidence of a public sports/recreation training ecosystem but does not establish a universal credential requirement for recreation-coordinator jobs.

## Free-first, funding, and scholarship strategy

### United States

- a **WIOA-Eligible Training Program Finder** linking to state directories of eligible training providers;
- an **American Job Center Finder** for free employment assistance, career counseling, training information, accessibility accommodations and referrals;

WIOA eligibility, local priorities, program approval and available funding vary. A program appearing in an eligible-provider directory does **not** guarantee that a specific applicant will receive funding.

### Canada

Training support, student aid and employment-service funding vary by province or territory, institution and personal eligibility. The revised guide should direct readers to current provincial/territorial employment services and recognized colleges or training institutions rather than promise a national funding entitlement.

### Colombia and Latin America

In Colombia, start with SENA/Betowa and SENA public employment services. Elsewhere in Latin America, use OIT/Cinterfor to identify the appropriate national public vocational-training institution and then verify current programs directly.

## Apprenticeships and work-based learning

There is no universal Registered Apprenticeship requirement for Recreation Workers. **Apprenticeship.gov** provides a current finder for apprenticeship jobs and programs. Readers should search recreation, youth-program, community-program, sports, parks, event, and related keywords by location rather than assume a registered apprenticeship exists for this exact title.

Work-based learning may also come through seasonal recreation programs, supervised volunteering, municipal parks programs, camps, community centers, senior programs, disability-inclusive programs, schools, resorts, nonprofit programs, or employer-paid onboarding. Any work with children or vulnerable participants must follow the employer's screening, supervision and safeguarding requirements.

## Employer-supported development

Current evidence supports emphasizing employer-provided or employer-recognized training in areas such as:

- site and program orientation;
- activity-specific safety procedures;
- emergency action plans;
- first aid/CPR where the employer requires it;
- safeguarding and mandatory-reporting procedures where applicable;
- incident documentation;
- accessibility and disability inclusion;
- de-escalation and professional boundaries;
- volunteer or participant supervision;
- records, scheduling and program software;
- privacy and cybersecurity;
- transportation procedures when driving is part of the job; and
- advancement into senior recreation, coordinator, supervisor or program roles.

Get any repayment obligation for employer-funded education in writing.

## Safety, safeguarding, accessibility, privacy, and authority boundaries

The revised guide must preserve the following boundaries:

- **Safety:** Follow the employer's risk assessment, equipment, weather, facility, staffing and emergency procedures. Do not improvise activities outside training or authorized scope.
- **Water activities:** Do not represent a general recreation worker as a lifeguard or aquatic-safety professional without the required certification and assignment.
- **Medical/clinical boundaries:** Recreation work is not recreational therapy, nursing, physical therapy, occupational therapy, athletic training, or medical treatment unless the worker holds the relevant credential and role.
- **Children and vulnerable participants:** Follow background-check, supervision, safeguarding and mandatory-reporting rules that apply to the employer and jurisdiction. Do not conduct unauthorized investigations.
- **Accessibility:** Plan inclusive participation and reasonable accommodations through employer procedures. Adaptive or inclusive recreation does not itself confer rehabilitation-clinician authority.
- **Transportation:** Drive participants only when formally authorized and when licensing, insurance, vehicle, training and supervision requirements are satisfied.
- **Privacy:** Protect participant, child, family, disability, health, incident and emergency-contact information. Use only approved systems and role-based access.
- **Cybersecurity:** Use approved accounts/devices, multifactor authentication where provided, safe file sharing, phishing awareness and prompt incident reporting.
- **Artificial intelligence:** Do not place confidential participant, child, health, disability, safeguarding, payment or unpublished organizational information into a public AI service unless the organization has approved that system and data use. Human review remains required for schedules, safety communications, translations, participant information and commitments.
