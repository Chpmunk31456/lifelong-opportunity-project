## Critical classification boundary

`Data Analyst` is a cross-cutting job title rather than one exact occupation code across all employers or countries. The 2026 controlled revision must therefore use a **multi-benchmark model** and explain scope differences instead of asserting false one-to-one equivalence.

Planned controlled benchmarks:

- United States primary quantitative/statistical benchmark: O*NET-SOC **15-2041.00 — Statisticians**, because O*NET explicitly lists `Data Analyst`, `Data Analyst (Nof)` and `Junior Data Analyst` as approved apprenticeship titles and includes core data-analysis/data-quality/reporting activities;
- U.S. adjacent benchmark: O*NET-SOC 15-2051.01 — Business Intelligence Analysts for business/reporting-oriented roles, already treated separately in Guide 84;
- Canada: NOC **21223 — Database analysts and data administrators** for the Job Bank title `Data Analyst - Informatics and Systems`, with explicit scope caveat;
- Colombia: more than one CUOC group may be relevant depending on the job's actual functions, including database/data-management, BI/business analytics and statistics-heavy work.

- occupation-specific classification caveats;

## Baseline controls

- No legacy numeric claim is accepted without current-source verification.
- The U.S. Statisticians benchmark must be clearly described as more mathematically/statistically advanced and graduate-heavy than many commercial entry-level data-analyst jobs.
- Canada NOC 21223 must be described as database/data-administration oriented rather than a universal match for all data analysts.
- Colombia mapping must be function-based and may use multiple CUOC groups instead of one artificial universal code.
- Analysts must not silently alter source data, hide material exclusions, overstate causation, bypass access controls or present AI-generated conclusions as verified evidence.
- Publication remains fail-closed until trilingual parity, source/link checks and DOCX/PDF package QA pass.
