## United States — occupation, entry pathway, wages, and outlook

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics

The current Occupational Outlook Handbook profile for **Wind Turbine Service Technicians (SOC 49-9081)** reports:

- May 2024 median pay: **$62,580 per year / $30.09 per hour**.
- Lowest 10 percent: below **$49,110**; highest 10 percent: above **$88,090**.
- Employment: **13,600 in 2024**, projected to **20,500 in 2034**.
- Projected change: **6,800 jobs**, or **50 percent**, for 2024–2034. The BLS fastest-growing table gives the unrounded rate as **49.9 percent**.
- About **2,300 openings per year** on average over the decade.
- Typical entry education: **postsecondary nondegree award**; related work experience: **none**; typical training: **long-term on-the-job training**.

BLS describes inspection, testing, troubleshooting, maintenance, data collection, and electrical, mechanical, and hydraulic repair. It also records tower heights over 200 feet, outdoor and confined-space work, travel, on-call work, and physical demands including climbing and lifting more than 50 pounds. These occupational descriptions do not independently authorize a new worker to perform hazardous or regulated tasks.

Sources:

- https://www.bls.gov/ooh/installation-maintenance-and-repair/wind-turbine-technicians.htm
- https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/fastest-growing-occupations.htm

## United States — safety and controlled-work boundaries

### Falls and tower access

OSHA's wind-energy fall resource states that workers may be exposed to falls inside and outside towers over 100 feet. It distinguishes construction requirements, where fall protection generally applies at **6 feet (1.8 m) or more**, from general-industry maintenance requirements, where it generally applies at **4 feet (1.2 m) or more**, subject to the applicable standards and work conditions.

Version 2 must preserve that construction/maintenance distinction and must not teach climbing, anchor selection, harness setup, transfer, descent, or rescue. Workers must use the employer's approved access, fall-protection, communication, and rescue systems and stop when equipment, authorization, weather, or rescue readiness is uncertain.

Source: https://www.osha.gov/green-jobs/wind-energy/falls

### Electrical and hazardous energy

OSHA identifies shock, arc-flash, fall, and thermal-burn hazards in wind work. Its lockout/tagout material notes electrical, mechanical, and stored energy and states that only authorized employees may apply locks or tags. The applicable energy-control framework requires employer programs, training, inspection, isolation, and control when unexpected energization, startup, or stored-energy release could injure workers.

The guide must not teach lockout execution, verification, energized testing, electrical repair, switching, or release of stored energy. It must direct readers to employer procedures and qualified/authorized personnel and require stop-and-escalate action when energy state or authority is unclear.

Sources:

- https://www.osha.gov/green-jobs/wind-energy/electrical
- https://www.osha.gov/green-jobs/wind-energy/lockout-tagout
- https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.269

### Confined spaces, lifting, fire, and severe weather

OSHA notes that nacelles can be confined spaces and may be permit-required confined spaces when qualifying hazards exist. Its wind-energy crane/hoist material requires trained or qualified operators and highlights wind, lightning, visibility, and suspended-load hazards. Its fire material identifies electrical equipment, combustible materials, and lubricants and emphasizes emergency and evacuation planning.

Version 2 must not provide entry testing, permit-space, rigging, hoisting, firefighting, or rescue procedures. It must reinforce controlled access, exclusion zones, employer plans, trained/qualified roles, weather monitoring, emergency communication, and stop-work/escalation.

Sources:

- https://www.osha.gov/green-jobs/wind-energy/confined-spaces
- https://www.osha.gov/green-jobs/wind-energy/crane-hoist
- https://www.osha.gov/green-jobs/wind-energy/fire

## Canada — occupation mapping, wages, and trade boundaries

Government of Canada Job Bank maps the wind-turbine-technician title to **Construction millwrights and industrial mechanics (NOC 72400)**. This is the closest published occupational-group mapping, not a wind-specific national credential.

Current national Job Bank wage evidence, updated November 19, 2025, reports:

- Low: **CAD $24.00/hour**
- Median: **CAD $37.00/hour**
- High: **CAD $52.00/hour**

Job Bank states that secondary school and training/vocational education are usually required. Eligibility for trade certification usually requires a **three- to four-year apprenticeship** or more than five years of experience plus industry courses. Industrial mechanic (millwright) certification is available but voluntary across provinces and territories; the page also directs users to provincial/territorial regulatory authorities because requirements vary.

The Red Seal Program describes Red Seal as an endorsement on a provincial or territorial trade certificate, not a standalone wind credential. Wind work may cross millwright, electrical, crane/hoist, or other regulated scopes. Readers must verify the actual duties, apprenticeship status, compulsory/voluntary trade rules, registration, certification, and employer requirements with the responsible provincial or territorial authority.

## Colombia — related SENA pathways and legal boundaries

No current wind-turbine-specific SENA program was located during this research. SENA/Betowa currently publishes related industrial pathways:

- **Mantenimiento Electromecánico Industrial** — Tecnólogo, **3,984 hours**.
- **Electricidad Industrial** — Tecnólogo, **3,984 hours**.

These are related pathways, not wind-specific training, a guaranteed current cohort, or automatic legal authorization. Program availability, location, admission, and places must be checked in the live Betowa listing.

Sources:

- https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/mantenimiento-electromecanico-industrial?modality=P&offertype=company&programId=130695
- https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/electricidad-industrial?modality=P&offertype=company&programId=17003

The Ministry of Mines and Energy's current RETIE page states that electrical-installation design, construction, operation, maintenance, and intervention must be directed, supervised, and executed by technically and legally competent persons. Professional scope is determined through the applicable profession laws and councils, not by general training or RETIE alone.

The live Ministry page contains an internal year inconsistency for Resolution 40284: its introductory text and traceability table do not agree. The reader-facing guide must refer users to the **current RETIE published by the Ministry** and must not assert the disputed year without verification from the controlling resolution.

Source: https://www.minenergia.gov.co/es/misional/energia-electrica-2/reglamentos-tecnicos/reglamento-t%C3%A9cnico-de-instalaciones-el%C3%A9ctricas-retie/

Colombia's Resolution 4272 of 2021 framework defines work at height at exposure above **2.0 m** and establishes employer program, training, authorization, fitness, prevention/protection, and rescue-plan responsibilities. This threshold must not be blended with U.S. OSHA thresholds. Training or a certificate does not remove employer authorization, fitness, site controls, or rescue requirements.

Source: https://www.cancilleria.gov.co/sites/default/files/Normograma/docs/resolucion_mtra_4272_2021.htm

## Controlled Version 2 requirements derived from research

The English master must:

1. Keep U.S. SOC 49-9081, Canadian NOC 72400, and Colombia pathway evidence separate.
2. Distinguish observation, housekeeping, parts handling, documentation, and supervised support from climbing, rescue, energized work, LOTO, troubleshooting, torque/repair, confined-space entry, rigging/hoisting, blade repair, commissioning, and sign-off.
3. Preserve the exact jurisdictional fall-height controls: U.S. construction **6 feet (1.8 m)**, U.S. general-industry maintenance **4 feet (1.2 m)**, and Colombia **above 2.0 m**.
4. Require employer authorization, task-specific training, competent/qualified roles, approved procedures, PPE, communication, weather controls, energy control, exclusion zones, and rescue readiness as applicable.
5. Use stop-and-escalate language for uncertain energy state, fall protection, access, weather/lightning, lifting, confined/restricted space, damaged equipment, dropped-object exposure, fatigue, communications, or work outside authorization.
6. Avoid step-by-step instructions for hazardous maintenance, climbing, fall arrest, rescue, lockout/tagout, electrical testing/repair, hydraulics, stored energy, rigging/hoisting, blade repair, torque, fire response, or commissioning.
7. Present Apprenticeship.gov, FAFSA, SENA, and regional public vocational systems as live verification routes, never guaranteed admission, funding, placement, credential, or authorization.
8. Explain that Canadian requirements and regulated scopes vary by province/territory and that Red Seal does not override them.
9. Explain that Colombia's related SENA pathways are not wind-specific and that RETIE/professional-scope and work-at-height duties require current local verification.
10. Include responsible-AI limits: AI may assist study, terminology, and job-search organization but must not replace site procedures, manuals, hazard assessment, electrical/mechanical diagnosis, energy-control decisions, lift plans, weather decisions, rescue plans, inspections, or qualified-person judgment.
11. Protect site coordinates, access routes, turbine/SCADA data, credentials, customer/operator records, equipment serials, fault histories, photographs, and emergency information.
12. State that the guide does not guarantee employment, wages, openings, admission, funding, apprenticeship, certification, licensing, authorization, or earnings.
