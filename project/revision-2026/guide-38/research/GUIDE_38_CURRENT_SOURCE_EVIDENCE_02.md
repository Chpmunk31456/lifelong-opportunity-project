## United States — occupation scope and entry path

Primary occupational anchor: U.S. Department of Labor O*NET 43-5032.00, **Dispatchers, Except Police, Fire, and Ambulance**.

O*NET describes this occupation as scheduling and dispatching workers, crews, equipment, or service vehicles for movement of materials, freight, or passengers or for field service/repair work. Typical tasks include preparing daily schedules, relaying work orders, maintaining dispatch records, monitoring personnel/equipment locations, and advising personnel about traffic, weather, construction, or other hazards.

O*NET classifies the occupation in Job Zone 1–2. A high school diploma or GED is commonly expected, although requirements vary, and on-the-job training may range from a few days to one year. O*NET also identifies a Registered Apprenticeship title, **Dispatcher, Service**, with alternate title **Transportation Management Coordinator**.

Official U.S. 2025 wage data reported through O*NET/BLS: median **$24.20/hour** and **$50,340/year**. Employment was about **218,700 in 2024**; projected employment change for 2024–2034 is **-1% or lower**, with about **18,500 annual openings** from growth and replacement needs.

Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-5032.00

## Canada

Government of Canada Job Bank places truck-dispatcher work under **NOC 14404 — Dispatchers**. Typical requirements include completion of secondary school; non-emergency dispatchers generally receive informal on-the-job training. Job Bank states this occupation is not regulated nationally. National truck-dispatcher wages are currently shown at approximately **CAD $20.00 low / $28.00 median / $41.76 high per hour**, based on the cited Labour Force Survey reference period.

Sources:
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/19376/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/19376/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/19376/ca

For roles focused more heavily on route and crew scheduling than active dispatch, Job Bank uses **NOC 14405 — Transportation route and crew schedulers**. Current national summary information shows a median wage near **CAD $32.69/hour**; secondary school plus several years of relevant transportation-sector experience is usually required, and on-the-job training may be provided.

## Funding and work-based learning

United States: American Job Centers and CareerOneStop can help eligible learners identify WIOA-supported training. Funding is eligibility- and location-dependent and must not be presented as guaranteed. Registered Apprenticeship opportunities can be searched through Apprenticeship.gov.

Canada: eligible post-secondary learners may qualify for Canada Student Grants and Loans, subject to federal/provincial/territorial rules and program eligibility.

Employer support should be emphasized because dispatch and transportation-coordination roles often involve employer-specific systems, routing tools, radio/communications procedures, safety rules, and operating policies. Ask about paid onboarding, cross-training, tuition assistance, reimbursement, trainee programs, and internal promotion pathways.

## Safety, privacy, cybersecurity, and AI boundaries

The reconstructed guide should distinguish ordinary commercial/transportation dispatch from regulated emergency dispatch and specialized aviation/rail roles. Learners should be told to follow employer rules for driver/customer data, location data, credentials, routing systems, radio/communications tools, incident escalation, and safety-sensitive decisions.

AI may be useful for practice, drafting neutral non-sensitive updates, or explaining general routing concepts, but should not replace approved dispatch systems, legal/safety requirements, live operational judgment, or employer-authorized calculations. Confidential customer, employee, cargo, routing, pricing, security, or location data should not be pasted into public AI tools without explicit authorization.
