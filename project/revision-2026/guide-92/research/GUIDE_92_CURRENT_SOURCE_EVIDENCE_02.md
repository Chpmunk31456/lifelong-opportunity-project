# Guide 92 — Current-source Evidence 02

**Guide:** 92 — Geographic Information Systems Technician  
**Controlled branch:** `revision/guide-00-100-2026`  
**Research review date:** 2026-08-22

## Research conclusion
Geographic Information Systems Technician has strong direct title/function mappings across the three target jurisdictions:
- **United States:** O*NET-SOC **15-1299.02 — Geographic Information Systems Technologists and Technicians**;
- **Canada:** **NOC 22214 — Technical occupations in geomatics and meteorology**, which directly includes Geographic Information Systems (GIS) Technician;
- **Colombia:** **CUOC 31123 — Técnicos en cartografía**, which explicitly includes **Técnico sistemas de información geográfica**.

No blocker prevents controlled reconstruction. The critical U.S. disclosure is that O*NET wage and employment statistics for 15-1299.02 are collected from **Computer Occupations, All Other** and therefore are not a pure GIS-technician statistical population.

## United States — direct occupational profile
O*NET 15-1299.02 is **Bright Outlook** and updated for 2026. It describes GIS technologists/technicians who assist in building, maintaining, modifying or using GIS databases and may perform custom application development or user support.

Current O*NET tasks support guide content covering:
- data layers, maps, tables and reports;
- cartographic/graphic representations;
- GIS database maintenance;
- user/client technical support;
- programming/data analysis/software development for GIS;
- data entry/digitizing/vector conversion;
- data currency, accuracy, usefulness, quality and documentation review;
- geospatial modeling and spatial analysis;
- integrated spatial/non-spatial databases;
- remote sensing/cartographic data integration;
- aerial/orthophoto interpretation;
- internet mapping/GIS applications;
- user requirements, training and troubleshooting.

## Canada — NOC 22214
Canada Job Bank directly maps Geographic Information Systems (GIS) Technician to **NOC 22214 — Technical occupations in geomatics and meteorology**.

Current national summary (modified August 7, 2026) describes GIS/geomatics workers who gather, analyze, interpret and use geospatial information for natural resources, geology, environmental research, land-use planning and related applications.

### Canada wages
Updated November 19, 2025:
- low **C$23.08/hour**;
- median **C$38.10/hour**;
- high **C$53.85/hour**.

### Canada typical requirements
Job Bank says:
- secondary school completion is required;
- geomatics technologists/technicians typically require a college program in geomatics, cartography, photogrammetry, aerial survey, remote sensing, GIS or geomatics;
- in Quebec, membership in the regulatory body for professional technologists is required to use the title **Professional Technologist**.

Guide 92 must frame the Quebec point as a title/professional-status requirement rather than claiming every GIS technician role across Canada is regulated the same way.

### Canada outlook
Current Job Bank outlook varies by province/territory. National 2024–2033 projection indicates a **strong risk of labour surplus** for NOC 22214. Three-year outlooks range from Limited to Moderate in many provinces/territories. This must be presented candidly rather than obscured by the green-job label or national wage level.

## Colombia — CUOC 31123
OCUPACOL **CUOC 31123 — Técnicos en cartografía**, competence level 3, explicitly includes the occupational title **Técnico sistemas de información geográfica**.

Relevant functions include:
- assisting with map content, format and design;
- collecting information from aerial photographs, measurements, records and maps;
- generating maps, graphics and charts using digital/cartographic techniques;
- inspecting final work for completeness and accuracy;
- interpreting aerial photography;
- operating digital/computerized cartographic equipment and systems;
- remote-sensing support for geology, forestry, planning and science.

This is a direct and defensible Colombian comparison for Guide 92.

### Colombia wage caution
Do not fabricate a representative national GIS Technician wage from OCUPACOL's displayed historical labour-market indicators. Where methodology or representativeness is limited, state that limitation and point readers to live employer/local evidence.

## Core GIS technical model required for Guide 92

### Spatial data types
Readers should understand:
- vector points, lines and polygons;
- raster/grid data;
- attribute tables;
- joins/relates;
- geodatabases/spatial databases;
- imagery/remote-sensing data;
- tabular data with location fields.

### Coordinate reference systems
Required concepts:
- geographic versus projected coordinate systems;
- datum;
- units;
- EPSG/coordinate-reference identifiers conceptually;
- transformation/reprojection;
- distortion and fitness for purpose.

A layer displaying visually in the right place does not prove the CRS metadata is correct.

### Data quality and lineage
Required controls:
- source/provenance;
- capture date/time;
- positional accuracy;
- attribute accuracy;
- completeness;
- consistency;
- scale/resolution;
- CRS/datum;
- transformations;
- edit history;
- authoritative versus derived status.

Do not silently overwrite or transform authoritative source data without preserving lineage and change evidence.

### Geometry and topology
Guide should explain:
- invalid geometries;
- overlaps/gaps;
- dangling lines;
- duplicate features;
- self-intersections;
- snapping/tolerance;
- topology rules that depend on the business domain.

Automated topology checks can detect conditions but cannot decide every domain rule without requirements.

### Georeferencing and digitizing
Required workflow:
1. verify source and target CRS;
2. choose reliable control points;
3. use an appropriate transformation;
4. inspect residual/error evidence;
5. validate against independent known references where feasible;
6. document source, method and limitations.

### Cartography and communication
Maps should communicate rather than merely look attractive. Required topics:
- map purpose/audience;
- scale;
- symbology;
- classification method;
- legend/title/source/date;
- units;
- labels;
- uncertainty/limitations;
- color/contrast/accessibility;
- avoiding misleading area/size/color choices.

### Spatial analysis
Potential techniques include buffers, overlays/intersections, proximity, spatial joins, network analysis, raster analysis and terrain/surface analysis. The guide must teach readers to verify assumptions, units, CRS and data quality before interpreting results.

### SQL, Python and automation
SQL can support attribute/spatial queries and database work. Python/ArcPy or other scripting can automate repetitive geoprocessing. Automation must preserve inputs, parameters, outputs, version information and error handling where relevant.

Never run a destructive batch process against authoritative production data without backup/change authority and validation.

### Web GIS and sharing
Understand hosted layers, map/feature services, permissions, sharing groups, public/private exposure, caching and service dependencies at a support level. Publishing a map or layer can expose sensitive data even if the raw database remains protected.

## Privacy, security and sensitive locations
Geospatial data can reveal:
- home or person locations;
- customer/asset locations;
- critical infrastructure;
- environmental/cultural resources;
- vulnerable populations;
- operational routes or facilities.

Required controls:
- need-to-know access;
- approved sharing/publication;
- aggregation/generalization/redaction where required;
- removal of unnecessary identifiers;
- secure credentials/tokens;
- protected field-device/data handling;
- incident escalation for unintended exposure.

Do not upload protected geospatial data to public map services, consumer AI tools or personal cloud storage without authorization.

## Responsible AI
AI may help with code explanation, geocoding review, feature extraction, metadata drafts, classification support, documentation and synthetic practice datasets when policy allows.

Controls:
- do not upload protected location data, customer/asset coordinates, credentials or proprietary layers to unapproved tools;
- validate generated code and geoprocessing logic;
- check coordinate systems, units and spatial assumptions;
- assess bias/error in automated feature extraction or classification;
- preserve source lineage;
- do not let AI invent authoritative coordinates, legal boundaries or survey-grade accuracy;
- human/domain validation remains required.

## Accessibility
Maps can create accessibility barriers. Guide 92 should promote:
- readable text and contrast;
- non-color-only encoding;
- clear legends and labels;
- text/tabular alternatives for key map information where practical;
- keyboard-accessible web-map controls when applicable;
- alt text/context for static maps;
- avoiding unnecessarily dense visual layouts.

Automated accessibility checks do not establish legal compliance.

## Safe portfolio guidance
Use public/open datasets, personal field data collected lawfully, or synthetic data. Safe examples:
- public-data thematic map with documented CRS/source;
- georeferencing exercise using non-sensitive imagery;
- vector QA/topology project;
- spatial SQL/Python workflow on open data;
- accessible map plus text/table alternative;
- metadata and lineage record;
- synthetic asset-inspection map.

Never publish protected employer/client layers, exact critical-infrastructure locations, private personal addresses, restricted cultural/environmental locations or proprietary database schemas.
