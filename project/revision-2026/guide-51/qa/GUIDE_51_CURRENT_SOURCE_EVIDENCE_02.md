## 1. Research question and controlled conclusion

This intake tests whether the legacy Guide 51 can be revised against current, authoritative evidence without overstating occupational scope, entry requirements, certification, wages, funding, or regional equivalence.

**Controlled conclusion:** PASS. Current sources support a revised opportunity guide for sterile processing / medical equipment preparation. The revised guide must present this as a safety-critical, supervised healthcare support occupation; distinguish education, employer preference, certification eligibility, and legal requirements; avoid procedural clinical instruction; and require readers to verify local law, employer rules, program outcomes, costs, and aid eligibility before acting.

## 2. Occupation identity and work

The closest U.S. federal occupation is O*NET-SOC **31-9093.00, Medical Equipment Preparers**. O*NET describes workers who prepare, sterilize, install, or clean laboratory or healthcare equipment. Reported titles include Sterile Processing Technician, Central Service Technician, Certified Registered Central Service Technician, and Medical Equipment Preparer.

Supported task themes include:

- cleaning and decontaminating instruments and equipment;
- assembling, inspecting, packaging, and organizing instrument sets;
- operating sterilization equipment within approved procedures;
- recording sterilizer results and maintaining processing records;
- storing and distributing processed supplies; and
- identifying damaged, incomplete, or improperly functioning items and escalating them.

These themes define career awareness, not a procedure manual. Device manufacturers' instructions for use, facility policies, applicable standards, competency validation, and supervision govern actual work.

## 3. Safety and quality boundary

Sterile processing personnel can encounter blood and other potentially infectious materials while handling contaminated instruments and sharps. OSHA's Bloodborne Pathogens Standard, 29 CFR 1910.1030, and OSHA's hospital central-supply guidance support the need for exposure controls, training, personal protective equipment, sharps precautions, and an employer exposure-control plan.

WHO describes medical-device reprocessing as a sequential system that includes cleaning, disinfection, and/or sterilization and depends on suitable infrastructure, equipment, validation, monitoring, and quality assurance. Health Canada likewise states that reusable-device manufacturers must provide effective reprocessing directions and, where needed, functionality testing. Canadian public-health guidance says reusable devices should be reprocessed by trained personnel under appropriately trained supervision, with centralized and audited processes where possible.

**Editorial control:** The guide may explain why the work matters and what learners should expect. It must not teach cycle parameters, chemical concentrations, device-specific reprocessing steps, load release, or clinical decision-making. Learners must follow current manufacturer instructions, facility policy, validated processes, and supervisor direction.

## 4. United States pathway and certification distinctions

### 4.1 Entry and preparation

O*NET reports that new entrants commonly need a high-school diploma or equivalent plus occupation-specific preparation, but employers and jurisdictions vary. Training may be offered by community colleges, technical schools, adult-education providers, workforce programs, employers, or hospital systems.

A program listing does not prove quality or job placement. Readers should verify:

- total tuition, fees, books, uniforms, immunization/background-check costs, and exam fees;
- whether hands-on placement is included and who arranges it;
- completion, certification-exam, placement, and refund data;
- whether the program satisfies a target employer's and jurisdiction's rules; and
- whether credits transfer.

### 4.2 HSPA CRCST

HSPA's Certified Registered Central Service Technician (CRCST) route requires **400 documented hands-on hours** in a sterile processing department. HSPA permits full certification when qualifying hours were completed within the previous five years, or provisional certification after passing the exam if the hours are completed and documented within six months.

HSPA allocates the 400 hours across decontamination; preparation and packaging; sterilization/disinfection; storage/distribution; and quality assurance. HSPA states that it does not arrange placements. Therefore, a prospective learner must confirm how they will obtain qualifying experience before paying for training or an exam.

### 4.3 CBSPD CSPDT

CBSPD's Certified Sterile Processing and Distribution Technician (CSPDT) is a separate credential with separate eligibility. Its current technician page permits candidates to qualify through one of several routes, including specified sterile-processing employment, combined allied-health and sterile-processing experience, completion of a sterile-processing technician course with a passing grade of at least 70, or relevant healthcare-product sales/service experience. Current exam windows, fees, forms, and requirements must be rechecked directly before applying.

### 4.4 Legal and employer variability

Certification is not a universal nationwide prerequisite. HSPA's current legislative tracker identifies certification requirements in several states and active or future-dated provisions may differ by state and hire date. Employer requirements can be stricter than law.

**Required learner instruction:** Before choosing a credential or program, check the current law or regulator for the work state, read target-employer postings, and verify eligibility directly with HSPA or CBSPD. The guide must not imply that HSPA and CBSPD credentials are interchangeable or that either is mandatory everywhere.

## 5. U.S. wages and outlook

O*NET's 2026-updated occupation page, using BLS data, reports for Medical Equipment Preparers:

- **2025 median wage:** **$22.93 per hour / $47,700 per year**;
- **2024 employment:** **76,500**; and
- **2024–2034 outlook:** much faster than average (7% or higher), with approximately **10,900 projected openings**.

These are national occupation-level figures, not an entry wage or guarantee. Local pay varies by region, setting, shift, union coverage, experience, certification, and employer.

For the required non-government comparison, Salary.com displayed a current market estimate of approximately **$39,640 per year / $19 per hour** for the title Sterile Processing Technician when researched. This figure is methodology-dependent, is not a government statistic, and must be labeled separately. The large difference from the BLS/O*NET occupation median is another reason not to blend sources or promise pay.

## 6. Canada pathway

Canada Job Bank uses the title **Medical Device Reprocessing Technician** for the relevant pathway. Its national requirements page says the occupation usually calls for secondary-school completion and a **six- to nine-month** sterile-processing college program. Its national wage report for reference period 2023–2024 lists approximately:

- **low:** CAD **$18.00/hour**;
- **median:** CAD **$23.00/hour**; and
- **high:** CAD **$29.81/hour**.

Job Bank classifications are national summaries, not universal licensing or hiring rules. Requirements, job titles, collective agreements, and preferred credentials vary by province and employer. Readers must verify the applicable provincial regulator or health authority, target-employer posting, program admission requirements, practicum arrangements, and current Job Bank data.

## 7. Latin America and Colombia

The occupation may not map cleanly to a single standalone credential or job title across Latin America. Related work can sit within sterilization centers, surgical-instrumentation teams, nursing support, dental services, or institution-specific technical roles. The guide should support regional search terms while avoiding a false one-to-one equivalence with the U.S. occupation.

For Colombia, the Ministry of Health and Social Protection maintains an official medical-device and biomedical-equipment page with a section on reprocessing reusable medical devices and elements. The page links **Resolution 914 of 2025** and its technical manual. Ministry materials state that Resolution 914 was published on 2025-07-03 with an eighteen-month deferred effective period, pointing to **2027-01-03**.

At the research date, the Ministry had also published a **draft replacement resolution and manual for public consultation**. The draft says it would repeal Resolution 914, but a consultation draft is not enacted law.

**Controlled Colombia wording:**

- do not describe the 2026 draft as final or effective;
- tell readers and institutions to recheck the Ministry's current official page and enacted text before relying on any date or requirement;
- use SENA's Betowa training portal and Agencia Pública de Empleo as live discovery tools, not proof that a standalone sterile-processing program or vacancy is currently available;
- treat adjacent Colombian roles as related pathways, not automatic credential equivalence; and
- verify employer, occupational-profile, education, and health-sector requirements for the exact role.
