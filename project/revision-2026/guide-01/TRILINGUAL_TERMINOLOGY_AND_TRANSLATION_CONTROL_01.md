# Guide 01 — Trilingual terminology and translation control

**Guide:** Community Health Worker  
**Languages:** English, neutral Latin American Spanish (es-419), Brazilian Portuguese (pt-BR)  
**Control version:** 1.0  
**Date:** August 3, 2026  
**Status:** Completed terminology baseline for translation drafting and later parity QA

## 1. Purpose

This register controls high-risk occupation, credential, funding, privacy, and employment terms before translation of the Guide 01 English working master. It is designed to prevent false equivalence between occupations, credentials, legal statuses, and funding arrangements across jurisdictions.

Automated or AI-assisted translation must not be represented as independent professional translation certification. Final publication candidates remain subject to structural, terminology, link, DOCX, PDF, metadata, and rendering QA.

## 2. Core occupation terminology

| English source term | es-419 controlled rendering | pt-BR controlled rendering | Control note |
|---|---|---|---|
| community health worker | trabajador/a comunitario/a de salud | agente comunitário de saúde | Use a neutral generic label. Do not imply that every jurisdiction recognizes the same regulated occupation. |
| CHW | trabajador/a comunitario/a de salud (CHW, por sus siglas en inglés) on first use | agente comunitário de saúde (CHW, na sigla em inglês) on first use | Retain CHW only where it helps users identify English-language vacancies or official U.S. sources. |
| promotora/promotor de salud | promotora o promotor de salud | promotora ou promotor de saúde | Preserve as a regionally used title, not a universal equivalent. |
| patient navigator | navegador/a de pacientes | navegador(a) de pacientes | Do not imply clinical authority. |
| peer navigator | navegador/a par | navegador(a) de pares | Explain in context where the title may be unfamiliar. |
| outreach worker | trabajador/a de alcance comunitario | profissional de alcance comunitário | Avoid literal phrasing that sounds unnatural in the target language. |
| community health representative | representante comunitario/a de salud | representante comunitário de saúde | May have a specific institutional meaning in some Indigenous or public-health systems; retain caution. |
| health coach | orientador/a de salud | orientador(a) de saúde | Do not translate as physician, therapist, or licensed clinician. |
| lay health adviser | asesor/a comunitario/a no clínico/a de salud | orientador(a) comunitário(a) não clínico(a) de saúde | Make the non-clinical scope explicit. |

## 6. Income and labour-market controls

- Preserve all currencies exactly as stated in the controlled English source.
- Do not convert U.S. or Canadian wages into Colombian or other Latin American salary expectations.
- Translate **median** as **mediana** in Spanish and **mediana** in Portuguese, not as average.
- Translate **average** as **promedio** in Spanish and **média** in Portuguese.
- Preserve the distinction between annual, monthly, hourly, per-visit, and per-project compensation.
- Keep official government data separate from non-government market estimates.
- Any market estimate must retain its source, geography, date, sample limitations, and non-guarantee language.
- Do not imply that a national occupational group is perfectly equivalent to every local job title.

## 7. Geographic and institutional names

Official organization names and acronyms should normally remain unchanged on first reference, followed by a natural-language explanation where useful.

Examples:

- U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS)
- Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)
- Government of Canada Job Bank
- Servicio Nacional de Aprendizaje (SENA)
- Servicio Público de Empleo
- Empresas Promotoras de Salud (EPS)
- Instituciones Prestadoras de Servicios de Salud (IPS)
- Empresas Sociales del Estado (ESE)

Do not translate organization names in a way that creates a nonexistent legal entity.

## 8. Accessibility and plain-language rules

For both target languages:

1. Retain the numbered section sequence and heading hierarchy of the English master.
2. Use short paragraphs and direct sentences.
3. Avoid unnecessary anglicisms when a natural regional term exists.
4. Explain unavoidable English acronyms on first use.
5. Use gender-inclusive but readable language; avoid constructions that reduce screen-reader clarity.
6. Preserve warnings, limitations, and non-guarantee statements.
7. Keep links visible and usable in Markdown, DOCX, and PDF outputs.
8. Avoid literal translations that sound bureaucratic, clinical, or machine-generated.
9. Preserve list structure so trilingual parity can be checked mechanically.
10. Maintain UTF-8 encoding and correct accents, punctuation, and language-specific quotation conventions.

## 9. Required translation-parity checks

Before publication, verify that each target edition preserves:

- all 13 main sections;
- every warning about licensing, certification, accreditation, employment, and income;
- all U.S., Canada, Colombia, and broader Latin America pathways;
- all funding and work-based-learning categories;
- all official income figures and dates;
- every source URL;
- privacy and responsible-AI safeguards;
- the 30-day action plan;
- the final QA limitations.

Any deliberate adaptation must be recorded in a translation-change log with the source passage, target passage, rationale, and reviewer status.

## 10. QA gate result

**Result:** PASS for terminology-baseline readiness.

The English working master may proceed to es-419 and pt-BR translation drafting under this control register. This is not final translation approval. Final publication still requires bilingual editorial review where available, structural parity testing, terminology QA, live-link validation, DOCX/PDF generation, metadata checks, rendered-page inspection, checksums, and publication-manifest completion.
