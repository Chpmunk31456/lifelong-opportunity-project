## Status

- Guide: 01 — Community Health Worker
- Purpose: authoritative source baseline for the controlled English revision
- Review date: 2026-08-03
- Status: source research complete for this baseline; integration into the English master remains pending
- This record does not claim independent medical, legal, accreditation, accessibility, translation, or professional certification review.

## Editorial controls established

1. Treat `community health worker` as a broad occupational family whose titles, duties, education, certification, registration, supervision, and scope vary by jurisdiction and employer.
2. Do not equate a community health worker with a nurse, physician, psychologist, social worker, or other licensed clinician.
3. Distinguish voluntary certificates, employer training, academic credentials, government-recognized certification, registration, licensure, apprenticeships, and informal community experience.
4. Separate wages from stipends, grants, scholarships, reimbursements, and loans.
5. Present national wage data as a reference point rather than a guarantee; explain that local pay varies by region, employer, union status, schedule, language skills, and duties.
6. Protect patient and community information in all portfolio, interview, and AI-use examples.

## United States

### Role definition and scope

**Centers for Disease Control and Prevention — Resources for Community Health Workers**  
URL: https://www.cdc.gov/chronic-disease/php/community-health-worker-resources/index.html  
Current page date: December 3, 2024.

Use in revision:

- Define a CHW as a frontline public-health worker who is trusted by or closely connected to the community served.
- Explain the bridge role between residents and health or social services.
- Include commonly used titles such as `promotor(a) de salud`, peer navigator, lay health adviser, coach, and community health representative.
- Emphasize navigation, education, outreach, trust-building, and reduction of barriers related to social determinants of health.
- Do not present the CDC resource collection as a basic credential or license.

### Training resources

**CDC — A Community Health Worker Training Resource for Preventing Heart Disease and Stroke**  
URL: https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/dhdsp/programs/spha/chw_training/index.htm  
Last reviewed: April 27, 2022.

Use in revision:

- List as a free federal training/reference resource, particularly for cardiovascular-disease prevention and chronic-disease support.
- State that CDC describes the resource as more suitable for CHWs who already have some experience.
- State explicitly that it is not a replacement for basic CHW training and is not a universal credential.

### Employment and income

**U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Occupational Outlook Handbook: Community Health Workers**  
URL: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/community-and-social-service/community-health-workers.htm  
Last modified: August 28, 2025.

Use in revision:

- Use BLS as the primary U.S. occupation, duties, education, work-environment, employment, wage, and outlook source.
- Preserve the BLS occupation title and publication date in the source note.
- Do not convert national median wages into a promised entry salary.
- Explain that state certification or employer requirements may be more specific than the national occupation profile.

## Canada

### Occupational mapping and requirements

**Government of Canada Job Bank — Community Health Worker in Canada: Summary**  
URL: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/296075/ca  
Date modified: June 2, 2026.

**Government of Canada Job Bank — Community Health Worker in Canada: Requirements**  
URL: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/296075/ca  
Date modified: June 2, 2026.

Use in revision:

- Explain that Job Bank maps this title to NOC 42201, Social and community service workers.
- State that this Canadian mapping may cover broader social and community-service work than the U.S. BLS CHW occupation.
- Report that a college or university program in social work, child and youth care, psychology, or another social-science or health-related discipline is usually required.
- Report that relevant volunteer or support experience may replace formal education for some occupations in the unit group.
- Avoid claiming one national CHW license or one uniform Canadian certification pathway.

### Canadian wage reference

**Government of Canada Job Bank — Community Health Worker in Canada: Wages**  
URL: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/296075/ca  
Page modified: June 2, 2026; wage table updated November 19, 2025; reference period 2023–2024.

Controlled figures:

- Canada low: CAD 19.00 per hour
- Canada median: CAD 26.00 per hour
- Canada high: CAD 36.06 per hour
- Job Bank reports that 83.6% of workers in this occupation receive at least one non-wage benefit.

Use in revision:

- Label all figures as Job Bank national estimates for NOC 42201, not guaranteed CHW offers.
- Direct readers to province, territory, and regional tables because pay varies substantially.
- Keep nursing wages separate; a community health nurse is a different regulated occupation.

## Colombia

### Community organizations and health-system participation

**Ministerio de Salud y Protección Social — Resolución 2628 de 2025 materials for Organizaciones de Base Comunitaria**  
Repository index and supporting materials: https://www.minsalud.gov.co/sites/rid/lists/bibliotecadigital/forms/todos%20los%20items.aspx?p_id=3119&paged=true&pagedprev=true&pagefirstrow=1101&rootfolder=%2Fsites%2Frid%2Flists%2Fbibliotecadigital%2Fride%2Fvs&view=%7B11d53df9-d15a-4e4d-a04c-75416fa2a392%7D  
Supporting manuals published to the Ministry repository on June 30, 2026.

Use in revision:

- Add a Colombia-specific pathway that distinguishes employment by EPS, IPS, ESE, local government, NGOs, and community-based organizations.
- Explain that Resolution 2628 of 2025 establishes requirements and authorization processes for community-based organizations participating in integral health care.
- Do not imply that authorization of an organization automatically creates an individual professional license or certification for every worker.
- Direct readers to territorial health authorities for current local requirements.

## Funding, free-first learning, and work-based learning controls

The English revision must:

- Prioritize CDC and other government materials that are genuinely free.
- Direct U.S. readers to American Job Centers, state workforce agencies, community colleges, employers, public-health departments, and state CHW associations for current training support.
- Direct Canadian readers to provincial or territorial employment services, colleges, Indigenous health organizations, health authorities, and employer-sponsored training.
- Direct Colombian readers to SENA, territorial health secretariats, EPS/IPS/ESE employers, universities, NGOs, and authorized community-based organizations.
- Label every scholarship, grant, apprenticeship, stipend, tuition reimbursement, or employer program by jurisdiction and eligibility.
- Never describe a short course or completion certificate as equivalent to regulated licensure or an academic award.
