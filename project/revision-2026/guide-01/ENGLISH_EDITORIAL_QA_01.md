## 1. Review scope

This review examined the current English working master for:

- spelling, grammar, punctuation, and natural readability;
- heading hierarchy and plain-language organization;
- role-scope and credential controls;
- accessibility-oriented wording;
- funding, training, scholarships, employer support, apprenticeships, and paid work-based learning;
- United States, Canada, Latin America, and Colombia coverage;

This automated and editorial review is not independent human certification, professional translation certification, accreditation review, accessibility certification, legal review, medical review, or licensing advice.

## 2. Findings closed in the current master

### Language and readability

- The guide uses complete sentences, plain-language explanations, and consistent terminology.
- Abbreviations are introduced before repeated use.
- Dense regulatory distinctions are explained with examples rather than unexplained jargon.
- The tone is neutral, practical, and appropriate for adults entering or changing careers.
- The document avoids employment guarantees and unsupported claims of credential recognition.

### Structure and accessibility

- Headings follow a clear numerical sequence.
- Lists are used for scanability without replacing needed explanation.
- Duties, qualifications, income, funding, privacy, and action planning are separated into distinct sections.
- The guide does not rely on color, visual position, or images to convey essential meaning.
- Links are written as visible URLs so they remain usable in plain-text and assistive-technology contexts.

### Occupation and credential controls

- Community health work is distinguished from licensed clinical occupations.
- State, provincial, territorial, employer, and program variation is clearly stated.
- The guide distinguishes licenses, registrations, certifications, academic credentials, course-completion certificates, employer training, and reimbursement-related requirements.
- Canadian NOC 42201 is identified as a broader occupational group and is not presented as an exact U.S. CHW equivalent.
- Colombia pathways are presented as employer- and program-dependent rather than as a universal individual license.

### Opportunity and funding coverage

- Free and low-cost learning is presented before private paid programs.
- Funding, scholarships, employer-paid training, tuition reimbursement, stipends, internships, practicums, apprenticeships, and paid trainee roles are covered.
- Wages, stipends, grants, loans, reimbursements, and scholarships are explicitly separated.
- The guide warns against recruiter fees, guaranteed employment, and unsupported immigration or certification promises.

### Regional coverage

- United States, Canada, Colombia, and broader Latin America pathways are present.
- Regional titles are treated as potentially non-equivalent.
- Local legal status, contract type, language, fieldwork, driving, education, and residency requirements are identified as verification points.

### Income research controls

- U.S. BLS figures are labeled as official national reference data, not guaranteed entry wages.
- Canadian Job Bank figures are labeled as NOC 42201 estimates and not guaranteed CHW offers.
- U.S. and Canadian figures are not converted into Colombian salary expectations.
- Colombia and Latin America guidance prioritizes official salary schedules and disclosed employer vacancies before market estimates.
- The working master does not currently include a non-government estimate because no candidate has yet passed the evidence controls.

### Privacy and responsible AI

- The guide prohibits use of real patient or client information in portfolios.
- AI use is limited to approved, non-sensitive tasks and requires verification against official sources.
- The guide prohibits AI-based diagnosis, prescribing, eligibility decisions, or emergency decision-making.
- Employer-approved systems, retention requirements, and disclosure obligations are acknowledged.

## 3. Controlled corrections applied or confirmed

The current master was confirmed to use these preferred forms consistently:

- **community health worker** on first reference, with **CHW** introduced afterward;
- **health care** as a noun phrase;
- **public health** without unnecessary hyphenation;
- **work-based learning** when used as a compound modifier;
- **on-the-job training** when used as a compound modifier;
- **non-wage benefit** and **non-government estimate** with clear scope;
- **United States**, **Canada**, **Latin America**, and **Colombia** rather than ambiguous regional shorthand;
- **neutral Latin American Spanish** and **Brazilian Portuguese** for future edition labels.
