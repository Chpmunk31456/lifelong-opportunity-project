## Structural findings

The three working masters use the same controlled guide sequence and preserve the same substantive categories:

1. occupation description and scope;
2. suitability and transferable strengths;
3. education, training, and credential differences;
4. free and low-cost learning;
5. funding, scholarships, and employer support;
6. apprenticeships and paid work-based learning;
7. income and employment outlook;
8. ethical experience building;
9. responsible use of artificial intelligence;
10. job-search checklist;
11. first 30-day action plan;
12. controlled source register;
13. QA and review status.

The reviewed editions retain separate regional treatment for the United States, Canada, Colombia, and other Latin American countries. They also retain the distinction between employment, contract work, volunteer service, stipends, scholarships, grants, loans, reimbursement, apprenticeships, and certificates of completion.

## Controlled terminology findings

The following concepts remain materially aligned across the three editions:

- community health worker as a non-uniform occupational title;
- promotor/promotora de salud and agente comunitário de saúde as context-dependent titles rather than universal equivalents;
- explicit separation from licensed clinical professions;
- employer, jurisdiction, supervision, and local-law limitations;
- certificate of completion not being automatically equivalent to licensure, professional certification, accreditation, or an academic award;
- formal apprenticeship being distinguished from internships, practicums, placements, trainee roles, volunteer work, and stipend-supported projects;
- income figures being reference points rather than promises;
- official United States and Canadian data being presented with geography and occupational-group limitations;
- no direct conversion of United States or Canadian wages into Colombian salary expectations;
- no guarantee of employment, certification, registration, licensure, promotion, immigration result, or income.

## Regional parity findings

Each edition includes:

- United States pathways through official occupational, workforce, public-health, and apprenticeship resources;
- Canadian pathways through Job Bank, provincial or territorial services, public institutions, health authorities, Indigenous organizations, and employers;
- Colombian pathways through SENA, Servicio Público de Empleo, territorial health authorities, EPS, IPS, ESE, universities, employers, NGOs, and community organizations;
- broader Latin American pathways through ministries, municipal programs, primary-care networks, universities, NGOs, humanitarian organizations, and donor-funded projects.

## Income-data parity findings

The three editions preserve the same official reference points and limitations:

- United States Bureau of Labor Statistics figures for Community Health Workers;
- Government of Canada Job Bank figures for the broader NOC 42201 group;
- an explicit statement that no directly equivalent Colombian national wage series was verified in this batch;
- a requirement to label any non-government estimate by source type, geography, date, sample limitations, and occupational match;
- separation of wages from stipends, volunteer service, project payments, grants, and reimbursements.

No non-government income estimate is approved by this QA record. Such an estimate may be added only after it passes the evidence controls documented in the Guide 01 income register.
