# Guide 49 — Current Source Evidence 02

**Guide:** 49 — Dental Assistant  
**Branch:** `revision/guide-00-100-2026`  
**Research date:** August 19, 2026  
**Gate:** Research / Current Source Evidence  
**Research decision:** **PASS**

## Research boundary

This evidence set treats **Dental Assistant** as the U.S. occupation defined by O*NET/BLS and does not assume that a U.S. title, credential, radiography privilege, expanded function, or clinical task transfers directly to Canada, Colombia, or another Latin American jurisdiction. Dental-assisting scope is unusually jurisdiction-sensitive. Every clinical, radiographic, infection-control, credential, supervision, and delegated-duty statement in Version 2 must remain tied to applicable law, training, employer policy, and dentist supervision.

The research also distinguishes dental assistants from dental hygienists, dental therapists, dental laboratory assistants/technicians, and dentists. Similar or grouped occupational classifications must not be used to grant a Dental Assistant the higher-scope functions of another occupation.

## United States — occupation identity, duties, and scope

### O*NET OnLine — Dental Assistants, 31-9091.00

Current O*NET classifies Dental Assistants as **31-9091.00**, Bright Outlook, updated 2026. It describes the occupation as performing **limited clinical duties under the direction of a dentist**, with examples including patient preparation, instrument/equipment preparation and sterilization, chairside assistance, oral-health instructions, records, billing, scheduling, and related administrative work.

O*NET task examples also include exposing dental diagnostic x-rays, recording histories and vital signs, preparing materials, preliminary impressions, temporary restorations, fluoride, and other functions. These are occupational task examples, **not a universal authorization**. The Version 2 guide must state that radiography, expanded functions, coronal polishing, sealants, topical agents, impressions, restorative functions, and other hands-on duties depend on state law, required education/credentialing, supervision, and employer policy.

O*NET also identifies **Dental Assistant** as an example title approved for use in a U.S. Registered Apprenticeship Program.

Sources:  
https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/31-9091.00  
https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/31-9091.00

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — entry pathways, scope variation, pay, and outlook

BLS states that there are several paths into dental assisting. Some states require graduation from an accredited program and an exam; other states have no formal educational requirement for entry-level assisting and permit learning through on-the-job training. Many formal programs take about one year and lead to a certificate or diploma, while two-year associate-degree programs are less common.

BLS explicitly states that duties vary by state and dental office and that **each state regulates the scope of practice for dental assistants**. Some states require licensing, registration, or certification for entry or advancement and may set specific requirements for radiography, infection control, or expanded functions. The guide must direct readers to the applicable state dental board for current legal requirements before enrolling, testing, or performing regulated tasks.

BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook reports for Dental Assistants:

- **May 2024 median annual wage:** USD **$47,300**;
- median hourly wage: **$22.74**;
- lowest 10 percent: below **$36,190**;
- highest 10 percent: above **$61,780**;
- employment in 2024: approximately **381,900**;
- projected employment in 2034: approximately **406,300**;
- projected growth, 2024–2034: **6%**;
- projected numeric employment increase: approximately **24,400**;
- projected average annual openings: approximately **52,900**.

Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/dental-assistants.htm

## United States — state credential/radiography control

DANB maintains state-by-state dental assisting requirement compilations, including career levels, allowable functions, supervision, radiography requirements, expanded functions, and accepted/required exams. DANB is useful as a specialized national locator, but it does not replace the legally controlling state dental board, dental practice act, or administrative rules.

The Version 2 guide must therefore use this hierarchy:

1. applicable state dental board / state law and rules;
2. employer policy and dentist delegation/supervision within that legal scope;
3. DANB or other credential information as a supporting locator, not a universal national license.

Source: https://www.danb.org/state-requirements/state-publications-and-services

The guide must never say that DANB's CDA credential, radiography exam, or another national certification by itself authorizes every dental-assisting function in every state.

## United States — infection prevention and occupational safety

### CDC dental infection prevention

CDC's dental infection-prevention resources apply to dental health care personnel, expressly including dental assistants and dental-assisting students. Standard Precautions include hand hygiene, PPE, respiratory hygiene, sharps safety, safe injection practices, sterile instruments/devices, and environmental cleaning/disinfection.

CDC's sterilization guidance states that instrument reprocessing must be performed correctly and sequentially, according to manufacturer instructions, and assigned to personnel trained in the required reprocessing steps. Critical instruments should be heat sterilized; most semicritical items should also be heat sterilized when heat tolerant. Monitoring and recordkeeping are part of the infection-prevention system.

Sources:  
https://www.cdc.gov/dental-infection-control/hcp/summary/  
https://www.cdc.gov/dental-infection-control/hcp/summary/standard-precautions.html  
https://www.cdc.gov/dental-infection-control/hcp/dental-ipc-faqs/dental-sterilization.html

### OSHA dentistry / bloodborne pathogens

OSHA identifies dentistry as a work environment with potential exposure to bloodborne pathogens and other hazards. OSHA's Bloodborne Pathogens standard applies to occupational exposure to blood and other potentially infectious materials; saliva in dental procedures is treated as potentially infectious material for the standard's purposes. Dental assistants are among job classifications that may have occupational exposure.

Sources:  
https://www.osha.gov/dentistry  
https://www.osha.gov/bloodborne-pathogens/worker-protections

Version 2 must not reduce infection-control content to generic housekeeping. It must cover PPE, sharps and exposure response, contaminated instrument handling, sterilization/reprocessing training, manufacturer instructions, environmental controls, and workplace procedures.

## United States — patient privacy, records, and AI

HHS states that the HIPAA Privacy Rule protects medical records and other individually identifiable protected health information held by covered entities and limits uses/disclosures without authorization. The HIPAA Security Rule establishes administrative, physical, and technical safeguards for electronic protected health information (ePHI).

Sources:  
https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/index.html  
https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/index.html

The Version 2 guide must therefore prohibit copying patient names, images, radiographs, clinical histories, identifiers, insurance information, or other protected/confidential information into public or unauthorized AI systems. AI may support non-sensitive study, terminology practice, generic administrative drafting, or scenario rehearsal only when employer policy and privacy/security rules permit. AI must not diagnose, interpret radiographs for clinical decisions, determine treatment, prescribe, replace dentist judgment, or create unverified patient instructions.

## Canada — specific dental-assistant mapping, not a U.S. credential transfer

### NOC 33100 and OaSIS 33100.01

Canada's NOC 33100 is titled **Dental assistants and dental laboratory assistants**. Because the unit group combines distinct work, Version 2 should use the more specific OaSIS profile **33100.01 — Dental assistants** when explaining dental-assisting duties and must not import dental laboratory assistant duties into the role.

The NOC describes dental assistants as assisting dentists, dental hygienists, and dental therapists during patient examination/treatment and performing clerical functions. It explicitly excludes dental hygienists/dental therapists (32111) and dental technologists/technicians (32112).

Sources:  
https://noc.esdc.gc.ca/Structure/NOCProfile?GocTemplateCulture=en-CA&code=33100&version=2021.0  
https://noc.esdc.gc.ca/OaSIS/OaSISHierarchy

### Canada Job Bank — entry and regulatory requirements

Job Bank states nationally that dental assistants typically require either completion of an eight-month to one-year college or other dental-assisting program, or secondary school plus on-the-job training. It also states:

- registration with a regulatory body is required in all provinces except Quebec and Ontario;
- National Dental Assisting Examination Board certification is required to perform intra-oral duties in all provinces except Quebec;
- in Quebec, dental assistants do not perform intra-oral duties under the national occupational description.

Because occupational regulation can change and province-specific rules control practice, Version 2 must direct readers to the applicable provincial regulator and school/program requirements rather than presenting a single Canada-wide authorization.

Source: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/4475/ca

### Canada Job Bank — wages

Job Bank's current national wage page for Dental Assistant (NOC 33100) reports wages updated November 19, 2025, using a 2023–2024 reference period:

- national low: **CAD $21.00/hour**;
- national median: **CAD $27.00/hour**;
- national high: **CAD $35.00/hour**.

Job Bank also reports **72.3%** of workers in NOC 33100 receive at least one non-wage benefit. Provincial and regional figures vary materially, so the guide should link to Job Bank rather than imply the national median applies everywhere.

Source: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/4475/ca

### Canada student aid

For eligible students in qualified programs at designated schools, the Canada Student Grant for Full-Time Students can provide up to **CAD $4,200 per year / $525 per month of study** through the end of the 2026–27 school year, subject to financial need and program/school/province eligibility. Students apply through their province or territory. Quebec, the Northwest Territories, and Nunavut have their own student-aid arrangements rather than participating in this federal grant in the same way.

Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/education/student-aid/grants-loans/full-time.html

The guide must not assume every private dental-assisting program is designated or grant-eligible. Canada.ca also notes 2026 changes affecting federal aid eligibility at some private, for-profit schools; students must verify the current school/program status before relying on aid.

## Colombia — Auxiliares de salud oral, CUOC 53292

### Ministry of Labour OCUPACOL

Colombia's OCUPACOL identifies **CUOC 53292 — Auxiliares de salud oral**. Occupational denominations include auxiliar consultorio dental, auxiliar de consultorio odontológico, auxiliar de odontología, auxiliar de odontología e higiene oral, auxiliar de salud oral, auxiliar higiene oral, and ayudante odontología.

The profile describes workers who assist dentists during examinations/treatment and perform administrative, prevention, promotion, and oral-hygiene support functions. Listed functions include preparing the dental environment, preparing patients, assisting the dentist, sterilizing/organizing instruments, preparing materials, taking radiographs under technical procedure and dentist instructions, recording treatment information, managing supplies, giving oral-hygiene information, and assisting with impressions.

Source: https://ocupacol.mintrabajo.gov.co/Profile/OccupationalProfile/53292

OCUPACOL also contains a separate profile for **Higienistas y asistentes odontológicos** with broader functions. Version 2 must **not** copy higher-scope diagnostic, preventive, anesthetic, extraction, or other functions from that separate profile into CUOC 53292. The Guide 49 Colombia pathway is anchored to **Auxiliares de salud oral — CUOC 53292** unless a current regulator/source specifically establishes otherwise for the learner's credential and setting.

### Current Colombian auxiliary-health legal context

Decree 1409 of 2024 amended Colombia's health-sector regulatory decree and expressly lists **Auxiliar en Salud Oral** as auxiliary health personnel. The same decree establishes current certificate/training rules for auxiliary-health programs and states that holders must request the corresponding registration in **ReTHUS** through the Ministry of Health or its designated entity.

The decree distinguishes SENA/Subsistema de Formación para el Trabajo certificates from ETDH `Técnico Laboral por Competencias` certificates. Because these are regulatory requirements, Version 2 must tell learners to verify the current approved program, certificate type, ReTHUS process, and scope with the competent Colombian authorities rather than treating a generic private course as sufficient authorization.

Source: https://normograma.sena.edu.co/compilacion/docs/decreto_1409_2024.htm

### SENA — updated Técnico Laboral en Salud Oral pathway

SENA Resolution 2057 of July 4, 2025 records an update to the **Técnico Laboral en Salud Oral** program. SENA describes the updated program as preparing talent for administrative and assistive oral-health functions, promotion/prevention, support to the dentist in dental consultation, appointment scheduling, activity recording, risk management, and patient safety.

Source: https://normograma.sena.edu.co/compilacion/docs/resolucion_sena_2057_2025.htm

SENA's Betowa catalog currently displays **SALUD ORAL** as a presencial, titulada, technical pathway and shows cohort-specific offerings. Enrollment periods, centres, employer-requested cohorts, exact duration metadata, and availability change over time, so the guide must link learners to the current Betowa offer rather than promise an open seat.

Source: https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/salud-oral

### Colombia source discrepancy requiring fail-closed editorial treatment

The current Betowa program page/search metadata may display legacy/program duration information that does not obviously reconcile with the general auxiliary-health certificate duration language amended by Decree 1409 of 2024. SENA Resolution 2057 of 2025 confirms the program itself was updated, but the public sources reviewed do not provide a simple text-only reconciliation of every duration field.

**Control:** Version 2 must **not publish a single definitive Colombian program duration from these sources unless the exact current approved program/cohort documentation is verified**. It may state that SENA has a current/updated Técnico Laboral en Salud Oral pathway and instruct readers to verify the current Betowa listing, centre, curriculum, certificate, and ReTHUS requirements before enrollment.

### Reprocessing / sterilization context in Colombia

Ministry of Health Resolution 914 of 2025 establishes requirements for reprocessing reusable medical devices/elements and identifies auxiliary nursing or **auxiliar de salud oral**, as applicable, among personnel who may perform reprocessing activities under the regulated system. This supports strong sterilization/reprocessing content but does not authorize independent practice beyond the applicable workplace and professional framework.

Source: https://normograma.sena.edu.co/compilacion/docs/resolucion_minsaludps_0914_2025.htm

## Latin America and Caribbean pathway

Dental-assistant titles, training systems, dental practice laws, radiography rules, delegated duties, credentials, and scope vary across Latin America and the Caribbean. OIT/Cinterfor maintains a regional network of vocational-training institutions and should be used as a **locator**, after which the learner must verify the current national health, education, radiation-safety, and professional regulator requirements.

Regional source: https://www.oitcinterfor.org/  
Institutional network: https://www.oitcinterfor.org/red-institucional

The guide must not imply that a U.S., Canadian, Colombian, or other national dental-assisting credential automatically transfers legal scope across borders.

## Clinical safety, radiography, privacy, and AI controls required in the English master

The controlled English Version 2 must include explicit boundaries that:

- all clinical and intra-oral functions depend on applicable law, training, credential status, delegation/supervision, and employer policy;
- the learner must verify current radiography education, examination, registration/licensing, and supervision requirements before exposing dental radiographs;
- dental assistants do not independently diagnose oral disease, interpret radiographs for diagnosis, prescribe, determine treatment plans, perform irreversible procedures, or represent themselves as dentists/hygienists/therapists when they are not;
- infection prevention includes hand hygiene, PPE, sharps safety, exposure response, instrument transport, cleaning, disinfection, sterilization, environmental controls, manufacturer instructions, and required monitoring/records;
- protected/confidential patient information, images, radiographs, histories, insurance data, and identifiers must not be entered into public or unauthorized AI tools or systems;
- AI may assist with non-sensitive study, terminology practice, mock scheduling, generic communication drafts, or scenario rehearsal only when permitted;
- AI must not substitute for dentist judgment, radiographic interpretation, diagnosis, treatment planning, emergency decisions, medication instructions, infection-control policy, or approved patient records; and
- any patient-facing information drafted with AI must be checked against approved employer/clinical sources before use.

## Source hierarchy and evidence decision

The controlled revision will use official/public sources as the factual backbone: U.S. DOL O*NET/BLS, Apprenticeship.gov/CareerOneStop, CDC/OSHA/HHS, Government of Canada NOC/OaSIS/Job Bank/Canada Student Aid, Colombia Ministry of Labour OCUPACOL, SENA legal/training systems, Ministry of Health rules compiled by SENA, and OIT/Cinterfor. DANB is retained as a specialized state-requirements locator with state dental boards/law controlling. Salary.com is retained only as a clearly labeled current non-government U.S. market estimate.

**Research gate: PASS.** The evidence is sufficient to reconstruct an occupation-specific English Version 2 while preserving state/provincial/national distinctions, dental-scope boundaries, infection prevention, radiography controls, privacy, and responsible AI. The next permitted gate is **English Editorial / controlled Version 2 reconstruction**.
