## Occupational and authority parity

PASS:

- all three editions identify current O*NET **43-4031.00** as the combined Court, Municipal, and License Clerks occupation;
- none uses obsolete 43-4031.01 as the current code;
- all preserve the warning that U.S. national O*NET/BLS figures are not court-clerk-only statistics;
- federal Courtroom Deputy examples remain explicitly federal examples;
- court titles do not create judicial discretion, legal-advice authority, power to alter orders, waive requirements, disclose restricted information, or represent parties;
- procedural information remains distinct from individualized legal advice and merits analysis;
- legally trained judicial-law-clerk roles remain distinguished from administrative court-clerk work.

## Jurisdictional parity

PASS:

- Canada remains separately mapped to **NOC 14103** and employer-specific requirements;
- Colombia preserves the distinction between judicial `funcionarios` and other `empleados` without claiming cross-border title equivalence;
- the Colombian Auxiliar Judicial (Grado 4) description remains a specific institutional example rather than a universal definition;
- neither localization implies that U.S., Canadian, Colombian, or broader Latin American court authority is transferable by translation alone.

## Records, privacy, cybersecurity, and payment parity

PASS. All three editions preserve controls for sealed/restricted records, personal identifiers, juvenile, jury, witness, criminal-justice, financial, medical, immigration, credential, payment, and internal judicial information.

Federal CM/ECF redaction categories remain explicitly federal examples subject to governing rule, local procedure, case type, exception, sealing order, and employer policy.

MFA, phishing, unusual payment requests, credentials, software restrictions, official systems, and incident escalation remain present.

## Accessibility and language-access parity

PASS. The translations preserve court-specific accommodation, interpreter, and language-access verification. Neither claims that one U.S. court procedure or statute applies identically to all courts or foreign judicial systems.

## AI parity

PASS. All editions preserve the safe default that protected court information stays out of public/unapproved AI, require authoritative verification and human review, and prohibit treating generic AI as judge, adjudicator, authorized clerk, legal adviser, or official record.
