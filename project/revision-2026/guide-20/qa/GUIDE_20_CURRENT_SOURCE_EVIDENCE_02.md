# Guide 20 — Current-source evidence 02

**Guide:** 20 — Court Clerk and Judicial Services Assistant  
**Research date:** 2026-08-09  
**Status:** **PASS — controlled evidence set for English reconstruction**

This evidence ledger controls current factual claims for the 2026 Guide 20 revision. It prioritizes official occupational, judiciary, government, and education/workforce sources. Court titles and authority vary sharply by jurisdiction, so no source below should be generalized beyond its stated system.

## 1. United States occupational mapping — O*NET 43-4031.00

**Source:** O*NET OnLine, `43-4031.00 — Court, Municipal, and License Clerks`  
https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/43-4031.00

**Current status:** O*NET page updated 2026. The older `43-4031.01 — Court Clerks` code is no longer in use; O*NET directs users to 43-4031.00.

**Controlled occupational facts:**

- The combined occupation performs clerical duties for courts of law, municipalities, and governmental licensing agencies.
- Court-related tasks listed by O*NET include filing court documents, recording case dispositions and court orders, examining documents for adherence to court procedures, answering inquiries about judicial procedures, preparing documents recording outcomes of proceedings, preparing/issuing court orders within assigned processes, obtaining information for the court, and preparing dockets/calendars.
- This O*NET code combines court, municipal, and license-clerk work. National wage/employment values therefore must be described as the **combined 43-4031.00 occupation**, not as a court-clerk-only statistic.

**Job-zone and education controls:**

- Job Zone 1–2: Very Little to Some Preparation Needed.
- O*NET states the occupation usually requires a high-school diploma or GED, though some occupations may not.
- Training ranges from a few days to one year of on-the-job training.
- O*NET respondent education distribution currently displays 50% high-school diploma/equivalent, 18% some college/no degree, and 13% associate’s degree as the education needed for a new hire.

**Current U.S. labor-market controls reported through O*NET/BLS:**

- 2025 median wage for combined 43-4031.00: **USD 23.41/hour; USD 48,700/year**.
- 2024 employment: **180,400**.
- Projected 2024–2034 growth: **3% to 4%**.
- Projected job openings 2024–2034: **18,500**.
- O*NET reports **98%** of employment in government for the combined occupation.

**Interpretation control:** Do not describe the 18,500 figure as 18,500 new court-clerk jobs; O*NET states projected openings include growth and replacement. Do not imply that the combined occupation’s salary is a guaranteed court salary.

## 2. Current federal Judiciary role evidence

**Source:** U.S. Courts — Court Administration and Services Careers / Courtroom Deputy  
https://www.uscourts.gov/careers/who-works-judiciary/court-administration-and-services-careers

**Controlled facts:** Current federal court career guidance describes courtroom deputies as assisting judges with orderly processing and management of proceedings and a judge’s caseload. Functions may include calendaring, monitoring deadlines, preparing minutes, processing orders, filing documents, operating electronic court recording, organizing exhibits, tracking case progress, supporting jury selection, scheduling reporters/interpreters, and answering questions from the public and judge.

**Boundary control:** These are federal Judiciary examples, not universal authority for every U.S. state/local court employee or foreign judicial system.

## 3. Current federal job-posting evidence — requirements vary by court

**Sources:** Current 2026 U.S. Courts courtroom-deputy postings, including Utah and Maryland district-court examples.

Examples reviewed show that federal courtroom-deputy roles can require:

- high-school graduation/equivalent;
- progressively responsible clerical/administrative experience;
- recurring application of rules, regulations, directives, laws, specialized terminology, and automated systems;
- strong accuracy, deadline, communication, public-service, and computer skills;
- court-specific specialized experience for higher classification levels;
- background investigation/fingerprinting and suitability requirements in some courts;
- U.S. Judiciary employment eligibility/citizenship conditions as applicable;
- adherence to the Code of Conduct for Judicial Employees.

A current Maryland federal posting also illustrates duties such as attending proceedings, maintaining detailed logs, swearing witnesses, managing exhibits, preparing minute entries, opening/closing cases, docketing, transmitting notices/judgments/orders, and furnishing general information to the public and bar.

**Control:** These postings are examples of real current hiring conditions, not national minimum requirements. Every vacancy must be read individually.

## 4. Ethics, confidentiality, and public-information boundary

**Source:** U.S. Courts — Code of Conduct for Judicial Employees  
https://www.uscourts.gov/administration-policies/judiciary-policies/ethics-policies/code-conduct-judicial-employees

**Controlled facts:** The Code is a primary federal Judiciary ethical source for judicial employees. It emphasizes integrity and independence, avoidance of impropriety, appropriate standards in official duties, and restrictions on outside/political activities.

A published federal-court version of Canon 3 states that a judicial employee should not make public comment on the merits of pending or impending actions except within authorized official duties; it distinguishes explanation of court procedures from comment on the merits and prohibits disclosure of confidential information received through official duties except as required in performing those duties.

**Guide control:** Court-clerk service information must distinguish neutral procedural information from legal advice, merits commentary, judicial decision-making, or disclosure of confidential/nonpublic information.

## 5. Electronic filing, redaction, and privacy

**Source:** U.S. Courts — CM/ECF FAQs  
https://www.uscourts.gov/court-records/file-a-case-cm-ecf/faqs-case-management-electronic-case-files-cm-ecf

**Controlled federal examples:** Federal procedural rules and local court rules govern electronic filing. U.S. Courts describes federal redaction rules for specified personal identifiers, including Social Security/taxpayer-identification numbers, dates of birth, names of minor children, financial-account numbers, and home addresses in criminal cases subject to the governing rules and exemptions.

U.S. Courts also states that CM/ECF uses integrity-verification mechanisms for filed documents.

**Control:** The guide must not turn federal redaction examples into universal rules for every court. Users must follow the exact court, jurisdiction, case type, sealing order, local rule, and employer procedure.

## 7. Canada — NOC 14103 Court clerks and related court services occupations

**Sources:** Government of Canada Job Bank  
Summary: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/2312/ca  
Requirements: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/2309/ca

**Controlled current facts:**

- NOC **14103** covers Court clerks and related court services occupations.
- Job Bank describes administrative, security, and support functions such as scheduling trials, processing service fees, calling court to order, preparing dockets, maintaining exhibits, recording proceedings, maintaining court records, facilitating court security/public safety, liaising with law enforcement, and supporting the judiciary.
- Employment includes federal, provincial, territorial, and municipal courts and police forces.
- Job Bank summary currently reports a **C$29.81/hour national median wage**.
- The summary page was modified April 21, 2026.
- Requirements page indicates completion of secondary school is required; on-the-job training is provided for court officers, court clerks, and court liaison officers; some roles may require a 1–2 year post-secondary diploma; Special Constable Certification may be required for court officers/court liaison officers.
- Job Bank currently reports that this NOC occupation is not regulated nationally in Canada in its professional-certification/licensing field.

**Control:** “Not regulated” on Job Bank does not mean every court job has identical eligibility. Employer, province, security, appointment, language, education, and role-specific requirements still apply.

## 8. Colombia — judicial branch structure and employee boundary

**Sources:** Función Pública — Rama Judicial and Ley 270 de 1996 as amended by Ley 2430 de 2024  
https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/gestor-normativo/manual-estructura-del-estado/rama-judicial  
https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?0=&i=6548

**Controlled facts:** Colombia’s Rama Judicial includes courts and tribunals across multiple jurisdictions and administrative judicial bodies. Current statutory text distinguishes judicial **funcionarios** (including magistrates and judges and, under the cited statutory framework, prosecutors) from other **empleados** occupying posts in judicial corporations, chambers, and administrative judicial entities.

**Boundary control:** A judicial assistant/auxiliary or secretarial employee is not to be described as exercising the decision-making authority of a judge or magistrate merely by virtue of working in the Rama Judicial.

## 9. Colombia — example Auxiliar Judicial functions

**Source:** Función Pública Gestor Normativo — Acuerdo 006 de 2018, Auxiliar Judicial (Grado 4)  
https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?i=85519

This official role example describes an Auxiliar Judicial in a Secretaría Judicial supporting higher levels and includes functions such as reviewing documents/memorials and giving them the legally appropriate processing, organizing and updating records/files, complying with orders in judicial decisions, registering procedural actions in authorized information systems, and performing notifications according to applicable law.

**Control:** This is an official role example in a defined Colombian institutional context, not a universal job description for all Colombian courts or all “auxiliar judicial” titles.

## 10. Accessibility and language/service access

Current federal court sources demonstrate court-specific access-coordinator and accommodation procedures for participants with communication disabilities. The exact legal basis and procedure varies by court and system.

**Guide control:** Encourage candidates to learn and follow their court’s accessibility, interpreter, language-access, and accommodation procedures. Do not assert that a single U.S. disability statute or one court’s process applies identically to every judiciary or to foreign systems.

## 11. Responsible AI control

No generic AI tool should be treated as authorized to receive sealed, restricted, confidential, personally identifying, juvenile, criminal-justice, jury, witness, payment, authentication, or nonpublic court information.

The English reconstruction should require:

- employer/court approval before using AI with work content;
- no unapproved upload of case records or confidential information;
- authoritative verification of AI-generated legal/procedural claims;
- human review before docketing, issuing, filing, notifying, calendaring, or communicating official information;
- no delegation of judicial discretion or authoritative court decision-making to a generic AI system;
- retention of auditability and official-system records according to court policy.

This is an operational safety rule for the guide, not a claim that one universal court AI policy exists.

## 12. Research decision

**PASS.** The source set is sufficient to reconstruct the controlled English Guide 20 master without relying on stale legacy salary or credential claims.

The English revision must preserve these distinctions:

1. O*NET 43-4031.00 is a combined court/municipal/license-clerk occupation;
2. federal Judiciary role examples are not universal U.S. court rules;
3. procedural information is distinct from legal advice and merits commentary;
4. court orders, notifications, docket actions, fees, records, and courtroom functions occur only within assigned authority and approved procedures;
5. privacy/redaction/sealing requirements are court- and case-specific;
6. Canada NOC 14103 is separate from U.S. and Colombian systems;
7. Colombian judicial employees must not be equated with judges or judicial decision-makers;
8. employment requirements must be verified vacancy by vacancy;
9. AI and cybersecurity controls must be operational and fail closed for protected information.
