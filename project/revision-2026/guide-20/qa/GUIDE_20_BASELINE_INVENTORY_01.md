## Occupational-risk classification

Guide 20 is a public-sector/judicial-services guide with elevated procedural and legal-boundary risk. Court clerks and judicial-services assistants may receive filings, maintain dockets and records, process fees, prepare calendars, administer or support courtroom workflow, issue or process documents under delegated authority, and communicate procedural information. Exact duties, hiring systems, oath/background requirements, civil-service status, court technology, public-record rules, fee handling, language-access duties, and authority vary materially by jurisdiction and court level.

The revision therefore requires fail-closed controls for:

- distinction between procedural/administrative information and legal advice;
- no implication that a clerk may exercise judicial discretion or attorney authority unless a specific jurisdictional rule establishes it;
- court-specific filing, records, sealing, privacy, public-access, retention, and authentication requirements;
- cash/fee/payment controls where applicable;
- protection of personally identifiable, sealed, confidential, juvenile, medical, financial, criminal-justice, and other restricted information;
- accessibility and language-access obligations without inventing universal requirements;
- cybersecurity controls for electronic filing, case-management, identity, and payment systems;
- responsible AI controls appropriate to court records and adjudicative environments;
- jurisdiction-specific education, examination, civil-service, background, appointment, and training requirements;
- no assumption that U.S., Canadian, Colombian, or other Latin American court occupations are interchangeable.
