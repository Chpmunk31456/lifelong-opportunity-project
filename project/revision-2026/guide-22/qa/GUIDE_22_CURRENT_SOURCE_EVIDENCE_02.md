# Guide 22 — Current source evidence 02

**Guide:** 22 — Real Estate Sales Agent and Leasing Consultant  
**Research date:** 2026-08-10  
**Stage:** Current-source research  
**Status:** **PASS**

## Research boundary

This register supports the controlled 2026 revision of Guide 22. It separates regulated real-estate brokerage from unlicensed leasing, administrative, marketing, property-management, or customer-service work. Licensing, permitted duties, advertising, disclosures, agency relationships, handling of deposits, and continuing education vary by jurisdiction and must be checked before a learner pays for training or performs regulated work.

## United States — occupation, education, licensing, and official income

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics

**Source:** U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook, *Real Estate Brokers and Sales Agents*  
**URL:** https://www.bls.gov/ooh/sales/real-estate-brokers-and-sales-agents.htm  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

BLS states that brokers and sales agents help clients buy, sell, and rent properties. Typical entry education is a high school diploma or equivalent. Every U.S. state requires real-estate brokers and agents to be licensed; sales agents work with a real-estate broker, while brokers may be licensed to manage their own real-estate businesses. Learners must therefore use their state real-estate commission or board for current licensing requirements rather than treating one national course as sufficient.

For May 2024, BLS reports a **$56,320 median annual wage for real-estate sales agents** and **$72,280 for real-estate brokers**. BLS projects overall broker/sales-agent employment to grow **3% from 2024 to 2034**, with about **46,300 openings per year** on average over the decade. BLS also notes that many workers are self-employed, so occupation-level wage statistics should not be interpreted as a guaranteed salary or net business income.

The broader BLS quick-facts figure combines brokers and sales agents and should not replace the occupation-specific medians above when the guide discusses a sales-agent pathway.

## United States — current non-government income estimates

Private salary platforms are supplementary estimates, not official wage statistics. Their samples, title matching, treatment of commission income, and geographic mix differ.

- **Indeed — Real Estate Agent:** approximately **$100,988/year average base salary**, based on about 99,100 salary observations/job-posting records over the prior 36 months; page updated **July 20, 2026**. URL: https://www.indeed.com/career/real-estate-agent/salaries
- **Indeed — Leasing Consultant:** approximately **$19.71/hour average base salary**, based on about 17,800 salary observations/job-posting records over the prior 36 months; page updated **July 20, 2026**. URL: https://www.indeed.com/career/leasing-consultant/salaries

These private estimates should be labeled by title and date and should not be blended with BLS medians into a single figure. Commission structures, self-employment costs, brokerage splits, bonuses, occupancy incentives, location, property type, and experience can materially change actual earnings.

## United States — fair housing, advertising, and accessibility boundary

**Source:** U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development, *Housing Discrimination Under the Fair Housing Act*  
**URL:** https://www.hud.gov/helping-americans/fair-housing-act-overview  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

HUD states that the Fair Housing Act prohibits housing discrimination because of race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, or disability in renting, buying, mortgage-related activity, and other housing-related activities. HUD also identifies real-estate agents, owners, property managers, lenders, homeowners associations, and others affecting housing opportunities as potential subjects of discrimination complaints.

Guide 22 must therefore warn against discriminatory steering, availability statements, advertising, showing practices, screening, terms, or service. State and local protected classes can be broader than federal law and must also be checked.

Accessibility should be treated as an operational responsibility: provide usable communication and reasonable access consistent with applicable law and employer policy, and do not use disability-related assumptions to screen or steer prospects.

## United States — funding and lower-cost training controls

**Source:** CareerOneStop, Local Training Finder / WIOA training information, sponsored by the U.S. Department of Labor  
**URL:** https://www.careeronestop.org/Credentials/Toolkit/find-local-training-help.aspx  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

CareerOneStop identifies programs marked WIOA as eligible for WIOA training funding for people who qualify, while individual eligibility must still be determined. Learners should check their American Job Center and state eligible-training list before paying for real-estate, sales, business, property-management, customer-service, or related training. A program's WIOA eligibility does not itself satisfy a state's real-estate licensing requirements.

Employer-paid pre-licensing, exam preparation, onboarding, continuing education, software/CRM training, leasing training, or reimbursement may be available from some brokerages and property employers, but these are employer-specific benefits rather than guaranteed funding.

Learners may also search Apprenticeship.gov for current work-based-learning opportunities, but the guide must not imply that a registered apprenticeship for real-estate sales or leasing is available in every location or at every time.

## Canada — occupational mapping, licensing, and income

### Government of Canada Job Bank

**Source:** Job Bank, residential real-estate agent / NOC 63101  
**Wages URL:** https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/6181/ca  
**Requirements URL:** https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/6181/ca  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

Job Bank maps residential real-estate agents to **NOC 63101**. The national wage page currently displays annual earnings of approximately **CAD $32,867 low, $58,400 median, and $178,000 high**, updated November 19, 2025. Job Bank notes that many workers are self-employed and that these annual earnings are based on Census-derived information, so they must be dated and described as occupation-level estimates rather than guaranteed compensation.

Job Bank states that secondary school is generally required, completion of a real-estate training course is required, and **provincial or territorial licensure is required**. The guide must therefore direct learners to the current regulator in the province or territory where they intend to work. NOC classification alone does not authorize brokerage activity.

Leasing roles can differ from licensed residential real-estate agent roles. Duties and provincial rules should be checked before assuming that a leasing-consultant position requires, or does not require, real-estate registration.

## Colombia — real-estate and urban-residential leasing boundary

### Ley 820 de 2003 / SUIN-Juriscol

**Source:** Colombia, Ley 820 de 2003, Article 28, SUIN-Juriscol  
**URL:** https://suin-juriscol.gov.co/clp/contenidos.dll/Leyes/1669010  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

Article 28 provides that a natural or legal person whose principal activities include renting urban residential real estate owned by themselves or third parties, or commercial intermediation between landlords and tenants, in municipalities with more than 15,000 inhabitants must register with the competent administrative authority. The registration is indispensable for the activities described by the article. The provision also covers certain owners or sublessors with more than five rental contracts.

Guide 22 must present this as a **specific Colombian urban-residential leasing/intermediation rule**, not as a blanket statement that Colombia uses the same licensing model as a U.S. real-estate salesperson or a Canadian provincial registrant. Learners must verify current national and municipal requirements for the exact activity and location.

Property transfers, registry acts, notarial functions, mortgage advice, valuations, tax advice, and legal opinions must not be represented as automatically within a salesperson's or leasing worker's authority.

## Latin America — controlled regional framing

Real-estate brokerage, leasing, consumer protection, advertising, privacy, contract, notarial, and land-registration rules vary substantially across Latin America. The revised guide should teach learners to verify the competent national, state/provincial/departmental, municipal, consumer-protection, tax, notarial, and property-registration authorities for the country and activity involved. It should not present a U.S. real-estate licence, Canadian NOC, Colombian registration rule, or private certification as a regional credential.

Public vocational institutions, employment services, universities, technical institutes, employer training, chambers/industry bodies, and official scholarship programs should be checked before expensive commercial courses.

## Privacy, screening, and AI use

Sales and leasing workflows can contain identity documents, contact information, income/employment data, credit or screening information, bank or payment information, family information, accessibility requests, contracts, signatures, and other sensitive data. Workers must follow applicable privacy, consumer-reporting, security, fair-housing, contractual, and employer requirements and minimize data collection and retention.

AI may assist with study plans, generic marketing drafts, checklists, CRM-note cleanup, plain-language explanations, and non-authoritative first drafts when organizational policy permits. It must not be relied on as the authoritative source for licensing, protected-class screening, legal rights, contract terms, required disclosures, property valuation, mortgage eligibility, title status, tax consequences, or final housing-availability decisions.

Do not upload sensitive prospect, applicant, owner, tenant, buyer, seller, financial, identity, contract, or screening information to an external AI system unless the organization has explicitly authorized the tool and the use complies with applicable privacy, security, confidentiality, retention, and fair-housing requirements. Material facts and legally significant outputs require authoritative verification and appropriate human review.
