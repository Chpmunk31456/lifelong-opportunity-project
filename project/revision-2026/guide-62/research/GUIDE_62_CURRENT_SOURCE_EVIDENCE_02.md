## Research scope

## United States — occupation, entry requirements, wages, and outlook

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook describes teacher assistants as workers who support licensed teachers by giving students additional attention and instruction. Common titles include teacher aides, instructional aides, paraprofessionals, education assistants, and paraeducators.

BLS reports that teacher assistants typically need at least two years of college coursework. In public schools, workers generally need at least two years of college coursework or an associate degree. Requirements can be higher or otherwise modified by state, district, program, and Title I rules, so candidates must verify the actual local employer requirements rather than assuming a national credential automatically qualifies them.

BLS May 2024 occupational data report:

- median annual wage: **USD $35,240**;
- employment in 2024: approximately **1,422,800**;
- projected employment change, 2024–2034: **-1%**;
- projected annual openings: approximately **170,400**, driven largely by replacement needs.

Annual earnings require context because many school-based jobs follow a school-year calendar or part-time schedule. An annual figure should not be converted to an hourly rate without knowing the actual paid weeks and hours.

Official source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/education-training-and-library/teacher-assistants.htm

## U.S. occupation coding and apprenticeship status

The BLS wage/outlook series above is reported for the broader teacher-assistant occupational group. Apprenticeship.gov separately lists the detailed O*NET occupation **25-9042.00 — Teaching Assistants, Preschool, Elementary, Middle, and Secondary School, Except Special Education**. These should not be treated as interchangeable coding systems without explanation.

At the time of this research pass, Apprenticeship.gov reports that O*NET 25-9042.00 is **not currently approved for use in a Registered Apprenticeship Program**. Candidates should therefore not assume that a Registered Apprenticeship pathway exists nationally for this occupation. Employers or local systems may still offer paid onboarding, paraeducator development, work-based learning, or other non-Registered-Apprenticeship training.

Official source: https://www.apprenticeship.gov/apprenticeship-occupations/listings?occupationCode=25-9042.00

## U.S. student privacy boundary

Teacher assistants may have access to education records or sensitive student information only within the school’s authorized role structure. U.S. Department of Education FERPA guidance explains that schools may disclose education records to school officials, including qualifying support personnel, without consent only when the institution’s criteria are met and the person has a legitimate educational interest.

The Version 2 guide must therefore instruct readers to use only approved school systems, access only information needed for assigned duties, avoid unauthorized disclosure, and follow school/district rules for records, messaging, devices, cloud tools, and AI systems.

Official source: https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/faq/under-ferpa-may-educational-agency-or-institution-disclose-education-records-any-its-employees

## Canada — occupation, education, wages, and outlook

Government of Canada Job Bank identifies **Elementary and secondary school teacher assistants, NOC 43100**. The occupation supports students and assists teachers and counsellors with teaching and non-instructional tasks. Depending on the role, duties can include personal care, learning support, and behaviour support under authorized supervision.

Current national Job Bank requirements state that completion of secondary school is required and that teacher assistants usually require completion of a **10-month college program** in teaching assistance, educational assistance, or another social-science program. Roles supporting students with special needs usually require specialized training and experience. Requirements must still be verified with the employer and province because school systems and specialized roles differ.

Official requirements source: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/16551/ca

Job Bank national wage data, updated November 19, 2025, report approximately:

- **CAD $18.29/hour low**;
- **CAD $25.01/hour median**;
- **CAD $32.42/hour high**.

The wage reference period is 2023–2024. Provincial and regional wages vary materially.

Official wage source: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/16551/ca

Job Bank outlook information varies by province and region rather than supporting one universal national hiring forecast. Candidates should check the region in which they intend to work.

Official outlook source: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/outlookreport/occupation/26536

## Colombia — public training and employment pathways

A clean current national official wage series or one-to-one national occupational classification that maps perfectly to the Guide 62 title was not identified in this controlled research pass. The guide must not invent one.

SENA remains a practical public starting point for adjacent skill development. SENA Betowa currently lists **Inducción a procesos pedagógicos**, a complementary virtual program of approximately **40 hours** covering education and pedagogy, teacher/student roles, teaching-learning processes, curriculum, and assessment. This is useful adjacent training, not a universal legal prerequisite for becoming a school instructional aide.

Official/public training source: https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/induccion-a-procesos-pedagogicos?modality=V&offertype=com&programId=6477

SENA’s Agencia Pública de Empleo also provides free public employment services and periodically lists roles using titles such as *auxiliar docente*. Individual postings can help candidates understand employer-specific expectations, schedules, experience, and compensation, but a single posting must not be represented as a national wage benchmark.

Official/public employment locator: https://ape.sena.edu.co/

## Role and professional-boundary controls

The Version 2 guide must distinguish instructional support from independent professional authority. A teacher assistant or instructional aide may support lessons, student supervision, documentation, classroom routines, accommodations, and teacher-directed learning activities when authorized, but the guide must not imply that the role independently confers authority to:

- act as the teacher of record;
- diagnose a disability, learning disorder, behavioural condition, or mental-health condition;
- independently create, alter, or override an IEP, accommodation plan, behavioural plan, or clinical recommendation;
- provide professional counselling, nursing, therapy, legal advice, or other regulated services without the required credential and authorization;
- make independent safeguarding, disciplinary, or eligibility decisions reserved for designated school personnel or public authorities; or
- ignore teacher, school, district, or jurisdiction procedures because the worker believes a different action would be preferable.

When duties are unclear, the guide should direct the worker to the responsible teacher, supervisor, school administrator, or qualified professional.

## Safeguarding and emergency boundaries

Teacher assistants may observe or receive disclosures involving abuse, neglect, bullying, self-harm risk, violence, medical danger, exploitation, or other urgent concerns. Response obligations vary by jurisdiction, setting, student age, and role.

The guide must require workers to:

- follow school and employer safeguarding procedures;
- know the correct internal escalation path;
- follow applicable mandatory-reporting law and employer training;
- contact emergency services when required by policy or law;
- document facts accurately rather than conducting an unauthorized investigation; and
- avoid promises of confidentiality that conflict with legal or safety obligations.

Training material and AI tools are not substitutes for current local safeguarding policy or law.

## Disability, accessibility, and student-support boundaries

Teacher assistants can be important partners in inclusive education and special-needs support, but they should operate within authorized student plans and professional direction. The guide must support disability-inclusive communication, respectful assistance, accessible learning materials, and authorized assistive technology while avoiding claims that an aide can independently determine disability status, prescribe accommodations, or override an approved student-support plan.

Physical assistance, mobility support, feeding, toileting, health-related tasks, restraint, behaviour intervention, and other higher-risk duties may require specialized training, explicit employer authorization, and jurisdiction-specific rules.

## Responsible AI boundary

AI may help a worker practice generic interview questions, clarify non-sensitive terminology, draft generic study notes, or brainstorm classroom-support ideas when employer policy allows it.

The guide must prohibit placing identifiable or confidential student information into unapproved AI systems, including names, education records, IEP details, health information, behavioural records, safeguarding information, family information, or other protected data.

AI must not replace licensed teacher or specialist judgment, school safeguarding decisions, formal disciplinary processes, disability determinations, or approved student-support plans. AI-generated educational material must be reviewed by the responsible teacher or authorized professional before use with students.

## Employer-supported development to investigate

Candidates should ask employers and school systems about:

- paid onboarding and paraeducator orientation;
- district or school instructional-aide training;
- special-education and disability-support training;
- safeguarding and mandatory-reporting training;
- de-escalation and behaviour-support training;
- first aid/CPR when required;
- assistive-technology training;
- tuition assistance or reimbursement;
- language-learning support;
- pathways from aide/paraprofessional work into teacher education; and
- internal promotion or credential-development programs.

Any repayment obligation attached to employer-funded education should be obtained in writing before the worker commits money or time.
