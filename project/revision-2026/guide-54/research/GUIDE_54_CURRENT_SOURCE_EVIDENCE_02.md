## 1. United States — role, education, certification, and licensure

### Bureau of Labor Statistics

Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook — Occupational Therapy Assistants and Aides  
https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/occupational-therapy-assistants-and-aides.htm

Current BLS evidence reviewed August 19, 2026 states:

- Occupational therapy assistants help occupational therapists provide treatments and procedures to clients; aides perform selected routine/support tasks.
- Occupational therapy assistants typically need an associate degree from an accredited occupational therapy assistant program and usually need a state-issued license.
- Occupational therapy assistants held approximately **49,200 jobs in 2024**.
- Median annual pay for occupational therapy assistants was **$68,340 in May 2024**.
- Employment of occupational therapy assistants is projected to grow **19% from 2024 to 2034**, from about 49,200 to 58,700 jobs.
- May 2024 OTA median annual wages in selected major industries were: home healthcare services **$76,800**; nursing care facilities **$75,860**; offices of physical/occupational/speech therapists and audiologists **$65,590**; hospitals **$65,280**; educational services **$59,240**.

Control: do not mix the lower-paid occupational therapy aide occupation with the occupational therapy assistant occupation.

### ACOTE accreditation

Sources: Accreditation Council for Occupational Therapy Education (ACOTE)  
https://acoteonline.org/about/  
https://acoteonline.org/all-schools/  
https://acoteonline.org/accreditation-explained/standards/

Current ACOTE evidence reviewed August 19, 2026 states:

- ACOTE is the accrediting agency for occupational therapy and occupational therapy assistant educational programs and is recognized by the U.S. Department of Education and CHEA.
- Entry into the OTA profession may occur at the **associate or bachelor degree level**.
- ACOTE's current directory reports accredited OTA programs at both degree levels; program status must be verified in the live ACOTE directory before enrollment because accreditation status can change.
- Graduates of ACOTE-accredited OTA programs are eligible for the NBCOT certification examination and may apply for jurisdictional licensure subject to the applicable regulator's requirements.
- The 2023 ACOTE Standards are the current standards framework; ACOTE's interpretive guide was updated in 2026.

Control: never recommend a school solely because it uses the words “occupational therapy assistant.” Verify the exact program and current ACOTE status before tuition is paid.

### NBCOT certification and professional boundary

Sources: National Board for Certification in Occupational Therapy (NBCOT)  
https://www.nbcot.org/get-certified  
https://www.nbcot.org/get-certified/eligibility  
https://www.nbcot.org/occupational-therapy/what-is-an-otr-or-cota  
https://www.nbcot.org/get-certified/after-exam

Current NBCOT evidence reviewed August 19, 2026 states:

- A U.S.-educated COTA examination candidate must graduate from an **ACOTE-accredited occupational therapy assistant program**.
- Passing the NBCOT examination leads to the Certified Occupational Therapy Assistant (`COTA®`) certification when other certification requirements are met.
- Certification and state licensure are distinct. NBCOT explicitly directs candidates to meet the licensing/regulatory requirements of the jurisdiction before practice.
- NBCOT describes the OTR/COTA relationship as collaborative: the OTR evaluates the client, plans interventions and completes discharge documents; the COTA implements/documents therapeutic interventions and provides progress feedback under the professional relationship/supervision required by practice rules.
- International occupational therapist education is not automatically an alternative route to the U.S. COTA examination; NBCOT's current OTA eligibility rules must be checked directly.

Control: the revised guide must not imply that a COTA independently performs the occupational therapist's evaluation, intervention-plan, discharge, diagnosis, or other reserved functions.

## 3. Canada — occupational therapy assistant / rehabilitation-assistant context

Sources: Government of Canada Job Bank — Occupational Therapy Assistant, NOC 32109  
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/25666/ca  
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/25666/ca

Current Job Bank evidence reviewed August 19, 2026 states:

- “Occupational therapy assistant” is mapped within **NOC 32109 — Other technical occupations in therapy and assessment**.
- The occupational group may assist occupational therapists and other regulated professionals and is employed in hospitals, clinics, extended-care facilities, rehabilitation centres, community health centres, educational institutions, and private practices.
- Job Bank's national summary classifies the occupation as usually requiring college/apprenticeship-level preparation; exact employer and provincial requirements vary.
- National hourly wage estimates currently shown are **CAD $17.62 low / $26.85 median / $36.71 high**; the wage data reference period is 2023–2024 and Job Bank states the wages were updated November 19, 2025.
- Job Bank reports **78.8%** of workers in NOC 32109 nationally receive at least one non-wage benefit.
- Outlook varies by province/territory. For example, Job Bank reports a **Good** outlook in Ontario for 2025–2027; this must not be generalized to every province.

Control: Canadian NOC 32109 is broader than the U.S. COTA credential. Do not state that U.S. COTA certification automatically grants Canadian recognition, registration, title protection, or employment eligibility.

## 4. Colombia — professional-title boundary

Source: Colombia, Ley 949 de 2005, SUIN-Juriscol  
https://www.suin-juriscol.gov.co/viewDocument.asp?id=1671650

Current legal-source review August 19, 2026 confirms:

- Ley 949 de 2005 remains listed as **vigente** and regulates the profession of `Terapia Ocupacional` in Colombia.
- Article 1 defines occupational therapy as a **university-trained liberal profession**.
- Article 22 states that occupational-therapy assessment, diagnosis, or action planning involving service users may not be delegated by the occupational therapist to technicians, technologists, or other persons who are not competent under the law.
- Article 35 sets professional-title and professional-card requirements for lawful practice as a `Terapeuta Ocupacional` in Colombia, including title/convalidation requirements as applicable.

Controlled localization rule:

**A U.S. Occupational Therapy Assistant / COTA must not be translated, marketed, or represented as a Colombian `Terapeuta Ocupacional` solely on the basis of the U.S. assistant credential.** Colombia pathways must be presented as jurisdiction-specific exploration and verified with the competent Colombian education/professional authorities before enrollment or practice.
