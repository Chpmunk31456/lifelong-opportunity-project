## United States — occupation and wage evidence

### O*NET OnLine — U.S. Department of Labor
Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/27-3031.00

Controlled facts:
- O*NET-SOC: **27-3031.00 — Public Relations Specialists**; current profile is designated Bright Outlook.
- The occupation promotes or creates an intended public image for individuals, groups, or organizations and may write or select material for release to communications media.
- Current O*NET labour-market data show **2025 median wages of $35.94/hour and $74,750/year**.
- Employment: approximately **315,900 workers in 2024**.
- Projected growth: **5% to 6% from 2024 to 2034**.
- Projected openings: approximately **27,600 per year** over the projection period.

### O*NET national wage distribution / BLS source control
Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/27-3031.00

Controlled facts:
- O*NET identifies the wage source as **Bureau of Labor Statistics 2025 wage data**.
- National annual distribution: **$44,110 at the 10th percentile, $74,750 median, and $135,150 at the 90th percentile**.
- Hourly distribution: **$21.21 at the 10th percentile, $35.94 median, and $64.98 at the 90th percentile**.
- These values describe the broader Public Relations Specialists occupation and should not be presented as guaranteed pay for entry-level assistant or coordinator titles.

## Colombia — occupation and training pathways

### OCUPACOL — Ministerio del Trabajo: professional benchmark
Source: https://ocupacol.mintrabajo.gov.co/Profile/OccupationalProfile/24320

Controlled facts:
- **CUOC 24320 — Profesionales de relaciones públicas y comunicaciones**, competence level 4.
- Work includes planning, developing, and evaluating communication strategies and information programs; preparing reports, speeches, presentations, newsletters, press releases, and other material; maintaining media relationships; organizing special events; and acting as an organizational spokesperson where authorized.
- Knowledge areas include ICT, marketing/advertising, journalism/communications, customer service, foreign language, and laws/government.
- Skills include reading comprehension, assertive communication, judgment, active listening, adaptability, critical thinking, persuasion, writing, interpersonal relations, teamwork, and working under pressure.

### OCUPACOL — assistant benchmark
Source: https://ocupacol.mintrabajo.gov.co/Profile/OccupationalProfile/33392

Controlled facts:
- **CUOC 33392 — Asistentes de mercadeo, publicidad y comunicaciones**, competence level 3.
- Listed titles include **Asistente de comunicación**, **Asistente de comunicación y redes sociales**, **Asistente de marketing y comunicación**, and related support roles.
- OCUPACOL displays occupation-level market indicators and a salary range, but explicitly states that the associated data **do not have statistical representativeness under the applied methodology**. The controlled guide therefore must not present that range as a representative Colombian wage benchmark.
- The assistant classification is useful for distinguishing entry/support work from professional-level PR responsibility.

## Privacy, cybersecurity, accessibility, media, and responsible-AI controls

### CISA — Secure Our World
Source: https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world

Controlled use:
- Communications teams handle email accounts, social-media accounts, media lists, campaign files, executive information, embargoed material, event details, customer or community contacts, and sometimes credentials or sensitive internal information.
- The guide should reinforce phishing awareness, strong unique passwords/password managers, multifactor authentication, and prompt software updates through employer-approved systems.
- Unusual payment, password-reset, account-access, media-request, or file-sharing requests should be independently verified and escalated under organizational policy.

### U.S. Federal Trade Commission — advertising / endorsement transparency
Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews

Controlled use:
- Endorsement, influencer, testimonial, sponsorship, and review practices can trigger disclosure and truth-in-advertising obligations.
- A communications assistant should follow approved legal/compliance guidance and should not invent, hide, or mischaracterize material connections, endorsements, reviews, or claims.
- The guide is educational and must not provide jurisdiction-specific legal advice.

### U.S. Department of Justice — web accessibility guidance
Source: https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/

Controlled use:
- Public-facing communications, registration pages, digital documents, videos, and web content can create accessibility obligations depending on the organization and jurisdiction.
- Practical communication work should support captions/transcripts, readable structure, descriptive link text, alternative text where appropriate, accessible document formats, and an approved accommodation/contact process.
- The guide must not claim that these practices alone certify legal accessibility.

### NIST — AI Risk Management Framework
Sources:
- https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
- https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence

Controlled use:
- NIST AI RMF is a voluntary framework for managing AI risks; NIST also provides a Generative AI Profile.
- In communications work, approved AI tools may assist with brainstorming, outlines, first-pass drafts, summarization, headline options, translation support, or content calendars.
- Human verification is required for names, dates, quotations, claims, statistics, source attribution, legal/regulatory statements, crisis communications, executive statements, accessibility commitments, and other consequential content.
- Confidential drafts, embargoed information, private media lists, credentials, customer data, unpublished financial information, legal material, or sensitive personal data must not be entered into unapproved AI services.

## Editorial controls for the English revision
The revised master should:

1. distinguish assistant/coordinator support from spokesperson, manager, legal, crisis, and executive authority;
2. anchor the U.S. occupation to O*NET-SOC 27-3031.00, Canada to NOC 11202, and Colombia to CUOC 24320 / assistant comparator CUOC 33392 without claiming perfect scope equivalence;
3. cover writing/editing, media monitoring, press materials, internal communications, social content, event support, calendars, approvals, stakeholder lists, measurement, records, and escalation;
4. explain education as employer-dependent and show lower-cost entry through transferable writing, administrative, customer-service, event, digital-content, and portfolio skills;
5. include WIOA/American Job Center, Canada training agreements, SENA Betowa, and OIT/Cinterfor as locators without promising eligibility, funding, seats, or placement;
6. state that formal apprenticeships may be limited and present internships, supervised communications work, employer training, nonprofit/campus projects, and assistant roles as practical work-based-learning routes;
7. label all non-government compensation examples as estimates/examples and preserve date, employer/scope, and sample limitations where available;
8. require source verification and documented approval before publishing consequential facts, quotes, claims, statistics, legal positions, crisis statements, or executive communications;
9. include media-list, account-access, phishing, impersonation, file-sharing, confidential-draft, and social-account security controls;
10. include accessibility practices for web, social, video, documents, and events while avoiding unsupported certification claims;
11. include FTC-style transparency principles for endorsements/sponsorships where applicable, without giving legal advice;
12. permit responsible AI for low-risk drafting and organization only with human review, privacy controls, attribution/source checking, and employer approval;
13. warn against fabricating quotes, testimonials, reviews, endorsements, media coverage, engagement data, screenshots, statistics, citations, images, credentials, or stakeholder approvals;
14. preserve plain-language, dysgraphia-friendly structure, short steps, descriptive headings, checklists, and natural human readability;
15. explicitly correct the legacy `action_plan_ok: False` defect during controlled reconstruction rather than inheriting it into the release candidate.
