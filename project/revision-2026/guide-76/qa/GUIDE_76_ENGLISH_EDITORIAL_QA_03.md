### Occupation scope and authority
**PASS**

The revised English master distinguishes communications/PR assistant, coordinator, specialist, spokesperson, and manager responsibility levels. It does not grant junior workers authority to speak for an organization, publish crisis/legal/financial/personnel statements, commit organizational positions, or bypass approval processes.

### Current occupation and wage evidence
**PASS**

The master uses current controlled benchmarks:
- U.S. O*NET-SOC 27-3031.00 — Public Relations Specialists;
- U.S. 2025 BLS wage data surfaced through O*NET;
- Canada NOC 11202 and Job Bank wage/outlook context;
- Colombia CUOC 24320 professional benchmark and CUOC 33392 assistant comparator.

Non-government pay information is explicitly presented as individual current market examples, not representative averages or guaranteed compensation.

### Education, funding, and work-based learning
**PASS**

The master includes:

### Colombia pathway and evidence handling
**PASS**

The master distinguishes professional-level CUOC 24320 from assistant-level CUOC 33392. It preserves the OCUPACOL warning that occupation-level salary indicators are not statistically representative and does not convert that displayed range into a national salary benchmark.

### Communications ethics and fact verification
**PASS**

The master requires source verification, approval discipline, correct handling of names/dates/quotes/statistics, correction escalation, and preservation of factual meaning. It explicitly prohibits fabrication of quotes, reviews, testimonials, endorsements, media coverage, engagement statistics, screenshots, sources, credentials, and approvals.

### Privacy, confidentiality, records, and cybersecurity
**PASS**

The master covers confidential drafts, embargoed information, media lists, executive schedules, personal data, credentials, account access, file sharing, phishing, impersonation, MFA, password controls, unusual-request verification, and incident escalation. It directs readers to employer-approved systems and policies rather than inventing security controls.

### Accessibility
**PASS**

The revised master includes plain language, descriptive headings, meaningful links, alt-text awareness, captions/transcripts, accessible document structure, readable presentation practices, and accommodation-contact planning. It explicitly avoids claiming accessibility certification or universal legal compliance.

### Endorsement / sponsorship transparency
**PASS**

The master includes controlled FTC-style transparency principles for endorsements, sponsorships, reviews, and material relationships while clearly stating that the guide does not provide jurisdiction-specific legal advice.

### Responsible AI
**PASS**

The master permits low-risk AI assistance for brainstorming, outlining, routine non-sensitive drafting, summarization, checklist creation, and translation support only when employer-approved and subject to human review. It prohibits sole reliance on AI for consequential facts, quotations, crisis statements, legal/regulatory content, financial/personnel announcements, accessibility commitments, allegations, source attribution, or final consequential translations. Sensitive data must not be entered into unapproved AI services.

### Human readability and accessibility-oriented structure
**PASS**

The revised edition uses descriptive headings, short paragraphs, bullet lists, staged workflows, a 30-day starter plan, interview practice prompts, a publishing checklist, and plain-language explanations intended to reduce cognitive load and support dysgraphia-friendly use.
