## 1. United States occupation anchor — O*NET-SOC 19-4012.00

### Source
O*NET OnLine — Agricultural Technicians  
https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/19-4012.00

### Current controlled facts
O*NET lists **19-4012.00 — Agricultural Technicians**, updated 2026.

The occupation works with agricultural scientists in plant, fiber and animal research; assists with animal breeding and nutrition; sets up or maintains laboratory equipment; collects crop or animal samples; prepares specimens; records data; and conducts tests or experiments related to crop yield/quality and resistance to disease or insects.

Reported titles include Agricultural Research Technician, Agricultural Research Technologist, Agricultural Technician, Agriculture Assistant, Agronomy Research Technician, Field Assistant, Insect Research Technician, Laboratory Technician and Seed Analyst.

### Current O*NET/BLS wage and employment display
O*NET’s 2026 occupation page displays 2025 BLS wage data:
- median hourly wage: **$23.86**;
- median annual wage: **$49,630**;
- national 10th percentile: **$35,980/year / $17.30/hour**;
- 25th percentile: **$40,950/year / $19.69/hour**;
- 75th percentile: **$63,630/year / $30.59/hour**;
- 90th percentile: **$76,480/year / $36.77/hour**;
- employment in 2024: **18,600**;
- projected 2024–2034 growth: **average, 3%–4%**;
- displayed projected openings over 2024–2034: **2,900**.

O*NET identifies major employment sectors including educational services; professional, scientific and technical services; and agriculture, forestry, fishing and hunting.

### Editorial implication
Use **$49,630/year / $23.86/hour (2025)** as the most current official U.S. occupation-specific median in the guide, while preserving the dated BLS OOH May-2024 value separately.

## 9. U.S. pesticide safety and role boundary — EPA Worker Protection Standard

### Sources
EPA — Agricultural Worker Protection Standard  
https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-worker-safety/agricultural-worker-protection-standard-wps

EPA — Definition of a Pesticide Handler  
https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-worker-safety/definition-pesticide-handler-under-worker-protection-standard

EPA — Restrictions During Pesticide Applications  
https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-worker-safety/restrictions-during-pesticide-applications-under-worker-protection-standard

### Current controlled facts
EPA’s Worker Protection Standard (WPS), current through 2026 pages, distinguishes **agricultural workers** from **pesticide handlers**.

Handler activities can include:
- mixing, loading, transferring or applying agricultural pesticides;
- handling opened pesticide containers;
- cleaning, adjusting or repairing contaminated application equipment;
- assisting with applications;
- certain crop-advisor entry activities;
- disposing of pesticides or pesticide containers.

WPS protections include pesticide-safety training, pesticide/application information, Safety Data Sheet access, decontamination supplies, emergency assistance, restricted-entry intervals, application exclusion/restricted areas and handler-specific PPE/equipment protections.

### Editorial boundary
An agricultural technician title does **not** automatically authorize pesticide mixing/application or other handler work. The guide must direct readers to employer procedures, pesticide labeling, WPS requirements, applicable state/local law and required training/certification. It must not provide pesticide rates, mixtures, application directions, restricted-entry decisions or off-label instructions.

## 10. Agricultural machinery and tractor/PTO safety

### Sources
OSHA 29 CFR 1928 — Agriculture table of contents  
https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1928/1928TableofContents

OSHA 1928.57 — Guarding of farm field equipment  
https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1928/1928.57

OSHA Agricultural Operations — Hazards & Controls  
https://www.osha.gov/agricultural-operations/hazards

### Controlled facts
OSHA agriculture rules address tractor roll-over protection and guarding of agricultural equipment. Section 1928.57 requires guarding for PTO-driven equipment and power-transmission hazards including gears, belts, chains, pulleys, sprockets and shafts.

Current OSHA agricultural guidance emphasizes:
- keep guards/shields in place;
- inspect tractors/harvesters before operation;
- train operators;
- keep people clear before start-up or power engagement;
- control power before service/maintenance;
- recognize PTO entanglement hazards;
- use required ROPS/seat belts where applicable;
- manage heat, tools, slips/trips and other field hazards.

### Editorial boundary
The guide must not teach a learner to reach past guards, service running machinery, improvise tractor repairs, work around an energized PTO, bypass interlocks or perform regulated/high-risk operation without required training and authorization.

## 12. Sampling, laboratory and data integrity

Agricultural technicians may collect crop, soil, seed, feed or animal-related samples and support laboratory/field experiments. The guide should teach generic controls rather than inventing test methods:

- use the approved sampling plan/protocol;
- identify sample/location/date/time correctly;
- avoid cross-contamination;
- use required containers/preservatives only as specified by the approved method;
- maintain chain-of-custody or traceability when required;
- record instrument ID/calibration status;
- record units exactly;
- preserve raw data;
- document deviations and environmental conditions;
- do not delete inconvenient observations;
- escalate results that fall outside procedural limits;
- never fabricate missing readings or field observations.

Specific laboratory methods, animal procedures, pesticide analyses or regulated sampling methods must come from the employer/laboratory/regulator—not from this career guide.

## 13. Animal and biological boundaries

Because agricultural technicians may support animal breeding/nutrition or biological research, the guide must distinguish technical support from veterinary diagnosis/treatment and from independently authorized animal procedures.

Required principles:
- follow approved animal-care/research protocols;
- use required biosafety and hygiene controls;
- report bites, sharps incidents, unusual illness or suspected zoonotic exposure promptly;
- do not diagnose or prescribe veterinary treatment unless separately licensed/authorized;
- do not alter diets, medications, withdrawal periods, breeding protocols or research procedures outside delegated authority.

---

## 14. Heat and field-work controls

OSHA agricultural hazards guidance identifies heat as a field-work risk. The guide should support:
- employer heat-illness prevention plans;
- hydration;
- rest/cooling or shade practices;
- acclimatization and supervision where applicable;
- recognition/reporting of symptoms;
- emergency escalation;
- clothing/PPE consistent with the task and pesticide label when relevant.

The guide must not substitute for the employer’s site-specific heat program or medical advice.

## 15. Cybersecurity and connected agriculture

Agricultural technical work increasingly uses digital field records, GPS/GIS, sensors, data loggers, laboratory systems, connected equipment, cloud dashboards and farm-management systems.

The guide should require:
- employer-approved devices/accounts;
- MFA where available;
- protection of credentials;
- caution with removable media and vendor laptops;
- controlled firmware/software changes;
- backups and change control;
- protection of research, customer, farm, location and proprietary data;
- reporting suspicious messages, malware, unexplained equipment behavior or account compromise.

Current baseline reference:
CISA — Secure Our World  
https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world

## 16. Responsible AI boundary

### Sources
NIST AI Risk Management Framework  
https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework

NIST AI RMF Generative AI Profile  
https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence

### Safe use pattern
AI may assist with low-risk learning, drafting checklists, explaining public concepts, generating fictional practice questions, improving non-confidential writing or organizing public research—subject to human verification.

### Prohibited reliance
The guide must explicitly prohibit treating AI as final authority for:
- pesticide rates, tank mixes, labels, restricted-entry intervals or application decisions;
- fertilizer or chemical prescriptions;
- veterinary diagnosis or treatment;
- animal medication/dose/withdrawal decisions;
- regulated environmental or biosecurity decisions;
- field/laboratory results;
- seed/crop disease diagnosis without qualified verification;
- machine safety or guarding decisions;
- calibration acceptance;
- research conclusions;
- food/feed safety release decisions;
- legal/regulatory compliance.

AI must never be used to fabricate sample results, field observations, chain-of-custody entries, treatment records, animal records, test data or experimental outcomes.

Do not upload confidential research data, proprietary farm/customer data, credentials, regulated records or unpublished findings to unapproved AI services.

## 20. Controlled distinctions for the English reconstruction

The new English master must preserve these distinctions:

1. **Agricultural technician vs agricultural scientist/agronomist:** technician support does not automatically confer professional authority.
2. **Agricultural worker vs pesticide handler/applicator:** title alone does not authorize regulated pesticide handling/application.
3. **Plant/crop technician vs veterinary professional:** no diagnosis/prescription authority is implied.
4. **Field technician vs laboratory technician:** duties and hazard controls differ.
5. **Official wage statistics vs private platform estimates:** never blend them.
6. **Current training program vs guaranteed seat/funding:** enrollment and funding must be reverified.
7. **Educational demonstration vs regulated recommendation:** portfolio examples must not be presented as real pesticide, veterinary or agronomic prescriptions.
