# Guide 59 — Current-Source Evidence 02

**Guide:** 59 — Social and Human Service Assistant  
**Branch:** `revision/guide-00-100-2026`  
**Research date:** August 20, 2026  
**Stage:** Current-source Research — **PASS**

## Occupation scope

Guide 59 covers entry and intermediate support roles that help people locate, access, and navigate social, community, rehabilitation, housing, benefits, family, disability, aging, youth, behavioral-health, and related human-service resources. Titles vary by employer and jurisdiction and may include social and human service assistant, human services assistant, case aide, social services assistant, community support worker, community service worker, family service assistant, outreach worker, settlement/community-services worker, or related support titles.

This guide must not present these roles as equivalent to licensed social work, psychotherapy, clinical counseling, diagnosis, legal representation, benefits eligibility adjudication, emergency command, or another regulated profession.

## United States

### Classification, duties, entry, and outlook

O*NET classifies **Social and Human Service Assistants** as **21-1093.00**. The occupation supports clients in fields such as psychology, rehabilitation, and social work and includes helping clients identify and obtain available benefits and community services, coordinating with service providers, maintaining records, and following up on client needs.

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook states that social and human service assistants typically need at least a **high school diploma or equivalent**, although requirements vary, and that short-term on-the-job training is common. Employers may prefer additional education or experience for more complex settings.

Current O*NET/BLS data report:

- **2025 national median wage:** **USD $22.08/hour / $45,930/year**.
- **2024 employment:** approximately **449,600** workers.
- **Projected growth, 2024–2034:** **5% to 6%**, faster than average.
- **Projected openings:** approximately **50,600 per year** over the decade.

The BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook page still displays the May 2024 median of USD $45,120; the controlled Version 2 guide should use the newer O*NET page's BLS **2025 wage data** while identifying the year clearly.

### Training and funding

There is no single nationally mandatory private certificate for this occupation. A lower-risk entry strategy is to compare actual local postings first, then use employer training, community college or public-provider coursework, supervised volunteer/support experience, and workforce-funded training where appropriate.

CareerOneStop, sponsored by the U.S. Department of Labor, provides:

- an **American Job Center** locator for free career and training assistance;
- a **WIOA-Eligible Training Program Finder** for people who qualify for Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act training services; and
- training, scholarship, certification, license, and apprenticeship search tools.

WIOA support is eligibility- and location-dependent and must not be described as guaranteed funding.

Registered Apprenticeship may exist in selected human-service or adjacent occupations, but availability is employer- and location-specific. Users should search Apprenticeship.gov rather than assume an apprenticeship exists locally.

## Canada

### Occupational mapping and requirements

The closest broad Canadian comparison is **Social and community service workers, NOC 42201**. Job Bank states that these workers administer and implement social assistance and community-service programs and help clients deal with personal and social problems.

Job Bank reports that employment requirements usually include completion of a college or university program in social work, child and youth care, psychology, or another social-science or health-related discipline. For some occupations in the unit group, previous social-service experience as a volunteer or in a support role may replace formal education requirements. Some provinces may require registration for specific social-service-worker titles; the guide must distinguish that from generic support roles.

### Compensation

Government of Canada Job Bank wages for NOC 42201, updated November 19, 2025, report national hourly wages of approximately:

- **CAD $19.00 low**
- **CAD $26.00 median**
- **CAD $36.06 high**

The wage reference period is 2023–2024 Labour Force Survey data. Provincial and regional figures vary materially.

### Training and funding

Canada does not have one universal national credential for every role represented by NOC 42201. Candidates should verify provincial rules, college/community-service programs, employer requirements, and any title-specific registration requirements before paying for training.

## Colombia

### Occupational mapping

SENA's National Occupational Classification identifies **CNO 4211 — Asistentes en servicios social y comunitario**, in the social sciences, education, government services, and religion performance area. The classification describes a qualification level involving varied functions, responsibility, autonomy, and evaluative judgment.

Current SENA Agencia Pública de Empleo postings use titles such as **Auxiliar de servicio social y comunitario**, **Asistente de servicios sociales y comunitarios**, **Gestor comunitario**, and **Promotor social**. Current examples include duties such as:

- providing information and institutional referral routes;
- helping users identify options and access legal, medical, financial, housing, employment, transport, and other referral resources;
- completing or digitizing forms and service records;
- supporting workshops and psychosocial/community activities;
- acting and reporting under institutional protocols in crises, emergencies, community conflicts, refugee/disaster services, or child-protection contexts; and
- working under professional supervision when duties enter more specialized social or health-service domains.

These postings are evidence of current occupational use, not a universal national job description or guarantee that every listed activity is appropriate without employer training, supervision, or professional credentials.

### Public training examples

SENA Betowa currently lists complementary public training that can build adjacent community-support skills, including:

- **Implementación de la política de participación social en salud** — 48-hour complementary in-person course; and
- **Desarrollo de habilidades para el bienestar familiar y comunitario** — 48-hour complementary offerings, with eligibility varying by cohort/provider.

These courses should be presented as examples of public skill-building, **not** as mandatory credentials for CNO 4211 and not as substitutes for a professional social-work qualification where one is legally or professionally required.

## Latin America and the Caribbean

OIT/Cinterfor is the Inter-American Centre for Knowledge Development in Vocational Training and maintains a network of vocational-training institutions across Latin America and the Caribbean. It can be used to identify national public training institutions, qualification systems, competency-certification initiatives, and country-specific workforce-development resources.

The guide should direct users to the relevant national public training body rather than imply that one regional certificate applies across Latin America. Training, licensing, title protection, and professional social-work requirements vary by country.

## Compensation — official versus market estimates

Official occupational wage data and private market estimates must remain separate.

### Official U.S. benchmark

O*NET/BLS 2025 wage data for 21-1093.00:

- **USD $22.08/hour median**
- **USD $45,930/year median**

### Official Canada benchmark

Job Bank national NOC 42201 wages, updated November 19, 2025:

- **CAD $19.00/hour low**
- **CAD $26.00/hour median**
- **CAD $36.06/hour high**

### Current non-government U.S. estimate

Indeed reports an average base salary for the narrower title **Social Services Assistant** of approximately **USD $21.40/hour**, based on about 1,200 salaries from job postings in the preceding 36 months and updated August 1, 2026. This is a private job-posting estimate, not an official occupational median and not directly interchangeable with O*NET/BLS 21-1093.00.

Salary.com reports materially higher August 1, 2026 estimates for similarly named titles. Because its title definitions and methodology differ substantially from BLS/O*NET and from Indeed, the controlled guide should not blend these figures. If included at all, they must be explicitly labeled as private title-specific estimates.

No directly comparable, current national official wage series was identified in this research pass for Colombia that maps cleanly to the Guide 59 occupation. The guide should therefore avoid inventing a Colombian national salary figure and instruct readers to check current SENA APE and other verified local postings.

## Safety, ethics, privacy, and scope boundaries

Version 2 must explicitly state that support workers should:

- follow employer safeguarding, confidentiality, documentation, consent, and escalation rules;
- protect case notes, identity information, health information, immigration information, benefits information, family information, addresses, and other sensitive client data;
- avoid promising benefits, housing, legal outcomes, immigration outcomes, treatment outcomes, or emergency assistance they do not control;
- not diagnose mental-health or substance-use conditions unless separately licensed and authorized;
- not provide psychotherapy, legal advice, clinical treatment, or regulated social-work services outside their credentials and job scope;
- use approved crisis and emergency protocols and escalate imminent danger, abuse, neglect, exploitation, self-harm risk, violence risk, or medical emergencies to qualified personnel/emergency services according to local law and employer policy;
- use trauma-informed, disability-inclusive, culturally respectful communication without claiming clinical expertise; and
- document objectively and distinguish observed facts from interpretation.

## AI-use boundary

AI may help with generic study questions, role-play interviews, plain-language explanations of non-sensitive terminology, or drafts of generic checklists. It must not replace professional judgment, crisis assessment, benefits eligibility decisions, legal advice, clinical diagnosis, safeguarding decisions, or mandated reporting decisions.

Workers must not paste identifiable client records, case histories, medical/mental-health information, immigration details, addresses, benefits records, credentials, or other protected information into public AI tools without explicit organizational authorization and an approved privacy/security basis.

## Research decision

**Research: PASS.** The evidence is sufficient for controlled English Version 2 reconstruction, provided the guide preserves the occupation/support-role boundary, separates official wages from private estimates, avoids unsupported Colombia salary claims, and treats training/funding availability as conditional rather than guaranteed.
