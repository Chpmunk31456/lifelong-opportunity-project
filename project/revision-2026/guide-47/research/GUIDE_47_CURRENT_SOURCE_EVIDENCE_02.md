# Guide 47 — Current Source Evidence 02

**Guide:** 47 — Pharmacy Technician  
**Branch:** `revision/guide-00-100-2026`  
**Research date:** August 18, 2026  
**Stage:** Current-source Research  
**Result:** PASS

## Research purpose

This intake establishes the current evidence base for the controlled Version 2 reconstruction of Guide 47. `Pharmacy Technician` is a regulated or otherwise jurisdiction-bounded role in many places, and the same reader-facing title does not carry one universal legal scope, credential, registration rule, training model, or authority across countries, U.S. states, Canadian provinces/territories, or Colombian service settings.

The Version 2 guide must distinguish occupational description from legal authorization. It must direct readers to current jurisdictional regulators and employer procedures before they handle prescriptions, prepare or dispense medications, compound, administer vaccines or other medicines, access controlled substances, make insurance or billing changes, or perform any regulated act. It must never imply that training alone authorizes pharmacist-only clinical judgment, diagnosis, prescribing, therapeutic substitution, final clinical verification, unsupervised compounding, record falsification, diversion, or bypassing required supervision and quality controls.

No legacy wage, classification, credential, training, scope-of-practice, safety, or translation claim is accepted without current verification.

## United States — occupational anchor, duties, education, regulation, wages, and outlook

### O*NET OnLine

O*NET OnLine identifies **29-2052.00 Pharmacy Technicians**, updated in 2026. Its occupation summary states that pharmacy technicians prepare medications under the direction of a pharmacist and may measure, mix, count, label, and record medication amounts and dosages according to prescription orders.

O*NET's current 2025 BLS wage data report:

- **Median hourly wage: USD $22.00**.
- **Median annual wage: USD $45,750**.
- 10th percentile: **USD $17.32/hour / $36,020 annually**.
- 90th percentile: **USD $29.35/hour / $61,040 annually**.

O*NET reports **490,400 workers in 2024**, projected growth of **6 percent from 2024 to 2034**, and approximately **49,000 annual openings** over the projection period. The occupation is marked Bright Outlook.

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook

The BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, last modified April 24, 2026, states that pharmacy technicians help pharmacists dispense prescription medication to patients or health professionals. Typical duties include collecting information needed to fill prescriptions, measuring medication amounts, packaging and labeling prescriptions, managing inventory, processing payment and insurance claims, entering patient and prescription information, and arranging pharmacist consultation when customers have medication or health questions.

BLS explicitly places technicians **under pharmacist supervision** and states that pharmacists must review prescriptions before they are dispensed. BLS notes that scope varies by state: in most states trained technicians may compound or mix some medications or contact prescribers about refill authorizations, and in many states trained technicians may administer vaccines under pharmacist supervision. These are jurisdiction-dependent examples, not universal permissions.

BLS states that workers typically need a **high school diploma or equivalent** and may learn through on-the-job training or complete a postsecondary pharmacy-technology program. Training commonly lasts less than one year when learned on the job; postsecondary programs may award certificates in one year or less, while some lead to associate degrees and include clinical experience.

BLS states that **most states regulate pharmacy technicians in some way**. Typical state requirements may include education or training, an examination, fees, continuing education, a criminal background check, and minimum age. Some states and employers require certification. Readers therefore must verify the current state board or regulator and employer requirement for the specific state in which they plan to work.

The BLS page reports May 2024 median pay of **USD $43,460** and 6 percent projected growth for 2024–2034. For current wage figures in this Guide 47 revision, the more recent O*NET/BLS 2025 wage series above is the controlled U.S. wage anchor; the BLS OOH 2024 wage is retained only as contextual corroboration and must not be mixed with the 2025 series.

Source:

- https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/pharmacy-technicians.htm

## United States — licensing/certification locator and work-based learning

Because state requirements differ, the guide should direct readers to CareerOneStop's state licensing and certification resources rather than present one national license as universal. BLS specifically directs readers to CareerOneStop for state licensing-board requirements and certifications.

For work-based learning, BLS confirms that on-the-job training is a common entry path. Readers may also search **Apprenticeship.gov** for current Registered Apprenticeship opportunities, but availability varies by employer, occupation registration, and location; the guide must not imply that a pharmacy-technician apprenticeship is available in every area.

## Canada — occupation, education, registration, certification, and wages

Government of Canada Job Bank maps Pharmacy Technician to **NOC 32124**.

Job Bank's current requirements state that pharmacy technicians usually require either:

- a **two-year pharmacy technician college program**, or
- completion of a **two- to three-year college program in pharmaceutics plus supervised practical training**.

Job Bank further states that completion of the **Pharmacy Examining Board of Canada (PEBC) certification exams is required** for pharmacy technicians and that **registration with a regulatory body is required in all provinces except Quebec**. The guide must therefore direct readers to the actual provincial regulator and current PEBC pathway rather than assume one registration process applies nationally. Quebec's pathway should be verified separately before a reader acts because Job Bank explicitly distinguishes it from the other provinces.

Job Bank describes technicians as performing technical prescription functions including gathering patient information, product preparation, product distribution, and inventory control while working closely with pharmacists to support pharmaceutical care.

Current national wage evidence, updated November 19, 2025 and displayed on a page modified in 2026, reports:

- **CAD $17.50/hour low**.
- **CAD $24.83/hour median**.
- **CAD $34.20/hour high**.

These are national labour-market estimates for NOC 32124 and vary materially by province/territory and employer.

Sources:

- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/25660/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/25660/ca

## Colombia — occupational mapping and delegated/supervised scope

SENA's Observatorio Laboral y Ocupacional Colombiano identifies **CNO 3315 — Auxiliares en servicios farmacéuticos**. The occupational description states that these workers support reception, storage, dispensing, distribution, inventory, and related pharmacy-service functions **under delegation and supervision of professionals in the pharmaceutical service**.

The SENA occupational profile lists common titles including `Auxiliar de droguería`, `Auxiliar de droguería y farmacia`, `Auxiliar de farmacia`, `Auxiliar farmacéutico`, and `Auxiliar servicios farmacéuticos`. It also lists functions such as receiving and dispatching medications and medical devices, classifying/storing them under technical requirements, dispensing under delegation and health rules, maintaining inventory including controlled medicines, handling distribution documents, and implementing asepsis measures.

The profile includes a conditional function involving intramuscular medicine administration according to medical prescription in **authorized retail pharmaceutical establishments and under applicable health regulation**. Guide 47 must not convert that occupational statement into a universal training instruction or permission. Medication administration requires the actual establishment authorization, worker qualification, delegation, prescription, protocols, and current legal requirements.

Sources:

- https://observatorio.sena.edu.co/Clasificacion/CnoDetalle?tags=3315
- https://observatorio.sena.edu.co/Clasificacion/CnoDetalleFunciones?tags=3315

## Colombia — SENA Técnico en Servicios Farmacéuticos

SENA Betowa currently lists **Servicios Farmacéuticos** as a **Técnico** titled-training program with a duration of **2,640 hours**, minimum age **16+**, and an additional entry requirement of ninth-grade basic education plus successful completion of the admissions process. The current catalog lists competencies involving medication/device delivery according to delegation and health regulation, infection-control practices, occupational safety, person orientation, inventory/merchandising-related functions, and practical-stage outcomes.

The Betowa listing is current program-catalog evidence, not a guarantee of an open cohort, seat, specific location, admission, employment, legal authority, or cross-border recognition. Readers must verify current enrollment availability, location, schedule, requirements, and any employer/regulatory requirements before acting.

Source:

- https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/servicios-farmaceuticos?modality=P&programId=99375

## Colombia — pharmaceutical-service regulatory boundary

Colombia's compiled health-sector regulation in **Decreto 780 de 2016**, including the pharmaceutical-service provisions derived from Decreto 2200 de 2005, regulates pharmacy-service activities and establishments. The compilation states that pharmaceutical services may be dependent or independent; identifies functions such as selection, acquisition, receipt, storage, distribution, dispensing, quality management, and certain preparations; and establishes technical-direction requirements that depend on service complexity and establishment type.

The regulation distinguishes roles of the **Químico Farmacéutico** and **Tecnólogo en Regencia de Farmacia** for technical direction. Guide 47 must therefore not present the SENA Técnico/Auxiliar pathway as equivalent to Regencia de Farmacia or Química Farmacéutica, and must not imply that a technician/auxiliary may independently direct a pharmaceutical service, make pharmacist-only clinical judgments, or perform activities reserved to differently qualified personnel.

Source:

- https://normograma.sena.edu.co/compilacion/docs/decreto_0780_2016_pr024.htm

## Latin America and the Caribbean — public vocational-training locator

Pharmacy technician, pharmacy auxiliary, dispensary, pharmaceutical-service, and pharmacy-regency roles are not standardized across Latin America. Credential names, legal scopes, supervision requirements, medicine categories, dispensing rules, registration, and training systems vary by country.

OIT/Cinterfor is the ILO's specialized vocational-training center for Latin America and the Caribbean and maintains links to national training institutions. Readers outside Colombia should use the OIT/Cinterfor institutional network to identify the relevant public vocational-training body, then verify the country's health/pharmacy regulator and current legal scope before selecting training or accepting work.

## Safety, medication accuracy, data integrity, and AI boundaries for Version 2

The controlled English guide must use high-level safety and escalation language rather than procedural medication instruction. Required boundaries include:

1. **Prescription and patient identity:** never invent, infer, backfill, or silently change a patient, prescriber, medicine, strength, dosage form, quantity, directions, allergy, insurance, or prescription field. Missing, contradictory, illegible, or questionable information must be escalated through the pharmacy's approved workflow.
2. **Clinical judgment:** diagnosis, prescribing, therapeutic substitution, final clinical verification, counseling reserved to pharmacists/authorized clinicians, and decisions about contraindications or interactions must remain with the appropriately authorized professional.
3. **Medication preparation/compounding:** preparation, sterile or nonsterile compounding, hazardous-drug handling, vaccine administration, controlled-substance handling, and automated dispensing are conditional specialized activities requiring the jurisdiction's authorization, employer procedures, training, supervision, equipment, quality controls, and documented competence.
4. **Look-alike/sound-alike and selection risk:** readers should use the pharmacy's approved barcode, product-selection, independent-check, shelf-layout, alert, and escalation controls rather than rely on memory, package appearance, AI output, or informal shortcuts.
5. **Storage/cold chain/recalls:** follow manufacturer labeling, employer procedures, monitoring systems, quarantine/recall procedures, and qualified escalation. Do not improvise storage temperatures, stability periods, beyond-use dates, or disposition decisions.
6. **Hazardous drugs, sharps, exposure, infection prevention:** follow employer exposure-control plans, PPE requirements, engineering controls, spill/exposure procedures, infection-prevention rules, and specialist training. A career guide must not provide a substitute recipe for hazardous-drug handling or exposure response.
7. **Controlled substances and diversion prevention:** maintain exact authorized records and physical security. Never bypass perpetual inventory, discrepancy review, witness requirements, audit trails, access controls, or reporting obligations.
8. **Privacy and cybersecurity:** prescriptions, patient profiles, insurance/payment information, identifiers, medication histories, and pharmacy-system credentials are sensitive. Use only approved systems and access. Do not paste patient or prescription data into public AI services unless explicitly authorized and appropriately protected.
9. **AI and automation:** AI may assist with non-sensitive training explanations or practice questions, but must not generate or alter live prescription records, patient histories, medication selections, quantities, dosage calculations, controlled-substance records, compounding records, insurance claims, inventory corrections, expiration/beyond-use data, or final verification. Human review and the authorized pharmacy system remain controlling.
10. **Stop-work/escalation:** uncertainty about identity, prescription completeness, medicine selection, dose/quantity, allergy, interaction concern, broken packaging, storage excursion, recall, controlled-substance discrepancy, compounding deviation, system mismatch, or unauthorized request should trigger the employer's stop-and-escalate process rather than improvisation.

These controls are career-preparation boundaries. They do not constitute pharmacy practice instruction, medical advice, legal advice, regulatory interpretation, compounding instruction, vaccine-administration instruction, or independent professional review.

## Research decision

**Current-source Research: PASS.** The evidence is sufficient to reconstruct a controlled English Version 2 guide while preserving jurisdictional, supervision, medication-safety, pharmacy-scope, data-integrity, privacy, and AI boundaries.

The English master must not collapse U.S., Canadian, or Colombian roles into a single credential or legal scope; must distinguish official wage data from the current non-government estimate; must present funding as conditional; and must not claim independent human review, professional translation certification, accessibility certification, medical/pharmacy review, legal/regulatory review, accreditation, guaranteed licensure/registration, guaranteed funding, guaranteed employment, or guaranteed earnings.
