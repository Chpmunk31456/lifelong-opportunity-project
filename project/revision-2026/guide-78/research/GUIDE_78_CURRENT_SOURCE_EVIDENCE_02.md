# Guide 78 — Current Source Evidence 02

**Occupation:** Web and Digital Interface Designer  
**Stage:** Current-source research  
**Research date:** 2026-08-21  
**Controlled branch:** `revision/guide-00-100-2026`

## Research conclusion

Current authoritative evidence supports the Guide 78 scope as **Web and Digital Interface Designer**. The strongest cross-country anchors are:

- United States: **O*NET-SOC 15-1255.00 — Web and Digital Interface Designers**;
- Canada: **NOC 21233 — Web designers**;
- Colombia: **CUOC 21662 — Diseñadores de imágenes computarizadas e interacción digital**.

These sources support a guide centered on website and interface design, user interaction, prototypes, information architecture, usability, accessibility-aware design, responsive behavior, visual systems, testing, and collaboration with development teams. The guide must not imply that interface designers automatically hold software-engineering, cybersecurity-engineering, legal-compliance, accessibility-certification, product-management, deployment, or production-operations authority.

## 1. United States — O*NET occupation benchmark

O*NET identifies **15-1255.00 — Web and Digital Interface Designers** as a Bright Outlook occupation. Its current definition includes designing digital user interfaces or websites; developing and testing layouts, interfaces, functionality and navigation for browser/device compatibility and usability; evaluating design against web and accessibility standards; analyzing use metrics; and designing human-computer interaction with attention to usability and aesthetics.

O*NET also describes collaboration with front-end/back-end developers, user research, prototypes/models, technical documentation, testing, website updates, and in some roles supporting code. The guide therefore should explain that coding depth varies by job and that design responsibility is not identical to software-development responsibility.

Current O*NET workforce data show:

- employment, 2024: **128,900**;
- projected growth, 2024–2034: **7% or higher / much faster than average**;
- projected openings, 2024–2034: **9,100**.

These openings are projections over the full occupation and must not be represented as guaranteed jobs for an individual learner.

O*NET also lists apprenticeship examples for this occupation, including Digital Accessibility Technician, User Experience Designer, and Web & Digital Interface Designer. Availability is program- and geography-specific and must be verified at Apprenticeship.gov or through a local workforce system.

Sources:
- https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/15-1255.00
- https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/15-1255.00

## 5. Canada — NOC 21233 and current labour-market evidence

Canada Job Bank identifies **Web designers** as **NOC 21233**. Its current description says web designers develop the visual presentation, layout and functionality of websites and related applications and are responsible for ensuring that sites are easy to use and information is easy to access.

Job Bank's current national summary reports a median wage of **C$33.65/hour**. The national wage table updated November 19, 2025 reports:

- low: **C$20.00/hour**;
- median: **C$33.65/hour**;
- high: **C$57.69/hour**.

Job Bank's employment-requirements page says the occupation typically requires a bachelor's degree in computer science or graphic design, or completion of a college program in computer science, graphic arts or web design; experience as a graphic designer is usually required. Job Bank states that, according to its records, the occupation is **not regulated in Canada**. These are typical occupation requirements rather than a universal rule for every employer.

## 7. Colombia — CUOC 21662

OCUPACOL identifies **CUOC 21662 — Diseñadores de imágenes computarizadas e interacción digital**, skill level 4. Its functions directly support Guide 78, including:

- designing digital content for computer applications;
- proposing intuitive, functional and adaptable user interfaces;
- creating and testing low-, medium- or high-fidelity digital prototypes;
- integrating user needs and functional requirements;
- planning interaction flow and information architecture;
- developing wireframes and visual product/service designs.

The profile's listed denominations include **Diseñador de experiencia de usuario, Diseñador de interacción, Diseñador de interfaces de usuario, Diseñador UI, Diseñador UX, Diseñador web, Diseñador web y multimedia, Diseñador front-end** and related digital-interface titles.

OCUPACOL displays labour-market indicators for the occupation, but the guide should not convert those indicators into a universal salary or employment guarantee. Use the profile primarily as an occupational-classification and duty source unless methodology and reference period are explicitly suitable for a numeric claim.

Source:
- https://ocupacol.mintrabajo.gov.co/Profile/OccupationalProfile/21662

## 11. Accessibility — WCAG 2.2

W3C WCAG 2.2 remains an essential design reference. For common Level AA interface design work, controlled values include:

- normal text contrast: at least **4.5:1** under SC 1.4.3, subject to stated exceptions;
- large text contrast: at least **3:1**, subject to exceptions;
- text should be resizable to **200%** without loss of content or functionality under SC 1.4.4, subject to scope/technology conditions.

Guide 78 should additionally teach keyboard/focus, meaningful structure, reflow, target sizing, error identification, accessible names/instructions and testing with real implementation. A designer must not claim WCAG/legal accessibility conformance from a visual mock-up alone because implementation and assistive-technology behavior matter.

Source:
- https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/

## 12. Privacy and cybersecurity boundary

Interface designers routinely handle prototypes, analytics, test accounts, unpublished product information, user research data, screenshots and shared design systems. CISA Secure Our World materials reinforce practical account-security controls such as strong passwords/password managers, MFA, phishing awareness and software updates.

The guide should require approved storage/sharing systems, least-necessary access, avoidance of real sensitive data in public portfolio prototypes, and escalation of security/privacy questions to authorized teams. Designers should not invent security requirements or represent themselves as security engineers based solely on interface-design responsibility.

Source:
- https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world

## 13. Responsible AI boundary

NIST's AI Risk Management Framework is a voluntary resource for managing AI risk, and its Generative AI Profile addresses risks specific to generative AI. For Guide 78, AI can assist brainstorming, low-risk draft copy, wireframe alternatives, synthetic practice content, code explanations or design-system exploration only where policy allows.

Human verification is required for facts, accessibility, security/privacy implications, code, user flows, design tokens, licensing/provenance, and production decisions. Confidential product plans, user-research data, credentials, proprietary code or personal information must not be uploaded to unapproved AI services.

Sources:
- https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
- https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence
