## Research conclusion
Guide 90 has strong current-source support. The occupation is recognizable across the United States, Canada and Colombia, but readers must not be told that the three classification systems or their statistics are perfect one-to-one equivalents.

- **United States:** O*NET-SOC **15-1299.09 — Information Technology Project Managers** is the strongest current functional benchmark for IT project coordination/management work and is Bright Outlook, updated 2026.
- **Canada:** Job Bank directly maps **project coordinator - information technology (IT)** to **NOC 21222 — Information systems specialists**.
- **Colombia:** **CUOC 25190** explicitly includes the title **Coordinador de proyecto informático**. **CUOC 25110** is an adjacent comparison because it includes **Analista de proyecto TI (Tecnologías de la Información)**.

No blocker prevents controlled reconstruction.

## United States — O*NET 15-1299.09
O*NET describes Information Technology Project Managers as planning, initiating and managing IT projects; guiding technical staff; serving as liaison between business and technical aspects; planning stages; assessing business implications; and monitoring deadlines, standards and cost targets.

### Core current task evidence
Current O*NET tasks include:
- manage execution against budget, schedule and scope;
- identify and resolve project problems;
- track milestones and deliverables;
- submit deliverables against quality standards;
- assess customer needs and priorities;
- initiate/review/approve project-plan modifications within assigned authority;
- schedule/facilitate IT project meetings;
- coordinate project personnel;
- develop implementation and cost-benefit/ROI plans;
- identify resource needs;
- develop/update project plans including objectives, technologies, systems, information specifications, schedules, funding and staffing;
- perform risk assessments and response planning;
- prepare evidence-based project status reports;
- support vendor/consultant selection;
- manage or support budgets according to role;
- establish communication plans;
- develop/manage work breakdown structures (WBS);
- coordinate resources and suppliers.

The revised guide must scale these responsibilities to the coordinator title. A coordinator may support or administer many of these controls without holding final budget, staffing, architecture, procurement or release authority.

Current national summary:

- prospects vary by province/territory;
- national page modified **August 7, 2026**.

### Canada national wages
Current Job Bank national wages, updated November 19, 2025:
- low **C$28.85/hour**;
- median **C$46.15/hour**;
- high **C$68.68/hour**.

### Canada outlook
Job Bank's national 2024–2033 demand/supply view says labour demand and supply are expected to be **broadly in line**. Three-year prospects vary by province/territory, so readers must verify location-specific conditions.

### Canada typical requirements
Job Bank currently states that NOC 21222 typically requires:
- a bachelor's degree in computer science, computer systems engineering, software engineering, business administration or related discipline, **or** completion of a college computer-science program;
- computer-programming experience usually required;
- related IT professional certification may be required by some employers.

These are occupational-group requirements and not an absolute rule for every coordinator vacancy.

### Canada regulation caveat
Regulation is not uniform. Job Bank currently lists professional designations associated with this NOC as regulated in some provinces, including:
- Alberta — Information Systems Professionals (certified);
- Saskatchewan — Information Technology Professional.

The guide must not imply that every IT Project Coordinator job in Canada is regulated. Readers should check the province, exact title and duties.

## Colombia — CUOC 25190 primary exact-title comparison
OCUPACOL **CUOC 25190 — Desarrolladores y analistas de software y multimedia no clasificados en otras ocupaciones**, competency level 4, explicitly includes **Coordinador de proyecto informático** among its occupational titles.

Relevant official functions include:
- establish solution requirements and structure technical proposals for ICT services/products;
- evaluate and verify ICT solutions against user needs, directives, standards and reference models;
- coordinate other engineers/technicians in implementation of ICT services/products;
- manage risks of IT solutions;
- control service quality and test software solutions;
- perform related ICT implementation functions.

This is the strongest exact-title Colombian comparison, although its broader CUOC family also contains software-testing, deployment and other specialized roles.

OCUPACOL's displayed historical market indicators include a methodology warning that the figures do not have statistical representativeness. Therefore Guide 90 must **not** present its historical salary range as a representative national IT Project Coordinator salary.

## Colombia — CUOC 25110 adjacent comparison
CUOC **25110 — Analistas de sistemas** is useful as an adjacent functional comparison because its official titles include **Analista de proyecto TI (Tecnologías de la Información)**. It may be more relevant where the coordinator role is strongly requirements/systems-analysis oriented.

Guide 90 should keep CUOC 25190 as the primary exact-title comparison and use 25110 only as an adjacent role-family reference.

## Required professional-content controls for Guide 90

### Coordinator versus adjacent roles
The revised guide must distinguish:
- **IT Project Coordinator:** supports/coordinates schedules, actions, dependencies, evidence, meetings, documentation, status and delivery controls within assigned authority;
- **IT Project Manager:** typically holds broader accountability for scope, schedule, budget, risk, stakeholders and delivery decisions;
- **Program Manager:** coordinates related projects/outcomes across a larger program;
- **Scrum Master:** facilitates Scrum team process and impediment handling where Scrum is actually used;
- **Product Manager/Product Owner:** focuses on product value, priorities and product/backlog decisions according to the organization's model.

Titles vary by employer, so duties and decision rights matter more than labels.

### Project definition and governance
Before tracking work, establish or confirm:
- objective/outcome;
- sponsor/owner;
- scope and exclusions;
- deliverables;
- milestones;
- dependencies;
- budget/resource assumptions;
- quality/acceptance criteria;
- governance and decision rights;
- security/privacy/accessibility constraints;
- reporting cadence;
- definition of completion.

### Schedule and dependency controls
A coordinator should understand work packages, WBS/backlog concepts, milestones, dependencies, critical handoffs, target versus forecast dates, blocked work and ownership. Dates should not be silently changed to make status appear healthy.

### Risks, issues, assumptions and dependencies
The guide should teach a practical RAID-style discipline while acknowledging terminology varies. Every meaningful item should have an owner, status, response/action and due/review date where applicable.

### Meetings and action management
Meetings should have a purpose, relevant attendees, agenda, decisions, actions, owners and dates. Minutes must distinguish decisions from proposals and open questions. Do not manufacture agreement when it was not reached.

### Evidence-based status reporting
Status should be supported by actual milestones, deliverables, test results, approvals, costs, incidents and dependencies. The coordinator must not hide material red flags, change definitions retroactively or report green status merely because escalation is uncomfortable.

### Change control
Changes to scope, schedule, budget, requirements, architecture, security controls or release plans should be assessed and routed through the organization's approval mechanism. The coordinator can document/route change without assuming approval authority.

### Requirements and acceptance
Track requirements/acceptance criteria to deliverables and tests where appropriate. Do not declare acceptance unless the authorized stakeholder/process has accepted the deliverable.

### Agile, predictive and hybrid delivery
The guide may explain backlog, sprint, Kanban, WBS, milestones and stage-gate concepts, but must not imply one framework is mandatory or that a coordinator automatically owns Scrum Master/Product Owner/Project Manager authority.

### Vendors and procurement
Coordinators may collect requirements, quotes, deliverables, dates and evidence, but vendor selection, contractual commitments and purchase authorization remain with assigned roles.

### Release and production authority
Tracking release readiness does not itself grant authority to deploy, waive failed controls, approve security exceptions or accept production risk. Release/change authority must stay explicit.

### Cybersecurity and privacy coordination
Projects should identify security/privacy requirements early, track required reviews, avoid exposing secrets or protected data in project tools, and escalate findings to assigned security/privacy owners. A coordinator is not automatically the security approver or legal/privacy authority.

### Accessibility
Accessibility requirements should be captured early and tracked through design, development, testing and acceptance where relevant. Passing an automated accessibility scan is evidence, not proof of legal compliance.

### Responsible AI
Policy-approved AI may help draft agendas, summaries, action lists, risk ideas, status wording, synthetic portfolio artifacts or non-sensitive templates. Controls must include:
- no confidential project plans, credentials, customer data, source code, contracts or unreleased information in unapproved AI tools;
- validate dates, owners, decisions and risks against authoritative records;
- do not let AI invent approvals, commitments, requirements or project health;
- human accountability remains with assigned staff;
- AI does not approve changes, releases, budgets or security exceptions.

### Safe portfolio
Use synthetic or public scenarios. Useful portfolio artifacts include:
- project charter;
- stakeholder map;
- milestone schedule;
- RAID log;
- action/decision log;
- change request;
- weekly status report;
- requirements/acceptance traceability sample;
- release-readiness checklist;
- retrospective/lessons-learned summary.

Never publish employer roadmaps, contracts, credentials, customer information, incident details, architecture diagrams or confidential project evidence.
