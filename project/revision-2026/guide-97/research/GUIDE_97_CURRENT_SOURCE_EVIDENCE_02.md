## United States — official occupation, duties, wages, and outlook

### O*NET OnLine — U.S. Department of Labor
Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/17-3027.00

Controlled facts:
- O*NET-SOC **17-3027.00 — Mechanical Engineering Technologists and Technicians**; profile updated in 2026.
- O*NET defines the occupation as applying mechanical-engineering theory and principles to modify, develop, test, or adjust machinery and equipment under the direction of engineering staff or physical scientists.
- Reported job titles include Engineering Technician, Engineering Technologist, Manufacturing Engineering Technician, Mechanical Designer, Mechanical Technician, Process Engineering Technician, Process Technician, and Research and Development Technician.
- Core tasks include assembling/disassembling complex mechanical systems; interpreting engineering sketches/specifications/drawings; performing capacity calculations for engineering approval; reviewing drawings and test specifications; supporting mechanical design, fabrication, testing, and documentation; testing machines, components, materials, and products; analyzing test results; documenting test procedures/results; and preparing designs or sketches for mechanical systems and components.
- O*NET reports **2025 median wages of $35.82/hour and $74,510/year**.
- Employment: approximately **38,300 workers in 2024**.
- Projected growth: **little or no change from 2024 to 2034**.
- Projected openings: approximately **3,200 per year** over the projection period.
- O*NET identifies manufacturing as the largest sector, followed by professional, scientific, and technical services.

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Occupational Outlook Handbook
Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/mechanical-engineering-technicians.htm

Controlled facts:
- BLS describes mechanical engineering technologists and technicians as helping mechanical engineers design, develop, test, and manufacture machines and other devices.
- Typical entry education is an **associate's degree or other postsecondary training**, although actual employer requirements vary.
- The OOH reports a **May 2024 median annual wage of $68,730** and about **3,200 openings per year** over 2024–2034 despite little or no overall employment growth.
- BLS reports about **38,300 jobs in 2024**.
- Typical work environments include factories and research/development laboratories, and workers may encounter machinery, chemicals, or other industrial hazards requiring established safety procedures.
- Typical duties include reviewing drawings/specifications, preparing layouts, recommending equipment-design changes, planning and assembling parts, conducting tests, comparing results with specifications, and making calculations for cost/lifespan estimates.

## Canada — official occupation, wages, prospects, and requirements

### Job Bank — Mechanical engineering technologists and technicians
Sources:
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/3236/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/occupation/3236/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/3236/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/outlook-occupation/3236/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/3235/ca

Controlled facts:
- Canadian classification: **NOC 22301 — Mechanical engineering technologists and technicians**.
- Job Bank describes the occupation as providing technical support/services or working independently in design, development, maintenance, and testing of machines, components, tools, HVAC systems, power-generation/conversion plants, manufacturing plants, and equipment.
- Employers include consulting engineering, manufacturing and processing companies, institutions, and government departments.
- Canada-wide wages, updated November 19, 2025: **C$23.08 low, C$35.00 median, C$51.28 high per hour**; national reference period 2023–2024, Statistics Canada Labour Force Survey.
- Job Bank's national outlook page, updated in 2026, reports a **moderate risk of labour shortage nationally for 2024–2033**, while near-term prospects vary by province and territory.
- Job Bank reports approximately **21,900 workers in 2023** for the national 10-year outlook context.
- Typical requirements distinguish technologists and technicians: mechanical engineering technologists usually require a **two- or three-year college program** in mechanical engineering technology; mechanical engineering technicians usually require a **one- or two-year college program**.
- Certification through provincial engineering/applied-science technologist/technician associations is available and may be required for some positions; supervised experience is generally required before certification.
- In Quebec, use of the title **Professional Technologist** is regulated and requires membership in the relevant regulatory body.

## Professional scope and engineering-authority boundaries

The controlled guide must distinguish technician/technologist support work from professional-engineering authority.

A technician may support design, drafting, fabrication, testing, measurement, troubleshooting, maintenance, documentation, quality, and prototype work, but should not assume authority to:
- sign or seal engineering documents where a licensed/registered professional is required;
- approve structural, pressure, lifting, machine-safety, or other regulated designs beyond delegated authority;
- override engineering change control or configuration management;
- certify legal/regulatory compliance unless specifically authorized and qualified;
- bypass approved test procedures, guards, interlocks, lockout/tagout, or permit systems;
- direct hazardous work outside training and employer procedures.

Licensing, title protection, and engineering practice rules vary by jurisdiction. Readers must verify current local requirements with the applicable regulator/employer.

## Safety — hazardous energy, machinery, stored energy, pressure, lifting, and hot work

### OSHA — Control of Hazardous Energy (Lockout/Tagout)
Sources:
- https://www.osha.gov/control-hazardous-energy
- https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/OSHA3120.pdf

Controlled use:
- Lockout/tagout protects workers from unexpected energization/startup or release of hazardous energy during servicing and maintenance.
- Hazardous energy can include electrical, mechanical, hydraulic, pneumatic, chemical, thermal, gravitational, and stored energy.
- Isolation must follow the employer's established energy-control program and authorized-worker procedures.
- A technician should never infer that pressing stop, using software controls, or turning off a local switch alone proves zero energy.

### OSHA — machine guarding and mechanical power transmission
Controlled use:
- Machine guards and protective devices must not be bypassed casually for testing or adjustment.
- Tasks involving rotating shafts, belts, gears, pinch points, cutting tools, presses, conveyors, stored spring force, elevated loads, pressure systems, or hot surfaces require employer procedures, appropriate controls, and trained/authorized personnel.
- Lifting/rigging, pressure testing, welding/hot work, confined spaces, electrical work, and similar high-risk tasks may require specialized training, permits, qualified persons, or other controls depending on the workplace and jurisdiction.

## Cybersecurity and connected industrial systems

### CISA — Secure Our World
Source: https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world

Controlled use:
- CISA emphasizes phishing awareness, strong unique passwords/password managers, multifactor authentication, and software updates.
- Mechanical engineering technicians increasingly work with CAD/PDM/PLM systems, networked test equipment, programmable controls, data loggers, sensors, industrial PCs, remote-support tools, and cloud-connected documentation.
- The guide should require employer-approved accounts, least-necessary access, approved removable-media practices, verification of unusual credential or payment requests, prompt reporting of suspected compromise, and no unapproved connection of devices to production/industrial networks.
- A technician should not change firmware, PLC/controller logic, safety-system settings, or network configurations without explicit authorization, backups/change control, and competent review.

## Responsible AI

### NIST AI Risk Management Framework and Generative AI Profile
Sources:
- https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
- https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence-profile

Controlled use:
- NIST's AI RMF is a voluntary framework for managing risks from AI systems; the Generative AI Profile extends that risk-management approach to generative AI.
- Low-risk uses can include brainstorming test-checklist structure, drafting non-sensitive documentation, creating fictional practice problems, summarizing public standards guidance, or improving plain-language explanations.
- Human verification is mandatory for dimensions, tolerances, materials, loads, pressure/temperature limits, calculations, maintenance intervals, test acceptance criteria, safety instructions, regulatory requirements, engineering changes, and any other consequential technical decision.
- Confidential drawings, customer data, credentials, export-controlled information, proprietary designs, unpublished failure data, or production-system details should not be entered into unapproved AI tools.
- AI output must never substitute for an approved drawing, engineering calculation, signed procedure, calibrated measurement, equipment manual, safety standard, or authorized professional judgment.

## Accessibility and inclusive learning/work

The revised guide should:
- use plain-language explanations with descriptive headings and short checkable steps;
- explain that physical job demands vary widely across lab, CAD, manufacturing, maintenance, field, quality, and test roles;
- encourage readers to compare actual job essential functions rather than assuming every mechanical technician role requires the same lifting, standing, hearing, vision, mobility, or travel demands;
- encourage use of employer/school accommodation processes where applicable without promising a particular accommodation or legal outcome;
- make digital learning materials navigable and avoid relying only on color or dense visual layouts to convey critical information.
