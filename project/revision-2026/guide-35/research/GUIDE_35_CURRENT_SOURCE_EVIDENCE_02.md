## United States — occupational definition and duties

### U.S. Department of Labor O*NET OnLine — official occupational source

Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-5061.00

O*NET-SOC 43-5061.00, **Production, Planning, and Expediting Clerks**, was updated in 2026. O*NET defines the occupation around coordinating and expediting the flow of work and materials within or between departments according to production schedules.

Representative duties supported by the current O*NET record include:

- distributing production schedules or work orders;
- revising schedules when designs, labor, materials, backlogs, or other interruptions change;
- reviewing schedules, work orders, staffing tables, and material priorities;
- arranging delivery, assembly, or distribution of supplies or parts;
- coordinating with personnel, vendors, and customers to resolve delays;
- maintaining inventories needed for production;
- compiling production, inventory, cost, and progress information; and
- documenting delays, production problems, and changes.

Current reported titles include Expeditor, Inventory Control Specialist, Material Requirements Planner (MRP), Materials Coordinator, Materials Planner, Production Assistant, Production Clerk, Production Controller, Production Planner, and Production Scheduler.

### U.S. preparation level

O*NET places the occupation in Job Zone 1–2, indicating very little to some preparation. Its current summary says a high school diploma or GED is usually required, although requirements vary by employer, and job training may range from a few days to one year.

O*NET respondent education data should be described carefully rather than converted into a universal requirement: 37% reported a high-school diploma or equivalent required for a new hire, 21% some college without a degree, and 19% less than high school.

### U.S. work context and skills

O*NET shows strong emphasis on computer use, communication, information processing, monitoring, planning, scheduling, recordkeeping, exactness, coordination, and problem solving. Current software categories include ERP systems, MRP/logistics and supply-chain software, spreadsheets (including Microsoft Excel), office suites, project-management tools, inventory software, databases, and procurement software.

The guide should therefore present spreadsheet literacy, production scheduling, inventory/materials concepts, ERP/MRP familiarity, clear written communication, and careful data entry as practical employability skills while avoiding claims that a particular proprietary platform is universally mandatory.

- Median annual wage: **$59,650**

Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-5061.00

## United States — apprenticeship and work-based learning

O*NET lists three Registered Apprenticeship titles associated with this occupation, including Material Coordinator and Production, Planning, and Expediting Clerk, and points users to Apprenticeship.gov.

Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-5061.00

Apprenticeship.gov describes Registered Apprenticeship as a paid, employer-driven pathway combining on-the-job learning, mentorship, progressive wage increases, related classroom instruction, and a portable nationally recognized credential.

Source: https://www.apprenticeship.gov/career-seekers

The guide should tell readers to verify that a specific apprenticeship is currently open and occupation-relevant rather than implying that every locality has an available production-planning apprenticeship.

## United States — funding and free/low-cost training pathways

### WIOA / American Job Centers

CareerOneStop maintains a WIOA-Eligible Training Program Finder and directs prospective participants to American Job Centers to verify eligibility for Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act training services.

Source: https://www.careeronestop.org/LocalHelp/EmploymentAndTraining/find-WIOA-training-programs.aspx

Funding eligibility varies by person, state, local workforce area, training provider, and program. The guide must not promise WIOA payment.

### Employer-supported learning

Because this occupation commonly involves employer-specific production systems, ERP/MRP tools, inventory rules, and workflows, readers should ask employers about paid onboarding, cross-training, tuition assistance, software training, shift-differential learning opportunities, and advancement pathways before paying privately for expensive credentials. This is career-planning advice, not a claim that any employer must provide such support.

## Canada — comparable occupational pathway

### Government of Canada Job Bank — Production logistics workers, NOC 14402

Current Job Bank profiles for Production logistics workers (NOC 14402) describe work that expedites the flow of work and materials within an establishment and prepares merchandise for shipment. Employment is found in manufacturing, construction, printing/publishing, and other industrial establishments.

Sources:

- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/occupation/2460/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/2477/ca

Current national requirements state that completion of secondary school is usually required. Job Bank reports no identified professional certification requirement and classifies the occupation as not regulated in Canada in its current records. This does not eliminate employer-specific hiring requirements.

### Canada wages

Job Bank's current national summary for Production Clerk / Production logistics workers reports a median wage of approximately **CAD $30.77/hour** in Canada.

Source: https://on.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/2474/ca

Regional wage and outlook conditions vary materially. For example, current Job Bank summaries report approximately CAD $31.73/hour in the Toronto region, while prospects there are rated very limited. Readers should use the Job Bank location filter rather than treating a national median as a local promise.

### Canada education funding

The Canada Student Financial Assistance Program provides grants and loans for eligible post-secondary study, with eligibility and designated programs determined through federal/provincial/territorial systems.

Sources:

- https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/education/student-aid/grants-loans.html
- https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/education/student-aid/grants-loans/how-funding-works.html

Canada.ca states that grants do not need to be repaid and loans must be repaid; eligibility depends on factors including province/territory, income, dependants, tuition/living expenses, disability status, school, and program. Readers must verify current eligibility before enrolling or borrowing.

## Colombia — occupation and training pathways

### SENA — Planeación de la Producción

SENA's current Betowa catalog lists **Planeación de la Producción** as an open virtual complementary program of **48 hours**. The course develops the ability to prepare a general production plan based on sales plans, inventory levels, and production capacity. Topics include production costs, capacity calculations, and material requirements planning (MRP).

Source: https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/planeacion-de-la-produccion?modality=V&offertype=open&programId=175616

This is directly relevant low-cost/public training evidence for Guide 35, but availability, cohort status, admission rules, and program details must be rechecked at the time of enrollment. It should not be described as guaranteeing employment or as equivalent to a degree.

### Colombia Sistema Nacional de Cualificaciones

The Ministry of Labour's Sistema Nacional de Cualificaciones portal provides searches for Formación para el Trabajo and recognition of prior learning. Its current public information describes Formación para el Trabajo as competency-focused and aligned to productive-sector needs, including dual models combining study with workplace practice.

Sources:

- https://cualificaciones.mintrabajo.gov.co/
- https://cualificaciones.mintrabajo.gov.co/ciudadanos/informacion/ft

This provides a Colombia-specific pathway for readers to investigate recognized training and prior-learning recognition rather than assuming a single academic degree is required.

### Colombia occupational exploration

OCUPACOL, the Ministry of Labour's occupational catalog, provides occupational profiles, employment-title data, and statistical indicators intended to connect education/training with productive-sector needs.

Source: https://ocupacol.mintrabajo.gov.co/

The revised guide should direct Colombian readers to search related titles such as planeador/programador de producción, auxiliar de producción, coordinador de materiales, logística de producción, inventarios, and abastecimiento as appropriate, while verifying the exact occupational profile and employer requirements rather than asserting a one-to-one title equivalence.

## Latin America and Caribbean — regional training pathway

OIT/Cinterfor is the ILO's specialized vocational-training center for Latin America and the Caribbean. Its regional materials emphasize competency development, vocational/professional training institutions, qualification frameworks, and recognition/certification of prior learning across the region.

Sources:

- https://www.oitcinterfor.org/
- https://www.oitcinterfor.org/node/7055
- https://www.oitcinterfor.org/node/9051

The Guide 35 regional section should therefore tell readers to search their country's public vocational-training institution and national qualifications/competency-recognition system, rather than assuming SENA rules or U.S./Canadian credentials transfer automatically across Latin America.

Examples of public vocational-training institutions commonly represented in OIT/Cinterfor's regional network include SENA (Colombia), SENCE (Chile), INTECAP (Guatemala), INFOTEP (Dominican Republic), and INEFOP (Uruguay). Program availability and funding rules must be checked in the relevant country.

## Scholarship and low-cost search strategy

The revised guide should use a free-first sequence:

1. Search public vocational/workforce systems and employer-paid training first.
2. Check WIOA/American Job Centers in the United States where eligible.
3. Check Canada Student Grants and Loans plus provincial/territorial aid for eligible post-secondary programs in Canada.
4. In Colombia, check SENA Betowa and the Sistema Nacional de Cualificaciones before buying private training.
5. Elsewhere in Latin America, locate the national public vocational-training institution through OIT/Cinterfor and verify current enrollment/funding rules.
6. Ask employers about paid onboarding, cross-training, tuition assistance, certification reimbursement, apprenticeships, internships, or work-study options.
7. Compare total cost, refund/withdrawal rules, time commitment, employer recognition, and actual job-posting requirements before paying for a private certificate.

No scholarship, grant, loan, apprenticeship, or employer benefit should be presented as automatic or guaranteed.

## Editorial implications for the English revision

The revised English master should:

- use the exact occupation title **Production Planning and Expediting Clerk** while explaining common adjacent titles;
- distinguish entry-level clerk/coordinator work from more advanced Production Planner or Scheduler roles;
- make high-school completion a common pathway, not an absolute universal requirement;
- emphasize scheduling, material-flow coordination, inventory awareness, spreadsheets, ERP/MRP exposure, communication, and accuracy;
- include realistic work context such as time pressure, production-floor interaction, and possible PPE/noise exposure depending on facility;
- state the BLS/O*NET 2025 median wage separately from private 2026 planner/scheduler estimates;
- include free-first U.S., Canada, Colombia, and Latin America training/funding pathways;
- include Registered Apprenticeship as an availability-dependent option rather than a guaranteed route;
- avoid implying that private certificates are mandatory;
- explain that advanced planner roles may require stronger ERP/MRP, analytics, manufacturing, supply-chain, or post-secondary preparation;
- preserve accessibility, plain language, privacy, ethical AI use, and anti-scam guidance; and
- require readers to verify current local requirements, wages, program availability, work authorization, and employer expectations.
