# Guide 82 — Current Source Evidence 02

**Occupation:** Computer Support and Help Desk Technician  
**Stage:** Current-source Research  
**Research date:** 2026-08-21  
**Controlled branch:** `revision/guide-00-100-2026`

## Research conclusion

Current evidence supports Guide 82 as **Computer Support and Help Desk Technician**, centered on first-line user support, diagnosis, safe remediation, escalation, ticket documentation, hardware/software setup, account/access support within assigned authority, user education, and secure handling of organizational information.

Strong occupational anchors are:

- United States: **O*NET-SOC 15-1232.00 — Computer User Support Specialists**;
- Canada: **NOC 22221 — User support technicians**;
- Colombia: **CUOC 35121**, whose occupational denominations explicitly include `Analista de computadores mesa de ayuda`, `Operador servicio de asistencia informática`, `Técnico de servicios informáticos a usuarios`, `Técnico de soporte de sistemas e informática`, `Técnico de soporte informático`, `Técnico en asistencia y soporte de tecnologías de la información`, and `Técnico soporte mesa de ayuda (help desk)`.

The guide must distinguish user-support authority from network/security architecture, privileged administration, software engineering, incident-response command, legal/privacy decisions, and business approval authority.

## 1. United States — O*NET occupation benchmark

O*NET identifies **15-1232.00 — Computer User Support Specialists**, updated in 2026. The occupation provides technical assistance to computer users in person, by telephone, or electronically and may support hardware, software, printing, installation, word processing, email, and operating systems.

Representative tasks include:

- answering user questions about software or hardware operation;
- reading technical manuals, conferring with users, and running diagnostics to investigate problems;
- setting up employee equipment and ensuring appropriate operating-system/software installation;
- installing and performing minor repairs to hardware, software, or peripherals according to specifications;
- entering commands and observing system operation to verify correct functioning and detect errors.

O*NET classifies the occupation in **Job Zone Three — Medium Preparation Needed**. This does not mean every employer has the same credential requirement.

Sources:
- https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-1232.00
- https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/15-1232.00
- https://www.onetonline.org/skills/zone/15-1232.00

## 3. United States — employment outlook

BLS projections surfaced through O*NET report:

- employment in 2024: **729,500**;
- projected employment in 2034: **702,500**;
- projected growth, 2024–2034: **-4%**;
- projected annual openings, 2024–2034: **40,800**.

The projected decline should be stated plainly. Annual openings remain significant because openings include replacement needs as well as growth. The guide should emphasize transferable troubleshooting, identity/access discipline, endpoint/cloud support, documentation, security awareness, automation supervision, and progression rather than promising that basic scripted help-desk work will expand indefinitely.

Source:
- https://www.onetonline.org/link/localtrends/15-1232.00

## 7. Canada — NOC 22221

Canada Job Bank identifies **User support technicians — NOC 22221**. The occupation provides first-line technical support to users experiencing difficulty with computer hardware, applications, and communications software.

Job Bank states typical employment requirements include:

- completion of a college program in computer science, computer programming, or network administration; or
- college/other courses in computer programming or network administration;
- vendor certification or training may be required by some employers.

Job Bank's summary describes the occupation as typically requiring college or apprenticeship preparation, while actual employer requirements vary.

## 13. Security and authority boundaries

A help-desk technician may have unusually powerful access to identities, endpoints, credentials, remote sessions, tickets, configuration details, and internal systems. Guide 82 should require:

- identity verification before sensitive account actions;
- least-privilege access;
- approved remote-support tools;
- no request for or recording of a user's password when policy prohibits it;
- no disabling of security controls merely to close a ticket;
- documented escalation for suspected phishing, malware, data exposure, account compromise, or privileged-access requests;
- no independent firewall/network/security-architecture changes outside assigned authority;
- secure handling of screenshots, logs, tickets, and customer/employee data.

CISA Secure Our World supports phishing awareness, strong passwords/password managers, MFA, and software updates.

Source:
- https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world

## 14. Accessibility and inclusive support

Support staff should know how to avoid breaking accessibility settings and how to escalate accessibility barriers. Section508.gov and WCAG 2.2 provide useful current accessibility references. Automated checks do not establish complete legal accessibility certification.

Sources:
- https://www.section508.gov/
- https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/

## 15. Responsible AI and automation

Approved AI can assist with low-risk tasks such as drafting ticket summaries, suggesting troubleshooting hypotheses, organizing knowledge-base notes, or explaining non-sensitive error messages. Human verification remains required before changing systems or giving consequential instructions.

Do not upload passwords, tokens, private user data, proprietary logs, confidential screenshots, security incidents, or other protected information to an unapproved AI service. AI-generated remediation must not bypass change, approval, security, or escalation controls.

NIST AI RMF and the Generative AI Profile provide voluntary risk-management references.

Sources:
- https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
- https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence

## Research gate decision

The current evidence is occupation-specific, recent enough for the 2026 revision cycle, and internally consistent. The declining U.S. headline outlook does not block the guide; it strengthens the need for an honest progression-oriented treatment of help-desk work.

**Research stage:** **PASS**.  
**Blockers:** none.
