## United States — official occupation and wage evidence

### O*NET OnLine — U.S. Department of Labor
Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/17-3011.00

Controlled facts:
- Current occupation: **17-3011.00 — Architectural and Civil Drafters**; O*NET page updated in 2026.
- O*NET states that workers prepare detailed drawings of architectural and structural features of buildings and drawings/topographical relief maps used in civil engineering projects such as highways, bridges, and public works.
- Reported titles include Architectural Drafter, Civil Drafter, CAD Designer, CADD Drafter, Drafting Technician, Draftsman, and Draftsperson.
- Current O*NET tasks include producing drawings with computer-assisted drafting systems, drafting plans from sketches/notes, coordinating structural/electrical/mechanical representations, analyzing building codes/by-laws and technical documents for design effects, and drawing maps, profiles, cross-sections, elevations, and topographical contours.

### O*NET / BLS 2025 wage control
Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/17-3011.00

Controlled facts:
- O*NET identifies the current wage source as **Bureau of Labor Statistics 2025 wage data**.
- U.S. annual distribution: **$46,260 10th percentile; $55,650 25th percentile; $66,150 median; $80,870 75th percentile; $99,710 90th percentile**.
- Hourly distribution: **$22.24; $26.76; $31.80 median; $38.88; $47.94** at the same percentiles.
- O*NET reports about **110,500 employees in 2024**, projected growth of **3% to 4% from 2024 to 2034**, and about **10,000 projected openings** over the displayed projection context.

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Occupational Outlook Handbook
Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/drafters.htm

Controlled facts:
- BLS describes drafters as using software to convert engineers’ and architects’ designs into technical drawings.
- Typical postsecondary preparation often comes through community college or technical-school programs leading to an associate degree, certificate, or diploma.
- BLS reports a **May 2024 median annual wage of $65,380** for drafters overall.
- Overall drafter employment is projected to show **little or no change from 2024 to 2034**, with about **16,200 openings per year** on average across drafter specialties, largely from replacement needs.
- This broad OOH category is not identical to O*NET 17-3011.00, so the controlled guide should use the newer occupation-specific 2025 O*NET/BLS wage values for the main U.S. wage table and use the OOH for broader preparation/work-environment context.

## Canada — official occupation and wage evidence

### Job Bank — Drafting technologists and technicians
Sources:
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/occupation/3437/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/3437/ca

Controlled facts:
- Canadian classification: **NOC 22212 — Drafting technologists and technicians**.
- Job Bank states that workers prepare engineering designs, drawings, related technical information, and building information models (BIM), working in multidisciplinary teams or supporting engineers, architects, or industrial designers; some may work independently within the applicable scope.
- Employers include consulting/construction companies, utilities, resource and manufacturing companies, governments, and other establishments.
- Job Bank’s current national summary states that the occupation usually requires a **college diploma, apprenticeship training of two or more years, or relevant supervisory experience**, depending on role and jurisdiction.
- Canada-wide wages, updated **November 19, 2025**: **C$21.50 low, C$31.79 median, C$48.38 high per hour**; national reference period 2023–2024, Statistics Canada Labour Force Survey.
- Prospects vary by province and territory.

## Colombia — official occupation and training pathways

### OCUPACOL — Ministerio del Trabajo
Source: https://ocupacol.mintrabajo.gov.co/Profile/OccupationalProfile/31181

Controlled facts:
- Colombian classification: **CUOC 31181 — Delineantes y dibujantes técnicos**, competence level 3.
- Listed titles include **Delineante de arquitectura, Delineante de ingeniería civil, Delineante de obra civil, Delineante de CAD, Dibujante de arquitectura, Dibujante de AutoCAD, Dibujante de ingeniería, Dibujante técnico, Técnico de dibujo arquitectónico, Técnico de dibujo computarizado**, and related titles.
- Functions include preparing and reviewing working drawings from sketches/specifications/engineering calculations, operating computer-aided design/drafting systems, converting printed plans/maps to digital form, and preparing detailed technical diagrams.
- OCUPACOL displays historic labour-market indicators and a broad salary range, but the controlled guide should not treat those displayed values as a current representative wage benchmark because the page’s indicators are older and occupation-level methodology can be limited.

### SENA Betowa — Diseño y planos en AutoCAD 2D
Source: https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/diseno-y-planos-en-autocad-2d?modality=V&programId=153415

Controlled facts:

- The program develops the competency to model construction plans according to specifications and digital techniques.
- The listing requires basic geometry and computer-tool knowledge and remains subject to current cohort availability/admission rules.
- Product-specific training can be useful but is not a universal legal credential or substitute for broader drafting/BIM/document-control skills.

## Professional boundaries, safety, cybersecurity, accessibility, and responsible AI

### Professional-scope control
- Architectural/civil drafting can support engineers, architects, surveyors, contractors, and public agencies, but a drafting title does not by itself confer authority to practice a licensed profession, sign/seal regulated plans, approve structural or life-safety decisions, certify code compliance, or make surveying/engineering/architectural determinations reserved by law or employer policy.
- The guide should tell readers to follow the responsible licensed professional, project authority, applicable law, employer procedures, and document-control system.

### Cybersecurity and document control
Source: https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world

Controlled use:
- Apply phishing awareness, strong unique passwords/password managers, multifactor authentication, and software-update practices through employer-approved systems.
- Drawings, models, geospatial/site information, client data, credentials, bids, contracts, and infrastructure details can be sensitive. Workers should use approved repositories, permissions, revision controls, naming/versioning rules, and escalation procedures.
- Do not transmit controlled project files through personal email/cloud accounts or approve unexpected payment/account changes without verification.

### Construction/site safety
Source: https://www.osha.gov/construction

Controlled use:
- Office-based drafting may include occasional field/site visits. Site entry, PPE, fall/electrical/traffic/heavy-equipment controls, hazard communication, and emergency procedures are employer/site specific.
- A drafter should not perform field measurements or enter restricted/hazardous areas beyond training, authorization, and site rules.

### Responsible AI
Sources:
- https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
- https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence

Controlled use:
- NIST’s AI RMF is voluntary guidance for managing AI risks; its Generative AI Profile addresses risks specific to generative systems.
- AI may assist with low-risk drafting support such as first-pass checklists, note organization, non-sensitive text, or learning exercises when employer policy permits.
- Human verification against authoritative drawings, models, calculations, specifications, codes, survey data, contracts, and licensed-professional direction is required for consequential work.
- Do not upload confidential plans, critical-infrastructure details, credentials, client data, proprietary models, unpublished specifications, or regulated information into unapproved AI tools.

## Editorial controls for the English revision
The revised master should:

1. anchor U.S. scope to O*NET-SOC 17-3011.00, Canada to NOC 22212, and Colombia to CUOC 31181 without pretending the classifications are perfectly identical;
2. distinguish drafting/BIM/documentation support from licensed architecture, engineering, surveying, inspection, code-enforcement, and approval authority;
3. use current 2025 occupation-specific U.S. wage data and clearly label broad BLS and non-government comparisons;
4. include current Canadian national wages and Colombia training pathways without promising employment, funding, seats, or recognition;
5. explain CAD/CADD/BIM, layers, dimensions, annotations, model/drawing coordination, revisions, issue sets, transmittals, file naming, version control, and markups in tool-neutral terms;
6. include architectural plans, civil/site drawings, profiles, sections, utilities, grading, roads, drainage, and public-works examples only within drafting-support scope;
7. include free/low-cost and funded-training locators for the U.S., Canada, Colombia, and broader Latin America;
8. present product-specific software as employer/tool context rather than universal credentials;
9. cover safe site visits and measurement boundaries without encouraging untrained field activity;
10. cover cybersecurity, confidential project information, access control, backups/versioning, and approved repositories;
11. permit responsible AI only for low-risk assistance with human verification and privacy controls;
12. repair the legacy `Action Plan 1 To 6: False` defect with a concrete, accessible action plan and checkable milestones;
13. maintain plain-language, dysgraphia-friendly structure, descriptive headings, short steps, and reusable checklists;
14. avoid any claim of independent human certification, accreditation, legal review, licensed-professional review, accessibility certification, guaranteed income, or guaranteed job outcome.
