## U.S. apprenticeship boundary

### BLS pathway statement

BLS identifies apprenticeship as the typical on-the-job training pathway for electricians.

### Apprenticeship.gov occupation finder

Source: https://www.apprenticeship.gov/apprenticeship-occupations/listings?occupationCode=47-2111.00

The live occupation-finder page for O*NET 47-2111.00 currently states that the occupation is **not currently approved for use in a Registered Apprenticeship Program** and provides an occupation-determination request route.

Controlled interpretation:

- It is accurate to say apprenticeships are a common/typical pathway in the electrician trade because BLS says so.
- The guide must **not** make a blanket current federal claim that O*NET 47-2111.00 itself is presently approved in Apprenticeship.gov as a Registered Apprenticeship occupation.
- A user considering a specific Registered Apprenticeship must verify the current sponsor, registered occupation, registration authority, wage progression, related instruction, duration, and local eligibility directly.

This distinction is mandatory in the v2 guide.

## U.S. electrical safety boundary

### OSHA electrical standards

Sources:

- https://www.osha.gov/electrical/standards
- https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910SubpartS
- https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.333
- https://www.osha.gov/electrical-contractors/standards

Controlled findings:

- OSHA Subpart S addresses electrical safety in general industry.
- Sections 1910.331–1910.335 address safety-related work practices.
- OSHA 1910.333 states that live parts to which an employee may be exposed generally must be deenergized before work on or near them unless the employer can demonstrate specified exceptions.
- Electrical-contractor guidance also points to 1910.147 lockout/tagout, electrical protective equipment, wiring/design standards, and other context-specific rules.

Guide rule: the manual must not teach readers to bypass deenergization, lockout/tagout, qualified-person requirements, PPE, employer procedures, permits, or local code/licensing rules. Training examples must use deenergized, simulated, or instructor-controlled conditions unless a qualified program explicitly authorizes otherwise.

### Red Seal — Construction Electrician

Sources:

- https://red-seal.ca/eng/trades/const-elect.shtml
- https://red-seal.ca/eng/trades/constelectric/previous/harmonization.shtml

Controlled findings:

- Construction Electrician is a designated Red Seal trade associated with NOC 72200.
- Red Seal describes work across residential, commercial, institutional, industrial, agricultural, transportation, and other environments.
- Provincial and territorial apprenticeship authorities remain responsible for their own apprenticeship programs; implementation and certification requirements vary by jurisdiction.
- The harmonization material recommends four technical-training levels; jurisdictional implementation may vary.

Guide rule: Red Seal is not to be described as a single universal license to work anywhere without checking provincial/territorial requirements.

### CONTE — professional matriculation boundary

Sources:

- https://www.conte.org.co/
- https://www.conte.org.co/nosotros/

CONTE states that it performs public functions including studying, processing, and issuing professional registrations (Matrículas Profesionales) for Técnicos Electricistas and maintaining the list of persons enabled for professional practice. Its institutional description links this authority to Colombian law and the legal exercise of the profession.

Guide rule: SENA training or employer experience must **not** be represented as automatically conferring the Colombian professional registration or legal scope required for technical-electrician practice. Readers must verify current CONTE category, eligibility, documentation, scope, fees, and any other applicable rules before paid practice.

## Controlled occupational-title distinction

“Electrician” and “electrical technician” can refer to materially different work depending on employer and jurisdiction. The v2 guide should center the BLS Electricians occupation while explaining that titles such as electrical technician, controls technician, maintenance electrician, construction electrician, and electrical/electronics repair technician may map to different occupational codes, licensing regimes, training pathways, and duties.

Do not merge adjacent electronics-engineering or power-system occupations into 47-2111 wage/outlook claims.

## AI and digital-tool boundary

AI may support safe learning tasks using public or fictitious information, such as vocabulary, code-navigation study, interview practice, calculation-checking concepts, or study-plan generation. It must not be used as sole authority for energized-work procedures, code compliance, conductor sizing, protective-device selection, arc-flash decisions, permit decisions, inspection conclusions, or legal/licensing determinations.

Any AI-generated electrical calculation, code citation, wiring recommendation, troubleshooting sequence, or safety instruction must be verified against current authoritative sources and qualified supervision before real-world use.
