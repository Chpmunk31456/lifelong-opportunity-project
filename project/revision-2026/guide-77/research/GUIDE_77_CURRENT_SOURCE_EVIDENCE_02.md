# Guide 77 — Current Source Evidence

## Occupation
Graphic Designer and Production Artist

## Research date
2026-08-21

## Controlled purpose
This evidence pack supports the controlled English revision of Guide 77. It separates official labour-market evidence from non-government market estimates and does not guarantee employment, income, admission, funding, certification, apprenticeship placement, freelance work, or client outcomes.

## United States — official occupation and wage evidence

### O*NET OnLine — U.S. Department of Labor
Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/27-1024.00

Controlled facts:
- O*NET-SOC: **27-1024.00 — Graphic Designers**, updated in 2026.
- O*NET explicitly lists **Production Artist** among reported job titles, supporting this guide’s combined title.
- The occupation designs or creates graphics for commercial or promotional needs, including packaging, displays, logos, publications, and digital materials.
- Current O*NET/BLS wage data show **2025 median pay of $30.27/hour and $62,960/year**.
- National 2025 wage distribution: **$39,520 at the 10th percentile, $62,960 median, $104,910 at the 90th percentile**.
- Employment: about **265,900 workers in 2024**.
- Projected growth: **1% to 2% from 2024 to 2034**, slower than average.
- Projected openings: about **20,000** over the projection period from growth and replacement needs.

### O*NET national wage page
Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/27-1024.00

Controlled use:
- Wage values are sourced from **BLS 2025 wage data**.
- The guide should use these official national values as the principal U.S. compensation benchmark and make clear that pay varies by geography, experience, industry, portfolio strength, specialization, employment arrangement, and scope.

## Canada — official occupation and wage evidence

### Job Bank — Graphic designers and illustrators
Sources:
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/5688/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/wagereport/occupation/5747

Controlled facts:
- Canadian classification: **NOC 52120 — Graphic designers and illustrators**.
- Job Bank describes work that conceptualizes and produces graphic art and visual materials for publications, advertising, films, packaging, posters, signs, and interactive media.
- Canada-wide wages updated **November 19, 2025**: **C$20.00/hour low, C$31.25/hour median, C$52.88/hour high**.
- The national wage reference period is 2023-2024 and the source is Statistics Canada’s Labour Force Survey.
- Job Bank notes that requirements vary and may include college-level training, apprenticeship-equivalent preparation, or experience depending on the role and province.

### Government of Canada — training and funding gateway
Sources:
- https://www.canada.ca/en/services/jobs/training.html
- https://www.canada.ca/en/services/jobs.html

Controlled use:
- Canada.ca provides information on student aid, grants and loans, short-term paid or free training, career exploration, work experience, youth/student programs, and provincial/territorial training supports.
- Eligibility, provider recognition, cost, aid, and program availability vary; the guide must not promise funding or admission.
- Graphic-design learners should verify the exact provider, credential, portfolio expectations, software requirements, and provincial labour-market conditions before paying for training.

Sources:

- https://ocupacol.mintrabajo.gov.co/Profile/OccupationalProfile/21662

Controlled facts:

## Current non-government market estimates — clearly labelled

These values are current market estimates, not government wage statistics and not guaranteed compensation.

### United States — Indeed
Source: https://www.indeed.com/career/graphic-designer/salaries

- Indeed reported an average base salary of **$23.41/hour** for U.S. Graphic Designers.
- The page showed a range of roughly **$13.25 to $41.36/hour**.
- The estimate was based on about **6.1k salaries from job postings over the prior 36 months** and was updated **August 2, 2026**.
- The guide must label this as a market estimate and keep it separate from BLS/O*NET official wage data.

### Colombia — Indeed junior market estimate
Source: https://co.indeed.com/career/dise%C3%B1ador-gr%C3%A1fico-junior/salaries

- Indeed reported an average of approximately **COP 1,902,446/month** for Diseñador/a gráfico/a junior in Colombia.
- The estimate used **26 reported salaries** and was updated **July 29, 2026**.
- This is a junior-title estimate, not a representative benchmark for all graphic designers or production artists. City, employer, portfolio, specialty, software skills, English ability, employment type, and seniority can materially change compensation.

## Copyright, accessibility, cybersecurity, and responsible-AI controls

### U.S. Copyright Office — visual and graphic artists
Sources:
- https://www.copyright.gov/engage/visual-artists/
- https://www.copyright.gov/registration/visual-arts/index.html

Controlled use:
- The Copyright Office states that original pictorial, graphic, and sculptural works can be protected by copyright once fixed in a tangible medium.
- Graphic artists must distinguish work they created or are licensed to use from third-party photographs, illustrations, fonts, stock assets, templates, trademarks, and client-owned material.
- The guide should teach permission/licensing checks, asset provenance, contract scope, and escalation to qualified legal or rights-management personnel for consequential disputes; it must not provide legal advice.

### W3C — WCAG 2.2 contrast and images of text
Sources:
- https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/
- https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast

Controlled use:
- WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 1.4.3 specifies at least **4.5:1 contrast for normal text** and **3:1 for large text**, subject to stated exceptions.
- SC 1.4.11 requires **3:1** contrast for meaningful graphical objects and user-interface components against adjacent colors, subject to exceptions.
- WCAG also favors real text over images of text when the same visual presentation can be achieved through text.
- The guide should teach accessible-design checks and handoff practices but must not claim that a learner or portfolio is automatically WCAG-conformant or accessibility-certified.

### CISA — account and file security
Source: https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world

Controlled use:
- CISA emphasizes phishing awareness, strong passwords/password managers, MFA, and software updates.
- Graphic-design workflows often involve shared cloud files, client assets, proofs, credentials, public links, vendor transfers, and large attachments. Learners should use approved systems, verify unusual requests, restrict access, protect credentials, and report suspected compromise.

### NIST — AI Risk Management Framework and Generative AI Profile
Sources:
- https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
- https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence

Controlled use:
- NIST’s AI RMF is a voluntary risk-management framework; NIST’s Generative AI Profile addresses risks specific to generative AI.
- For design work, generative AI may assist with ideation, rough concepts, layout alternatives, first-pass copy, or non-sensitive practice assets where employer/client policy permits.
- Human review is required for factual claims, brand compliance, accessibility, client approvals, copyright/licensing, confidential information, identity/likeness concerns, and final production output.
- The guide should require provenance records where feasible and prohibit uploading confidential client assets, credentials, private contracts, personal data, or unpublished business information into unapproved AI services.

## Editorial controls for the English revision
The revised English master should:

1. distinguish graphic designer, production artist, prepress/production support, digital designer, and multimedia-adjacent responsibilities without pretending the titles are identical;
2. anchor the U.S. benchmark to O*NET-SOC 27-1024.00, Canada to NOC 52120, and Colombia to the relevant OCUPACOL designer/production-support profiles;
3. use official 2025 U.S. wages and current Canada Job Bank wage data as official benchmarks, keeping non-government estimates separately labelled;
4. explain that portfolios and demonstrated production quality can be highly important, while education requirements vary by employer and market;
5. cover layout, typography, image editing, color, file formats, vector/raster concepts, print/digital production, proofing, export specifications, version control, accessibility, and client feedback;
6. include CareerOneStop/WIOA, Canada training/aid gateways, SENA Betowa, and OIT/Cinterfor as locators without promising eligibility or funding;
7. include internships, junior/assistant roles, supervised production work, employer training, freelance practice with clear boundaries, and portfolio projects as realistic work-based-learning routes;
8. repair the legacy `action_plan_ok: False` defect with a concrete, usable action plan;
9. cover copyright/licensing, fonts, stock assets, trademarks, releases, privacy, client confidentiality, and approval records without offering legal advice;
10. teach WCAG-informed contrast, real-text, alt-text/handoff awareness, accessible documents/digital assets, and design-for-low-vision practices without claiming certification;
11. cover phishing, MFA, secure file-sharing, account access, suspicious transfer or asset requests, and employer/client-approved systems;
12. permit responsible AI only with policy approval, provenance awareness, human verification, and protection of confidential information;
13. state clearly that AI-generated or AI-assisted assets can raise copyright, licensing, disclosure, brand, factual, and client-approval issues that require source and policy verification;
14. maintain plain-language, dysgraphia-friendly structure, short steps, checklists, descriptive headings, and human-readable examples.
