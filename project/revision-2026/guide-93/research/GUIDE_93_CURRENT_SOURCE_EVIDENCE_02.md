## Research conclusion
Surveying and Mapping Technician has a direct U.S. occupational benchmark and strong international technical comparisons. The 2026 reconstruction can proceed without a research blocker, provided it preserves the boundary between technician work and legally/professionally controlled surveyor functions.

- **United States primary benchmark:** O*NET-SOC **17-3031.00 — Surveying and Mapping Technicians**.
- **Canada primary comparison:** NOC **22213 — Land survey technologists and technicians**.
- **Colombia primary comparison:** CUOC **31122 — Técnicos en topografía**.
- **Brazil comparison:** CBO family **3123 — Técnicos em geomática**, including **3123-05 Técnico em Agrimensura**, **3123-10 Técnico em Geodésia e Cartografia**, and **3123-20 Topógrafo**.
- **Chile progression:** ChileValora currently lists both **Ayudante de Topografía** and **Topógrafo(a)** as active occupational profiles through **2028-12-30**.
- **Peru training comparison:** SENCICO offers the professional technical program **Topografía y Geodesia**, with a published duration of **3 years**.

## United States — occupation and duties
O*NET 17-3031.00 is a **Bright Outlook** occupation updated in 2026. It describes surveying and mapping duties, generally under the direction of an engineer, surveyor, cartographer or photogrammetrist, to obtain data for construction, mapmaking, boundary location, mining and related purposes.

Current O*NET task evidence supports a guide covering:
- field survey-data collection;
- measurement of angles, distances and elevations;
- total-station/GNSS/GPS-assisted work where assigned;
- mathematical survey calculations;
- construction staking support;
- map production and updating;
- map-layer accuracy checking;
- geographic/topographic database work;
- aerial/satellite/photogrammetric source use;
- coordinate calculation and documentation;
- quality/completeness review;
- controlled use of digital survey and mapping systems.

O*NET also lists approved Registered Apprenticeship titles associated with the occupation, including **Chief of Party**, **Geodetic Computator**, **Photogrammetric Technician**, and **Surveyor Assistant Instrument**. These approved titles do not guarantee a live apprenticeship opening in a particular location.

## U.S. education and training context
BLS states that surveying and mapping technicians typically need a **high school diploma or equivalent**; some employers prefer additional education or GIS/technology experience. BLS lists **moderate-term on-the-job training** for the occupation.

O*NET places the occupation in **Job Zone Three — Medium Preparation Needed**, where vocational-school training, related experience, an associate degree, and one to two years of on-the-job/informal training may be common depending on the role.

These are occupation-level patterns, not universal hiring requirements for every vacancy.

## U.S. official wages and outlook
BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook reports for Surveying and Mapping Technicians:
- median annual wage, May 2024: **$51,940**;
- median hourly equivalent: **$24.97**;
- lowest 10 percent: below **$36,910**;
- highest 10 percent: above **$80,870**;
- employment 2024: **59,400**;
- projected employment 2034: **62,100**;
- projected growth 2024–2034: **5%**;
- projected annual openings: about **7,600**.

BLS explains that continued demand for map information should support employment even as drones and other technologies improve efficiency, because technicians remain necessary to review and interpret outputs for accuracy and completeness.

## Technician versus licensed/professional surveyor boundary
BLS states that **licensing is not required for surveying and mapping technicians**, while experienced technicians with additional formal training may later qualify to become licensed surveyors.

Guide 93 must therefore distinguish:
- **technician work:** collecting measurements, operating equipment under assigned scope, calculations, drafting/mapping, data processing, staking support, QA and documentation;
- **licensed/authorized surveyor work:** jurisdiction-dependent legal boundary determination, certification, signing/sealing, authoritative cadastral/legal opinions and other reserved professional services.

The revised guide must not imply that GIS/CAD output, GNSS coordinates, drone imagery or technician experience alone creates legal authority to establish property boundaries.

## Drones / remotely piloted aircraft
BLS notes that surveying and mapping technicians may use drones to take photographs and gather information.

Where drone operations are part of work, aviation rules apply independently of surveying competence.

### United States
FAA states that operating a drone under the Small UAS Rule (**Part 107**) requires an FAA **Remote Pilot Certificate**. First-time applicants must meet eligibility requirements and pass the aeronautical-knowledge exam; certificate holders must maintain knowledge recency through recurrent training.

### Canada
Transport Canada requires drone pilots to hold the appropriate pilot certificate for the type of operation. Current categories include **Basic**, **Advanced**, and **Level 1 Complex** operations. The certificate category depends on what, how and where the pilot flies.

Guide 93 must not present possession of survey/mapping skills as a substitute for aviation authorization.

## Canada — NOC 22213
Job Bank maps land survey technologists and technicians to **NOC 22213** and describes work determining the exact locations and relative positions of natural features and structures on, under and above the earth's surface.

The national summary, modified **2026-08-07**, states that the occupation usually requires a **college diploma**, **apprenticeship training of 2 or more years**, or experience working in a supervisory occupation.

Current national wages, updated **2025-11-19**:
- low **C$22.00/hour**;
- median **C$29.75/hour**;
- high **C$46.00/hour**.

The wage reference period is 2023–2024 and provincial/territorial prospects vary. The guide must not imply that the national median means equally strong demand in every province or territory.

## Colombia — CUOC 31122
OCUPACOL **CUOC 31122 — Técnicos en topografía**, competence level 3, is a direct comparison.

Current listed functions include:
- technical work on civil-engineering and topographic surveys;
- operating instruments for distance, altimetry, angles, elevations and terrain/subsurface contours;
- digital terrain model work;
- field visits and recording measurements;
- latitude/longitude, angle and trigonometric calculations;
- georeferencing;
- graphical representations, plans, charts and map digitization;
- processing topographic, cadastral and geodetic survey data;
- locating road, urban and hydraulic works from plans/specifications;
- coordinating topographic field activities;
- compiling records associated with property-boundary measurement, surveying and multipurpose cadastre.

Occupational titles include Técnico de topografía, Técnico topográfico, Técnico agrimensor, Técnico de soporte en geomática, Tecnólogo en topografía and Topógrafo, among others.

### Colombia wage caution
OCUPACOL displays a historical range of **COP $350,000 to $2,910,053**, but explicitly warns that the underlying data **do not have statistical representativeness** under the applied methodology. Guide 93 must preserve that warning and must not present the displayed range as a reliable national salary benchmark.

## Brazil — CBO / technical pathway comparison
Brazil's official CBO list includes family **3123 — Técnicos em geomática**. Official/officially reproduced CBO mappings include:
- **3123-05 — Técnico em Agrimensura**;
- **3123-10 — Técnico em Geodésia e Cartografia**;
- **3123-20 — Topógrafo**.

The associated activities include geodetic/topohydrographic surveys, field implementation of project points, analysis of cartographic information and production of cartographic documents.

Brazilian technical-course catalog material for Técnico em Agrimensura identifies work environments such as mapping/topographic-survey firms, aerolevantamento, public-service concessionaires and government/regulatory organizations, supported by laboratories for spatial-data collection, geoprocessing, technical drawing, topography and photogrammetry.

Guide 93 must distinguish occupational classification and technical education from any profession-specific registration or reserved-practice requirements that may apply to particular services or jurisdictions.

## Field safety
Surveying fieldwork can involve heat, terrain, traffic, construction sites, excavation, water, remote areas, equipment transport and other hazards.

OSHA heat-prevention guidance supports:
- sufficient cool water/fluids;
- rest breaks that increase with heat stress;
- shade or other cool recovery areas;
- supervisor/worker training;
- emergency procedures;
- extra protection for new/unacclimatized workers;
- gradual acclimatization.

Guide 93 should also teach readers to follow site-specific traffic control, excavation, electrical/utility, fall, PPE, lone-worker, wildlife/weather and equipment safety requirements where applicable. It must not substitute general educational guidance for an employer's hazard assessment or jurisdiction-specific safety rules.

## Responsible AI
NIST's AI Risk Management Framework and Generative AI Profile support a risk-managed approach to AI use.

Guide 93 may show policy-approved AI assisting with:
- explaining survey calculations;
- drafting field-note templates;
- documenting workflows;
- checking code/scripting concepts;
- summarizing non-sensitive specifications;
- generating synthetic practice data.

Controls are mandatory:
- do not upload protected coordinates, client/site data, legal-boundary records, restricted infrastructure information, credentials or proprietary plans to unapproved tools;
- validate calculations, units, coordinate reference systems and generated code;
- verify invented functions, equipment procedures and legal claims;
- preserve source/measurement lineage;
- do not allow AI to invent authoritative coordinates, monuments, control points, survey-grade precision or legal boundaries;
- human/domain validation remains required.

AI output is not evidence that a survey, measurement, calibration or legal-boundary determination was performed correctly.

## Accessibility and inclusion
The revised guide should include practical accommodations and alternative learning/work methods where compatible with essential duties, including:
- larger text/high-contrast digital plans;
- keyboard-accessible software workflows where supported;
- checklists and structured field-note templates;
- visual plus audible/haptic cues where equipment supports them;
- explicit task sequencing;
- safe team-based allocation of field duties;
- accessible text alternatives for diagrams and maps used in learning materials.

Accessibility guidance must not imply that essential safety, mobility, vision, hearing or licensing requirements can always be waived; accommodations depend on the actual job, equipment, employer and jurisdiction.
