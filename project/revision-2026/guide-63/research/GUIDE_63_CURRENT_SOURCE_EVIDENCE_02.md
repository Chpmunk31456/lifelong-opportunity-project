## Research purpose

This evidence package refreshes the occupational facts needed to reconstruct the English Version 2 master. It distinguishes assistant/clerical work from technician work, preserves jurisdiction-specific classifications, and avoids implying professional librarian authority, universal licensing, guaranteed employment, or blanket copyright/privacy permissions.

## United States

### Classification, duties, education, pay, and outlook

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics groups library technicians and assistants in one Occupational Outlook Handbook profile while preserving two separate SOC occupations:

- **Library Technicians — SOC 25-4031**
- **Library Assistants, Clerical — SOC 43-4121**

BLS states that technicians and assistants help librarians with library operations. Typical settings include public, school, university, corporate, and specialty libraries.

Education expectations are different by role:

- Library technicians typically need a **postsecondary certificate / nondegree award**.
- Library assistants typically need a **high school diploma or equivalent**, together with short-term on-the-job training.

BLS May 2024 median hourly wages:

- Library technicians: **$19.22/hour**
- Library assistants, clerical: **$17.31/hour**
- Combined profile median: **$18.05/hour** / **$37,540 per year**

Employment is projected to decline **7% from 2024 to 2034** for the combined group. BLS nevertheless projects about **25,800 openings per year**, on average, over the decade, attributed to replacement needs rather than net occupational growth.

Official source:  
https://www.bls.gov/ooh/education-training-and-library/library-technicians-and-assistants.htm

BLS occupational projections also identify library technicians as SOC 25-4031 and a postsecondary nondegree-award occupation.

Official source:  
https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm

### Copyright boundary

The U.S. Copyright Office explains that Section 108 provides specific copyright exceptions for qualifying libraries and archives under defined conditions. The guide must not convert this into a blanket permission to copy, digitize, distribute, or share copyrighted materials. Staff should follow institutional policy and escalate uncertain copyright questions.

Official source:  
https://www.copyright.gov/docs/section108/

## Canada

Canada separates technician work from assistant/clerk work.

### Library and public archive technicians — NOC 52100

Government of Canada Job Bank identifies **Library and public archive technicians — NOC 52100**. Typical work includes assisting users in accessing resources, describing new acquisitions, participating in archive processing and storage, and conducting reference searches.

Typical employment requirement for library technicians is completion of a **two- to three-year college program in library and information technology**. Job Bank states that, according to its records, the occupation is **not regulated in Canada**.

The national median wage shown for library technicians is **C$28.00/hour**. Prospects vary by province or territory.

Official sources:  
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/8072/ca  
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/8072/ca

### Library assistants and clerks — NOC 14300

Government of Canada Job Bank identifies **Library assistants and clerks — NOC 14300**. Typical work includes issuing and receiving materials, sorting and shelving books, providing general library information, and performing clerical functions.

Completion of **secondary school is usually required**. Job Bank states that, according to its records, this occupation is **not regulated in Canada**.

The national median wage shown for library assistants is **C$23.17/hour**, with published national range figures of **C$15.80 low / C$23.17 median / C$34.00 high**. Prospects vary by province or territory.

Official sources:  
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/24861/ca  
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/24861/ca

## Colombia

### Occupational classification and duties

The SENA Observatorio Laboral y Ocupacional Colombiano lists **Auxiliares de biblioteca — CNO 1351**.

The official functions include:

- orienting users in access to books, periodicals, audiovisual materials, maps, documents, and other information resources;
- supporting cataloguing of new books and information resources;
- lending, receiving, sorting, filing, and organizing materials;
- conducting manual or computerized reference searches;
- maintaining acquisition, loan, and return records;
- supporting events, communications, programs, inventories, reports, and bibliographic-material handling.

This classification supports an assistant-level pathway. It must not be presented as equivalent to a professional bibliotecólogo credential or as universal authority to perform professional librarian functions.

Official sources:  
https://observatorio.sena.edu.co/Clasificacion/CnoDetalle?carac=&tags=1351  
https://observatorio.sena.edu.co/Clasificacion/CnoDetalleFunciones?tags=1351

### Colombian library context and training

The Ministry of Education's National Plan for Reading, Writing, Orality and Libraries (PNLEO) includes strengthening school libraries and training for teachers, school librarians, and mediators. This supports a public-policy pathway for skills development but does not establish one universal hiring credential for all library-assistant or technician jobs.

Official source:  
https://www.mineducacion.gov.co/portal/micrositios-preescolar-basica-y-media/Plan-Nacional-de-Lectura-Escritura-Oralidad-y-Bibliotecas-PNLEO-/

The Biblioteca Nacional de Colombia's training materials identify library-sector education routes and emphasize continuing formation for library workers. Any specific current course or enrollment claim must be verified at the provider before the guide tells a reader to spend money or enroll.

Official source:

## Controlled role boundaries for the English master

The Version 2 guide must preserve these distinctions:

1. **Assistant/clerk is not the same occupation as technician** in U.S. and Canadian classification systems.
2. **Technician is not synonymous with professional librarian.** Duties vary by employer, collection, system, and jurisdiction.
3. Do not claim universal licensing, certification, or degree requirements where official sources do not establish them.
4. Copyright exceptions are conditional; the guide must not provide individualized legal advice or blanket reproduction permission.
5. Patron records, account information, borrowing history, identity data, credentials, and internal systems should be handled according to applicable law and employer policy; the guide must not encourage unnecessary collection, disclosure, or retention of personal data.
6. Accessibility should be treated as a service and workplace design obligation, not as authority for an assistant or technician to independently certify legal compliance.
7. AI tools may assist with drafting, discovery, metadata suggestions, translation support, or routine administrative work only within employer policy. Do not place confidential patron data, licensed content, credentials, or unpublished/internal records into unapproved AI systems.
8. Cybersecurity boundaries include protecting library-system accounts, using approved authentication, recognizing phishing/social-engineering risk, and escalating suspected compromise rather than attempting out-of-scope forensic or administrative actions.
9. Physical duties such as shelving, moving boxes, ladders/step stools, equipment handling, or collection maintenance must remain non-procedural and subject to employer safety rules and training.
10. Wages, openings, and outlook figures are descriptive labor-market data, not earnings or employment guarantees.
