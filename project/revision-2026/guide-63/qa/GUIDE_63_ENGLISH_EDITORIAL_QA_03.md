## Editorial controls checked

### 1. Occupation identity and role separation — PASS

The master preserves the distinction between assistant/clerical and technician work rather than collapsing them into one credential or scope.

Controlled classifications retained:

- United States: **SOC 25-4031 — Library Technicians**
- United States: **SOC 43-4121 — Library Assistants, Clerical**
- Canada: **NOC 52100 — Library and public archive technicians**
- Canada: **NOC 14300 — Library assistants and clerks**
- Colombia: **CNO 1351 — Auxiliares de biblioteca**

The text explicitly avoids equating either support occupation with professional librarian authority.

### 2. Education and credential language — PASS

The master preserves current official education distinctions:

- U.S. technicians: postsecondary certificate / nondegree award pathway.
- U.S. assistants: high school diploma or equivalent plus short-term on-the-job training.
- Canada technicians: two- to three-year college program in library and information technology.
- Canada assistants: secondary school usually required.

No universal license, certification, or professional-librarian credential is invented.

### 3. Wage and outlook controls — PASS

The master preserves the controlled figures from the research evidence:

- U.S. library technicians: **$19.22/hour** median, May 2024.
- U.S. library assistants, clerical: **$17.31/hour** median, May 2024.
- U.S. combined profile: **$18.05/hour / $37,540 per year**.
- U.S. combined outlook: **-7% from 2024 to 2034** with approximately **25,800 openings per year** on average from replacement needs.
- Canada NOC 52100: **C$28.00/hour** national median.
- Canada NOC 14300: **C$15.80 low / C$23.17 median / C$34.00 high**.

The guide labels these as descriptive labor-market data and does not promise earnings or employment.

### 4. Colombia scope — PASS

The master uses **CNO 1351 — Auxiliares de biblioteca** as an assistant-level occupational classification and does not present it as equivalent to a professional `bibliotecólogo` credential.

It does not fabricate a directly comparable official national wage series where the research did not establish one.

### 5. Copyright boundary — PASS

The master correctly treats U.S. Copyright Act Section 108 as a conditional exception for qualifying libraries and archives rather than blanket authority to copy, digitize, reproduce, distribute, or publish protected works.

Complex rights questions are escalated to authorized institutional or professional contacts.

### 6. Privacy and records boundary — PASS

Patron identities, borrowing histories, account information, credentials, internal records, and other sensitive data are treated as controlled information. The master emphasizes minimum necessary access, approved systems, authorized disclosure, and incident escalation without inventing one universal privacy rule for all jurisdictions.

### 7. Accessibility boundary — PASS

Accessibility is presented as inclusive service and barrier reduction. The master does not grant assistants or technicians independent authority to certify legal accessibility compliance.

### 8. Cybersecurity boundary — PASS

The master covers approved credentials, MFA, phishing recognition, screen locking, approved software/storage, and prompt incident reporting. It explicitly avoids out-of-scope forensic, administrative, or security-control bypass work.

### 9. Responsible-AI boundary — PASS

AI is restricted to low-risk, policy-approved uses. The master prohibits placing confidential patron data, borrowing histories, credentials, internal records, licensed content, unpublished collections, or restricted archives into unapproved AI systems.

AI output is not treated as authoritative for copyright, privacy, accessibility certification, archival description, cataloguing, or factual reference work.

### 10. Physical-safety boundary — PASS

Shelving, carts, boxes, step stools/ladders, equipment, and storage-area work remain non-procedural and subject to employer training and safety rules.

### 11. Free-first and anti-scam controls — PASS

The master directs readers to public and employer-supported options before private spending and warns against guaranteed employment, wages, government funding, universal licenses, and automatic professional status.

### 12. Structure and readability — PASS

The English master is organized in plain-language sections covering role identity, permitted duties, boundaries, work environment, privacy, copyright, accessibility, skills, education, jurisdiction pathways, wages/outlook, free-first training, funding, work-based development, AI, cybersecurity, starter plan, advancement, scams, sources, and limitations.

The prose is designed for human readability and does not claim independent human certification, professional translation certification, accreditation, accessibility certification, or legal review.
