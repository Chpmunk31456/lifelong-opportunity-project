# Guide 71 — Current Source Evidence

## Occupation
Event Coordinator and Meeting Assistant

## Research date
2026-08-21

## Controlled purpose
This evidence pack supports the English revision of Guide 71. It distinguishes official labour-market evidence from current non-government market estimates and does not guarantee pay, employment, admission, funding, certification, licensing, or apprenticeship placement.

## United States — official occupation and wage evidence

### O*NET OnLine — U.S. Department of Labor
Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/13-1121.00

Controlled facts:
- O*NET-SOC: **13-1121.00 — Meeting, Convention, and Event Planners**; page updated in 2026 and designated Bright Outlook.
- The occupation coordinates staff, convention personnel, and clients to arrange meetings, events, and conventions.
- Reported job titles include **Event Coordinator**, Events Manager, Conference Planner, Conference Services Manager, and Special Events Coordinator.
- Core work includes determining client objectives; reviewing bills; coordinating accommodation, transportation, facilities, catering, signage, displays, special-needs requirements, printing, and event security; arranging audiovisual resources; inspecting facilities; maintaining financial records; and monitoring compliance and problem resolution.
- O*NET reports **2025 median wages of $29.41/hour and $61,160/year**.
- Employment: about **155,800 workers in 2024**.
- Projected growth: **5% to 6% from 2024 to 2034**.
- Projected openings: about **15,500 per year** over the projection period.

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Occupational Outlook Handbook
Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/meeting-convention-and-event-planners.htm

Controlled facts:
- BLS describes meeting, convention, and event planners as arranging all aspects of events and professional gatherings.
- Typical entry education is a bachelor’s degree, although experience related to event planning can be helpful and employer requirements vary.
- The OOH reports a **May 2024 median annual wage of $59,440** and **5% projected employment growth from 2024 to 2034**, with about **15,500 openings per year**.
- Work may include offices and event sites, travel, and schedules that extend beyond regular hours around events.

### Current BLS wage-year control
Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/13-1121.00

Controlled use:
- O*NET’s wage page identifies its current national wage values as sourced from **BLS 2025 wage data**.
- National 2025 wage distribution shown there: **$36,830 at the 10th percentile, $61,160 median, and $101,700 at the 90th percentile annually**.
- The guide should use the newer 2025 wage data for the principal U.S. wage table while preserving the OOH’s 2024-to-2034 projection context.

## Canada — official occupation, wages, and training supports

### Job Bank — Conference and event planners
Sources:
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/12546/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/12546/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/12546/ca

Controlled facts:
- Canadian classification: **NOC 12103 — Conference and event planners**.
- Work includes planning, organizing, and coordinating conferences, conventions, meetings, seminars, exhibitions, trade shows, festivals, and other events.
- Employers include tourism associations, trade/professional associations, convention and conference centres, governments, and event-planning companies; self-employment is also possible.
- Canada-wide wages, updated November 19, 2025: **C$17.65 low, C$28.37 median, C$47.53 high per hour**; national reference period 2023-2024, Statistics Canada Labour Force Survey.
- Job Bank’s detailed requirements state that a university degree or college diploma in business, tourism, or hospitality administration is usually required; several years of relevant hospitality/tourism/public-relations experience may substitute for formal education; event/meeting/conference certification may be required by some employers.
- Prospects vary by province and territory.

### Government of Canada — training and employment supports
Sources:
- https://www.canada.ca/en/services/jobs/training.html
- https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/programs/training-agreements.html
- https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/programs/training-agreements/lmda.html

Controlled use:
- Canada.ca provides a national training gateway for student aid, short-term paid or free courses from recognized providers, EI-and-training rules, career exploration, and other skills supports.
- Canada’s Labour Market Development Agreements and Workforce Development Agreements fund provincial/territorial training, upskilling, employment supports, career counselling, job-search help, work experience, and in some programs wage subsidies.
- Current federal material reports approximately **C$3 billion annually** through LMDAs and WDAs combined, with over **C$2.1 billion annually** through LMDAs.
- Eligibility and program design vary by province/territory and individual circumstances; the guide must not promise funding.

## Colombia — official occupation and training pathways

### OCUPACOL — Ministerio del Trabajo
Source: https://ocupacol.mintrabajo.gov.co/Profile/OccupationalProfile/33320

Controlled facts:
- Colombian classification: **CUOC 33320 — Coordinadores y productores de conferencias, eventos y espectáculos**; competence level 3.
- Listed occupational titles explicitly include **Coordinador de eventos**, Coordinador de conferencia, Administrador de eventos, Gestor de eventos, Organizador de conferencias y eventos, Planeador de eventos, Productor de eventos, and related roles.
- Functions include identifying client needs, coordinating facilities and event services, negotiating services within budget, monitoring budgets and administration, handling participant registration/program materials, supervising support staff, operating events under technical/regulatory requirements, and preparing financial records.
- Knowledge areas include administration, disaster-risk management, hospitality/catering, laws/government, ICT, mathematics, human resources, customer service, and office/administrative services.
- OCUPACOL displays labour-market indicators, but its page also states that the associated occupation-level data **do not have statistical representativeness under the applied methodology**. The controlled guide therefore must not present the displayed OCUPACOL salary range as a representative wage benchmark.

### SENA Betowa — event-organization learning
Sources:
- https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/estrategias-aplicadas-a-la-organizacion-de-eventos?modality=P&offertype=company&programId=89086
- https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/gestion-administrativa?programId=159

Controlled facts:
- **Estrategias aplicadas a la organización de eventos** is a SENA complementary special course with **40 hours** of training; its stated competency is organizing events according to the service portfolio and market study.
- Current Betowa listings show that cohorts, cities, dates, modality, seats, and admission conditions can change.
- **Gestión Administrativa** is a SENA **Tecnólogo** program listed at **24 months**; its competencies include organizing business-relations events along with ICT, documentation, customer service, information processing, and administrative work.
- SENA admissions remain subject to available capacity and the applicable selection procedure. The guide must tell readers to verify live Betowa availability rather than promise a course or seat.

## Latin America and the Caribbean — regional vocational-training locator

### ILO/Cinterfor
Sources:
- https://www.oitcinterfor.org/
- https://www.oitcinterfor.org/statsfp/paises

Controlled use:
- OIT/Cinterfor is the ILO’s specialized centre for vocational training and coordinates a network of vocational-training institutions across Latin America and the Caribbean and other participating countries.
- Its country/institution resources can help readers identify national vocational-training bodies and compare training systems.

## Current non-government market estimates — clearly labelled

These values are current market estimates, not government wage statistics and not guaranteed compensation.

- Indeed reported an average base salary of **$22.75/hour** for U.S. Event Coordinators, based on about **5.1k salaries from job postings over the prior 36 months**, updated **August 3, 2026**.

- These figures cover the market title “Event Coordinator,” which can include roles with narrower duties than the broader O*NET occupation.

## Accessibility, cybersecurity, safety, and responsible-AI evidence

### U.S. Department of Justice — ADA
Sources:
- https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/ada/
- https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/

Controlled use:
- U.S. convention centers, auditoriums, lecture halls, and other places of public gathering can be public accommodations under Title III; applicable entities have disability nondiscrimination and access obligations.
- DOJ web-accessibility guidance notes that public-facing services such as event registration can raise accessibility obligations and identifies aids such as captions, interpreters, notetakers, and assistive-listening devices in appropriate contexts.
- The guide should teach planners to ask about access needs early and coordinate with the venue/employer; it must not give jurisdiction-specific legal advice or claim accessibility certification.

### CISA — Secure Our World
Source: https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world

Controlled use:
- CISA recommends recognizing/reporting phishing, using strong passwords/password managers, enabling multifactor authentication, and keeping software updated.
- Event work commonly involves vendor, attendee, payment, registration, travel, schedule, and contact information. The guide should instruct learners to use employer-approved systems, minimize unnecessary exposure of personal data, verify unusual vendor/payment requests, and follow organizational security policy rather than inventing their own controls.

### OSHA — emergency planning
Sources:
- https://www.osha.gov/etools/evacuation-plans-procedures/eap/
- https://www.osha.gov/emergency-preparedness/getting-started

Controlled use:
- OSHA explains that emergency action plans organize employer/employee actions during workplace emergencies and identifies evacuation procedures, reporting methods, accountability, emergency roles, and training as core elements where the applicable standards require an EAP.
- The guide should teach an event coordinator to identify the venue’s emergency procedures, chain of command, exits, accessibility needs, incident contacts, and escalation authority—not to act beyond training or replace venue/emergency professionals.

### NIST — AI Risk Management Framework
Sources:
- https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
- https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence

Controlled use:
- NIST’s AI RMF is a voluntary framework for managing risks to individuals, organizations, and society from AI systems; NIST also provides a Generative AI Profile.
- For event work, generative AI can assist with brainstorming, draft agendas, checklists, routine communications, or first-pass summaries, but the controlled guide should require human verification for dates, prices, contracts, vendor terms, accessibility commitments, safety instructions, attendee information, travel/visa rules, and other consequential details.
- Sensitive attendee/client data, payment details, private contracts, credentials, or confidential business information should not be entered into unapproved AI tools.

## Editorial controls for the English revision
The revised master should:

1. distinguish entry-level event assistant/coordinator support from full planner/manager accountability while showing the progression between them;
2. anchor the occupation to U.S. SOC 13-1121.00, Canada NOC 12103, and Colombia CUOC 33320 without pretending the scopes are perfectly identical;
3. cover client requirements, budgets, vendor coordination, registration, facilities, catering, audiovisual needs, accommodation/transport, schedules, staffing, accessibility, event security, and post-event reconciliation;
4. explain that education expectations vary: bachelor’s-level expectations are common in U.S. planner roles, while relevant diplomas/degrees and experience pathways are common in Canada and technical/technologist routes exist in Colombia;
5. treat certifications as employer- or market-dependent rather than universal legal credentials;
6. include WIOA/American Job Center, Canada federal/provincial supports, SENA Betowa, and OIT/Cinterfor as locators without promising eligibility, seats, or funding;
7. explain that registered apprenticeships may be limited and that internships, assistant roles, supervised event work, employer training, hospitality pathways, and work experience can be practical work-based-learning routes;
8. label all non-government salary values as estimates and preserve source date/sample context;
9. include accessible event/registration planning and accommodation coordination without claiming legal or accessibility certification;
10. cover phishing, account security, vendor/payment verification, attendee-data handling, and employer-approved systems as practical cybersecurity responsibilities;
11. cover emergency plans, exits, incident contacts, chain of command, and escalation while keeping the learner within role/training boundaries;
12. permit responsible AI for low-risk drafting and organization only with human verification, privacy controls, and authoritative-source checking for consequential claims;
13. explain vendor contracts, deposits, cancellation terms, insurance requirements, permits, taxes, alcohol/service rules, crowd limits, and other legal/financial obligations as jurisdiction- and event-specific matters that require employer, venue, insurer, authority, or qualified-professional confirmation;
14. maintain plain-language, dysgraphia-friendly structure, descriptive headings, short planning steps, and reusable checklists without presenting the guide as professional certification.
