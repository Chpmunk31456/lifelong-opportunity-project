## Editorial checks

### Occupation scope — PASS
- Primary U.S. benchmark is correctly identified as O*NET-SOC 13-1121.00 — Meeting, Convention, and Event Planners.
- Canadian comparison is NOC 12103 — Conference and event planners.
- Colombian comparison is CUOC 33320 — Coordinadores y productores de conferencias, eventos y espectáculos.
- The master explicitly explains that entry-level assistant/coordinator titles may carry less authority than the broader planner occupation.

### Current labour-market facts — PASS
- U.S. 2025 BLS-derived median wage values are separated from the BLS OOH’s May 2024 wage value and 2024-2034 projections.
- Canada wage values preserve the November 19, 2025 Job Bank update context and Statistics Canada reference period.
- Colombia OCUPACOL is used for occupational scope/functions but its displayed salary range is deliberately not used as a representative wage benchmark because OCUPACOL itself flags lack of statistical representativeness.

### Market-estimate labeling — PASS
- U.S. Indeed Event Coordinator pay is clearly identified as a non-government market estimate with update date/sample context.
- Colombia Indeed estimates are clearly identified as non-government estimates with small-sample limitations.
- No market estimate is presented as a guaranteed wage or official government statistic.

### Training, funding, and work-based learning — PASS
- U.S. WIOA/CareerOneStop is presented as a locator with eligibility caveats.
- Formal Registered Apprenticeship is not implied to be routinely available; internships, assistant roles, hospitality work, supervised event work, and employer training are described as practical work-based-learning routes.
- Canada public training and labour-market agreements are described without promising eligibility or funding.
- Colombia SENA Betowa options include current program type/duration information and explicit live-availability caveats.
- OIT/Cinterfor is correctly framed as a Latin America/Caribbean vocational-training network and locator, not a guaranteed course or funding source.

### Accessibility — PASS
- Accessibility is integrated from planning through delivery, rather than treated as an afterthought.
- U.S. ADA examples are limited to educational planning guidance and do not claim legal compliance or certification.
- The master instructs workers to collect only information needed for approved accommodations and to use organizational processes.

### Safety and authority boundaries — PASS
- OSHA emergency-planning principles are used as a practical planning reference without converting the guide into safety certification or legal advice.
- The master identifies venue/employer/event-owner responsibility, established chain of command, exits, emergency contacts, escalation, and qualified-specialist boundaries.
- High-risk event categories are flagged for specialized/local-authority involvement rather than self-certification.

### Privacy and cybersecurity — PASS
- Attendee, vendor, payment, travel, accessibility, virtual-meeting, and contract data are treated as sensitive operational information where appropriate.
- CISA-aligned phishing, MFA, credential, software, and vendor-payment verification practices are incorporated.
- The master repeatedly directs learners to employer-approved systems and security policy instead of inventing controls.

### Responsible AI — PASS
- AI is limited to low-risk drafting, organizing, proofreading, fictional practice, or summarization use cases when approved.
- Consequential facts and decisions require human verification against authoritative current sources.
- Sensitive attendee/client, payment, banking, credential, contract, accessibility, and confidential business information is excluded from unapproved AI tools.
- NIST AI RMF is presented as a voluntary risk-management resource, not a certification.

### Readability and accessibility of prose — PASS
- Descriptive headings and short sections reduce cognitive load.
- Bullets and reusable checklists support readers who benefit from structured, dysgraphia-friendly presentation.
- Jargon is introduced in context and operational terms are explained through examples.
- The writing avoids unnecessary motivational claims and does not promise career outcomes.

### Career practicality — PASS
- The guide includes entry routes, low-cost skill building, work-based learning, portfolio ideas, résumé evidence, interview preparation, a 30-day learning plan, job-search titles, freelance cautions, common mistakes, and reusable planning checklists.
- Advice distinguishes evidence from unsupported résumé inflation.
- Paid credentials are treated as an investment to verify against employer demand rather than an assumed requirement.

### Legal / professional assurance boundary — PASS
The master does not claim:
- independent human certification;
- professional translation certification;
- legal review;
- accessibility certification;
- event-safety certification;
- accreditation;
- licensure approval;
- funding approval;
- apprenticeship placement;
- employment or earnings guarantees.
