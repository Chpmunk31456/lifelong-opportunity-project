# Guide 24 — Current source evidence 02

**Guide:** 24 — Facilities Coordinator and Building Operations Assistant  
**Research date:** 2026-08-10  
**Stage:** Current-source research  
**Status:** **PASS**

## Research boundary

This register supports the controlled 2026 revision of Guide 24. The occupation is intentionally framed as a coordinator/assistant pathway: scheduling, records, vendor access, work-order follow-up, inspections, basic building-operations support, inventory, occupant communication, emergency-procedure support, and escalation. It does **not** imply authority to independently perform electrical, HVAC/refrigeration, plumbing, fire/life-safety, elevator, engineering, structural, environmental, hazardous-material, security-system, or other work that may require a licensed, certified, qualified, or otherwise authorized person. Requirements vary by jurisdiction, building type, employer, equipment, and assigned duties.

## United States — occupational mapping and work

### O*NET OnLine / U.S. Department of Labor

**Source:** O*NET OnLine, occupation 11-3013.00, *Facilities Managers*  
**URL:** https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-3013.00  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

O*NET's 2026 occupation record describes facilities managers as workers who plan, direct, or coordinate operations and functionalities of facilities and buildings, including surrounding grounds or multiple facilities on a campus. Reported job titles include **Facilities Coordinator**, Facilities Operations Specialist, Building Services Supervisor, and related facilities titles. The revised guide should use this occupation as the closest authoritative U.S. management-side mapping while making clear that an entry-level coordinator or building-operations assistant may carry a narrower scope and lower pay than a facilities manager.

### Bureau of Labor Statistics — current official wage reference

**Source:** U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, May 2025 national results  
**URL:** https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ocwage.t01.htm  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

The May 2025 OEWS national table reports for **Facilities Managers** approximately **156,180 employed**, a **mean hourly wage of $56.20**, a **mean annual wage of $116,890**, and a **median hourly wage of $51.28**. These are occupation-level manager statistics and must **not** be presented as a typical starting wage for a facilities coordinator or assistant.

**BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook cross-check:** https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/administrative-services-managers.htm  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

The current OOH page reports a **May 2024 median annual wage of $104,690 for facilities managers** and projects **4% employment growth from 2024 to 2034** for administrative services and facilities managers overall. It also notes that facilities managers often are on call for problems arising outside normal hours. Because the OEWS 2025 table is newer, the revised guide should treat the 2025 OEWS figure as the primary current official manager-level wage reference and label the OOH value explicitly as May 2024 when used.

## United States — current non-government income estimate for the coordinator title

Private salary sites are supplementary market estimates, not government statistics. They reflect title mix, location, employer, seniority, sample method, and posting behavior.

**Indeed — Facility Coordinator**  
**URL:** https://www.indeed.com/career/facility-coordinator/salaries  
**Observed:** 2026-08-10; page states update **July 19, 2026**

Indeed reports a U.S. **average base salary of $24.72/hour**, with a displayed low of **$16.88/hour** and high of **$36.19/hour**, based on about **2.6k salaries from job postings over the prior 36 months**. This is a useful current market-title estimate because it is materially closer to the guide's coordinator scope than the BLS facilities-manager occupation.

The English revision should therefore present two clearly separated references: (1) the official BLS manager-level occupation data, with an explicit warning that it overstates many coordinator/assistant roles, and (2) the current Indeed coordinator-title estimate, clearly labeled non-government and non-guaranteed. Learners should compare local postings and total compensation before making a decision.

## United States — education, funding, and work-based learning

**CareerOneStop / U.S. Department of Labor — WIOA-eligible training finder**  
**URL:** https://www.careeronestop.org/LocalHelp/EmploymentAndTraining/find-WIOA-training-programs.aspx  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

CareerOneStop directs eligible learners to state directories of Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act (WIOA) approved training providers and advises people to contact an American Job Center regarding eligibility. The guide should instruct learners to verify that a specific program is eligible **before enrollment or payment**; WIOA support is not automatic.

**Apprenticeship.gov — construction industry**  
**URL:** https://www.apprenticeship.gov/apprenticeship-industries/construction  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

Apprenticeship.gov identifies Registered Apprenticeship as an employer-based pathway for construction and related skilled-trades talent and reports **480,399 apprentices served in construction in 2025**. Facilities learners may use Apprenticeship.gov to search for building-maintenance, electrical, HVAC, plumbing, stationary-engineer, energy, or related pathways where available. The guide must not imply that completing such an apprenticeship automatically authorizes work outside the apprentice's occupation or applicable licensing rules, and it must not claim a dedicated facilities-coordinator apprenticeship is available everywhere.

Employers may also fund training in computerized maintenance management systems (CMMS), work-order systems, safety procedures, access control, building automation, energy management, vendor procedures, first aid/CPR, or role-specific technical credentials. Employer-paid training and tuition reimbursement should be checked before self-funding; they are not universal benefits.

## Canada — occupational mapping, requirements, and wages

### Job Bank — Facilities Coordinator / NOC 70012

**Source:** Government of Canada Job Bank, *Facilities Coordinator*  
**Summary:** https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/297406/ca  
**Requirements:** https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/297406/ca  
**Wages:** https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/297406/ca  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

Job Bank maps the title **Facilities Coordinator** to **NOC 70012 — Facility operation and maintenance managers**. It describes facility-operation managers as planning, organizing, directing, controlling, and evaluating operations of commercial, transportation, recreational, and other facilities, while maintenance managers lead maintenance departments. Job Bank states that facility-operation managers typically need a college or university program related to business administration or facility operation/maintenance, or an equivalent combination of technical training and experience; maintenance managers typically need a related engineering/building-maintenance program or equivalent training and experience, and several years of supervisory experience are usually required. Job Bank currently states that this occupation is **not regulated in Canada** at the NOC level, while specific trades, systems, provinces, and assigned duties can still be regulated.

The national wage page, updated **November 19, 2025**, shows approximately **CAD $25.96/hour low, $45.20/hour median, and $70.67/hour high** for NOC 70012, using Labour Force Survey reference data. Because Guide 24 targets coordinators and assistants as well as operations support, these manager-level figures must be labeled carefully.

### Job Bank — Building Operator - Maintenance / NOC 73201

**Source:** Government of Canada Job Bank  
**Wages:** https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/14403/ca  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

For the more hands-on **Building Operator - Maintenance** title under **NOC 73201**, Job Bank shows national wages of approximately **CAD $16.55/hour low, $25.00/hour median, and $38.46/hour high** (updated November 19, 2025). This provides a more realistic lower-scope comparison for some building-operations-assistant roles. The guide should explain that facilities coordinator, building operator, maintenance worker, and facilities manager are not interchangeable titles and may map to different NOCs and licensing/trade boundaries.

## Latin America — pathways and portability

Across Latin America, facilities work can intersect with national and municipal building codes, electrical and gas rules, occupational safety, fire protection, elevator regulation, environmental requirements, contractor registration, data protection, labor law, and professional licensing. Requirements differ materially by country and sometimes by city or state/province. A credential, apprenticeship, authorization, or technical license earned in one country should not be presented as automatically portable to another.

The guide should direct learners first to public technical institutes, national employment services, ministries of labor/education, official scholarship systems, municipal or national regulators, and employer-funded training in the country where they intend to work. In Colombia, SENA/Betowa and the Agencia Pública de Empleo are priority no-cost public starting points.

## Accessibility, occupant safety, privacy, cybersecurity, and access control

Facilities staff may handle badge/access records, visitor logs, CCTV-related requests, emergency contacts, work orders, vendor credentials, floor plans, equipment inventories, incident reports, keys, alarm information, tenant/occupant information, and building-system data. The revised guide should require least-privilege access, approved systems, secure credential handling, accurate records, escalation of lost keys/badges and suspicious access, and compliance with employer privacy/security policies.

Accessibility work should be framed conservatively: a coordinator may document barriers, route accommodation/facility requests, schedule authorized work, and verify completion against approved specifications, but should not independently decide legal accessibility compliance or substitute personal judgment for qualified design, engineering, code, occupational-health, or legal review.

## AI use — controlled safety position

AI may assist with study plans, checklists, plain-language explanations, draft routine communications, non-authoritative work-order categorization, inventory normalization, preventive-maintenance reminders, and training scenarios. It must not be treated as an authoritative source for electrical/HVAC/plumbing/fire/life-safety decisions, emergency response, code compliance, accessibility compliance, environmental/hazardous-material decisions, lockout/tagout or other high-risk procedures, contractor licensing, or engineering judgments.

Sensitive floor plans, access-control data, alarm details, camera information, credentials, personal information, incident reports, security vulnerabilities, or building-system configuration data should not be uploaded to external AI tools unless the employer explicitly authorizes that use and applicable privacy, confidentiality, cybersecurity, vendor, retention, and contractual requirements are satisfied.
