## United States — occupation, entry pathway, wages, and outlook

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics

The current Occupational Outlook Handbook profile for **Water and Wastewater Treatment Plant and System Operators (SOC 51-8031)** reports:

- May 2024 median pay: **$58,260 per year / $28.01 per hour**.
- Lowest 10 percent: below **$37,870**; highest 10 percent: above **$86,160**.
- Employment: **132,400 in 2024**, projected to **123,800 in 2034**.
- Projected change: **-8,700 jobs**, or **-7 percent**, for 2024–2034.
- About **10,700 openings per year** on average over the decade, all attributed by BLS to replacement needs rather than occupational growth.
- Typical entry education: **high school diploma or equivalent**; related work experience: **none**; typical training: **long-term on-the-job training**.

BLS states that a state license is usually required, although entry-level workers often complete licensing requirements during on-the-job training. Requirements and license levels vary widely by state. Trainees work under an experienced operator and may begin with observation, readings, sampling, and simple maintenance; the occupational description does not authorize independent process changes, chemical dosing, compliance decisions, or hazardous maintenance.

BLS also records 24/7 operations, rotating shifts, emergency callouts, computerized controls, chemical leaks, oxygen-deficient conditions, dangerous gases, slippery walkways, machinery hazards, and difficult-to-access locations.

Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/production/water-and-wastewater-treatment-plant-and-system-operators.htm

### O*NET

O*NET OnLine identifies **51-8031.00 Water and Wastewater Treatment Plant and System Operators**. Its occupational data covers monitoring processes, documenting operational data, sampling and testing, inspecting equipment, operating treatment systems, cleaning, maintenance, and repair. O*NET places the occupation in Job Zone 1–2 and lists U.S. Department of Labor-vetted apprenticeship titles including Waste Treatment Operator, Wastewater Systems Operator, Water Systems Operation Specialist, and Water Treatment Plant Operator.

O*NET task statements and apprenticeship titles are occupational evidence, not proof of a current opening, certification, employer authorization, or permission to act as an operator in responsible charge.

Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/51-8031.00

## United States — certification and controlled-work boundaries

### Operator certification

EPA's January 2025 **Summary of State Operator Certification Programs** documents state drinking-water operator-certification systems and their differing facility classifications, operator levels, education, experience, examination, renewal, and enforcement requirements. The controlling state program and the classification of the actual facility must be checked. A certificate, license, operator-in-training status, or reciprocity decision from one jurisdiction must not be assumed valid in another.

Wastewater operator requirements are also state- and facility-specific. The guide must direct readers to the applicable state drinking-water and wastewater authorities and employer before they rely on a course, examination, certificate, or prior experience.

Source: https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2025-01/summary_of_state_operator_certification_programs.pdf

### Confined spaces and toxic or oxygen-deficient atmospheres

OSHA identifies sewer and wastewater treatment as settings where hydrogen sulfide may occur. It can accumulate in pits, manholes, sewers, vaults, and other low or enclosed spaces; smell is not a reliable warning. OSHA's permit-required confined-space framework assigns entry, atmospheric evaluation, attendant, rescue, and employer-program responsibilities.

Version 2 must not teach atmospheric testing, entry permitting, ventilation, confined-space entry, or rescue. It must require site classification, authorization, trained roles, approved monitoring, communications, and rescue arrangements, with stop-and-escalate action for alarms, odors, symptoms, uncertain atmosphere, ventilation failure, loss of communication, or an unauthorized space. An entrant must never attempt an improvised rescue.

Sources:

- https://www.osha.gov/hydrogen-sulfide/hydrogen-sulfide-workplaces
- https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.146

### Chemicals, biological exposure, and hazardous energy

Water and wastewater facilities may involve disinfectants and treatment chemicals, incompatible substances, corrosives, toxic gases, sewage, sludge, sharps, aerosols, electrical systems, pumps, pressure, rotating equipment, vehicles, and stored energy. OSHA's Hazard Communication, bloodborne-pathogens, and lockout/tagout standards establish employer programs and trained or authorized roles where applicable.

The guide must not teach chemical selection, dosing, mixing, transfer, neutralization, spill response, disinfection release, pathogen-risk classification, lockout/tagout execution, electrical work, line opening, pressure release, bypassing, or return to service. Safety data sheets, labels, exposure-control plans, energy-control procedures, permits, PPE, hygiene, vaccination/medical provisions, and emergency actions remain employer- and hazard-specific.

Sources:

- https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1200
- https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1030
- https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147

## Canada — occupation mapping, wages, and certification boundaries

Government of Canada Job Bank maps **Wastewater Treatment Plant Operator** to **Water and waste treatment plant operators (NOC 92101)**. Current national wage evidence, updated November 19, 2025, reports:

- Low: **CAD $25.00/hour**
- Median: **CAD $36.06/hour**
- High: **CAD $48.00/hour**

Job Bank states that secondary school is required; college or industry courses in chemistry, pollution control, or related subjects are usually required; on-the-job training is provided; and previous field experience may be required. It states that water-treatment and distribution certification and wastewater collection and treatment certification are required at levels related to plant classification, while regulatory bodies and exact requirements vary by province or territory.

Health Canada states that provinces and territories are primarily responsible for drinking-water safety and that municipalities usually handle treatment and distribution. Environment and Climate Change Canada states that wastewater system owners and operators may also be subject to federal Wastewater Systems Effluent Regulations. These layers do not create a single national operator license.

Readers must verify the occupation title, facility class, operator class, operator-in-training status, education, experience, examination, continuing education, reciprocity, and responsible-charge rules with the relevant provincial or territorial regulator and employer.

Sources:

- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/20827/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/20827/ca
- https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/environment/drinking-water.html

## Colombia — SENA pathways and competency boundaries

SENA's Betowa catalog identifies related pathways including **Operación de sistemas de potabilización de agua** (Técnico, 12 months) and **Saneamiento y salud ambiental** (Técnico, 2,208 hours). The catalog also identifies shorter complementary offerings such as **Análisis de riesgos en sistemas de agua y saneamiento** (48 hours). Availability is dynamic: a program profile may exist while no current cohort or places are available. Readers must verify the live listing, location, modality, dates, admission requirements, and places.

These pathways are education evidence, not automatic employment, plant assignment, independent operating authority, or certification for every required competency.

## Controlled Version 2 requirements derived from research

The English master must:

1. Keep U.S. SOC 51-8031, Canadian NOC 92101, and Colombia pathway evidence separate.
2. Distinguish entry-level observation, readings, housekeeping, documentation, and supervised sampling or maintenance from operator-in-responsible-charge duties and regulated decisions.
3. State that certification, license, facility class, operator level, reciprocity, and supervision rules are jurisdiction- and facility-specific.
4. Require employer authorization, site-specific training, approved procedures, PPE, communication, hygiene, energy control, permits, emergency planning, and qualified roles as applicable.
5. Use stop-and-escalate language for alarms, unexplained readings, chemical leaks, incompatible or unlabeled substances, symptoms, gas or oxygen concerns, confined spaces, sharps, uncontrolled energy, guards, pressure, falls/drowning, electrical hazards, uncertain samples, SCADA anomalies, spills, bypasses, and work outside authorization.
6. Avoid step-by-step instructions for dosing or mixing chemicals, confined-space entry or rescue, lockout/tagout, energized work, line breaking, process-control changes, bypasses, disinfection, laboratory release, compliance determinations, or hazardous maintenance.
7. Treat sampling, calculations, laboratory tests, operational logs, alarms, and SCADA data as controlled work whose methods and decisions come from the facility's approved procedures and authorized personnel.
8. Present Apprenticeship.gov, FAFSA, SENA, and regional public vocational systems as live verification routes, never guaranteed admission, funding, placement, certification, licensing, or employment.
9. Explain Canada's province/territory and facility-class variation and Colombia's current competency-verification requirement without implying national or cross-border portability.
10. Include responsible-AI limits: AI may assist study and job-search organization but must not set doses, interpret compliance samples, change controls, diagnose plant hazards, generate permits, authorize entry, override alarms, select PPE, or replace approved procedures and qualified judgment.
11. Protect facility diagrams, access details, SCADA credentials/data, treatment settings, chemical inventories, security information, customer records, laboratory results, incident details, and public-health or environmental reporting information.
12. State that the guide does not guarantee employment, wages, openings, admission, funding, apprenticeship, certification, licensing, authorization, or earnings.
