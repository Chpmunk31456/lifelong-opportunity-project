# Guide 53 — Current Source Evidence 02

**Guide:** 53 — Physical Therapist Assistant  
**Branch:** `revision/guide-00-100-2026`  
**Research date:** August 19, 2026  
**Research status:** **PASS**

## Research objective

Rebuild Guide 53 around current authoritative evidence while preserving a strict boundary between the U.S. **Physical Therapist Assistant (PTA)** credential and rehabilitation-support or physiotherapy roles in other jurisdictions. The evidence below controls the Version 2 English master and downstream localization.

## United States — occupation, supervision, education, licensure, pay and outlook

### Occupational scope and supervision

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics states that physical therapist assistants work **under the direction and supervision of physical therapists**. PTAs provide direct patient care such as observing patients, helping patients perform exercises that are part of the physical therapist's plan of care, using specified therapeutic techniques or equipment, educating patients/families, recording progress, and reporting treatment results to the physical therapist.

This source boundary is controlling: Guide 53 must not imply that a PTA independently diagnoses, creates the physical therapy plan of care, substitutes for a licensed physical therapist, or practices outside state law or employer policy.

Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook — Physical Therapist Assistants and Aides  
https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/physical-therapist-assistants-and-aides.htm

### Education and accreditation

BLS states that entrants typically need an associate degree from an accredited program and a license or certification. BLS describes PTA programs as generally about two years and including supervised clinical work.

CAPTE is the recognized specialized accreditor for entry-level U.S. physical therapist and physical therapist assistant education programs. Its current directory should be used to verify program accreditation before enrollment. CAPTE reports 379 accredited/candidacy PTA programs and 10,393 enrolled PTA students from 2025 annual accreditation reports. CAPTE's current accreditation handbook states that programs are expected to meet the newer standards and required elements as of January 1, 2026.

Sources:

- CAPTE — About CAPTE: https://www.capteonline.org/about-capte
- CAPTE — Data and Research: https://www.capteonline.org/faculty-and-program-resources/data-and-research
- CAPTE — Accreditation Handbook: https://www.capteonline.org/faculty-and-program-resources/resource_documents/accreditation-handbook

### Licensure / certification and NPTE

FSBPT states that U.S. jurisdictions require a license or certification, depending on the jurisdiction, to work as a physical therapist assistant. A candidate should apply through the jurisdiction where they intend to work, pass the National Physical Therapy Examination (NPTE) when required, and meet any additional jurisdiction-specific requirements. FSBPT emphasizes that each licensing authority sets its own eligibility criteria.

The NPTE is an important part of the licensing process but is not the only requirement. FSBPT states that the PTA exam assesses entry-level competence after graduation from an accredited program or an equivalent non-accredited program. The current NPTE registration price listed by FSBPT for the PTA examination is **USD $485**; candidates must still verify jurisdiction and testing requirements before paying.

Sources:
- FSBPT — State Licensure: https://www.fsbpt.org/Secondary-Pages/Licensees/Getting-Licensed

- FSBPT — Exam Registration and Payment: https://www.fsbpt.org/Our-Services/Candidate-Services/Exam-Registration-Payment

## Canada — related technical therapy occupation, not automatic U.S. credential equivalence

Canada's Job Bank maps the title **physical therapist assistant (PTA)** to **NOC 32109 — Other technical occupations in therapy and assessment**. Job Bank describes these workers as performing technical therapy and assessment functions; some assist professionals including physiotherapists and occupational therapists.

Job Bank's national summary says this occupation usually requires a college diploma, apprenticeship training of two or more years, or supervisory occupational experience at the NOC-group level. Because NOC 32109 covers multiple technical therapy occupations, Guide 53 must not convert this generic NOC requirement into a universal Canada-wide credential or licensure rule for every PTA-titled job. Verify the actual employer, province/territory, program, and scope before enrollment or practice.

Sources:
- Job Bank — PTA summary, NOC 32109: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/4466/ca
- Job Bank — PTA description: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/occupation/4466/ca
- Job Bank — PTA requirements: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/4466/ca

### Official Canadian wage evidence

Job Bank reports national PTA/NOC 32109 hourly wages, updated November 19, 2025, of:

- **Low:** CAD **$17.62/hour**
- **Median:** CAD **$26.85/hour**
- **High:** CAD **$36.71/hour**

Job Bank identifies the reference period as 2023–2024 Labour Force Survey data from Statistics Canada. It also reports that **78.8%** of workers in NOC 32109 receive at least one non-wage benefit. Treat these figures as NOC-group labour-market data, not a guaranteed PTA wage.

Source: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/4466/ca

### Canadian outlook

Job Bank reports varying three-year prospects by province/territory and a **moderate risk of shortage** nationally for NOC 32109 over 2024–2033. This is group-level outlook evidence and should not be presented as a guarantee for an individual PTA program or location.

Source: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/outlook-occupation/4466/ca

## Colombia — do not present U.S. PTA as a direct Colombian professional equivalent

Colombia regulates **Fisioterapia** as a university-educated health profession under **Ley 528 de 1999**. The law defines physiotherapy as a liberal health profession with university education and regulates professional practice, including intervention, physiotherapeutic assessment/diagnosis, and professional registration requirements. It requires the corresponding physiotherapy degree and professional credential for professional practice.

This creates a critical translation and scope boundary: the U.S. PTA credential must **not** be relabeled as `fisioterapeuta`, nor should Guide 53 imply that a short course, assistant certificate, U.S. PTA education, or unrelated rehabilitation-support training confers the legal authority of a Colombian physiotherapist.

Authoritative sources:
- SUIN Juriscol — Ley 528 de 1999: https://www.suin-juriscol.gov.co/viewDocument.asp?ruta=Leyes%2F1662075
- Ministerio de Salud y Protección Social — Profesiones y ocupaciones / Talento Humano en Salud: https://www.minsalud.gov.co/salud/PO/Paginas/talento-humano-salud.aspx

### Colombian support/auxiliary pathways

Colombia's health-workforce framework also recognizes health **occupations** that provide support/complementary functions based on defined competencies. SENA's occupational classification includes auxiliary health-service occupations whose functions are carried out under delegation, protocols, and applicable regulation. These are not automatically equivalent to either the U.S. PTA credential or the Colombian university profession of physiotherapy.

For a lower-cost public training search, candidates should use SENA/Betowa and the SENA occupational/employment systems to look for currently offered health-support, patient-care, physical-activity, rehabilitation-adjacent, or related programs. Availability and eligibility change by intake. A current SENA `Actividad física` technologist program is one rehabilitation-adjacent example, but it is **not** a physiotherapy or U.S.-PTA credential and must not be represented as one.

Sources:
- SENA Observatorio — occupational classification / auxiliary health services: https://observatorio.sena.edu.co/Clasificacion/CnoDetalle?tags=3311
- SENA Betowa — Actividad física: https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/actividad-fisica?modality=P&programId=166414

## Latin America and Caribbean — use national training and regulatory systems

Titles, professional regulation, vocational systems, and permissible clinical functions differ substantially by country. OIT/Cinterfor coordinates a regional network of vocational-training institutions and provides a practical starting point for identifying national public training bodies; its network includes SENA in Colombia and institutions across Latin America and the Caribbean.

Use OIT/Cinterfor as a **locator**, then verify the actual occupation, health-profession rules, training recognition, cost, admission requirements, and clinical scope with the relevant national authority before paying or practicing.

Sources:

Start with public or national training institutions identified through SENA/Betowa and OIT/Cinterfor before purchasing private training. Public availability, admission, and cost vary. Never imply that a public vocational course confers a regulated health-profession title unless the competent national authority explicitly establishes that equivalence.

## Apprenticeship / work-based learning boundary

The U.S. PTA entry pathway documented by BLS is an accredited associate-degree program with supervised clinical education and state licensure/certification requirements, **not a generic apprenticeship substitute**. Clinical education inside an accredited program is work-based learning, but it should not be marketed as an apprenticeship unless the program/employer is formally structured and recognized as such.

Canada's NOC-group summary includes `college or apprenticeship` at the broad NOC 32109 level; because this group covers multiple occupations, Guide 53 must not claim that apprenticeship is a standard PTA credential pathway throughout Canada without program- and province-specific evidence.

## Safety, privacy, and AI controls for Version 2

The English master and translations must preserve the following safeguards:

- Follow the licensed physical therapist's plan of care, supervision requirements, state/provincial/national law, employer policy, and documented clinical procedures.
- Do not independently diagnose, alter a treatment plan, introduce an unapproved intervention, or present assistant training as professional licensure.
- Escalate unexpected pain, neurological changes, cardiopulmonary symptoms, falls, skin/wound concerns, abnormal responses to exercise, equipment problems, or other clinical deterioration according to protocol.
- Use proper body mechanics, transfer techniques, gait belts/assistive devices when indicated, infection-control practices, and required personal protective equipment.
- Protect patient health information and credentials; use only employer-approved systems and communication channels.
- Public AI tools may help with general study, terminology, or non-sensitive practice questions but must not receive identifiable patient data, proprietary clinical records, access credentials, or unapproved protected information.
- AI output must not replace licensed clinical judgment, the plan of care, emergency procedures, official protocols, or jurisdiction-specific scope rules.

## Research QA decision

**PASS.** Current authoritative evidence is sufficient to reconstruct the Version 2 English master.
