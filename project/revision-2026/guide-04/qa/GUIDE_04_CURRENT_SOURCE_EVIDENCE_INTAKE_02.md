# Guide 04 — Current-Source Evidence Intake 02

Date checked: 2026-08-08  
Branch: `revision/guide-00-100-2026`  
Guide: 04 — Project Coordinator

## Purpose

Establish a current, auditable evidence base for the revised English edition before drafting or freezing the 2026 working master. This gate deliberately separates exact-title market evidence from broader occupational proxies and does not treat a proxy wage as a Project Coordinator wage.

## United States — occupational anchor and income labeling

### Official occupational anchor/proxy

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) Occupational Outlook Handbook for **Project Management Specialists (SOC 13-1082)** is suitable as a broad occupational anchor for project-coordination work, but it is **not an exact-title Project Coordinator wage series**.

BLS reports:

- median annual wage: **US$100,750 in May 2024**;
- projected employment growth: **6% from 2024 to 2034**; and
- about **78,200 openings per year on average** over the decade.

BLS describes project management specialists as coordinating budgets, schedules, staffing, milestones, deliverables, project documents, vendors, and other project details. The revised guide may use these duties to explain role adjacency, while clearly warning that coordinator jobs can be more junior and can pay materially less.

Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook, Project Management Specialists  
https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/project-management-specialists.htm

### Current non-government exact-title estimate

ZipRecruiter’s national **Project Coordinator** salary page, accessed during this evidence check, reports an average of **US$59,915/year (US$28.81/hour)** and labels the estimate **“As of Jul 20, 2026.”** The page states that its salary estimates are derived from employer job postings and third-party data sources.

This may be used only as a **non-government market estimate**, not as an official wage, guaranteed pay level, or expected starting salary. The displayed date and amount must be rechecked again immediately before final publication because market pages are mutable.

Source: ZipRecruiter, Project Coordinator Salary  
https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Salaries/Project-Coordinator-Salary

## United States — funding and lower-cost pathways

### American Job Centers and WIOA

CareerOneStop, sponsored by the U.S. Department of Labor’s Employment and Training Administration, states that American Job Centers provide free job-search and career services and may provide training services, counseling, skills testing, labor-market information, supportive-service referrals, and accessibility accommodations. Comprehensive AJCs include Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act (WIOA) partners.

U.S. Department of Labor WIOA guidance confirms that allowable training can include occupational-skills training, on-the-job training, incumbent-worker training, workplace training combined with related instruction, skill upgrading/retraining, entrepreneurial training, and customized training subject to eligibility and program rules.

Controlled wording for the guide: **contact the local American Job Center to determine individual eligibility and whether a specific training provider/program can be funded; never promise WIOA payment.**

Sources:  
https://www.careeronestop.org/LocalHelp/AmericanJobCenters/american-job-centers.aspx  
https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/advisories/training-and-employment-guidance-letter-no-08-19

### Registered Apprenticeship and work-based learning

Apprenticeship.gov defines Registered Apprenticeship as a paid, employer-driven pathway combining mentored work experience, progressive wage increases, related classroom instruction, and a nationally recognized credential. Its Job Finder allows career seekers to search current opportunities.

The revised guide must **not claim that a generic Project Coordinator Registered Apprenticeship is universally available**. It should instruct readers to search current listings by title, related project/operations terms, employer, and location and to verify the specific program’s registered status.

Sources:  
https://www.apprenticeship.gov/career-seekers  
https://www.apprenticeship.gov/apprenticeship-job-finder

### Federal student aid and scholarships

Federal Student Aid states that the FAFSA can provide access, subject to eligibility and school/program participation, to federal grants such as Pell Grants, Federal Work-Study, and federal student loans for college, career school, or trade school. Federal Student Aid also advises students with aid gaps to investigate scholarships and other need-based options.

Controlled wording for the guide: **verify that the school and program are eligible before assuming federal aid applies; prefer grants, scholarships, employer support, workforce funding, and low-cost training before debt.** No scholarship or grant is guaranteed.

Sources:  
https://studentaid.gov/articles/fafsa-student-steps/  
https://studentaid.gov/articles/financial-aid-not-enough/

### Employer support

The guide may encourage readers to ask an employer about tuition assistance, professional-development budgets, reimbursement for approved courses/exams, internal project assignments, mentoring, and paid learning time. These are employer-specific benefits, not universal entitlements. Readers should verify written eligibility, repayment/clawback terms, tax treatment where relevant, and what happens if employment ends.

## Canada — role matching before wage use

Government of Canada Job Bank demonstrates why a single Canadian wage must not be assigned to every job titled “Project Coordinator.” Examples include materially different occupational mappings:

- **Project coordinator — research** maps to **NOC 11201** and shows national hourly wages of **C$27.00 low / C$44.10 median / C$69.71 high**, updated November 19, 2025, with source data from Statistics Canada’s Labour Force Survey.
- **Construction project coordinator** is associated with a different occupational group, **NOC 70010**, and Job Bank reports different regional wage values.

Therefore, the revised guide must tell Canadian readers to match the vacancy’s actual duties, industry, and NOC before using Job Bank wages or requirements. A research, construction, IT, nonprofit, administrative, or implementation coordinator may belong to different occupational groups.

Sources:  
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/297410/ca  
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/24320/ca

## Colombia — public pathways

### SENA training

SENA’s current Betowa catalog includes project-related training that can support entry or progression, subject to current availability and admission rules. Verified examples include:

- **Gestión de proyectos de desarrollo económico y social** — Tecnólogo, 4,656 hours;
- **Formulación y evaluación de proyectos** — complementary special course, 48 hours, with current modality/location offerings varying by intake; and
- **Gestión de proyectos comunitarios** — complementary special course, 48 hours.

SENA reported in February 2026 that its first 2026 classroom/distance intake included more than 41,000 **free** places and was open to Colombians and foreigners with regular migration status, subject to program requirements and places available.

Controlled wording: SENA is a strong public-first pathway, but **course availability, modality, location, intake dates, prerequisites, and admission are variable and must be checked in Betowa at the time of application.**

### Servicio Público de Empleo

Colombia’s Servicio Público de Empleo (SPE) provides a public network for job search, vacancy access, employment orientation, and labor-market intermediation. Its current 2026 materials show continuing nationwide employment services and employability activities.

The revised guide should direct readers to the SPE and its authorized providers for current vacancies and orientation rather than inventing a national Project Coordinator salary. Where Colombian income evidence is used later, it must identify the exact title/duties, employer/sector, date, pay period, employment arrangement, and whether the figure is an official series, a vacancy observation, or a non-government estimate.

Sources:

## Broader Latin America — portability control

Job titles, qualification systems, pay conventions, labor law, and public-training systems vary across Latin America. The guide must not extrapolate a U.S., Canadian, or Colombian salary to the region.

ILO/Cinterfor’s current regional work on mutual recognition of skills certifications illustrates an important portability trend, but the mechanism is still developing; the ILO reported that the Skills Passport platform was expected to begin operations in November 2026. The guide may describe this as an emerging regional development, **not as an already universal recognition system**.

Source: International Labour Organization  
https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/latin-america-and-caribbean-moves-closer-mutual-recognition-skills

For any country-specific edition or example, readers should be directed first to that country’s official employment service, labor ministry, and vocational-training system and then to current employer vacancies for the actual title used locally.

## Editorial controls derived from this evidence

The revised English master must:

1. distinguish **Project Coordinator** from the broader BLS **Project Management Specialist** occupation;
2. label every non-government wage estimate as such and include its displayed as-of/access date;
3. avoid guarantees of employment, salary, apprenticeship availability, funding, scholarship eligibility, credential recognition, or remote work;
4. explain that certifications such as CAPM/PMP or platform/tool certificates are optional or employer-dependent unless a specific vacancy requires them;
5. recommend free/public-first learning and workforce routes before expensive private training;
6. tell readers to verify employer tuition-assistance repayment clauses before accepting reimbursement;
7. preserve accessibility-first formatting, plain-language explanations, descriptive links, and text alternatives for meaningful visuals;
8. include cybersecurity/privacy hygiene for project files, collaboration tools, access permissions, meeting recordings, AI assistants, and confidential client/employer information; and
9. treat generative AI as an assistive productivity tool requiring human verification, authorization, confidentiality controls, and accountability—not as a substitute for project judgment.
