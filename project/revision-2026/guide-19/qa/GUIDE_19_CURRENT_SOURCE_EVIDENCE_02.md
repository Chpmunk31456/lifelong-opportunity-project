## 1. United States occupation definition — O*NET

**Source:** O*NET OnLine, `23-2011.00 — Paralegals and Legal Assistants`  
https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/23-2011.00

**Current state:** Updated 2026.

Controlled findings:

- Paralegals and legal assistants assist lawyers by investigating facts, preparing legal documents, and researching legal precedent.
- O*NET includes titles such as Immigration Paralegal, Legal Analyst, Legal Assistant, Legal Clerk, Litigation Paralegal, Paralegal, Paralegal Specialist, and Real Estate Paralegal.
- The occupation involves substantive legal-support work; this is materially different from general administrative support.

**Control:** occupational descriptions do not create legal authority. Jurisdiction, employer supervision, court/agency rules, and applicable professional-responsibility law determine what an individual may actually do.

https://www.bls.gov/ooh/legal/paralegals-and-legal-assistants.htm

Controlled findings:

- Approximate median hourly equivalent reported by BLS: **USD 29.33/hour**.
- Employment in 2024: **376,200**.
- Projected employment in 2034: **376,800**.
- Projected 2024–2034 percent change: **0% (little or no change)**.
- Projected numeric change: **+600**.

- Typical entry-level education: **associate’s degree**.

**Control:** annual openings are not employment growth. The occupation is projected to show little or no net change.

## 3. ABA definition and delegated-work boundary

**Sources:** American Bar Association — Current ABA Definition of Paralegal; Information for Lawyers: How Paralegals Can Improve Your Practice  
https://www.americanbar.org/groups/paralegals/profession-information/current_aba_definition_of_legal_assistant_paralegal/  
https://www.americanbar.org/groups/paralegals/profession-information/information_for_lawyers_how_paralegals_can_improve_your_practice/

Controlled findings:

- ABA defines a paralegal as a person qualified by education, training, or work experience who is employed or retained by a lawyer, law office, corporation, governmental agency, or other entity and performs specifically delegated substantive legal work for which a lawyer is responsible.
- ABA guidance says delegated work can include factual and legal research, document preparation, interviewing clients and witnesses, and trial/closing support when properly supervised and permitted by law.
- ABA guidance states that paralegals must avoid unauthorized practice of law and generally may not give legal advice, establish the attorney-client relationship, or set legal fees. Representation or other nonlawyer practice may be permitted in particular agencies or jurisdictions and must be verified.

**Mandatory editorial control:** the guide must not imply that the occupational title grants independent authority to practice law.

## 4. U.S. regulation, certification, and education-program distinctions

**Source:** ABA Educational Information for Paralegals, updated May 8, 2026  
https://www.americanbar.org/groups/paralegals/profession-information/educational-information-for-paralegals/

Controlled findings:

- ABA states that **California is currently the only state that directly regulates paralegals** by requiring people using paralegal/legal-assistant titles to meet specified education/experience and continuing-education requirements.
- ABA states there is **no mandatory certification examination for paralegals anywhere in the United States**.
- Individual certification offered by nongovernmental organizations is distinct from completion of a paralegal certificate program.
- ABA **does not certify individual paralegals**; people should not describe themselves as “ABA-certified paralegals.”

**Education-program source:** ABA Approved Paralegal Education Programs / Approval Process  
https://www.americanbar.org/groups/paralegals/approval-resources/  
https://www.americanbar.org/groups/paralegals/paralegal-resource-directory/

Controlled findings:

- ABA approves qualifying **education programs**, not individual paralegals.
- The current ABA directory is updated on a recurring basis; programs not listed are not ABA-approved.
- Program approval and institutional accreditation are distinct concepts and must not be conflated.

## 5. Limited-license programs are not ordinary paralegal status

**Source:** ABA, February 2, 2026 — limited licensed legal paraprofessionals  
https://www.americanbar.org/groups/paralegals/blog/how-states-are-using-non-lawyers-to-address-the-access-to-justice-gap/

Controlled finding:

- ABA reports that Arizona, Minnesota, Oregon, and Utah currently issue some form of limited license allowing qualifying nonlawyers to provide specified legal services in defined circumstances.

**Control:** these programs are distinct from ordinary paralegal employment and must never be presented as a general U.S. paralegal license. Eligibility, scope, supervision, title, and permitted services are state-specific.

## 6. Canada — NOC 42200 and current wages

**Sources:** Government of Canada Job Bank — Paralegals and related occupations (NOC 42200), requirements and wages  
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/16074/ca  
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/wagereport/occupation/16094

Controlled findings:

- Canadian occupational grouping: **NOC 42200 — Paralegals and related occupations**.
- Job Bank states that paralegals in law firms typically require a bachelor’s degree in law or a law-college diploma plus in-house training.
- Independent paralegals typically require legal knowledge obtained through industry training/experience or a community-college paralegal program.
- Job Bank states that paralegals require a licence to perform advanced legal services.
- National wages updated November 19, 2025:
  - low: **C$21.63/hour**;
  - median: **C$33.05/hour**;
  - high: **C$52.19/hour**.
- Wage reference period: Labour Force Survey 2023–2024, Statistics Canada.

**Control:** licensing and permitted independent services are province-specific. The national profile must not be interpreted as one uniform Canadian licence or scope.

## 8. Colombia — training and program-verification pathway

**Sources:**

- SENA Betowa — Asistencia Administrativa  
  https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/asistencia-administrativa?offertype=company&programId=179054
- Ministerio de Educación Nacional — SNIES information  
  https://www.mineducacion.gov.co/m/1757/w3-article-156293.html

Controlled findings:

- SENA’s current **Asistencia Administrativa** offering is a **Titulada**, **Técnico**, **2,208-hour** program, displayed for ages **14+**, with admission subject to available places and SENA selection procedures.
- It develops transferable administrative skills such as customer service, communication, document preparation/processing, information handling, and digital tools.
- This is an administrative foundation, **not a paralegal licence, law degree, or authorization to perform reserved legal services**.
- SNIES allows the public to verify registered higher-education institutions and programs and whether programs have the required `Registro Calificado` to be offered.

**Controlled pathway:** in Colombia, learners should verify legal/justice-related education through SNIES and treat SENA administrative training only as a possible foundation. They should also verify the employer’s required qualifications and the legal limits applicable to the actual role.

Controlled findings:

## 11. Responsible AI and confidentiality in legal work

**Source:** American Bar Association — Formal Opinion 512 generative-AI guidance  
https://www.americanbar.org/news/abanews/aba-news-archives/2024/07/aba-issues-first-ethics-guidance-ai-tools/

Controlled implications for this guide:

- Legal work using generative AI raises competence, confidentiality, communication, supervision, candor, and fee concerns.
- Paralegals/legal assistants must use employer-approved tools and preserve client/prospective-client confidentiality, privilege, work product, nonpublic case information, filing credentials, and internal information.
- Public/unapproved AI tools must not receive protected matter data.
- AI-generated legal research, authorities, citations, summaries, drafts, or factual assertions require authorized human review and source verification.

## 13. Market and automation control

BLS projects little or no net employment change for 2024–2034 and explicitly notes that AI and other technologies may make research and document preparation more efficient, potentially limiting demand.

**Career-planning implication:** Guide 19 should emphasize judgment, source verification, factual investigation, client communication, litigation/case workflow, specialized legal knowledge, confidentiality, e-discovery/document systems, and supervised use of technology rather than presenting document drafting alone as durable differentiation.

## Research gate decision

**PASS.** The evidence set is sufficient for controlled English reconstruction, with mandatory controls:

1. no independent-practice implication from the title “paralegal”;
2. distinguish ordinary paralegal employment from state limited-license programs;
3. distinguish education certificate, individual certification, ABA program approval, institutional accreditation, regulation, registration, and licensure;
4. preserve California’s distinct regulation as a state-specific exception rather than a national rule;
5. preserve province-specific Canadian licensing/scope cautions;
6. use OCUPACOL for Colombian occupation mapping but not its stale displayed salary figures as current 2026 wage evidence;
7. use SENA administrative training only as transferable foundation, not paralegal authorization;
8. require SNIES verification for Colombian higher-education programs before payment;
9. label private salary estimates clearly;
10. retain confidentiality, cybersecurity, AI-verification, supervision, and unauthorized-practice safeguards;
11. make no claim of independent legal review, professional translation certification, accessibility certification, accreditation, or guaranteed employment.
