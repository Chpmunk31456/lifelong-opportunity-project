## Controlled reconstruction scope

Guide 81 must be reconstructed around the real health-information lifecycle rather than generic medical-office work:

1. collect, abstract, classify, code, record, validate and retrieve health information;
2. support electronic health record (EHR), registry, database and health-information systems;
3. preserve completeness, accuracy, provenance and auditability of clinical/administrative records;
4. resolve unclear coding or documentation through authorized clinical/coding review rather than inventing diagnoses;
5. protect health-information confidentiality, privacy and cybersecurity;
6. understand disease/tumor/other registry work without claiming independent clinical authority;
7. distinguish health information technologist, medical registrar, medical records specialist, coder, health informatics specialist, database administrator, clinician, privacy officer and HIM manager responsibilities;
8. understand structured data, reporting, classification systems, data quality and system implementation concepts;
9. use AI, NLP, coding assistance and automation only under approved human review and data-governance controls;
10. preserve accessibility and safe handling of electronic and paper health records.

## High-risk authority boundaries

A health-information title does **not** automatically authorize a worker to:

- diagnose, treat, prescribe or independently interpret clinical findings;
- invent, alter or infer diagnoses to make a record easier to code;
- assign codes without the required documentation and applicable coding rules;
- alter a clinician's source documentation without an approved amendment/correction workflow;
- determine legal/privacy/regulatory compliance independently unless that role is formally assigned;
- disclose protected health information merely because technical access exists;
- change record-retention rules, legal holds or destruction schedules independently;
- design production security architecture without assigned authority;
- use real protected health information in a public portfolio or unapproved AI/cloud system;
- claim professional certification, registration, licensure, coding credentials or accreditation that has not actually been obtained.
