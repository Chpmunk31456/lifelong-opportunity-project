# Guide 50 — Current Source Evidence 02

**Guide:** 50 — Phlebotomy Technician  
**Branch:** `revision/guide-00-100-2026`  
**Research date:** August 19, 2026  
**Gate decision:** Research Helper — **PASS**

## Research purpose

This evidence set refreshes the occupation, entry pathways, compensation, safety boundaries, funding/training options, and regional context required before controlled reconstruction of the English Version 2 master. It is intentionally conservative because phlebotomy involves direct patient contact, blood exposure, sharps, specimen identity, and jurisdiction-specific healthcare rules.

The guide must remain career education. It must **not** function as a venipuncture procedure manual, substitute for supervised clinical training, replace employer protocols, or authorize any person to perform a regulated or employer-restricted clinical task.

## United States — occupation and outlook

### Occupation identity

O*NET OnLine classifies **Phlebotomists** as **31-9097.00**. Core work includes drawing blood, correctly identifying and labeling specimens, preparing specimens for testing, maintaining collection equipment, and safely disposing of contaminated sharps. O*NET also identifies a vetted U.S. Department of Labor Registered Apprenticeship occupational title for **Phlebotomist**, so apprenticeship is a legitimate work-based-learning pathway to search for where employers or sponsors actually offer it.

Official source:

- O*NET OnLine — Phlebotomists 31-9097.00: https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/31-9097.00

### Entry pathway

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics states that phlebotomists typically enter with a certificate from a postsecondary phlebotomy program, while some qualify with a high school diploma and on-the-job training. Employers may prefer candidates who hold professional certification. Version 2 must therefore avoid claiming that one national certification is legally required in every U.S. jurisdiction or setting.

Official source:

- U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook — Phlebotomists: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/phlebotomists.htm

### Official U.S. compensation and outlook

O*NET’s current national wage page uses **2025 Bureau of Labor Statistics wage data** and reports:

- median hourly wage: **USD $21.75**;
- median annual wage: **USD $45,230**;
- 10th percentile: **USD $17.20/hour / $35,780/year**;
- 90th percentile: **USD $28.26/hour / $58,780/year**.

O*NET/BLS also reports about **139,700 workers in 2024**, projected growth of approximately **5%–6% from 2024 to 2034**, and about **18,400 projected openings per year** from growth and replacement.

The BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook still displays May 2024 median pay of USD $43,660. Version 2 should use the newer 2025 BLS wage values surfaced by O*NET for the primary current wage statement, while citing BLS OOH for entry requirements and projections. The guide should never present national medians as guaranteed starting pay.

Official sources:

- O*NET national wages — Phlebotomists: https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/31-9097.00
- O*NET occupation detail — Phlebotomists: https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/31-9097.00
- BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook — Phlebotomists: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/phlebotomists.htm

## Canada — occupation, requirements, wages, and outlook

### Occupational grouping

Government of Canada Job Bank maps the title **phlebotomist** into **Medical laboratory assistants and related technical occupations, NOC 33101**. The Canadian occupational grouping is broader than the standalone U.S. phlebotomist title and includes pre-analysis/pre-testing tasks such as collecting blood and other samples, processing specimens, preparing reagents, routine laboratory tasks, and equipment setup/cleaning.

Official source:

- Government of Canada Job Bank — Phlebotomist summary: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/4220/ca

### Employment requirements

Job Bank states that medical laboratory assistants and related technical workers typically require completion of a **college certificate program in medical laboratory science** and that **certification by the Canadian Society for Medical Laboratory Science is usually required by employers**. Job Bank’s national record says this occupation is **not regulated in Canada** according to its current records.

Version 2 must preserve the distinction between an employer preference/requirement and statutory occupational regulation, and should instruct readers to verify the current provincial/territorial and employer rules where they intend to work.

Official source:

- Government of Canada Job Bank — Phlebotomist requirements: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/4220/ca

### Official Canadian wages

Job Bank’s current national wage page, updated November 19, 2025, reports approximately:

- **CAD $19.18/hour low**;
- **CAD $27.00/hour median**;
- **CAD $36.11/hour high**.

These figures apply to the broader NOC 33101 occupational group and are not guaranteed phlebotomist starting wages. Readers should use Job Bank location filters for current provincial, territorial, and regional figures.

Official source:

- Government of Canada Job Bank — Phlebotomist wages: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/4220/ca

### Outlook

Job Bank indicates that job prospects vary by province and territory. The controlled guide should avoid turning a national occupational signal into a promise of local hiring. Readers should verify the province/territory they are targeting.

Official source:

- Government of Canada Job Bank — Phlebotomist prospects: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/outlook-occupation/4220/ca

## Colombia — relevant training pathway and scope boundary

SENA Betowa currently lists **Toma de muestras para procesamiento en el laboratorio clínico**, a **48-hour**, in-person, complementary special course. Crucially, the current eligibility requirements state that the learner must be a **técnico o auxiliar en enfermería**, have a presentation letter issued by the requesting institution, and complete the selection process defined by the center.

This is therefore a relevant **upskilling pathway for eligible healthcare workers**, not a general beginner phlebotomy course open to anyone. Version 2 must not imply that completing this short course independently qualifies a person to practice phlebotomy or laboratory work outside current Colombian healthcare, employer, professional, and institutional requirements.

Official source:

- SENA Betowa — Toma de muestras para procesamiento en el laboratorio clínico: https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/toma-de-muestras-para-procesamiento-en-el-laboratorio-clinico?location=57073001&modality=P&offertype=company&programId=84606

For Colombia, readers should also verify current healthcare-service, biosafety, specimen-handling, scope-of-practice, employer, and credential rules before paying for training or accepting clinical duties. The guide is not legal or clinical-practice advice.

Version 2 should use OIT/Cinterfor as a **locator**, then direct readers to verify the current national training authority, healthcare credential rules, employer requirements, and clinical-practice boundaries in their own country.

## Infection control, sharps, and worker-safety boundary

Phlebotomy creates occupational exposure to blood and contaminated sharps. Version 2 must include a prominent safety boundary but must not reproduce operational clinical instructions that could encourage unsupervised practice.

The U.S. OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens standard, **29 CFR 1910.1030**, applies to occupational exposure to blood or other potentially infectious materials. It addresses engineering controls, work-practice controls, personal protective equipment, exposure control, training, and contaminated-sharps handling. OSHA also requires employers to evaluate and use appropriate engineering/work-practice controls to eliminate or minimize exposure.

Official sources:

- OSHA — Bloodborne Pathogens standard, 29 CFR 1910.1030: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1030
- OSHA — Bloodborne Pathogens: Evaluating and Controlling Exposure: https://www.osha.gov/bloodborne-pathogens/evaluating-controlling-exposure

CDC Standard Precautions apply to patient care and include hand hygiene, PPE based on anticipated exposure, safe injection/sharps practices, and appropriate equipment/environmental handling. The guide should instruct readers to learn and follow the current facility/employer protocols rather than providing a step-by-step collection procedure.

Official source:

- CDC — Standard Precautions for All Patient Care: https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/basics/standard-precautions.html

### Required Version 2 safety language

The controlled English master must clearly communicate that:

- blood collection and specimen handling require appropriate training, authorization, supervision, and employer/facility procedures;
- patient identification, specimen labeling, infection control, sharps safety, exposure response, and transport/storage rules are safety-critical;
- a reader must not practice venipuncture on themselves or another person merely from written or AI-generated instructions;
- AI must not replace approved protocols, clinical judgment, licensed supervision, laboratory information-system controls, patient-identification procedures, or emergency/exposure escalation;
- protected health information, patient identifiers, test orders/results, credentials, internal laboratory data, and employer-confidential information must not be pasted into unapproved public AI services;
- users should verify jurisdiction-specific certification, employer, laboratory, and healthcare-service rules before paying for training or accepting duties.

## Free/low-cost training, funding, and work-based learning

### United States

CareerOneStop, sponsored by the U.S. Department of Labor, provides training and credential locators. Its **WIOA-Eligible Training Program Finder** identifies state directories of programs that may be eligible for Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act training support. Eligibility is individual and local; WIOA funding is not guaranteed.

Official sources:

- CareerOneStop — WIOA-Eligible Training Program Finder: https://www.careeronestop.org/LocalHelp/EmploymentAndTraining/find-WIOA-training-programs.aspx
- CareerOneStop — Find Training: https://www.careeronestop.org/FindTraining/find-training.aspx

O*NET identifies Phlebotomist as a U.S. Registered Apprenticeship occupational title. Version 2 should recommend searching current **Apprenticeship.gov** opportunities while explicitly stating that availability varies by location, employer, sponsor, and time.

Official source:

- Apprenticeship.gov: https://www.apprenticeship.gov/

Employer support may include paid orientation, supervised on-the-job training, clinical placement, tuition assistance, uniforms/PPE, certification-exam reimbursement, or internal advancement. These benefits vary and should be verified in writing before enrollment or spending money.

### Canada

Because the Canadian pathway commonly involves a college certificate, learners should first compare public-college programs and verify whether a program/school is designated for student aid. The **Canada Student Financial Assistance Program** offers grants and loans through participating provinces and territories for eligible post-secondary students and qualified programs. Eligibility, school designation, amounts, and provincial/territorial rules vary.

Official sources:

- Government of Canada — Canada Student Grants and Loans: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/education/student-aid/grants-loans.html
- Government of Canada — Apply with your province or territory: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/education/student-aid/grants-loans/province-apply.html

Version 2 should avoid stating that every phlebotomy/medical-laboratory-assistant program qualifies for federal aid. The learner must verify the specific school, program, province/territory, and personal eligibility.

### Colombia

SENA should be checked before paying a private provider. The currently identified SENA sample-collection course is restricted to eligible nursing technical/assistant personnel and institutional referral, so it must not be presented as a universal free-entry pathway.

### Latin America and Caribbean

Use OIT/Cinterfor to identify current national vocational-training institutions, then verify country-specific public training, scholarships, employer programs, healthcare credential requirements, and eligibility.

## Training-provider and scam controls

Before paying for training, Version 2 should advise readers to verify:

1. whether local employers actually recognize or request the credential;
2. whether clinical practice hours or supervised placements are included and who is responsible for arranging them;
3. total tuition, supplies, uniforms/PPE, vaccination/health-screening, background-check, exam, recertification, and retesting costs;
4. refund and withdrawal terms;
5. whether the program is eligible for applicable public funding or student aid;
6. whether the provider makes unsupported job-placement, licensing, or salary guarantees; and
7. whether the program’s credential is distinct from any legally required license, employer certification preference, or laboratory/healthcare-facility authorization.

## Claims that Version 2 must avoid

Do **not** state or imply that:

- certification is legally mandatory everywhere in the United States;
- one short private phlebotomy course guarantees employment or authorization to draw blood;
- the SENA 48-hour course is an open-entry beginner pathway for the general public;
- the Canadian NOC 33101 grouping is identical in scope to the standalone U.S. phlebotomist occupation;
- Canadian CSMLS certification is the same thing as statutory nationwide regulation;
- national wage statistics are starting salaries or guaranteed compensation;
- written instructions or AI tools can substitute for clinical training, supervision, infection-control requirements, specimen-identification controls, or employer procedures;
- the controlled revision has independent human, clinical, legal, accreditation, or professional translation certification.
