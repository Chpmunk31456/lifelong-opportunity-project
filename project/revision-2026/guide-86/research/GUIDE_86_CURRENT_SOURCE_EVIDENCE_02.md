# Guide 86 — Current-source Evidence 02

**Guide:** 86 — Database Administrator  
**Controlled branch:** `revision/guide-00-100-2026`  
**Research review date:** 2026-08-22

## Research conclusion

The occupation has a strong direct three-country mapping and sufficient current-source evidence for controlled reconstruction.

- **United States:** O*NET-SOC **15-1242.00 — Database Administrators**.
- **Canada:** NOC **21223 — Database analysts and data administrators**, with Database Administrator (DBA) as a Job Bank title.
- **Colombia:** CUOC **25210 — Diseñadores y administradores de bases de datos**, explicitly including **Administrador de base de datos**.

The U.S. outlook must be presented candidly: total employment is projected to decline slightly from 2024 to 2034, while replacement and other turnover still produce thousands of annual openings. The guide must not turn the 3,800 annual openings into a claim of expanding total employment.

## United States — occupation definition and duties

O*NET 15-1242.00, updated 2026, defines Database Administrators as workers who administer, test and implement computer databases; coordinate changes; identify and resolve performance, capacity and scalability issues; and may plan, coordinate and implement security measures.

Current O*NET tasks directly support a guide centered on controlled production operations rather than unrestricted administrator authority. Core tasks include:

- modify existing databases and DBMS platforms or direct approved changes;
- plan, coordinate and implement security measures against accidental or unauthorized damage, modification or disclosure;
- plan and install DBMS upgrades to improve performance;
- specify users and access levels;
- test database/application/system changes;
- correct database or program errors;
- train users and support junior staff/clients;
- approve, schedule, plan and supervise installation/testing of new systems or databases where assigned.

O*NET also identifies detailed activities including database creation, performance assessment, security implementation, system testing, specification/model development and technical recommendations.

### Job Zone and education

O*NET classifies the occupation as **Job Zone Four — Considerable Preparation Needed**. It notes that most occupations in this zone require a four-year bachelor's degree, but some do not, and often require considerable related experience and/or vocational/on-the-job training.

O*NET education responses for new hires show:

- **89%** bachelor's degree;
- **4%** post-baccalaureate certificate;
- **3%** associate degree.

The guide must distinguish these occupation-level survey results from an absolute credential requirement for every individual DBA posting.

## Canada — NOC and requirements

Canada Job Bank maps Database Administrator (DBA) to **NOC 21223 — Database analysts and data administrators**.

Job Bank describes database analysts as designing, developing and administering data-management solutions, while data administrators develop and implement data-administration policies, standards and models.

Current employment requirements state that:

- a bachelor's degree or college program, usually in computer science, computer engineering or mathematics, is usually required;
- computer programming and related experience is usually required.

Job Bank currently reports that the occupation is **not regulated in Canada**. This does not mean employers cannot require specific technical experience, certifications, clearance, platform knowledge or internal authorization.

### Canada wages

National wages, updated November 19, 2025, are:

- low: **C$25.00/hour**;
- median: **C$40.87/hour**;
- high: **C$61.03/hour**.

Job Bank's wage page was modified August 7, 2026. These values apply to NOC 21223, not every possible database-related title.

## Colombia — CUOC 25210

Colombia has a direct mapping in **CUOC 25210 — Diseñadores y administradores de bases de datos**, competency level 4.

Official occupational denominations include:

- Administrador de base de datos;
- Administrador de datos;
- Analista de base de datos;
- Arquitecto de bases de datos;
- Data manager;
- Desarrollador de base de datos;
- Diseñador de bases de datos;
- Gerente de base de datos;
- Programador de base de datos.

The official description says these workers design, develop, administer, control, maintain and support optimal database performance, service operation and security.

Official functions include:

- designing/developing database architectures and data structures;
- building, modifying, integrating, commissioning and testing DBMS solutions;
- advising on database-management tools;
- creating policies/procedures for access and use;
- backup and data-recovery procedures;
- enforcing database security and integrity controls;
- administering data/network technology solutions;
- applying data-risk management policies;
- implementing and controlling information/database security;
- coordinating technical personnel where assigned.

OCUPACOL currently shows no available occupied-worker count for this profile. The guide must not invent a representative national Colombian DBA salary from non-representative or unavailable occupation-level indicators.

## Database integrity and operational-control requirements for the revised guide

The reader-facing guide must distinguish DBA skill from unrestricted production authority.

### Access and privileged administration

Required guidance:

- least privilege and role-based access;
- named/approved privileged accounts where policy requires;
- MFA and approved credential practices;
- no shared secrets in code, chat, tickets or personal storage;
- separation of duties where required;
- no curiosity access to production data;
- access reviews and prompt privilege removal;
- auditability of privileged activity.

### Changes, patches and migrations

The guide must require:

- approved change tickets/processes where applicable;
- tested scripts/migrations;
- peer review for high-impact changes where required;
- maintenance-window awareness;
- backup/restore or rollback readiness;
- validation before and after change;
- clear stop/escalation conditions;
- preservation of audit/change evidence.

A script running successfully is not proof that a production change is safe.

### Backup, restore and recovery

The guide must explain that a successful backup job is not the same as proven recoverability. Practical controls include:

- approved backup schedules and retention;
- protected/encrypted backup storage where required;
- restore testing;
- recovery procedures/runbooks;
- recovery objectives defined by the organization (including RPO/RTO concepts where used);
- replication/failover testing where applicable;
- disaster-recovery exercises;
- escalation when backup, replication or restore tests fail.

### Performance, capacity and availability

Core topics:

- monitoring;
- query/index performance;
- locks/contention;
- capacity and storage growth;
- connection/resource limits;
- replication lag;
- maintenance;
- high availability and failover;
- load/performance testing;
- evidence-based tuning rather than uncontrolled production experimentation.

### Privacy, retention and data governance

DBA access may expose highly sensitive customer, employee, financial, health, authentication or business data. The guide must require approved access/use, minimum necessary exposure, retention/deletion controls, secure exports, recipient verification and incident escalation. Query capability does not itself authorize use or disclosure.

### Cloud and managed databases

Cloud/managed services can shift some infrastructure tasks to a provider, but do not eliminate customer responsibilities such as:

- identity and access;
- network exposure/configuration;
- data classification;
- encryption/key choices where configurable;
- backup/retention settings;
- audit/logging configuration;
- secure application credentials;
- change governance;
- resilience design;
- cost/capacity monitoring;
- understanding provider/customer responsibility boundaries.

The guide must avoid implying that cloud automation removes the need for DBA judgment.

## Responsible AI and automation

Possible low-risk uses, when organizational policy allows, include:

- drafting SQL or administrative scripts;
- explaining an execution plan;
- drafting documentation/runbooks;
- proposing test cases;
- generating synthetic data;
- summarizing non-sensitive logs or public documentation;
- suggesting monitoring queries.

Required controls:

- never upload production data, credentials, connection strings, private schemas or protected logs to an unapproved AI tool;
- treat generated SQL/scripts as untrusted until reviewed and tested;
- do not let AI autonomously execute production changes outside approved controls;
- validate object names, scope, transaction behavior, permissions and rollback logic;
- human approval remains accountable;
- retain change/audit evidence where required;
- review recommendations for security, privacy, integrity, availability and cost impact.

NIST AI RMF and Generative AI Profile are voluntary risk-management guidance, not substitutes for organizational change/security governance.

## Accessibility and portfolio controls

The revised guide should promote:

- readable diagrams and runbooks;
- meaningful table/header structure;
- sufficient contrast;
- text alternatives for diagrams where needed;
- keyboard-accessible tools/documentation where supported;
- plain-language incident/change steps.

Portfolio work must use public, licensed or synthetic data. It must never contain production credentials, customer data, employer schemas, connection strings, private IPs, secrets, screenshots of protected consoles or proprietary backup/configuration details.
