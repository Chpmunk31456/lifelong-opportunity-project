## Domain-mapping control

The strongest content risk in this guide is false equivalence across compliance specialties. The English source explicitly prevents that by distinguishing generic support work from regulatory, financial, AML/KYC, privacy, cybersecurity, healthcare, safety, environmental, tax, licensing, and other specialist domains. Canada is intentionally shown with two different Job Bank mappings to demonstrate this issue rather than hide it.

## Colombia and Latin America control

No unsupported Colombia or Latin America salary is stated. No single SENA course is claimed to qualify a person for all compliance work. Readers are directed to current public training/employment sources and to match learning to the actual compliance domain.

## Claims intentionally not made

The guide does **not** claim:

- that a coordinator or assistant may independently provide legal advice;
- that any certification is universally required;
- that an employer will provide Section 127 assistance;
- that any specific training program is funded for the reader;
- that a generic compliance role maps to one Canadian NOC in all cases;
- that automated accessibility checking is certification;
- that AI may make final legal, regulatory, disciplinary, sanctions, safety, or investigation decisions; or
- that the guide received independent human, legal, professional translation, accreditation, or accessibility certification.
