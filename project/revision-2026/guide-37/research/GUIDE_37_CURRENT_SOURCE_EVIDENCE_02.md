## Occupation scope and current United States evidence

The controlled 2026 occupation anchor is U.S. Department of Labor O*NET **43-5071.00 — Shipping, Receiving, and Inventory Clerks**. O*NET describes the occupation as verifying and maintaining records for incoming and outgoing shipments involving inventory, arranging transportation, and preparing items for shipment. Reported titles include Shipping Clerk, Receiving Clerk, Shipping Coordinator, Receiving Coordinator, Shipper, Receiver, Materials Control Associate, Order Fulfillment Specialist, and Traffic Assistant.

Current O*NET task evidence supports duties such as checking shipment contents against manifests, invoices, or orders; preparing bills of lading and shipping orders; recording weights, charges, damages, shortages, or discrepancies; coordinating with carriers; and calculating shipping or storage charges. O*NET also identifies ERP, inventory-management, warehouse-management, EDI, spreadsheet, barcode-labeling, and related logistics software as relevant technologies, without making any single vendor platform universally mandatory.

O*NET classifies this occupation in Job Zone 1–2. Its 2026 profile says a high school diploma or GED is usually required, although employer requirements vary, and on-the-job training can range from a few days to one year.

## Canada pathway and wage evidence

Government of Canada Job Bank maps closely comparable work to **Shippers and receivers, NOC 14400**. Job Bank states that shippers and receivers ship, receive, and record the movement of parts, supplies, materials, equipment, and stock to and from establishments.

Current national requirements state that completion of secondary school is usually required; related clerical or warehouse experience may be required; and, depending on the job, a forklift certificate or driver's licence may be required. Job Bank currently records this occupation as not regulated nationally.

Job Bank's current national wage table reports **CAD $17.00 low, $22.50 median, and $31.00 high per hour**, based on 2023–2024 Labour Force Survey data and updated November 19, 2025. Local and provincial wages vary.

## Safety, privacy, cybersecurity, and responsible AI controls

Guide 37 should treat safety as employer- and task-specific. Shipping and receiving work can involve moving goods, forklifts, conveyors, loading areas, lifting, sharp packaging materials, vehicle traffic, and hazardous or regulated goods. Workers should follow employer procedures, PPE requirements, equipment authorization rules, hazard communication, and emergency procedures. The guide must not imply that a generic online course authorizes a worker to operate powered industrial equipment.

Shipping and inventory systems can contain customer names and addresses, supplier information, shipment values, account identifiers, customs or carrier documents, inventory levels, and other commercially sensitive information. The revised guide should instruct readers to follow approved access controls, multifactor authentication, phishing controls, file-sharing rules, and incident-reporting procedures.

AI tools may be used for low-risk learning or drafting only when employer policy allows. The guide should prohibit putting confidential manifests, customer or supplier data, tracking information, credentials, internal inventory data, security information, or proprietary documents into public AI systems without explicit authorization. AI-generated quantities, addresses, routing information, customs information, shipping classifications, rates, or compliance statements must be independently verified before operational use.
