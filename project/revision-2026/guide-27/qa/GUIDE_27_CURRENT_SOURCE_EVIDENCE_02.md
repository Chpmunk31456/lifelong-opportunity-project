## 1. United States occupation, work, preparation, pay, and outlook

**Primary source:** U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook, “Diesel Service Technicians and Mechanics.”  
https://www.bls.gov/ooh/installation-maintenance-and-repair/diesel-service-technicians-and-mechanics.htm

BLS identifies the occupation as technicians who inspect, repair, and overhaul buses, trucks, and other diesel-powered vehicles or engines. Modern work includes mechanical and electrical diagnosis, computer diagnostic equipment, exhaust/emissions systems, documentation, and repair communication.

Current BLS figures and pathway statements:

- 2024 employment: approximately **319,900** jobs.
- May 2024 median: **$60,640 per year / $29.15 per hour**.
- May 2024 lowest 10 percent: below **$41,670**; highest 10 percent: above **$85,980**.
- 2024–2034 projected employment change: **2%**, or about **7,800** net jobs.
- BLS projects about **26,500 openings per year on average** over the decade, primarily replacement openings.
- Typical entry-level education: **high school diploma or equivalent**; BLS describes **long-term on-the-job training** and notes employers may prefer postsecondary diesel-engine-repair programs.
- Experienced workers must continue learning new equipment and techniques; BLS specifically notes employer/manufacturer/vendor training.
- Employers may require or prefer industry certification, and some workers may need a commercial driver’s license to test-drive buses or large trucks. These requirements must be checked by state, employer, vehicle, and duty.

BLS also cautions that technicians lift heavy parts and tools, work in uncomfortable positions, and face sprains/cuts; well-ventilated shops are typical. The v2.0 guide should not present this as a low-physical-demand occupation.

## 2. Current non-government compensation estimate — keep separate from BLS

**Private-market source:** Indeed Career Explorer, “Diesel mechanic salary in United States.”  
https://www.indeed.com/career/diesel-mechanic/salaries

Indeed reported an average base salary of approximately **$30.32 per hour**, based on about **37,900 salary observations from job postings over the preceding 36 months**, updated July 20, 2026. Indeed also displayed a broad low/high range, illustrating substantial location and experience variation.

This is **not a government statistic** and must not replace BLS OEWS data. The English guide should label it as a current job-posting-derived market estimate and give BLS the primary compensation role.

Cross-check only: ZipRecruiter reported about **$58,798/year / $28.27/hour** as of July 29, 2026. Because methodology and job-title matching differ between commercial sites, the controlled guide should avoid averaging private estimates into an invented “true” wage.

## 3. U.S. training, workforce funding, scholarships, and employer support

https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/wioa/programs

DOL states that WIOA programs provide career and training services through the American Job Center network, including classroom and work-based learning. Eligibility and funding are individual and local; the guide must direct readers to the local American Job Center rather than promise payment.

**CareerOneStop — WIOA-Eligible Training Program Finder**

### Important apprenticeship correction

**Apprenticeship.gov Occupation Finder — Bus and Truck Mechanics and Diesel Engine Specialists, O*NET 49-3031.00**  
https://www.apprenticeship.gov/apprenticeship-occupations/listings?occupationCode=49-3031.00

As checked for this revision, Apprenticeship.gov states that this occupation is **not currently approved for use in a Registered Apprenticeship Program**. Therefore Guide 27 must **not** claim that a generic federal Registered Apprenticeship pathway presently exists for this exact occupation solely because diesel work is commonly learned on the job.

Readers may still find employer trainee programs, OJT, fleet/dealer training, related approved trades, or locally structured work-based learning. Those opportunities must be verified individually. WIOA explicitly supports several work-based training forms, including OJT and customized training, subject to program rules.

### Federal aid, grants, and scholarships

**Federal Student Aid — financial aid / scholarship guidance**  
https://studentaid.gov/articles/financial-aid-dictionary/  
https://studentaid.gov/articles/dont-miss-out-on-pell-grants/

### Employer education assistance

**IRS 2026 Section 127 update**  
https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-updates-frequently-asked-questions-about-section-127-educational-assistance-programs

For calendar years 2025 and 2026, qualifying employer educational assistance under a compliant Section 127 plan can generally be excluded from U.S. federal gross income up to **$5,250 per employee per year**. The employer must actually maintain an eligible written plan and determine covered expenses. The guide should tell readers to ask the employer/HR or benefits administrator rather than assume this benefit exists.

## 4. U.S. regulated-task and safety boundaries

### Motor-vehicle air-conditioning refrigerant service

**U.S. EPA — Section 609 Technician Training and Certification Programs**  
https://www.epa.gov/mvac/section-609-technician-training-and-certification-programs

EPA states that a person who repairs or services a motor vehicle air-conditioning system for consideration must be properly trained and certified under Clean Air Act Section 609 through an EPA-approved program. EPA also regulates service equipment and refrigerant practices. This applies when diesel technicians actually perform covered MVAC work; it is not a blanket diesel-mechanic license.

EPA also states that MVAC-like equipment is used in some farm and heavy-duty equipment. The guide should instruct readers to verify whether Section 609 or Section 608 rules apply to the exact equipment and service task rather than generalize.

### Diesel exhaust and shop exposures

**OSHA — Diesel Exhaust Standards**  
https://www.osha.gov/diesel-exhaust/standards

OSHA notes there is no single OSHA standard uniquely for diesel exhaust, while specific exhaust components and related hazards are addressed through applicable general-industry or other standards.

**NIOSH Pocket Guide — Diesel exhaust**  
https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/npg/npgd0207.html

NIOSH identifies inhalation and eye/skin contact as exposure routes and describes diesel exhaust as a potential occupational carcinogen. The controlled guide should emphasize ventilation/exhaust capture, avoiding unnecessary idling, following the employer’s hazard controls, and using appropriate PPE and procedures—not improvised respirator recommendations.

Noise, lifting, rotating machinery, stored energy, hot surfaces, batteries/electrical systems, fluids/chemicals, vehicle support/lifts, roadside work, welding, and power tools may add hazards depending on the shop and duties. Readers should follow employer procedures, equipment instructions, applicable OSHA/state-plan rules, and task-specific training.

### Emissions-system integrity

BLS expressly includes work on exhaust/emission-control systems. The guide should teach diagnosis and lawful repair to manufacturer/regulatory requirements and must not suggest emissions-control defeat, tampering, bypass, or disabling safety/environmental systems.

## 5. Canada — distinguish truck/transport from heavy equipment

Diesel work in Canada spans more than one occupational/trade classification. The v2.0 guide must tell readers to match the pathway to the equipment they intend to service.

### Truck and Transport Mechanic — NOC 72410

**Government of Canada Job Bank**  
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/14814/ca

Job Bank places truck and transport mechanic work within **NOC 72410** and reported a Canada median of approximately **C$29.89/hour**, with prospects varying by province/territory. Job Bank describes college/apprenticeship as a common pathway.

**Red Seal — Truck and Transport Mechanic**  
https://red-seal.ca/eng/trades/truck-transp-mech.shtml

The Red Seal program describes truck and transport mechanics as working on commercial trucks, buses, emergency vehicles and road-transport systems, including engines, brakes, electrical/electronic and high-voltage systems, HVAC, emissions and fuel systems. The trade is Red Seal-designated in multiple provinces and territories.

Job Bank indicates that a four-year truck-and-transport or truck-trailer apprenticeship, or an equivalent combination of experience and industry courses, is commonly required to become eligible for trade certification, and that compulsory/voluntary certification status varies by province. Readers must verify the current provincial/territorial apprenticeship authority before paying for training or assuming they may practise a regulated trade.

### Heavy-duty equipment mechanic — NOC 72401

**Government of Canada Job Bank**  
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/7497/ca

For heavy diesel engine/heavy equipment work, Job Bank maps the occupation to **NOC 72401 — Heavy-duty equipment mechanics**. As of the current source record it reported a Canada median of about **C$37.12/hour** and described college or apprenticeship as a usual pathway.

**Red Seal — Heavy Duty Equipment Technician**  
https://www.red-seal.ca/eng/trades/heavydutyequiptech/overview.shtml

Red Seal maintains an occupational standard for Heavy Duty Equipment Technician. The English guide should present this as a separate but adjacent pathway for diesel technicians focused on construction, mining, forestry, agricultural, material-handling, and similar mobile equipment.

Canadian wage figures must remain labeled in Canadian dollars and must not be converted into U.S. wage claims.

## 6. Colombia — SENA and public-employment pathways

### SENA technical training

**SENA Betowa — Mantenimiento de los motores diesel**  
https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/mantenimiento-de-los-motores-diesel?modality=P&programId=122854

SENA lists a **Técnico, Mantenimiento de los motores diesel**, with a stated duration of **2,208 hours**. Competencies include diagnosing and repairing diesel engines and fuel systems, preventive maintenance, workplace safety/environmental practices, technical procedures, digital tools, and a practical stage. Cohort locations and enrollment windows change; the guide must direct readers to current Betowa availability rather than promise a seat.

### SENA complementary training

**SENA Betowa — Mantenimiento preventivo a los motores diesel**  
https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/mantenimiento-preventivo-a-los-motores-diesel?modality=P&offertype=company&programId=145143

SENA also lists shorter complementary training in preventive diesel-engine maintenance. Current offerings have location, prerequisite, and enrollment-window constraints. These courses are useful as upskilling examples but must not be presented as equivalent to the full technician program.

### Agencia Pública de Empleo SENA

Current APE postings use the occupation title **Mecánico de motores diésel** and show duties such as diagnosis, preventive/corrective maintenance, fuel/injection systems, electrical and mechanical components. Example current public listing:  
https://ape.sena.edu.co/spe-web/spe/demanda/solicitud-sintesis/4119251

Job listings are time-limited evidence of market terminology, not guarantees of employment or permanent vacancies. The guide should direct Colombian readers to the current Agencia Pública de Empleo search and current SENA Betowa offerings.

SENA enrollment, training availability, admission, credentials, and employer requirements must be verified at the time of application.

## 7. Latin America pathway rule

“Diesel mechanic” does not have one transferable licensing, certification, or training rule across Latin America. English v2.0 and both localizations must instruct readers to verify, in their own country and locality:

1. the national or regional vocational-training authority;
2. public employment-service and employer trainee programs;
3. transport/heavy-vehicle licensing or inspection rules relevant to the actual duties;
4. occupational-safety and environmental/emissions rules;
5. whether refrigerant, electrical/high-voltage, welding, pressure-system, or other specialist tasks require separate authorization or training;
6. current scholarships/subsidies and admission windows.

Colombia’s SENA/APE pathway may be given as a concrete country example, but it must not be presented as governing Mexico, Brazil, Chile, Peru, Argentina, Central America, the Caribbean, or other jurisdictions.

## 8. Editorial implications for English v2.0

The controlled English reconstruction should therefore:

- keep BLS May 2024 median pay and 2024–2034 outlook as the primary U.S. statistical baseline;
- label Indeed July 2026 compensation explicitly as a non-government market estimate;
- distinguish truck/bus diesel service from heavy-mobile-equipment specialization;
- state that the exact U.S. Apprenticeship.gov occupation is not currently approved for a Registered Apprenticeship Program, while still showing WIOA OJT, employer trainee, manufacturer/dealer, and other verified work-based routes;
- prioritize free/low-cost and employer-supported routes before debt;
- include FAFSA/Pell/scholarship locators without promising eligibility;
- include Canada NOC 72410 and NOC 72401 pathways with provincial/territorial verification and Red Seal context;
- include current SENA diesel training and APE as Colombia pathways;
- define Section 609 only for covered paid MVAC service;
- strengthen diesel-exhaust, noise, lifting, electrical, vehicle-support, emissions-integrity, and roadside safety boundaries;
- make AI a verification/documentation/learning aid only: do not use generated instructions as a substitute for manufacturer procedures, service information, safety rules, diagnostic confirmation, or regulated-task training;
- prohibit entering customer VINs, diagnostic logs, telematics, personal data, proprietary fleet data, or credentials into public AI systems unless authorized and protected by policy;
- preserve accessible formatting, plain-language explanations, and alternatives for learners who need accommodations.
