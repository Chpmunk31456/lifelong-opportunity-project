**Status:** **PASS**

## Scope decision

Guide 52 is reconstructed around the U.S. occupation **Surgical Technologists (SOC/O*NET 29-2055.00)**. The guide must not imply that similarly named roles in Canada or Colombia are direct credential equivalents. Surgical technology is supervised clinical operating-room work with sterile-field, infection-prevention, patient-safety, and jurisdiction-specific credential boundaries.

## United States — occupation and work scope

### O*NET OnLine — Surgical Technologists
Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-2055.00

O*NET identifies Surgical Technologists as occupation **29-2055.00**, Bright Outlook, updated 2026. Surgical technologists assist in operations under the supervision of surgeons, registered nurses, or other surgical personnel. Representative tasks include operating-room setup, patient preparation/transport, adjusting lights and equipment, passing instruments and supplies, holding retractors, cutting sutures, and helping count sponges, needles, supplies, and instruments.

Editorial control: the guide must consistently frame these duties as supervised team functions rather than independent diagnosis, prescribing, anesthesia, surgical decision-making, or unsupervised invasive practice.

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Surgical Assistants and Technologists
Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/surgical-technologists.htm

BLS reports that surgical technologists typically need a certificate or associate's degree. Employers may require or prefer certification, and some states regulate these workers. BLS reports **115,600 surgical technologist jobs in 2024** and projects **120,800 in 2034**, a **4% increase** (5,200 jobs). Surgical assistants and technologists combined are projected to have about **8,700 openings per year** over 2024–2034.

Official U.S. wage control, May 2024:

- median annual wage for surgical technologists: **USD $62,830**;
- lowest 10 percent: below **USD $43,290**;
- highest 10 percent: above **USD $90,700**.

These are national occupational statistics, not guaranteed entry-level wages.

## U.S. education, accreditation, certification, apprenticeship, and funding

### Apprenticeship.gov — Healthcare
Source: https://www.apprenticeship.gov/apprenticeship-industries/healthcare

The U.S. Department of Labor lists **Surgical Technologist** among high-demand healthcare Registered Apprenticeship occupations and provides a competency-based occupational framework for Surgical Technologist / Operating Room Specialist. Registered Apprenticeship is therefore a valid work-based-learning pathway where an active employer-sponsored program exists; availability must be verified locally and must not be presented as universal.

### CareerOneStop — WIOA and Local Training Finder
Sources:
- https://www.careeronestop.org/LocalHelp/EmploymentAndTraining/find-WIOA-training-programs.aspx
- https://www.careeronestop.org/Toolkit/Training/find-local-training.aspx

CareerOneStop provides state WIOA-eligible training directories and a Local Training Finder. Current results include Surgical Technology/Technologist programs, some explicitly marked WIOA eligible. WIOA eligibility is program- and participant-specific; the guide must direct readers to an American Job Center/local workforce agency rather than promise funding.

### CAAHEP — accredited-program verification
Source: https://www.caahep.org/students/for-students

CAAHEP advises students to verify accreditation status and the certification/licensure requirements of the profession before enrolling. Surgical technology is among the health disciplines for which program accreditation can matter to subsequent credential eligibility.

### NBSTSA — Certified Surgical Technologist (CST) eligibility
Source: https://www.nbstsa.org/cst-eligibility

NBSTSA states that eligibility for the CST examination generally requires graduation from a CAAHEP- or ABHES-accredited surgical technology program, with separate pathways for qualifying military program graduates and specified legacy/accreditation circumstances. The guide may explain CST as one recognized credential path but must not describe it as universally required in every U.S. jurisdiction or job.

## Canada — materially different occupational mapping

### Government of Canada Job Bank — Operating Room Technician
Sources:
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/4380/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/4380/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/4380/ca

Job Bank maps **Operating Room Technician** to **Licensed practical nurses, NOC 32101**, not to a direct U.S.-style surgical-technologist occupation. Typical requirements include completion of an approved practical-nursing program, registration with a regulatory body in every province/territory, a registration examination, and additional academic training in operating-room techniques.

National wage data updated November 19, 2025 (reference period 2023–2024):

- low: **CAD $25.00/hour**;
- median: **CAD $31.32/hour**;
- high: **CAD $38.00/hour**.

Editorial control: do **not** suggest that a U.S. surgical-technology certificate automatically qualifies someone to work as an operating-room technician in Canada. Readers must verify the provincial/territorial nursing regulator and employer requirements.

Canada's federal skilled-trades apprenticeship supports are not automatically applicable to this healthcare/nursing pathway. General training assistance should be described conservatively through provincial/territorial education, employment, student-aid, employer tuition, and Job Bank resources unless a reader is separately enrolled in an eligible designated apprenticeship.

## Colombia — professional instrumentación quirúrgica is broader

### Ministerio del Trabajo — OCUPACOL
Source: https://ocupacol.mintrabajo.gov.co/Profile/OccupationalProfile/22402

OCUPACOL classifies **Instrumentadores quirúrgicos** as **CUOC 22402**, competence level 4, with occupational titles including *Instrumentador quirúrgico*, *Instrumentador quirúrgico Profesional*, and *Profesional en instrumentación quirúrgica*. The profile includes preparing and managing surgical instruments/equipment, technical assistance during surgery, asepsis/disinfection/sterilization, instrument control, sterilization-center functions, technology/quality activities, and support involving medical devices.

This scope is broader and more professionalized than a simple one-for-one U.S. surgical-technologist equivalence. The guide must not imply automatic credential transfer.

### Ministerio de Educación / SNIES framework
Sources:
- https://snies.mineducacion.gov.co/
- https://www.mineducacion.gov.co/CNA/1741/article-187352.html

Instrumentación Quirúrgica is recognized within Colombia's higher-education health field, and the profession is among those subject to specific professional regulation. Readers in Colombia should verify the current SNIES program status, Ministry of Education recognition, applicable professional registration/credential requirements, and employer rules before enrolling or practicing.

Editorial control: do not represent a short U.S. certificate, online-only course, or private certificate as equivalent to Colombian professional Instrumentación Quirúrgica.

## Latin America and Caribbean pathway

### OIT/Cinterfor
Source: https://www.oitcinterfor.org/

OIT/Cinterfor is the ILO's regional technical service for vocational training and maintains a network of vocational-training institutions across Latin America and the Caribbean. Because surgical/operating-room occupations and health-profession regulation differ materially by country, the guide should use OIT/Cinterfor as a regional locator and direct readers to each country's education, health, labor, and professional-regulatory authorities rather than asserting a single regional credential.

## Supplementary current non-government U.S. salary estimate

### Salary.com — Surgical Technologist
Source: https://www.salary.com/research/salary/standard/surgical-technologist-salary

As of **August 1, 2026**, Salary.com reports an estimated U.S. surgical technologist salary of approximately **USD $62,143/year ($30/hour)**, with a reported 25th–75th percentile range of about **USD $55,721–$68,991**.

This is a **non-government market estimate**, not BLS data, and methodology/title definitions differ. It must remain clearly separated from the official BLS May 2024 median of $62,830.

## Safety, privacy, and AI controls for the English reconstruction

The controlled Version 2 master must state that:

- surgical technologists work within employer, facility, supervisor, and jurisdictional scope;
- sterile technique, counts, specimen handling, infection prevention, sharps safety, positioning, and equipment processes must follow approved clinical protocols;
- training material or AI output must never replace required clinical instruction, competency validation, manufacturer instructions, facility policy, or direction from licensed/authorized surgical personnel;
- patient identifiers, images, operative details, protected health information, credentials, or confidential facility data must not be entered into public AI tools without explicit authorization and compliant safeguards;
- AI may assist with non-sensitive study aids, terminology explanations, practice questions, or general career planning, but generated clinical steps, device instructions, drug information, measurements, or citations must be independently verified before any real-world use;
- certification, licensing/registration, accreditation, and employer requirements vary by jurisdiction and must be checked before paying for training.
