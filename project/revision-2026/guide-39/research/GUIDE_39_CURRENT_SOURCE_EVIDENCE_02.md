# Guide 39 — Heavy Equipment Operator

**Current-source evidence record — 2026-08-13**  
**Gate:** Research Helper — PASS

## Research scope

This evidence set supports the controlled Version 2 reconstruction of Guide 39. The target is heavy construction-equipment operation. Adjacent occupations such as crane/tower operation, forklift/material-moving work, surface-mining equipment operation, and heavy-equipment mechanics must not be treated as equivalent unless explicitly labeled as adjacent.

## United States — occupation, work, wages, outlook

### O*NET / BLS occupational anchor

O*NET occupation **47-2073.00 — Operating Engineers and Other Construction Equipment Operators** is the principal U.S. occupational anchor. O*NET includes reported titles such as Heavy Equipment Operator and describes operation of construction equipment used for tasks such as grading, excavation, loading, and moving materials.

Current O*NET/BLS data report:

- 2025 national median wage: **$28.78/hour** and **$59,850/year**;
- 10th percentile: **$20.28/hour** and **$42,190/year**;
- 90th percentile: **$48.60/hour** and **$101,090/year**;
- 2024 employment: about **489,300** workers;
- projected 2024–2034 growth: **3%–4%**; and
- about **41,900 projected annual openings** over the projection period from growth and replacement needs.

These are national occupational statistics, not promised starting wages. Location, union status, project type, equipment class, shift, prevailing-wage rules, weather, experience, and employer can materially change pay.

Sources:
- https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/47-2073.00
- https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/47-2073.00?p=hourly

### Safety and training boundary

The controlled guide should emphasize that machine operation is safety-critical. Operators must follow employer/site procedures, equipment manuals, inspection requirements, traffic-control and signaling practices, PPE rules, utility-clearance procedures, and applicable occupational-safety regulations. The guide must not imply that a generic online certificate authorizes a person to operate equipment on every site or in every jurisdiction.

O*NET identifies safety-rule compliance, hazard avoidance, machine operation, and coordination through hand/audio signals among occupation-relevant work activities and tasks. Employer qualification and site-specific training remain essential.

Source:
- https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/47-2073.00

## United States — apprenticeship, public training support, free-first pathway

O*NET links the occupation to Registered Apprenticeship pathways, including Operating Engineer and equipment-specific operator titles. Apprenticeship availability depends on employer, sponsor, trade organization, location, and current openings. The guide should direct learners to Apprenticeship.gov rather than imply that an apprenticeship is universally available.

For eligible U.S. job seekers, CareerOneStop provides a **WIOA-Eligible Training Program Finder** and directs users to American Job Centers for eligibility and local service questions. WIOA support is conditional and is not guaranteed simply because a training program appears in a directory.

## Canada — occupation, requirements, wages

Government of Canada Job Bank places comparable work under **NOC 73400 — Heavy equipment operators**. The occupation includes heavy equipment used in construction and maintenance of roads, bridges, pipelines, tunnels, buildings and related work; apprentices are included in the unit group.

Current national requirements state that:

- some secondary-school education is typically required;
- a one- to two-year apprenticeship program, or relevant school/industry courses combined with on-the-job training, is typically required;
- trade certification for heavy equipment operator categories such as dozer, excavator and tractor-loader-backhoe is **compulsory in Quebec** and available but voluntary in several other provinces; and
- some employers may require internal company qualification/certification.

The controlled guide must therefore avoid describing Canada as having one universal national licensing rule.

Job Bank currently reports national wages of approximately:

- **CAD $24.00/hour low**;
- **CAD $32.50/hour median**; and
- **CAD $45.00/hour high**.

The Job Bank wage page states that these figures were updated November 19, 2025 and use Statistics Canada Labour Force Survey reference-period data for 2023–2024. Provincial/local values should be checked before making training or relocation decisions.

Sources:
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/15029/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/15029/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/wagereport/occupation/15029

## Canada — education funding

Canada Student Grants and Loans may support eligible learners in qualifying post-secondary programs. Grants generally do not need repayment; loans do. Eligibility and amounts depend on province or territory, income, family circumstances, program and designated-school status. Federal rules changed for some private for-profit schools beginning August 1, 2026, so the guide should tell learners to verify current school/program eligibility before relying on aid.

## Colombia — public vocational pathway

SENA currently lists **Operación de maquinaria pesada para excavación**, a presencial **Técnico** program of **2,208 hours**. The current Betowa listing indicates an 18+ minimum age and includes competency in operating excavation equipment according to technical manuals, environmental protection, occupational safety and health, and a practical stage. Individual cohorts, locations and enrollment windows change; the existence of the program does not guarantee a current seat in the learner's city.

The controlled guide should use SENA as a public/free-first training locator and tell readers to verify the current Betowa offer, cohort, location and admissions status before making plans.

Source:
- https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/operacion-de-maquinaria-pesada-para-excavacion?modality=P&offertype=company&programId=157615

**Link-control note:** program URLs and cohorts can change, so publication QA must verify the current Betowa path again before source freeze/publication.

Colombia's **Sistema Nacional de Cualificaciones** provides public pathways for training and recognition of prior learning. It should be presented as a locator/recognition framework, not as evidence that every heavy-equipment occupation has one mandatory national credential.

Source:
- https://cualificaciones.mintrabajo.gov.co/

## Latin America and the Caribbean — public-system locator

OIT/Cinterfor is the ILO's specialized vocational-training center for Latin America and the Caribbean and maintains a network of national and associated vocational-training institutions. It is suitable as a regional locator for public training systems when a country-specific heavy-equipment course has not yet been verified.

Examples in the network include SENA (Colombia), SENCE (Chile), INTECAP (Guatemala), INFOTEP (Dominican Republic), and INEFOP (Uruguay). Learners must verify the current program, cost, eligibility, recognition and enrollment conditions in their own country.

Sources:

- https://www.oitcinterfor.org/statsfp/instituciones

## Current non-government U.S. salary estimates — supplementary only

Private salary sources should remain visually and textually separated from official BLS/O*NET occupational statistics.

- **Indeed**, for the broader title *Equipment Operator*, reported an average U.S. base salary of about **$24.76/hour**, updated July 20, 2026. Because the title is broader than O*NET 47-2073.00, this is an adjacent-title market estimate, not an official occupational median.
- **Salary.com**, for *Heavy Equipment Operator*, reported an average of about **$82,368/year ($40/hour)** as of July 1, 2026, with a stated 25th–75th percentile range of about **$70,908–$94,649**.
- Salary.com's separate *Heavy Equipment Operator I* benchmark reported about **$45,277/year ($22/hour)** as of July 1, 2026, illustrating how private-title definitions and experience bands can produce materially different figures.

These estimates are not guaranteed earnings and should not replace official BLS/Job Bank data.

## Employer support and work-based learning controls

The Version 2 guide should explicitly tell readers to ask employers, unions, apprenticeship sponsors, contractors and public workforce systems about:

- paid onboarding and supervised equipment qualification;
- employer-paid safety training;
- apprenticeship or trainee pathways;
- cross-training across equipment types only when authorized;
- tuition assistance or reimbursement; and
- internal advancement from laborer, yard, maintenance-support or entry operator roles where appropriate.

Reimbursement conditions should be obtained in writing before spending money. No employer benefit, union access, apprenticeship seat or promotion should be presented as guaranteed.

## AI, privacy and cybersecurity boundary for reconstruction

AI can be discussed only as a secondary learning/admin aid—for example, explaining terminology, generating practice questions, or drafting non-sensitive training notes. It must not replace the equipment manual, pre-use inspection, lift/load/grade calculations, spotter/signaler instructions, utility-location procedures, safety rules, or qualified human supervision.

The guide should warn against uploading site plans, customer/project data, access credentials, incident details, proprietary drawings, geolocation-sensitive infrastructure data or other protected information into unapproved public AI systems.
