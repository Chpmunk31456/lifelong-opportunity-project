# Guide 25 — Current-source evidence 02

**Guide:** 25 — General Maintenance and Repair Worker and Building Maintenance Technician  
**Research date:** 2026-08-10  
**Stage:** Current-source research  
**Status:** **PASS for editorial intake**

This evidence register establishes the current factual floor for the English Version 2.0 revision. It does not by itself make the legacy guide publication-ready.

## 1. United States occupation, entry pathway, pay, and outlook

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Occupational Outlook Handbook

Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/installation-maintenance-and-repair/general-maintenance-and-repair-workers.htm

Verified current page, last modified April 24, 2026.

Use in Guide 25:

- Occupation: Maintenance and Repair Workers, General, SOC 49-9071.
- General maintenance and repair workers maintain and repair machines, mechanical equipment, and buildings.
- The occupation commonly covers general work that is not complex enough to require the specialized training of a licensed trade such as a plumber or electrician. The guide must preserve this scope boundary and direct readers to jurisdiction-specific licensing rules for regulated work.
- Typical entry-level education: high school diploma or equivalent, with skills commonly learned on the job.
- May 2024 U.S. median annual pay: **$48,620**.
- Employment: about **1,629,700** jobs in 2024, projected **1,692,100** in 2034.
- Projected growth 2024–2034: **4%**, with about **159,800 openings per year** on average over the decade.
- BLS specifically points readers to Apprenticeship.gov for apprenticeship opportunities and CareerOneStop for state licensing-board requirements and certifications.

These are national occupational statistics, not local, entry-level, or guaranteed wages.

## 3. U.S. free/low-cost training, workforce funding, and apprenticeship pathways

### Apprenticeship.gov

Source: https://www.apprenticeship.gov/apprenticeship-job-finder

The U.S. Department of Labor Apprenticeship Finder allows users to search open apprenticeship opportunities and registered apprenticeship partners. BLS also directs maintenance workers to Apprenticeship.gov. Availability varies by occupation and location; the guide must not imply an apprenticeship is guaranteed.

### Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act / American Job Centers

Sources:

- https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/wioa/programs
- https://www.careeronestop.org/LocalHelp/AmericanJobCenters/american-job-centers.aspx

The U.S. Department of Labor states that WIOA programs provide career and training services, including classroom and work-based learning, through the American Job Center network. CareerOneStop describes free employment assistance, training information, career counseling, skills testing, accessibility accommodations, supportive-service referrals, and other services. Eligibility and available funding vary locally.

Editorial rule: tell readers to ask their local American Job Center specifically about WIOA eligibility, Eligible Training Provider programs, work-based learning, supportive services, and local funding before paying for training.

### Employer education assistance

Existing Guide 25 reference intake includes IRS Section 127 employer educational assistance and federal education-credit sources. These must be rechecked at editorial time before any tax-dollar limit or eligibility statement is inserted. Employer reimbursement is never presumed; readers should verify policy, approval, grade/service requirements, repayment clauses, and tax treatment in writing.

## 4. U.S. safety and regulated-task boundary

### OSHA — Control of Hazardous Energy / Lockout-Tagout

Sources:

- https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147
- https://www.osha.gov/etools/hospitals/facilities-management/lockout-tagout

OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147 requires an energy-control program, procedures, training, and periodic inspections when servicing or maintenance may expose employees to unexpected energization, startup, or release of stored energy. The Guide 25 revision must direct workers to employer procedures and training rather than presenting lockout/tagout as a self-taught task.

The guide must distinguish general maintenance from work requiring a licensed or specially qualified electrician, plumber, HVAC/R technician, boiler operator, pesticide applicator, asbestos/lead professional, elevator technician, or other regulated specialist under applicable jurisdictional rules.

## 5. Canada occupational comparison

### Government of Canada Job Bank — NOC 73201

Source: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/wagereport/occupation/14386

Occupation: General building maintenance workers and building superintendents / building handyperson, **NOC 73201**.

Canada-wide hourly wages, updated November 19, 2025 using a 2023–2024 Labour Force Survey reference period:

- Low: **C$16.55/hour**
- Median: **C$25.00/hour**
- High: **C$38.46/hour**

Job Bank requirements guidance for NOC 73201 indicates secondary school is usually required, prior maintenance experience may be required, and building-superintendent roles may require a trade or building-operator certificate. Regulation/licensing can vary by province/territory and task.

Requirements example/source: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/14404/BC

Editorial rule: use Canada Job Bank as the official Canadian wage/requirements comparison and direct users to the province/territory licensing authority when regulated work is involved.

## 6. Colombia pathways

### SENA Betowa

Source: https://betowa.sena.edu.co/

SENA's official Betowa portal provides free technical, technological, complementary, virtual, and in-person training offers subject to availability and admission requirements. Search terms for Guide 25 should include `mantenimiento`, `mantenimiento locativo`, `mantenimiento preventivo`, `electricidad`, `plomería`, `construcción`, `refrigeración`, and related building-services skills rather than promising one permanent program title.

A current example in the SENA catalog is **Mantenimiento autónomo**, a 60-hour complementary course, showing that maintenance-related short training is available but varies by location and enrollment cycle.

### Servicio Público de Empleo / Agencia Pública de Empleo

Source: https://personas.serviciodeempleo.gov.co/

The Colombian Public Employment Service carries maintenance-related vacancies and links job seekers to authorized providers. A 2026 example for an `Ayudante de mecánica de mantenimiento` required six months of experience and referenced SENA/related technical maintenance preparation, illustrating that entry requirements vary by employer and subfield.

Editorial rule: direct readers to SENA/Betowa, the Agencia Pública de Empleo SENA, and the Servicio Público de Empleo for current training, vacancy, orientation, and employer-linked opportunities. Do not treat a single vacancy as representative wage evidence.

## 7. Latin America coverage

The Guide 25 regional section should use a locator-first approach because training titles, subsidies, licensing, and apprenticeship systems differ by country. Readers should be directed first to their country's public vocational-training institution, public employment service, labor ministry, municipal training programs, and employer/union training options. Colombia receives the concrete SENA/SPE pathway above; other Latin American country examples may be added only when their official current portals have been individually verified.

Do not imply that U.S. WIOA, Canadian programs, or Colombian SENA eligibility transfers across borders.

## 8. Funding and affordability editorial requirements

The revised guide must instruct readers to verify, before paying:

1. public workforce funding or vouchers;
2. free public vocational training;
3. registered apprenticeship or paid work-based learning;
4. employer-paid training, tuition reimbursement, tools/PPE support, or certification fees;
5. community-college grants/scholarships and recognized financial aid where applicable;
6. union or industry training centers where available;
7. repayment, employment-duration, tax, or reimbursement conditions in writing.

No scholarship, subsidy, apprenticeship seat, reimbursement, employment, credential, or wage is guaranteed.
