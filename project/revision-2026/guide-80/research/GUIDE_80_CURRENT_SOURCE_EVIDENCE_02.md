# Guide 80 — Current Source Evidence 02

**Occupation:** Data Entry and Records Specialist  
**Stage:** Current-source Research  
**Research date:** 2026-08-21  
**Controlled branch:** `revision/guide-00-100-2026`

## Research conclusion

Current evidence supports Guide 80 as a blended entry-level information-operations guide rather than a single perfectly matched occupation. The strongest controlled anchors are:

- United States: **O*NET-SOC 43-9021.00 — Data Entry Keyers**;
- Canada: **NOC 14111 — Data entry clerks**;
- Colombia data capture/digitalization: **CUOC 41321 — Grabadores de datos**;
- Colombia records/archive operations: **CUOC 44150 — Empleados de archivos**.

The title **Data Entry and Records Specialist** is therefore useful as a reader-facing umbrella, but the guide must state that data entry, document control, archives, records management, database administration, privacy governance, accounting, clinical records, legal records and formal records-management authority are not interchangeable roles.

## 1. United States — O*NET benchmark

O*NET identifies **43-9021.00 — Data Entry Keyers**, updated in 2026. Reported titles include Data Capture Specialist, Data Entry Clerk, Data Entry Operator, Data Entry Specialist, Data Transcriber and Records Clerk.

Current core tasks include:

- locate and correct data-entry errors or report them to supervisors;
- compile, sort and verify data before entry;
- compare entered data with source documents or re-enter data for verification;
- store completed documents in appropriate locations;
- enter source-document data using keyboards, scanners or other tools;
- maintain records/logs and document work.

This evidence strongly supports a guide built around source verification, controlled correction, accurate capture, storage and traceability—not speed alone.

Sources:
- https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/43-9021.00
- https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-9021.00

## 3. United States — automation and employment outlook

O*NET/BLS 2024–2034 projections report:

- employment in 2024: **141,600**;
- projected employment in 2034: **104,900**;
- projected change: **-26%**, a substantial decline;
- projected **annual** job openings: **9,500**.

The guide must communicate both facts together. Automation/OCR/workflow software can reduce routine manual entry, but the projected annual openings show continuing replacement and hiring needs. The guide should therefore prepare learners for hybrid work that combines capture with validation, exception handling, records/document workflows, customer/operations support, spreadsheets, databases and quality control.

## 6. Canada — NOC 14111

Canada Job Bank identifies **Data entry clerks — NOC 14111**. The occupation inputs coded, statistical, financial and other information into computerized databases, spreadsheets or other templates using keyboards, mice, optical scanners, speech-recognition software or other data-entry tools.

Job Bank says the occupation usually requires a secondary school diploma or several weeks of on-the-job training; its detailed requirements state that completion of secondary school may be required and college or other data-entry courses may be required. Current Job Bank records say the occupation is **not regulated in Canada**.

## 9. Colombia — CUOC 41321 data capture/digitalization

OCUPACOL identifies **CUOC 41321 — Grabadores de datos**, competency level 2. The description and functions align closely with the data-entry side of Guide 80:

- receive and prepare invoices, forms, records and other source documents for capture;
- digitalize documents and transcribe numeric data, codes and text;
- verify accuracy, completeness and image quality and correct entered data when appropriate;
- store generated digital files using recommended preservation media;
- import/export data between systems and database software;
- index images;
- register coded, statistical and financial data in established databases/systems.

Occupational denominations include **Auxiliar de entrada de datos, Digitalizador, Operador de entrada de datos, Procesador de datos, Registrador de datos, Tabulador de datos** and **Técnico de Introducción de datos**.

OCUPACOL displays a derived salary range of **COP 1,002,500–2,209,640**, but the same page explicitly warns that the data **do not have statistical representativeness under the applied methodology**. The controlled guide will therefore **not** use this range as a representative current salary benchmark.

- https://ocupacol.mintrabajo.gov.co/Profile/OccupationalProfile/41321

## 14. Data integrity and records-control boundary

The guide must teach that an accurate record is not merely a clean-looking record. Workers should:

- preserve the relationship between entered data and the authorized source;
- distinguish formatting correction from substantive factual correction;
- record/escalate discrepancies under procedure;
- avoid silent overwrites that destroy auditability;
- use required reason codes, comments, approvals or change logs;
- never fabricate, backdate or suppress data;
- never delete or destroy records merely because they appear obsolete;
- follow assigned retention, disposition, legal-hold, archive and transfer rules rather than inventing them.

The role can execute approved records procedures, but it does not automatically own legal retention policy, privacy interpretation, clinical diagnosis, accounting judgment or security authorization.

## 15. Privacy and cybersecurity boundary

Data-entry and records workers may handle identifiers, financial information, health information, employee files, legal documents, customer records, credentials, contracts, scans and internal business data. The controlled guide should require:

- authorized systems and approved storage locations;
- least-necessary access;
- strong account-security practices and MFA where supported;
- careful recipient verification before transmitting records;
- phishing awareness;
- screen/workstation privacy in shared environments;
- secure handling of exported spreadsheets and downloads;
- prompt reporting of misdirected records, malware, suspected account compromise or unauthorized access;
- no real confidential records in public portfolios, unapproved cloud tools or unapproved AI systems.

The worker follows organizational controls; the guide must not imply independent authority to define privacy law or cybersecurity architecture.

Reference:
- https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world

## 16. OCR, automation and responsible AI

OCR, import tools, spreadsheet functions, validation rules, scripts and AI can reduce repetitive work but can also introduce systematic errors at scale. For controlled records:

- compare uncertain OCR output with the source;
- validate field mapping before bulk import;
- use test/sandbox data where practical;
- reconcile record counts and exceptions;
- sample-check automated output according to policy;
- do not invent missing values;
- preserve required audit logs;
- use only approved AI services for approved data;
- verify AI-generated classifications, summaries, normalization suggestions or extracted fields before acceptance.

NIST AI RMF is voluntary risk-management guidance and the Generative AI Profile addresses risks specific to generative AI. These sources support a human-verification and governed-use boundary, not an assertion that AI use is mandatory.

Sources:
- https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
- https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence

## 17. Accessibility and sustainable work practice

Data-entry systems and training should be usable with appropriate accessibility features and accommodations. The guide should encourage keyboard-accessible workflows, readable interfaces, accessible electronic documents where relevant, assistive technology where authorized, and reasonable workstation/ergonomic practices. Accessibility or ergonomic guidance must remain educational and must not be represented as individualized medical advice or legal certification.

Reference:
- https://www.section508.gov/create/

## 18. Scam boundary

“Remote data entry” is frequently used as a generic lure in employment scams. The guide should teach readers to verify the employer and domain, distrust requests for upfront fees, gift cards, cryptocurrency, check-overpayment schemes or purchases from a recruiter-selected vendor, avoid sending sensitive identity/banking data before legitimate hiring/HR steps, and independently verify suspicious offers.

This anti-scam section is occupationally important because low-barrier remote-job wording can attract impersonation and advance-fee fraud.

## Research gate decision

The evidence is current enough, occupation-specific and internally coherent to support reconstruction. The guide must be candid about the **-26% U.S. projected employment change** while also preserving the **9,500 projected annual openings** and emphasizing progression toward broader records/data-quality/document-control work.

**Research stage:** **PASS**.  
**Blockers:** none.
