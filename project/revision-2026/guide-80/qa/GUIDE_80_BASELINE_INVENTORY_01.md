Legacy availability is evidence of a prior publication baseline only. It does not establish current labour-market accuracy, exact translation parity, current link validity, accessibility conformance, or completion of the 2026 controlled revision gates.

## Controlled reconstruction scope

Guide 80 will be rebuilt around the real work lifecycle rather than typing speed alone:

1. receive and identify the authoritative source record;
2. prepare, scan, transcribe, import, or capture data through approved tools;
3. validate completeness, format, identity, and source-to-entry accuracy;
4. resolve or escalate discrepancies rather than guessing;
5. classify, index, name, store, and retrieve records under assigned procedures;
6. preserve auditability, permissions, and chain of custody where relevant;
7. protect confidential, personal, financial, health, employee, customer, legal, and proprietary information;
8. follow approved retention, transfer, archival, correction, and destruction rules;
9. use OCR, automation, spreadsheets, databases, and AI only with verification and authorized data handling;
10. maintain ergonomic, accessible, and sustainable work practices.

## Occupational-mapping requirement

The title combines two related but distinct work families. Current-source Research must therefore test and document more than one occupational comparison where needed rather than pretending a single code fully covers the title.

Expected mapping questions include:

- United States data-entry benchmark and overlap with records-clerk duties;
- Canada data-entry-clerk benchmark;
- Colombia data-entry/digitization benchmark;
- Colombia archive/records benchmark;
- whether records-management authority, archival appraisal, privacy decisions, accounting, clinical interpretation, legal conclusions, or database administration sit outside the entry-level role.

## Key authority boundaries to preserve

A Data Entry and Records Specialist does not automatically have authority to:

- alter a source record to make it appear correct;
- fabricate, backdate, delete, or suppress records;
- decide retention/destruction periods independently;
- disclose records merely because the worker can technically access them;
- override segregation-of-duties or approval controls;
- make accounting, medical, legal, HR, compliance, privacy, or security determinations outside assigned authority;
- correct a substantive source fact without the approved owner/process;
- treat OCR, spreadsheet formulas, scripts, or AI output as authoritative without validation;
- use real confidential data in an unauthorized training, portfolio, or AI tool;
- claim professional records-management, privacy, cybersecurity, accessibility, legal, or regulatory certification unless separately obtained.
