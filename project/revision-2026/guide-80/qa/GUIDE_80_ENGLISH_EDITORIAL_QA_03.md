## Editorial review scope

The controlled English master was reviewed for occupational fit, natural readability, internal logic, current-source framing, plain-language accessibility, automation honesty, professional boundaries, practical usefulness and publication readiness before source freeze.

## Occupation fit — PASS

The master does not reduce the role to typing. It consistently covers the combined occupation as:

- data capture and import;
- source-to-entry validation;
- discrepancy handling;
- scanning/OCR and digitization;
- indexing/classification;
- records retrieval and transmission;
- access/audit controls;
- assigned retention/disposition processes;
- spreadsheets and database fundamentals;
- privacy/cybersecurity;
- responsible automation and AI.

The text explicitly distinguishes Data Entry Specialist, Records Clerk/Specialist, Document Controller, and higher-authority Records Manager/Archivist/Privacy Officer/DBA/Data Steward roles.

## Authority and safety boundaries — PASS

The guide clearly prevents common overreach. It does not authorize the reader to:

- invent or rewrite source facts;
- backdate/falsify/suppress records;
- decide retention/destruction rules independently;
- remove legal holds;
- disclose restricted data merely because access exists;
- override segregation-of-duties controls;
- make accounting/clinical/legal/HR/privacy/security decisions outside assigned authority;
- place confidential data in public portfolios or unapproved AI tools.

The distinction among transcription error, source ambiguity and substantive source error is especially clear and operationally useful.

## Automation/outlook framing — PASS

The master states the U.S. **-26% projected employment change** and does not soften it into an unsupported optimistic claim. It also preserves the **9,500 projected annual openings** and explains the difference between declining total employment and continuing replacement/opening activity.

The career strategy is framed around progression toward validation, records operations, document control, quality, spreadsheets/databases and broader information operations.
