# Guide 21 — Current source evidence 02

**Research date:** 2026-08-10  
**Stage:** Current-source research

## Research boundary

This register supports the controlled 2026 revision of Guide 21. It distinguishes occupational research and public-record support from legal opinions, title-insurance underwriting, conveyancing, closing authority, official record alteration, or professional licensure. Requirements vary by jurisdiction and employer and must be checked before a learner pays for training or accepts regulated work.

## United States — occupation, work, education, and income

### O*NET / U.S. Department of Labor

**Source:** O*NET OnLine, occupation 23-2093.00, *Title Examiners, Abstractors, and Searchers*  
**URL:** https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/23-2093.00  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

Current O*NET content identifies the occupation as searching real-estate records, examining titles, and summarizing pertinent legal or insurance records. Controlled examples include mortgages, liens, judgments, easements, legal descriptions, ownership, restrictions, recorded instruments, and title-related reports. O*NET also shows that entry requirements are not uniform: respondents report high-school-equivalent, postsecondary-certificate, and bachelor's-level entry paths for different jobs. The guide must therefore avoid presenting one credential as a universal national requirement.

O*NET's current workforce panel reports 2025 U.S. median wages of **$28.20/hour and $58,650/year**, with a 10th-to-90th percentile annual range of **$38,170 to $90,830**. It reports 57,400 workers in 2024, projected 2024–2034 growth of 1%–2%, and about 5,400 projected openings over that period. These are occupation-level government estimates, not guaranteed individual earnings.

O*NET also links learners to Apprenticeship.gov for related apprenticeship opportunities. The guide may recommend checking the official apprenticeship finder, but it must not claim that a title-examiner apprenticeship is available in every state or at every time.

## United States — current non-government income estimates

Private salary sites are supplementary estimates, not official wage statistics. Their methodologies and samples differ, so the guide must label source, date, and scope and must not average them into a false single authoritative wage.

- **Indeed — Title Examiner:** **$60,541/year average base salary**, based on 184 salary observations from job postings over the prior 36 months; page states updated **July 17, 2026**. Verified 2026-08-10. URL: https://www.indeed.com/career/title-examiner/salaries
- **ZipRecruiter — Title Examiner:** **$51,098/year average**, approximately **$24.57/hour**, stated **as of July 27, 2026**; reported central range includes approximately $38,000–$59,500 for the 25th–75th percentiles. Verified 2026-08-10. URL: https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Salaries/Title-Examiner-Salary

The gap between the official median and private averages is itself a useful learner warning: title, geography, experience, employer, data window, and methodology materially affect estimates.

## United States — licensing and insurance boundary

**Source:** National Association of Insurance Commissioners, Producer Licensing  
**URL:** https://content.naic.org/insurance-topics/producer-licensing  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

NAIC states that people who sell, solicit, or negotiate insurance in the United States must be licensed as insurance producers and that licensing is administered by state insurance regulators. The NAIC State Licensing Handbook separately includes a chapter for title-insurance agents. Therefore, a learner who performs title research or examination must not assume that the occupational title itself grants authority to sell, solicit, negotiate, issue, or underwrite title insurance. State-specific regulator requirements control regulated insurance activity.

**Supplemental source:** https://content.naic.org/state_licensing_handbook.htm

## United States — privacy and information security

**Source:** Federal Trade Commission, Safeguards Rule  
**URL:** https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/safeguards-rule  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

The FTC Safeguards Rule requires covered financial institutions under FTC jurisdiction to maintain safeguards for customer information. The FTC's business guidance explains that coverage depends on the activities an organization performs, not merely its business label, and that covered customer information includes nonpublic personal information handled by or for a covered financial institution.

Guide 21 should therefore teach a conservative rule: public-record availability does not mean all transaction, customer, lender, escrow, identity, or financial data may be copied, republished, uploaded into public AI systems, or retained without authorization. Workers must follow employer policy, applicable privacy/security requirements, contractual restrictions, and data-minimization practices.

**Supplemental FTC guidance:** https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftc-safeguards-rule-what-your-business-needs-know

## Canada — occupational mapping and income

### Job Bank

**Source:** Government of Canada Job Bank, *Title Searcher in Canada*  
**Wages URL:** https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/16033/ca  
**Requirements URL:** https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/16033/ca  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

Job Bank maps title searcher / land-titles-examiner searches to **NOC 42200, Paralegals and related occupations**. Its national wage page reports, for the current displayed reference, **CAD $21.63/hour low, $33.05/hour median, and $52.19/hour high**, updated November 19, 2025 from Statistics Canada reference-period data. Job Bank also reports that requirements vary within NOC 42200 and may include legal/paralegal education, industry-sponsored courses, experience, and licensing for advanced legal services.

This mapping is useful but broad. The guide must not imply that every Canadian land-title examination position is identical to an independent paralegal role or that NOC classification alone authorizes legal services.

Job Bank also reports regional variation and currently lists land-titles-examiner opportunities under the broader NOC. Learners should verify the province, employer, duties, and any provincial legal-services or land-registration requirements before relying on a generic national profile.

## Colombia — property-registration pathway and authority boundary

### Superintendencia de Notariado y Registro (SNR)

**Primary service source:** https://www.supernotariado.gov.co/atencion-servicios-ciudadania/tramites-y-servicios/  
**Legal framework index:** https://www.supernotariado.gov.co/transparencia/leyes/  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

The SNR identifies the **Certificado de Tradición y Libertad** as the official service used to know the registered legal situation and history of real property. Its legal-framework page lists **Ley 1579 de 2012 — Estatuto de Registro de Instrumentos Públicos**. The SNR also operates the public registration system and Offices of Public Instruments Registration.

The Colombia pathway must therefore be described as jurisdiction-specific property-record and document-research work, not as a direct equivalent of a U.S. title-insurance examiner. Learners should use SNR/VUR and other authoritative Colombian systems for official records and should not treat a privately prepared summary, AI output, screenshot, or copied database entry as a substitute for an official certificate or registrar action.

## Funding, scholarships, employer support, and training-cost controls

### United States

**CareerOneStop / U.S. Department of Labor:** https://www.careeronestop.org/LocalHelp/EmploymentAndTraining/find-WIOA-training-programs.aspx  
CareerOneStop states that eligible people may use WIOA training services with eligible providers and directs users to American Job Centers for eligibility. The guide may recommend checking WIOA-eligible legal studies, real-estate, records, GIS, document-management, or related programs locally, but must not state that any specific program is funded until the learner verifies current eligibility and provider status.

Employer-paid training should be checked before self-funding because title companies, law firms, lenders, local governments, registries, and real-estate service employers may train workers on proprietary title plants, recording systems, closing software, GIS, document standards, and local procedures. Employer assistance is an option to investigate, not a guaranteed benefit.

### Canada and Latin America

Training and funding mechanisms vary by province/country. The guide should prioritize public employment services, public colleges/technical institutions, employer training, and official scholarship or workforce programs before commercial bootcamps. In Colombia, SENA is a primary free public-skills source to check for records, document-management, administrative, digital, customer-service, and related offerings.

## AI use — controlled safety position

AI may assist with plain-language explanations, checklists, document organization, drafting non-authoritative summaries, study plans, and practice exercises. It must not be presented as an authoritative source of ownership, lien priority, legal description, marketability, record status, underwriting decision, closing instruction, or registrar action.

High-impact facts must be verified against authoritative records and reviewed by the appropriately authorized professional or official where the task requires one. Sensitive or nonpublic customer/property information should not be uploaded to external AI tools unless the organization has explicitly authorized that use and applicable privacy, confidentiality, security, contractual, and retention requirements are satisfied.
