## Occupational-risk classification

Guide 21 has elevated legal/property/financial-data risk. Title examination and property-record research can involve chains of title, deeds, mortgages, liens, judgments, easements, restrictions, legal descriptions, tax records, probate or court records, recorded instruments, title plants, geographic/property identifiers, and nonpublic transaction information.

The 2026 revision must fail closed on these distinctions:

- factual public-record research versus legal opinion on ownership or marketable title;
- title examination/search support versus title-insurance underwriting authority;
- research/report preparation versus attorney, notary, registrar, recorder, escrow, settlement, conveyancing, or closing authority;
- occupational titles versus jurisdiction-specific professional or insurance licensing;
- public-record availability versus permission to access, disclose, aggregate, retain, or repurpose personal/nonpublic data;
- record interpretation versus unauthorized alteration of official land/property records;
- U.S., Canadian, Colombian, and other Latin American land-registration systems are not interchangeable;
- AI-generated ownership, lien, legal-description, priority, or title conclusions require authoritative record verification and authorized professional review.
