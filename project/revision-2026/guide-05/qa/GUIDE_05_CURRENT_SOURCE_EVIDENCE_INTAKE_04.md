# Guide 05 — Current-Source Evidence Intake 04

Date: 2026-08-08
Branch: `revision/guide-00-100-2026`
Guide: 05 — Bookkeeping and Payroll Specialist

## Gate purpose

Establish a current, role-specific evidence base before constructing the 2026 English working master. Bookkeeping and payroll are related but distinct labor-market categories; this gate keeps their official and market data separate. It does not claim independent human certification, accreditation, legal or tax review, or publication approval.

## United States — official occupational evidence

### Bookkeeping

U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Bookkeeping, Accounting, and Auditing Clerks (SOC 43-3031):
https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/bookkeeping-accounting-and-auditing-clerks.htm

Current OOH data report a May 2024 median annual wage of **$49,210**, with the lowest 10 percent below **$34,600** and highest 10 percent above **$72,660**. Employment is projected to decline **6% from 2024 to 2034**, while about **170,000 openings per year** are projected on average because of replacement needs. BLS explicitly notes that software automation reduces routine entry work and shifts remaining work toward analysis and advisory support.

### Payroll

U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Financial Clerks:
https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/financial-clerks.htm

BLS reports a May 2024 median annual wage of **$55,290** for Payroll and Timekeeping Clerks. This is the appropriate official U.S. payroll occupational wage reference in the guide; it must not be blended with the bookkeeping median into a single official salary.

## Education, credentials, funding, and employer support — United States

Entry requirements vary. BLS states that some bookkeeping employers require or prefer college coursework or a degree while others hire high-school graduates and train on the job. The guide must not represent a private certification as legally mandatory when it is only optional or employer-preferred.

PayrollOrg provides two industry credentials that may be useful depending on role and experience:
- Fundamental Payroll Certification (FPC): https://payroll.org/education-certification/education/learning-paths/fpc-certification
- Certified Payroll Professional (CPP): https://payroll.org/education-certification/education/learning-paths/cpp-certification

PayrollOrg describes FPC as a fundamental payroll credential and CPP as an advanced credential. Its current exam-preparation information states that FPC has no payroll experience requirement, while CPP requires one of the organization’s eligibility paths. These are industry credentials, not government licenses.

Free-first and funding routes to check before paying include American Job Centers/WIOA services, public community colleges, libraries, employer training, Federal Student Aid eligibility for qualifying institutions/programs, scholarships and grants, and registered apprenticeship or other work-based-learning opportunities where actually available. Apprenticeship status must be verified through Apprenticeship.gov or the responsible state/federal registration authority; the guide must not imply that a bookkeeping or payroll Registered Apprenticeship exists everywhere.

Employer educational assistance must be described from the written employer plan rather than a generic service-period assumption. IRS April 2026 Section 127 guidance states that up to **$5,250** of qualifying educational assistance can be excluded from gross income for calendar years 2025 and 2026 under a qualifying written employer program. Readers must verify the employer’s actual plan, covered expenses, repayment terms, tax treatment, and any service obligation.

## Canada — separate official occupational mappings

### Bookkeeping

Government of Canada Job Bank maps bookkeeper/accounting technician work to **Accounting technicians and bookkeepers, NOC 12200**.

- Summary: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/12564/ca
- Wages: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/wagereport/occupation/24500

National wage table reviewed for this gate: **C$19.55 low / C$28.02 median / C$45.07 high per hour**. Job Bank states that the wage data were updated **November 19, 2025**, with a **2023–2024 reference period** from the Statistics Canada Labour Force Survey. Job Bank indicates that employment requirements typically include secondary school plus accounting/bookkeeping education or a combination of courses and experience; readers must still verify exact employer and provincial requirements.

### Payroll

Government of Canada Job Bank maps payroll administrator work to **NOC 13102**.

- Summary: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/25792/ca
- Wages: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/wagereport/occupation/1636

National wage table reviewed for this gate: **C$20.00 low / C$30.00 median / C$43.27 high per hour**. Job Bank states that these wages were updated **November 19, 2025**, with a **2023–2024 reference period** from the Statistics Canada Labour Force Survey. The summary page was modified **June 2, 2026** and states that requirements usually involve post-secondary/apprenticeship-level preparation or more than six months of on-the-job training. The guide must present this as the Canadian NOC profile, not as a universal credential rule.

## Latin America

Titles, tax/payroll rules, social-security systems, employer obligations, credentials, and software differ by country. A U.S., Canadian, or Colombian payroll credential or course does not automatically transfer. Readers should verify the exact local employer title, tax/social-security authority, labor rules, data-protection requirements, software expectations, and recognized education before paying or claiming qualification.

## Safety, privacy, and scope controls for the revised master

The 2026 working master must:
- separate bookkeeping work from payroll work where wages, credentials, and rules differ;
- prohibit altering, backdating, inventing, concealing, or destroying records;
- require escalation for unexplained differences, unauthorized bank/payee changes, payroll exceptions, suspected fraud, and unclear authority;
- avoid individualized tax, accounting, benefits, legal, or financial advice beyond the worker’s qualifications;
- protect payroll, banking, tax, identity, wage, benefits and employee data;
- require employer-approved systems and prohibit putting confidential records into public AI services without authorization;
- emphasize reconciliation, controls, exception handling, audit trails and judgment as automation changes routine data-entry work.
