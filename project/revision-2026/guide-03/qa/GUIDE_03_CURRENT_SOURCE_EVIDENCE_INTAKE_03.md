## United States — occupation, pay, outlook, and entry preparation

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — official occupational evidence

Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/medical-records-and-health-information-technicians.htm

BLS Medical Records Specialists (SOC 29-2072) is the strongest official U.S. occupational anchor for the coding portion of this guide because BLS explicitly describes workers who review records, assign clinical codes, maintain information for insurance reimbursement and data analysis, and protect confidentiality. The category is broader than the exact combined title “Medical Billing and Coding Specialist,” so the guide must label it as an **official occupational anchor/proxy rather than an exact-title wage series**.

Current BLS facts to preserve with that qualification:

- May 2024 median annual wage: **US$50,250** (**US$24.16/hour**).
- 2024 employment: approximately **194,800**.
- Projected 2024–2034 growth: **7%**.
- Projected average openings: approximately **14,200 per year** over the decade.
- Typical entry education: postsecondary nondegree award, while some workers qualify with a high school diploma and others need an associate or bachelor’s degree.
- BLS notes that certification may be required or preferred depending on the employer.
- BLS also notes that AI-powered coding tools may affect demand; the guide should therefore teach verification, documentation quality, compliance, exception handling, denial/revenue-cycle understanding, and responsible use of automation rather than promising that routine code assignment alone is future-proof.

### Current non-government U.S. market estimate

Source: https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Salaries/Medical-Billing-Coding-Specialist-Salary

ZipRecruiter reported, **as of July 20, 2026**, an average U.S. Medical Billing Coding Specialist estimate of **US$45,672/year (US$21.96/hour)**. The page states that its estimates are derived from employer job postings and third-party data sources. This must remain clearly labeled as a **non-government market estimate**, not a BLS statistic, guaranteed wage, or credential premium.

## United States — coding systems, privacy, and role boundaries

### Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services — ICD-10

Source: https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coding-billing/icd-10-codes

CMS states that ICD-10 applies to HIPAA-covered parties, not only Medicare or Medicaid billers. Current files include the April 1, 2026 updates and the FY 2027 code files that become effective October 1, 2026. The revised guide should teach learners to verify the correct effective-date code set instead of treating a printed or downloaded codebook as timeless.

### Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services — HCPCS

Source: https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coding-billing/healthcare-common-procedure-system

CMS describes HCPCS as essential to standardized claims processing. HCPCS Level I is CPT, maintained by the American Medical Association, and HCPCS Level II identifies many products, supplies, and services not included in CPT. The CMS page was updated July 24, 2026 and the quarterly-update page lists a July 2026 Level II file. The guide must distinguish ICD-10-CM/PCS, CPT, and HCPCS rather than presenting them as interchangeable systems.

Quarterly update source: https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coding-billing/healthcare-common-procedure-system/quarterly-update

### HHS Office for Civil Rights — HIPAA Privacy Rule

Source: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/index.html

HHS states that the HIPAA Privacy Rule protects medical records and other individually identifiable health information held by covered entities, establishes safeguards and limits on uses/disclosures, and provides individual rights. Billing, payment, claims, and medical records can be within designated record sets. The guide should use accurate scope language: not every employer, person, or data item is automatically governed by HIPAA, and state law or other privacy obligations may add requirements.

Minimum-necessary guidance: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/minimum-necessary-requirement/index.html

## United States — credentials

### AHIMA

CCA: https://www.ahima.org/certification-careers/certifications-overview/cca/

AHIMA states that the Certified Coding Associate (CCA) requires a high school diploma for exam eligibility; coding experience or specified training is recommended, not universally mandatory. AHIMA also states that exams delivered on or after May 1, 2026 use the 2026 codebook list.

CCS: https://www.ahima.org/certification-careers/certifications-overview/ccs/

AHIMA describes CCS as an experienced-practitioner coding credential and lists recommended preparation routes such as advanced coursework plus experience, two years of coding experience, or certain existing credentials plus experience. These are certification-specific recommendations/requirements and must not be rewritten as a universal legal license to work as a medical coder.

CCS-P: https://www.ahima.org/certification-careers/certifications-overview/ccs-p/

AHIMA describes CCS-P as focused on physician-based/outpatient coding. The guide should explain credential fit rather than suggesting one credential is automatically best for every setting.

### AAPC

Source: https://www.aapc.com/

AAPC currently lists CPC, COC, CIC, CRC, CPB, CPMA and other credentials. The revised guide should distinguish coding, billing, auditing and specialty credentials and tell readers to verify current exam, membership, codebook, continuing-education and apprentice-status rules directly with AAPC before paying. Commercial training or certification-provider claims must not be presented as government licensing requirements.

## United States — free-first funding and work-based learning

### U.S. Department of Labor — American Job Centers / WIOA

American Job Centers: https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/training/onestop

WIOA programs: https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/wioa/programs

CareerOneStop WIOA-eligible training finder: https://www.careeronestop.org/LocalHelp/EmploymentAndTraining/find-WIOA-training-programs.aspx

DOL states that American Job Centers provide training referrals, career counseling, job listings and related employment services. WIOA programs can include classroom and work-based learning, subject to local eligibility and funding. The guide should instruct readers to check eligibility and whether a specific coding/billing program is on the applicable eligible-training-provider list before paying personally.

### Apprenticeship.gov

Healthcare overview: https://www.apprenticeship.gov/apprenticeship-industries/healthcare

Apprenticeship.gov documents substantial healthcare Registered Apprenticeship activity, but the evidence intake does **not** establish that a registered Medical Billing and Coding Specialist apprenticeship exists in every state or locality. The guide should direct readers to search the live apprenticeship finder and verify sponsor, registration status, wages, related instruction, supervision, progressive skills and credential outcomes.

## Canada — occupational mapping and wages

The exact U.S. combined title does not map cleanly to one Canadian NOC. The 2026 guide should present at least two **role-dependent official occupational groupings** rather than forcing one false equivalence.

### Health records technician / Health information management occupations — NOC 12111

Summary: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/25614/ca

Canada Job Bank states that Health information management workers collect, code, record, review and manage health information. The summary identifies **NOC 12111**, reports a national median wage of **C$30.51/hour**, and indicates that the broader occupation usually requires college/apprenticeship-level preparation or relevant supervisory experience. The page was modified June 2, 2026. This is the better official Canadian anchor for records/coding-intensive roles.

Wages: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/25614/ca

The guide must use the national/region-specific wage table as published and avoid implying that every billing/coding job belongs to NOC 12111.

### Patient-billing clerk — billing-oriented comparator

Wages: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/14119/ca

Canada Job Bank reports national patient-billing-clerk wages of **C$18.46 low / C$25.00 median / C$35.71 high per hour**, updated November 19, 2025, reference period 2023–2024, sourced from the Statistics Canada Labour Force Survey. This can be used as a **billing-oriented comparator**, not a coding-specialist wage.

### Medical records clerk — clerical comparator, not coding specialist

Summary: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/25761/ca

Wages: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/25761/ca

Job Bank maps this title to General office support workers, **NOC 14100**, with national wages of **C$16.55 low / C$23.99 median / C$34.00 high per hour**, updated November 19, 2025. This classification is a clerical comparator and must not be substituted for NOC 12111 coding/HIM work.

## Colombia — current adjacent pathway evidence

### SENA — Facturación de los servicios en salud

Virtual program: https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/facturacion-de-los-servicios-en-salud?modality=V&programId=110493

SENA currently lists a **40-hour virtual complementary program** titled “Facturación de los servicios en salud.” The content includes current billing regulation, health tariff manuals, responsible payment entities, clinical history and liquidation of health services. This is useful **adjacent/public training evidence**, but it must not be described as equivalent to a U.S. CPC, CCA, CCS or other foreign coding credential.

Presential program: https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/facturacion-de-los-servicios-en-salud?programId=110492

SENA also lists a 48-hour in-person complementary version, subject to available cohorts and admissions procedures. Availability changes by center and date, so the guide should link to the live SENA offering rather than promise a permanent seat.

### Colombia coding-system caution

The Colombia section should be written around the actual local health-system, billing, tariff and classification requirements used by Colombian employers and payers. It must not imply that U.S. HIPAA, CPT/HCPCS, Medicare/Medicaid billing rules or AHIMA/AAPC credentials automatically govern Colombian work. U.S. coding knowledge may be relevant for outsourcing/BPO roles serving U.S. healthcare clients, but that is an employer/client-specific pathway and should be labeled as such.

No directly comparable official Colombian national wage series for the exact U.S. combined title has been verified in this intake. **Do not invent one.** Use current Colombian postings and official labor/employment sources for the actual local title when the English master is drafted.

## Editorial controls established by this intake

The revised English guide must:

1. distinguish medical coding, medical billing, health-information management, records work, denial/revenue-cycle support and clinical documentation roles;
2. label BLS Medical Records Specialists as the official U.S. occupational anchor/proxy, not an exact-title wage series;
3. label ZipRecruiter separately as non-government market evidence with source date;
4. explain that coding systems and rules change by effective date;
5. distinguish ICD-10-CM/PCS, CPT and HCPCS at a practical beginner level without reproducing copyrighted codebook content;
6. avoid implying that AHIMA or AAPC credentials are government licenses or universally required;
7. present Canadian NOC 12111, patient-billing clerk and NOC 14100 as role-dependent groupings/comparators rather than one forced mapping;
8. present SENA healthcare-billing training as adjacent Colombian preparation, not foreign-credential equivalence;
9. include free-first funding and work-based-learning locators before recommending private training debt;
10. include privacy, security, minimum-necessary access, denial/escalation, documentation quality and responsible automation/AI controls; and
11. avoid any claim of guaranteed employment, salary, remote work, certification transferability, accreditation or independent human review.
