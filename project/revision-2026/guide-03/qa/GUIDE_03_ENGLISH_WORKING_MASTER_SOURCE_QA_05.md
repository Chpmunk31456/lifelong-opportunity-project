## Gate purpose

Verify the highest-impact factual claims in the newly constructed English working master against current authoritative sources and confirm that exact-title market evidence is clearly separated from official occupational proxies. This gate is not independent human certification, legal review, coding certification, accessibility certification, or publication approval.

## United States — official occupation and outlook

**PASS.** U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Medical Records Specialists remains an appropriate official occupational anchor for coding-intensive health-records work, with the required qualification that it is broader than the exact combined title “Medical Billing and Coding Specialist.”

Verified current BLS facts retained in the master:

- May 2024 median annual wage: **US$50,250**.
- 2024 employment: approximately **194,800**.
- Projected 2024–2034 growth: **7%**.
- Approximately **14,200 openings per year** on average over the decade.
- Typical entry education is a postsecondary nondegree award, while some workers qualify with a high school diploma and others need an associate or bachelor’s degree.
- Certification may be required or preferred depending on employer.

Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/medical-records-and-health-information-technicians.htm

## United States — non-government exact-title estimate

**PASS with explicit market-source labeling.** ZipRecruiter currently reports the “Medical Billing Coding Specialist” estimate used in the master as **US$45,672/year (US$21.96/hour)** with source date **July 20, 2026**. The master labels this as non-government market evidence derived from postings/third-party data rather than a guaranteed wage or BLS statistic.

Source: https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Salaries/Medical-Billing-Coding-Specialist-Salary

## Coding systems and effective dates

**PASS.** CMS currently states that ICD-10 applies to HIPAA-covered parties and has published October 1, 2026 updates for FY 2027 ICD-10-CM/PCS. The master correctly teaches effective-date verification instead of treating codebooks as timeless.

Source: https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coding-billing/icd-10-codes

The master also distinguishes ICD-10-CM/PCS, CPT and HCPCS Level II rather than presenting them as interchangeable systems.

Source: https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coding-billing/healthcare-common-procedure-system

## HIPAA and minimum necessary

**PASS.** HHS guidance supports the master’s statement that billing/payment activities can involve protected health information and that covered entities generally must apply reasonable minimum-necessary policies to uses, disclosures and requests for PHI for payment and health-care operations, subject to regulatory exceptions. The master appropriately avoids saying that every employer, worker or health-related data item is automatically governed by HIPAA.

Sources:
- https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/index.html
- https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/minimum-necessary-requirement/index.html
- https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/disclosures-treatment-payment-health-care-operations/index.html

## AHIMA CCA

**PASS.** AHIMA currently states that CCA candidates must have a high school diploma. Six months of coding experience, an AHIMA-approved coding program, or other specified coding training are listed as recommended rather than required preparation. AHIMA also states that exams delivered on or after May 1, 2026 require the applicable 2026 codebooks. The master preserves these distinctions.

Source: https://www.ahima.org/certification-careers/certifications-overview/cca/

## Registered Apprenticeship and public workforce support

Source: https://www.apprenticeship.gov/apprenticeship-industries/healthcare

## Colombia

**PASS.** SENA currently lists `Facturación de los servicios en salud` as complementary training with a **40-hour virtual** offering and a **48-hour in-person** offering, subject to cohort availability and admissions procedures. The master correctly treats these as adjacent Colombian training rather than equivalence to U.S. private professional credentials.

Sources:
- https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/facturacion-de-los-servicios-en-salud?modality=V&programId=110493
- https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/facturacion-de-los-servicios-en-salud?programId=110492

No directly comparable official Colombian national wage series for the exact combined U.S. title was verified. The master correctly declines to invent one.

## Editorial/source controls

**PASS.** The master:

- separates coding, billing, health-information, records and revenue-cycle functions;
- labels the BLS category as an occupational anchor rather than an exact-title wage series;
- labels ZipRecruiter as non-government market evidence;
- avoids treating AHIMA/AAPC credentials as government licenses;
- avoids implying universal apprenticeship availability;
- distinguishes U.S., Canadian and Colombian pathways;
- warns against importing U.S. HIPAA/CPT/HCPCS assumptions into local Colombian work;
- includes free-first/public workforce options before private training debt; and
- retains explicit anti-guarantee and source-verification controls.
