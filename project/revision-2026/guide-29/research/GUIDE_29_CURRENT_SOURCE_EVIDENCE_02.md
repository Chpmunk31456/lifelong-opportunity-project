## United States — occupation, pay, outlook, and training

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics
Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/installation-maintenance-and-repair/heating-air-conditioning-and-refrigeration-mechanics-and-installers.htm

The current BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook profile for **Heating, Air Conditioning, and Refrigeration Mechanics and Installers (SOC 49-9021)** states that these workers install, maintain, and repair HVAC/R systems. BLS reports:

- 2024 employment: about **425,200**;
- May 2024 median pay: **$59,810 per year / $28.75 per hour**;
- lowest 10 percent: less than **$39,130**; highest 10 percent: more than **$91,020**;
- projected employment growth: **8% from 2024 to 2034**, much faster than the all-occupation average shown by BLS;
- about **40,100 openings per year**, on average, over the decade;
- typical entry education: a **postsecondary nondegree award**, although some workers enter with less education;
- postsecondary HVAC/R programs generally last **6 months to 2 years** and may lead to a certificate or associate degree;
- newly hired technicians usually complete lengthy on-the-job training, and some technicians train through apprenticeship programs lasting several years.

BLS also notes that modern HVAC/R systems include computerized components and networking features, and that technicians must inspect, test, program, and troubleshoot systems. This supports v2.0 coverage of building controls, connected equipment, diagnostic data, privacy/cybersecurity awareness, and careful human verification rather than treating AI-generated diagnostic advice as authoritative.

**Interpretation control:** BLS national figures are not entry-level offers, local wage guarantees, or forecasts for an individual worker.

## United States — refrigerant handling and environmental rules

### EPA Section 608 Technician Certification
Source: https://www.epa.gov/section608/section-608-technician-certification-requirements

EPA states that technicians who maintain, service, repair, or dispose of equipment that could release regulated refrigerants must obtain Section 608 Technician Certification by passing an EPA-approved test. EPA lists four certification categories: Type I, Type II, Type III, and Universal. The current EPA page states that Section 608 credentials do not expire. Apprentices may perform covered work without their own certification only when closely and continually supervised by a certified technician, subject to the rule.

### EPA refrigerant-management framework
Sources:
- https://www.epa.gov/section608
- https://www.epa.gov/section608/refrigerant-sales-restriction
- https://www.epa.gov/section608/refrigerant-recovery-and-recycling-equipment-certification

EPA's current stationary refrigeration and air-conditioning materials cover the venting prohibition, service-practice requirements, refrigerant sales restrictions, recovery/recycling/reclamation, safe disposal, recordkeeping, and regulatory updates. EPA states that only appropriately certified technicians may purchase refrigerants intended for stationary refrigeration and air-conditioning equipment under the Section 608 sales restriction.

**Regulatory boundary:** Section 608 is a federal refrigerant-handling credential. It must not be described as replacing state/local HVAC contractor, trade, electrical, gas, building-code, or business licensing requirements. Readers must verify every jurisdiction in which they intend to work.

## United States — safety and regulated-task boundaries

### BLS work-environment evidence
BLS reports that HVAC/R technicians may work outdoors, in cramped spaces, and in extreme temperatures and identifies electrical/chemical burns, strains/sprains, heavy equipment, and refrigerant hazards among occupational risks. It states that appropriate PPE and proper lifting practices are necessary.

### OSHA control of hazardous energy
Source: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147

OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147 applies to covered servicing/maintenance where unexpected energization, startup, or release of stored energy could injure workers. Employers must establish energy-control procedures, training, and periodic inspections within the standard's scope.

Guide 29 must not teach readers to bypass lockout/tagout, electrical qualification boundaries, pressure-system controls, manufacturer procedures, refrigerant-handling requirements, fall protection, confined-space rules, combustion-safety procedures, or local code/licensing requirements.

**Safety boundary:** A career guide cannot substitute for employer training, qualified supervision, PPE selection, manufacturer instructions, site-specific procedures, or jurisdiction-specific occupational-safety requirements.

### Red Seal — Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Mechanic

A 2024 Red Seal Occupational Standard issued by Employment and Social Development Canada covers residential, commercial, industrial, and institutional HVAC/R installation, maintenance, service, retrofit, controls, piping/tubing, wiring/cabling, climate control, and air-quality systems.

Source: https://red-seal.ca/eng/trades/refridgacmech/harmonization.shtml

**Jurisdiction control:** Apprenticeship registration, compulsory/voluntary status, certification, refrigerant rules, exam eligibility, hours, ratios, and licensing are provincial/territorial matters and must be checked where the reader intends to work. Red Seal is not a universal legal license.

## Funding and cost-control principles for v2.0

Guide 29 should use a **free-first** decision sequence:

1. Verify local licensing, refrigerant, electrical/gas, safety, and task-authorization requirements before paying for training.
2. Check employer-paid onboarding, manufacturer/OEM instruction, union/industry programs, paid work-based learning, public workforce funding, and public vocational programs first.
3. In the U.S., check American Job Center/WIOA eligibility, FAFSA/Pell/work-study eligibility at participating schools, scholarships, and employer Section 127 educational assistance before self-funding.
4. Verify whether any advertised apprenticeship is actually registered, paid, and recognized; do not assume the label alone establishes those facts.
5. In Canada, check provincial/territorial apprenticeship authorities, Red Seal information, Job Bank, and current federal/provincial supports.
6. In Colombia, check SENA Betowa and SENA APE before paying a private provider for equivalent foundational training.
7. Compare total tuition, tools/PPE, refrigerant-certification/testing costs, travel, unpaid time, credential recognition, refund terms, completion outcomes, and employer acceptance in writing.
8. Avoid providers promising guaranteed jobs, guaranteed earnings, guaranteed certification, or legal authorization to perform regulated or safety-critical work.
