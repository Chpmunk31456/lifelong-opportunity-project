## Occupational mapping

The controlled title maps directly to **Insurance Claims and Policy Processing Clerks, SOC 43-9041 / O*NET 43-9041.00**. O*NET (updated 2026) describes the occupation as processing new policies, policy modifications and claims forms; verifying completeness and accuracy; updating records; transmitting claims; and obtaining missing information. O*NET explicitly treats claims/policy processing as a distinct occupation from claims adjusters, examiners and investigators.

Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/43-9041.00

## U.S. official employment, outlook and wage benchmark

BLS 2024–2034 projections for SOC 43-9041 report:

- 2024 employment: **256,700**

- change: **-9,500 / -3.7%**

- 2024 median annual wage: **$48,450**
- typical entry education: **high school diploma or equivalent**
- typical training: **moderate-term on-the-job training**

BLS also states that increased use of computer software, including AI-enhanced software, is expected to reduce the occupation's employment share in major insurance industries. This makes process literacy, exception handling, documentation quality, customer communication and careful human verification especially important.

## Role-boundary and licensing control

This guide covers administrative claims and policy processing. It does **not** imply authority to sell insurance, bind coverage, make independent legal determinations, perform regulated adjusting, or exercise underwriting/settlement authority. Job duties and licensing requirements vary by jurisdiction and employer. Learners must verify the exact role description and regulator/employer requirements before paying for licensing or credential preparation.

## Core control themes for the revised guide

- verify coverage and claim/policy data against authoritative records;
- protect personal, financial, health and incident information;
- keep audit-ready notes and document provenance;
- identify exceptions, suspected fraud indicators and escalation triggers without making unsupported accusations;
- distinguish administrative processing from licensed decision authority;
- treat AI output as draft/assistive work only, never as an unverified coverage, eligibility, fraud or payment decision;
- preserve human review for consequential decisions and customer communications.
