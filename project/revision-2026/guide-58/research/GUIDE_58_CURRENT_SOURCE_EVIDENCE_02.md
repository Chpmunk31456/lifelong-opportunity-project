## United States — occupation, entry pathway, pay, and outlook

### O*NET occupation scope

O*NET OnLine currently identifies **31-9096.00 — Veterinary Assistants and Laboratory Animal Caretakers** as a Bright Outlook occupation, updated in 2026. The occupation includes routine animal-care and support work such as feeding, watering, examining nonfarm animals for visible signs of illness or injury, cleaning and disinfecting cages/work areas, sterilizing equipment, and selected support tasks performed **under supervision** of veterinary or laboratory animal technologists/technicians, veterinarians, or scientists.

Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/31-9096.00

Controlled scope implication: the guide may describe supervised support duties, but must not imply independent diagnosis, prescribing, surgery, or other licensed veterinary practice.

### BLS education, wage, and outlook

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook states that most veterinary assistants and laboratory animal caretakers have a **high school diploma or equivalent** and learn the occupation through **short-term on-the-job training**.

BLS reports a **median annual wage of USD $37,320 in May 2024** and **$17.94 per hour**. Employment was approximately **117,800 in 2024** and is projected to reach **128,100 in 2034**, a **9% increase**, with about **22,200 openings per year** on average over the decade.

Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/veterinary-assistants-and-laboratory-animal-caretakers.htm

These are national occupational statistics, not guaranteed starting wages.

### U.S. training and funding locators

CareerOneStop, sponsored by the U.S. Department of Labor, provides a Local Training Finder and a WIOA-Eligible Training Program Finder. Eligible individuals can use the WIOA directory to locate programs approved for potential Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act training funding; eligibility is determined through local/state workforce systems and is not guaranteed.

Sources:
- https://www.careeronestop.org/FindTraining/find-training.aspx
- https://www.careeronestop.org/LocalHelp/EmploymentAndTraining/find-WIOA-training-programs.aspx

A cost-conscious pathway is to compare employer-provided/on-the-job training first, then public/community-college or WIOA-eligible options where the local market actually requests formal training.

## Canada — correct occupational lane and title ambiguity

### Veterinary attendant / animal care worker lane

Government of Canada Job Bank maps **Veterinary Attendant** and **Animal Care Worker (except farm)** to **NOC 65220 — Pet groomers and animal care workers**. National requirements state that secondary school is usually required; depending on duties, animal-handling, grooming, dog-training, animal-health, or veterinary-assistant courses may be required. Job Bank records this occupation as **not regulated in Canada** at the national level.

Sources:
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/14241/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/14234/ca

Job Bank national wage data, updated November 19, 2025, reports approximately:
- **CAD $15.00/hour low**
- **CAD $18.00/hour median**
- **CAD $25.50/hour high**

Source: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/14241/ca

### Do not conflate veterinary attendant with veterinary technician/technologist

Canadian employers may use overlapping English titles. Guide 58 must stay in the lower-level attendant/animal-care support lane unless a posting explicitly requires a regulated or higher-skilled veterinary technician/technologist credential. Veterinary technician/technologist pathways should not be presented as equivalent entry requirements for this guide.

## Colombia — training, employment evidence, and professional boundary

### SENA current training

SENA Betowa currently lists **Cuidado de animales de compañía**, a **144-hour**, in-person complementary special course. The verified offer develops the competency to care for companion animals according to service protocols. Availability is location- and intake-specific and must be rechecked before enrollment.

Source: https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/cuidado-de-animales-de-compania?modality=P&offertype=company&programId=109052

This is a useful animal-care pathway; it is **not** evidence that the learner becomes a licensed veterinarian or veterinary professional.

### SENA Agencia Pública de Empleo — current employer-demand example

SENA's Agencia Pública de Empleo has published current veterinary-clinic assistant demand. One verified **Auxiliar clínica veterinaria** posting includes grooming, helping veterinarians and animal-health technicians with injections/treatments, cleaning/disinfecting cages and surroundings, monitoring animal behavior, and scheduled feeding. SENA APE states that its services are public and free.

Sources:
- https://ape.sena.edu.co/spe-web/spe/public/buscarsolicitudSinExperiencia
- https://ape.sena.edu.co/spe-web/spe/demanda/solicitud-sintesis/4174930

Employer postings are demand examples, not national legal authorization for every listed task. Clinical duties remain subject to employer protocol, supervision, training, and Colombian law.

### Colombian professional-practice boundary

**Ley 576 de 2000** governs professional veterinary ethics and professional practice. Articles 63–64 require current professional registration and matrícula for veterinarians/veterinary-zootechnicians/zootechnicians to exercise their profession in Colombia. The controlled guide must therefore state clearly that an assistant/caretaker role does not substitute for the registered professional and must not independently diagnose, prescribe, perform surgery, or represent itself as professional veterinary practice.

Source: https://www1.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?i=66201

## Occupational safety and animal-health boundaries

CDC/NIOSH identifies veterinary medicine and animal-care work hazards that include bites, scratches, kicks, crush injuries, strains/sprains from lifting or restraint, zoonotic infections, sharps injuries, allergens, and noise. Prevention emphasizes species-appropriate handling/restraint, infection-control practices, PPE, training, hazard communication, and safe sharps practices.

Sources:
- https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/veterinary/about/hazards.html
- https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/veterinary/prevention/index.html
- https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-pets/hcp/clinical-resources/veterinary-resources.html

Controlled safety boundary for the guide:
- never improvise restraint or handling beyond training and authorization;
- use employer-approved infection-control, cleaning/disinfection, PPE, sharps, waste, and injury-reporting procedures;
- escalate bites, scratches, needlesticks, exposures, animal distress, medication questions, and abnormal clinical signs promptly;
- do not use training text or AI output as a substitute for workplace animal-handling, infection-control, medication, anesthesia, or emergency procedures.

## Responsible AI, privacy, and records boundary

AI can assist with general terminology, non-sensitive study notes, interview practice, and administrative drafting. It must **not** diagnose an animal, prescribe or select treatment, calculate or authorize medication without approved clinical controls, replace a veterinarian's judgment, or determine emergency disposition.

Do not upload client identity data, payment information, veterinary medical records, diagnostic images/results, prescriptions, proprietary clinic protocols, credentials, or internal incident information to public AI tools unless the employer has explicitly approved that use and required safeguards are in place.

All AI-derived animal-care or clinical information must be verified against veterinarian-approved instructions, employer protocols, and current authoritative sources before use.

1. Keep the Guide 58 scope centered on veterinary assistant / veterinary attendant / animal-care support work rather than licensed veterinarian or veterinary technician practice.
2. Preserve supervision/delegation language wherever medication, samples, postop observation, or clinical assistance appears.
3. Separate employer preferences from legal/regulated requirements.
4. Label official wages separately from private salary estimates and include dates.
5. Include no-job-guarantee and no-funding-guarantee language.

8. Include animal handling, zoonotic/infection-control, sharps, PPE, lifting/restraint, and incident-escalation safeguards.
9. Include AI/privacy limits appropriate to veterinary client and animal-health records.
10. Preserve CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and the project's assurance boundary: AI-assisted controlled review does not equal independent human certification, professional accreditation, certified translation, legal review, or guaranteed employment.
