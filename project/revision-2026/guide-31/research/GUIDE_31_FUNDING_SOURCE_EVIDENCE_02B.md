## Federal Student Aid

Source: https://studentaid.gov/articles/fafsa-student-steps/

Federal Student Aid's current 2026–27 FAFSA guidance states that submitting the FAFSA can provide access to federal grants, Federal Work-Study, and federal student loans for eligible attendance at participating college, career-school, or trade-school programs. FAFSA submission is free.

Controlled use: do not imply that a particular private plumbing school or apprenticeship-related course is aid-eligible. Verify the institution, program, student eligibility, deadlines, and award directly.

## WIOA and American Job Centers

Sources:

- https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/wioa/programs
- https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/american-job-centers

The U.S. Department of Labor states that WIOA programs provide career and training services, including classroom and work-based learning, through the public workforce system. American Job Center services and eligibility vary by location.

Controlled use: readers may ask their local workforce system about plumbing, construction-trade, pre-apprenticeship, apprenticeship-referral, on-the-job-training, supportive-service, and approved-provider options. No funding or service is guaranteed.

## Employer educational assistance — Section 127

Source: https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-updates-frequently-asked-questions-about-section-127-educational-assistance-programs

IRS guidance dated April 20, 2026 states that under a qualifying employer Section 127 educational-assistance program, up to **USD 5,250** of qualifying educational assistance may be excluded from an employee's gross income for calendar year 2026, subject to the tax rules and the employer's written plan. Cost-of-living adjustments apply after 2026.

Controlled use: this is not a promise that an employer offers USD 5,250, that a plumbing course qualifies, or that reimbursement is automatic. Obtain written employer-plan terms before enrolling or relying on reimbursement.
