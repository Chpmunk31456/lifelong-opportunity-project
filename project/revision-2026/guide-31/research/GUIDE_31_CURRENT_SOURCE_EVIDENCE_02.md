## United States — occupation and labor market

### Bureau of Labor Statistics — Plumbers, Pipefitters, and Steamfitters

Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/plumbers-pipefitters-and-steamfitters.htm

Current BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook evidence reviewed for this edition:

- occupation: Plumbers, Pipefitters, and Steamfitters, SOC 47-2152;
- 2024 employment: **504,500**;
- projected 2034 employment: **527,200**;
- projected 2024–2034 growth: **4%**;
- projected numeric growth: **22,700**;
- approximately **44,000 openings per year** over the decade;
- May 2024 median pay: **USD 62,970/year** or **USD 30.27/hour**;
- lowest 10 percent: below **USD 40,670**;
- highest 10 percent: above **USD 105,150**;
- typical entry education: high school diploma or equivalent;
- typical on-the-job training: apprenticeship.

BLS states that most workers learn through a **4- or 5-year apprenticeship** and that apprentices typically receive **2,000 hours of paid on-the-job training each year** plus technical instruction. BLS also states that most states and some localities require plumbers to be licensed; some states also require pipefitters/steamfitters to be licensed and may require special licensing for gas-line work.

Controlled interpretation: BLS national statistics cover the combined occupation. They must not be represented as a plumber-only, pipefitter-only, gasfitter-only, or entry-level wage guarantee.

## U.S. apprenticeship boundary

BLS provides strong current evidence that apprenticeship is the standard training route for the combined occupation. It identifies 4- or 5-year apprenticeships, paid supervised work, and related technical instruction.

Controlled rule: the guide may say apprenticeship is the typical pathway because BLS supports it, but it must not make a new blanket claim about the exact current Registered Apprenticeship approval status of 47-2152.00 unless that status is directly verifiable. A specific program should be checked by sponsor, registered occupation/code, registration authority, paid employment status, wage progression, work hours, instruction, completion credential, costs, and local licensing credit.

## U.S. safety boundaries

### Hazardous energy / lockout-tagout

Source: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147

OSHA 1910.147 covers servicing and maintenance where unexpected energization, startup, or release of stored energy could injure workers in covered general-industry situations. The definition of energy includes electrical, mechanical, hydraulic, pneumatic, chemical, thermal, and other energy; line valves and blinds are examples of energy-isolating devices. The standard also addresses stored/residual energy and verification of isolation.

Controlled rule: workers must not assume a closed valve, pump stop, control switch, or pressure reading makes a system safe. Follow the employer's applicable energy-control procedure, verify isolation, relieve/control stored energy, and stay within authorized-worker boundaries.

### Confined spaces in construction

Source: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.1201

OSHA's construction confined-space standard identifies examples directly relevant to plumbing/piping work, including manholes, sewers, storm drains, water mains, tanks, pits, lift stations, cesspools, boilers, and similar spaces. Entry may require a formal permit-space program, testing, ventilation, attendants, rescue arrangements, and other controls depending on the conditions and applicable rule.

### Excavations and underground utilities

Sources:

- https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.650
- https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.651

OSHA Subpart P applies to excavations, including trenches. OSHA 1926.651 requires the estimated location of underground utilities such as sewer, fuel, electric, and water lines to be determined before opening an excavation and requires contact with utility companies/owners as specified.

Controlled rule: the guide must not teach unsupervised trench entry, confined-space entry, hot tapping, work on pressurized systems, fuel-gas work, welding/brazing/hot work, or hazardous-energy isolation as beginner practice. These require the applicable employer program, competent/qualified personnel, permits, protective systems, testing, PPE, and jurisdiction-specific authorization.

## U.S. licensing and code boundary

BLS states that most states and some localities require plumbers to be licensed and that licensing often involves experience plus an exam. Some states require additional licensing for pipefitters, steamfitters, or gas-line work.

The v2 guide must therefore tell readers to verify the exact state/local licensing authority, apprentice/trainee registration, journey/master categories, contractor rules, accepted experience hours, examinations, adopted plumbing/mechanical/fuel-gas codes, permits, inspection rules, and reciprocity before paying for training or performing independent work.

No national plumbing license or universal reciprocity claim is permitted.

## Canada

Source: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/wagereport/occupation/4754

Government of Canada Job Bank maps plumber-related work to **NOC 72300**. National wage data updated November 19, 2025, using a 2023–2024 reference period, report:

- **C$21.00/hour low**;
- **C$34.00/hour median**;

These are national reference values, not guaranteed offers.

### Red Seal — Plumber

Source: https://red-seal.ca/eng/trades/plumber.shtml?wbdisable=true

Red Seal identifies **Plumber** as NOC 72300 and a designated Red Seal trade in participating provinces and territories. The trade description includes planning, installing, testing, and servicing water, hydronic, drainage/waste/vent, low-pressure steam, residential fire, chemical, irrigation, medical gas, process piping, compressed air, water conditioning, fuel piping, sewage/water treatment, and related systems.

Controlled interpretation: this broad trade description demonstrates why task scope matters. Specialized systems may carry additional legal, safety, certification, code, or employer requirements. Red Seal does not erase province/territory-specific apprenticeship, compulsory-trade, gas, medical-gas, backflow, contractor, permit, or inspection requirements.

## Colombia

### SENA — hydrosanitary training

Current SENA Betowa evidence includes:

1. **Instalación de redes hidrosanitarias** — complementary in-person course, **48 hours**, focused on installing hydrosanitary networks according to technical procedures and standards: https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/instalacion-de-redes-hidrosanitarias?modality=P&offertype=company
2. **Instalación de redes hidrosanitarias en PVC y griferías** — complementary in-person course, **48 hours**, with the same controlled competency focus: https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/instalacion-de-redes-hidrosanitarias-en-pvc-y-griferias?modality=P&offertype=company&programId=216296
3. **Básico de instalaciones sanitarias domiciliarias** — complementary in-person course, **60 hours**, covering drainage installations and verification against standards/plans/specifications: https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/basico-de-instalaciones-sanitarias-domiciliarias?modality=P&offertype=company&programId=11816
4. **Construcción de edificaciones** — titled **Técnico**, **2,160 hours**, includes installing hydrosanitary networks among broader construction competencies: https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/construccion-de-edificaciones?modality=P&programId=195656

Controlled interpretation:

- SENA is a strong free-first training locator.
- A 48- or 60-hour complementary course is not to be represented as equivalent to a multi-year U.S./Canadian apprenticeship or as universal professional authorization.
- The 2,160-hour Construction Technician program is broader than plumbing and should be described as an adjacent foundation, not as a stand-alone international plumber license.
- Cohorts, city, modality, dates, admission, and availability are dynamic and must be rechecked in Betowa.

The controlled research did not establish one national Colombian “plumber license” that can be safely generalized to every plumbing, gas, pressure, fire-protection, public-utility, or building-system task. The guide therefore must direct readers to verify current Colombian technical regulations, local building/permit requirements, employer/contractor conditions, public-utility rules, gas-system authorization where applicable, and any competency/certification requirement relevant to the actual task.

## Occupational-title distinction

The v2 guide must distinguish:

- plumber: typically water, drainage, fixtures, sanitary and related building systems;
- pipefitter/steamfitter: often industrial/commercial piping, process systems, heating/cooling, chemicals, gases, or higher-pressure systems;
- gasfitter/sprinklerfitter: potentially specialized regulated work;
- plumbing technician: employer-dependent title that may refer to helper, maintenance, service, facilities, installation, or more advanced duties.

Combined BLS statistics must remain labeled as combined SOC 47-2152.

## Responsible AI and digital-tool boundary

AI may assist with low-risk learning using public or fictitious information: terminology, blueprint-reading concepts, interview practice, study plans, unit-conversion practice, or questions for a licensing authority or school.

AI must not be used as sole authority for real-world:

- fuel-gas or medical-gas work;
- pressure-system isolation, testing, or hot tapping;
- backflow/cross-connection decisions;
- potable-water contamination control;
- pipe sizing or code compliance;
- trench or confined-space entry;
- hazardous-energy isolation;
- welding/brazing/hot-work procedures;
- permit or inspection decisions;
- licensing/credential scope.

Real work must be verified against current adopted codes/standards, manufacturer information, employer procedures, competent/qualified supervision, and the authority having jurisdiction.

## Research exclusions / fail-closed decisions

The v2 guide will not claim:

- one U.S. national plumbing license;
- universal reciprocity;
- a verified blanket Registered Apprenticeship approval status for O*NET 47-2152.00 without direct current evidence;
- that every plumber may legally perform gas, medical-gas, sprinkler, pressure, pipefitting, steamfitting, or contractor work;
- that Red Seal replaces province/territory legal requirements;
- that a short SENA complementary course creates independent professional authorization;
- that the SENA Construction Technician program is an international plumber credential;
- guaranteed wages, employment, apprenticeship placement, funding, licensing, or certification; or
- AI as authority for safety-critical plumbing/piping decisions.
