## United States — official occupation and wage evidence

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Occupational Outlook Handbook
Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/sales/travel-agents.htm

Controlled facts:
- Occupation: Travel Agents, SOC 41-3041.
- Typical entry education: high school diploma or equivalent.
- Typical on-the-job training: moderate-term.
- Median annual wage: **$48,450** in May 2024 (**$23.29/hour**).
- Employment: about **65,700** jobs in 2024.
- Projected employment growth: **2% from 2024 to 2034**, slower than average.
- Projected openings: about **7,100 per year** on average over the decade.
- BLS explicitly notes that corporate travel agents/travel coordinators make business travel arrangements, while leisure agents may specialize by destination or travel type.

### O*NET OnLine
Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/41-3041.00

Controlled facts:
- O*NET-SOC: **41-3041.00 Travel Agents**; page updated in 2026.
- Core work includes determining destination, transportation, dates, costs, and accommodations; arranging itineraries; selling tour packages; collecting payment; maintaining client/vendor records; and assisting with travel problems.
- Current employer-posting technology signals include office software and travel/reservation systems; the detailed page also identifies platforms such as Amadeus and Sabre among occupation-relevant software.
- The occupation combines customer service, sales, coordination, recordkeeping, financial handling, and travel-information work.

## Editorial controls for the English revision
The revised master should:

1. distinguish travel-agent sales/service work from internal corporate travel coordination while explaining their overlap;
2. cover reservations, itinerary building, disruption handling, supplier coordination, documentation, and customer communication;
3. treat GDS/reservation-system capability (for example, Amadeus/Sabre) as employer-dependent, not a universal legal credential;
4. explain payment-card, identity-document, passport/visa, itinerary, and traveller-location data as sensitive operational information requiring privacy and cybersecurity discipline;
5. state that visa, entry, health, safety, and destination rules change and must be checked with official authorities rather than generated or guessed by AI;
6. include accessible customer-service practices and reasonable-accommodation planning without claiming certification;
7. include U.S. WIOA/American Job Center, Canadian federal/provincial training supports, and SENA/Betowa as funding or low-cost training locators without promising eligibility;
8. state that apprenticeships/work-based learning in this occupation are employer- and jurisdiction-specific; on-the-job training, internships, trainee roles, and employer-sponsored GDS/product training may be more common than a formal registered apprenticeship;
9. label all non-government salary values as estimates and preserve source date/context.
