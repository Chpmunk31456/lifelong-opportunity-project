## United States — occupation, entry pathway, wages, and outlook

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics

Current BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook evidence for **Solar Photovoltaic Installers (SOC 47-2231)** states:

- Solar PV installers assemble, set up, and maintain rooftop or other systems that convert sunlight into energy.
- Typical entry-level education: **high school diploma or equivalent**.
- Typical on-the-job training: **moderate-term on-the-job training**; BLS further states that installers typically receive on-the-job training lasting up to 1 year.
- Employment in 2024: **28,600**.
- Projected employment in 2034: **40,600**.
- Numeric projected growth, 2024–2034: **12,000**.
- Projected growth rate, 2024–2034: **42%**.
- Projected openings: about **4,100 per year** on average over the decade.
- May 2024 median annual wage: **$51,860**.
- May 2024 median hourly wage: **$24.93**.
- Lowest 10%: below **$39,070**.
- Highest 10%: above **$80,150**.

BLS also makes an essential scope distinction: depending on the job and state law, PV installers may connect panels to the electrical grid, but electricians sometimes perform that task. Installation may require roofers and electricians. Version 2 must not turn BLS's broad occupational-duty description into a claim that every entry-level installer is legally authorized to perform grid interconnection, service-panel work, electrical design, inspection, commissioning, or energized electrical work.

Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/solar-photovoltaic-installers.htm

### O*NET

O*NET OnLine identifies **47-2231.00 Solar Photovoltaic Installers** as a Bright Outlook occupation, updated 2026. It describes workers as assembling, installing, or maintaining PV systems on roofs or other structures in accordance with site assessment and schematics and notes that the occupation **may perform minor electrical work such as current checks**.

The wording “may perform minor electrical work” is a task description, not a universal licensing authorization. Version 2 must retain the jurisdiction/employer/qualified-person boundary.

Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/47-2231.00

### Current non-government compensation supplement

Indeed's U.S. Solar Installer salary page, updated July 17, 2026, reports an average base salary of **$26.12/hour**, with a displayed low of **$16.37/hour** and high of **$41.68/hour**, based on approximately 2.9k salaries from job postings over the prior 36 months. It also displays a separate overtime indicator of $7,500/year.

This is **non-government, title-specific market evidence**, not an official occupational wage statistic and not a guaranteed starting rate. It may be used only as a clearly labeled supplement after the BLS evidence.

Source: https://www.indeed.com/career/solar-installer/salaries

## United States — safety and regulated-work boundaries

### OSHA solar-energy hazard framework

OSHA's Solar Energy hazard resources identify serious PV-sector hazards including electrical shock, arc flash, falls, and thermal burns. OSHA's solar electrical page states that workers can be exposed to electrical shock and burns while hooking panels to electrical circuits and describes circumstances where 29 CFR 1910 Subpart S or 29 CFR 1910.269 may apply.

Controlled interpretation for Version 2:

- Do not teach DIY energized electrical procedures.
- A PV module can generate electricity when illuminated; “turning off the building” does not necessarily make every PV conductor or module electrically safe.
- Electrical connection, testing, lockout/tagout, grounding/bonding, service equipment, utility interconnection, commissioning, and energized work must follow employer procedures, applicable code/regulation, manufacturer instructions, and the authorization/qualification rules of the jurisdiction.
- Stop and escalate when electrical isolation/status is uncertain, when required qualified-person supervision is absent, or when work is outside the worker's authorization.

Sources:
- https://www.osha.gov/green-jobs/solar/
- https://www.osha.gov/green-jobs/solar/electrical

### Falls and roof integrity

OSHA's solar-fall page specifically notes that workers installing or maintaining panels often work on roofs, ladders, scaffolds, near roof edges, skylights, and roof hatches. For construction installation, OSHA 29 CFR 1926.501 generally requires fall protection for employees exposed to unprotected sides/edges **6 feet (1.8 m) or more** above a lower level, subject to the standard's detailed provisions.

OSHA 1926.501(a)(2) also requires the employer to determine whether walking/working surfaces have the strength and structural integrity to support employees safely before employees work on them.

Version 2 must not prescribe a universal anchor, harness, ladder, scaffold, warning-line, or roof-access setup. It should tell readers to use the employer's approved fall-protection/access system and stop when roof integrity, edge/skylight controls, weather, access, or fall-protection status is unclear.

Sources:
- https://www.osha.gov/green-jobs/solar/falls
- https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.501

## Canada — occupation mapping, wages, and electrical-trade boundary

### Job Bank — solar panel installer

Government of Canada Job Bank maps the title **solar panel installer** to **Residential and commercial installers and servicers (NOC 73200)**.

Current national wage evidence, updated November 19, 2025 (Job Bank page modified June 2, 2026):

- Low: **CAD $18.65/hour**
- Median: **CAD $26.00/hour**
- High: **CAD $40.00/hour**

Job Bank's national summary states that this occupational group usually requires a college diploma, an apprenticeship of less than two years, or more than six months of on-the-job training; regional requirements can differ. A separate Alberta requirements page for the same NOC says some secondary school plus on-the-job training and several months of related installation/repair/service experience are usually required. These are occupational-group indicators, not a universal solar licensing rule.

Sources:
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/27267/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/27267/ca

### Construction Electrician / Red Seal boundary

The Red Seal Program identifies **Construction Electrician (NOC 72200)** as a designated Red Seal trade across participating provinces/territories. The trade includes planning, assembling, installing, altering, repairing, inspecting, verifying, commissioning, connecting, operating, maintaining, and decommissioning electrical systems. Red Seal materials also identify solar power systems as an emerging technology affecting construction electricians.

Controlled interpretation:

- A Canadian “solar panel installer” occupation profile does not by itself authorize electrical work reserved by a province/territory to electricians or other certified/registered persons.
- Apprenticeship, compulsory-trade status, permits, inspection, utility interconnection, and scope-of-practice requirements must be verified with the relevant provincial/territorial authority.
- Red Seal is an interprovincial trade framework; it is not a universal license overriding provincial/territorial law.

Source: https://red-seal.ca/eng/trades/const-elect.shtml

## Colombia — SENA pathways and RETIE boundary

### SENA technical program

SENA/Betowa currently lists **Mantenimiento e instalación de sistemas solares fotovoltaicos** as a **Titulada**, **Técnico**, **2,200-hour** program. Current listings show cohort/location-specific offerings; availability and enrollment windows change.

Source: https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/mantenimiento-e-instalacion-de-sistemas-solares-fotovoltaicos?modality=P&programId=72121

### SENA complementary program

SENA/Betowa also lists **Diseño y montaje de sistemas solares fotovoltaicos** as a **Complementaria**, **curso especial**, **96-hour** program. It states competencies involving sustainable-energy network design and renewable-energy system assembly according to technical procedure and electrical regulation.

A 96-hour complementary course is not equivalent to the 2,200-hour Técnico and must not be described as a universal electrical license or independent authorization to design, sign off, inspect, energize, or interconnect installations.

Source: https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/diseno-y-montaje-de-sistemas-solares-fotovoltaicos?offertype=company&programId=178494

### RETIE

The Ministry of Mines and Energy's current RETIE page identifies the **Reglamento Técnico de Instalaciones Eléctricas (RETIE)** as the governing technical-regulation framework for electrical installations and includes specific photovoltaic-system references and inspection/conformity requirements for certain systems.

The current Ministry webpage contains an internal metadata inconsistency: its introductory text says Resolution 40284 is dated June 23, **2026**, while its traceability table displays June 23, **2025**. Because this research gate must fail closed on ambiguity, Version 2 should refer readers to the **current RETIE published by the Ministry** and should not assert the disputed year/date for Resolution 40284 unless the controlling resolution document itself is separately verified.

The page also states that photovoltaic systems for self-consumption with injection of surplus into the distribution network are subject to specified inspection/conformity requirements and refers to NTC 2050 Section 690 plus applicable generation/interconnection rules.

Source: https://www.minenergia.gov.co/es/misional/energia-electrica-2/reglamentos-tecnicos/reglamento-t%C3%A9cnico-de-instalaciones-el%C3%A9ctricas-retie/

Controlled reader-facing boundary:

- SENA training does not automatically confer legal authorization for every electrical activity.
- Verify current RETIE, applicable NTC 2050 requirements, electrical-professional competency/registration rules, design/signature responsibilities, inspection requirements, and distribution-network interconnection procedures before performing or accepting responsibility for regulated electrical work.

## Controlled Version 2 requirements derived from research

The English reconstruction must:

1. Distinguish **mechanical/module/racking/roof work** from regulated electrical design, connection, inspection, energization, commissioning, and utility-interconnection work.
2. Explain that job titles vary and that “solar installer,” “PV installer,” “solar technician,” and electrician-linked titles can carry very different legal scopes.
3. Preserve the U.S. BLS SOC 47-2231 evidence and not blend it with electrician wage statistics.
4. Label Indeed figures as non-government market estimates and not guaranteed starting wages.
5. Keep Canada's NOC 73200 solar-panel-installer data separate from the Construction Electrician / Red Seal NOC 72200 trade boundary.
6. Present SENA's 2,200-hour Técnico and 96-hour complementary course as different pathways; neither should be called a universal license.
7. Treat the current RETIE as a live regulatory source and explicitly avoid the unresolved Resolution 40284 year/date discrepancy.
8. Use stop/escalate language for uncertain electrical status, roof integrity, fall protection, structural suitability, weather/heat, damaged modules/connectors/cables, unapproved equipment, and tasks outside authorization.
9. Avoid hazardous step-by-step energized work, roof-access, fall-arrest setup, service-panel, meter, grounding/bonding, commissioning, or interconnection instructions.
10. Include free-first pathways: employer training, public technical/community college options, Apprenticeship.gov, American Job Centers/WIOA, eligible FAFSA-supported career/trade programs, SENA, and regional public vocational systems.
11. Include responsible-AI boundaries: AI may support terminology/study/job-search organization but must not replace site assessment, structural/electrical design, code interpretation, conductor/protection sizing, fall-protection planning, lockout/tagout, commissioning, inspection acceptance, manufacturer instructions, or utility procedures.
12. Include privacy/cybersecurity controls for customer addresses, system diagrams, inverter/monitoring portals, Wi-Fi credentials, utility/customer account data, equipment serial numbers, production data, and site photographs.
13. State clearly that the guide does not guarantee employment, wages, admission, funding, certification, licensing, permit approval, inspection approval, utility interconnection, or earnings.
