# Guide 74 — Current Source Evidence 02

**Occupation:** Ecommerce Specialist and Marketplace Coordinator  
**Research date:** 2026-08-21  
**Gate target:** Current-source Research

## Controlled occupation framing

“Ecommerce Specialist” and “Marketplace Coordinator” are employer labels rather than one globally standardized occupation. The controlled guide should therefore use a blended benchmark and explain the scope difference instead of presenting one code as an exact match.

### United States — O*NET primary benchmarks

**O*NET-SOC 13-1199.06 — Online Merchants**  
https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/13-1199.06

Controlled facts:
- O*NET describes Online Merchants as conducting retail activities for online businesses and includes business strategy, merchandise buying, inventory, marketing, online order fulfillment, and financial records.
- Typical tasks include order and invoice management, customer transaction confirmations, customer correspondence, product availability work, transaction processing, and online-store operations.
- O*NET marks the occupation Bright Outlook and reports 2025 wage data using the broader BLS category **Business Operations Specialists, All Other**.
- Current national benchmark: **$39.93/hour median and $83,050/year median**; 10th percentile **$47,880/year** and 90th percentile **$150,010/year**.
- These wages are for the broader parent BLS category and must not be presented as an exact ecommerce-specialist wage.

National wage page:  
https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/13-1199.06

**O*NET-SOC 13-1161.01 — Search Marketing Strategists**  
https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/13-1161.01

Controlled facts:
- O*NET describes work that increases visibility and engagement with products or services on internet-enabled devices and interfaces.
- Relevant tasks include web-metric analysis, digital content strategy, ecommerce-trend research, conversion optimization, coordinating product catalogs with merchandising/operations/inventory, online customer-service processes, and assisting with ecommerce functionality and transaction/security policies.
- The code replaced the retired **15-1099.12 Electronic Commerce Specialists** classification and is useful for the marketing/content/analytics side of modern ecommerce roles.
- O*NET reports 2025 BLS wage data for the parent category **Market Research Analysts and Marketing Specialists**: **$37.87/hour median and $78,760/year median**, with a 10th percentile of **$43,390/year** and 90th percentile of **$155,480/year**.
- The parent category had about **941,700 workers in 2024**, projected growth of **7% or higher from 2024 to 2034**, and about **87,200 projected openings** over that period according to O*NET’s current summary.

National wage page:  
https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/13-1161.01

### United States — current non-government estimate

**Indeed — E-Commerce Specialist salary**  
https://www.indeed.com/career/e%20commerce-specialist/salaries

Controlled use:
- Indeed reported an average base salary of **$19.85/hour** for U.S. E-Commerce Specialists, based on about **4.4k salaries from job postings over the prior 36 months**, updated **August 2, 2026**.
- Indeed displayed a range of approximately **$11.91 to $33.07/hour**.
- This is a current non-government market estimate, not an official wage statistic and not guaranteed compensation.
- The large difference between Indeed’s employer-title estimate and the broader O*NET/BLS parent-category medians is itself an important warning that ecommerce titles span very different authority levels, from listing/order support to analytics, strategy, merchandising, and management.

## Canada — official occupation and wage evidence

**Job Bank — Electronic commerce (e-commerce) specialist**  
Summary: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/296165/ca  
Wages: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/296165/ca

Controlled facts:
- Job Bank maps the title to **NOC 62101 — Retail and wholesale buyers**.
- The national profile describes retail/wholesale buying and merchandising operations; therefore it is only a partial benchmark for marketplace merchandising and product-selection work and should not be treated as equivalent to every ecommerce specialist role.
- Current national wage data, updated **November 19, 2025**, show **C$19.23/hour low, C$30.00/hour median, and C$48.08/hour high**.
- National reference period is 2023–2024 and the source is Statistics Canada’s Labour Force Survey.
- Job Bank states that the title’s occupation usually requires college/apprenticeship-level preparation or relevant supervisory experience; actual employer requirements vary by scope.
- Current prospects vary by province or territory.

## Consumer protection, advertising, reviews, and marketplace boundaries — United States

Federal Trade Commission — Online Advertising and Marketing:  
https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/online-advertising-marketing

Federal Trade Commission — Advertising and Marketing Basics:  
https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing

Controlled use:
- Advertising claims must be truthful, non-deceptive, and evidence-based.
- Endorsements, influencer relationships, and customer reviews can trigger disclosure and authenticity requirements.
- The FTC’s current ecommerce guidance includes the **INFORM Consumers Act** for certain online marketplaces and high-volume third-party sellers.
- FTC online-selling guidance addresses shipment promises, delays, returns, and refunds. The guide should teach workers to follow approved company policies and current legal/compliance guidance rather than inventing terms or overriding authorized refund controls.
- Marketplace coordinators should not fabricate ratings, reviews, testimonials, scarcity, discounts, product claims, certifications, origin claims, or availability.

## Cybersecurity, payment, privacy, and fraud controls

CISA Secure Our World:  
https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world

Controlled use:
- CISA emphasizes phishing recognition/reporting, strong passwords/password managers, multifactor authentication, and keeping software updated.
- Ecommerce operations are high-value fraud targets because workers may handle marketplace accounts, product listings, customer information, order systems, seller portals, refunds, promotions, and vendor/payment instructions.
- The guide should require employer-approved systems, MFA where supported, least-necessary access, independent verification of unusual bank/payment changes, and prompt escalation of suspected phishing, account takeover, fraudulent refunds, credential exposure, or data loss.
- Workers must not store or transmit payment-card data outside approved systems or bypass payment-security controls.

## Accessibility and inclusive digital commerce

U.S. Department of Justice ADA resources:  
https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/

Controlled use:
- Public-facing digital services can raise accessibility obligations depending on the entity and jurisdiction.
- The guide should teach practical inclusive habits such as descriptive text, meaningful link labels, clear headings, readable layouts, captions/alt text where applicable, keyboard-friendly workflows, and escalation of platform barriers.
- It must not claim legal compliance or accessibility certification from checklist use alone.

## Responsible AI boundaries

NIST AI Risk Management Framework:  
https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework

Controlled use:
- AI may assist with low-risk first drafts, product-description ideas, keyword clustering, routine summaries, image-alt-text drafts, or internal checklists when the employer permits it.
- Human verification is required for product specifications, prices, discounts, inventory, regulated claims, shipping promises, refund terms, legal disclosures, accessibility commitments, customer communications, and marketplace policy decisions.
- Customer personal information, payment data, seller credentials, private contracts, unpublished pricing, authentication secrets, and other confidential information should not be entered into unapproved AI tools.

## Editorial controls for the English reconstruction

The English master should:
1. distinguish listing/catalog/order-support work from marketing analytics, merchandising, marketplace management, and strategic authority;
2. use O*NET 13-1199.06 and 13-1161.01 as complementary U.S. benchmarks and label their broader BLS wage-parent categories explicitly;
3. treat Canada NOC 62101 as a partial merchandising/buyer benchmark, not an exact ecommerce-equivalent code;
4. include marketplace listing quality, catalog taxonomy, product data, inventory coordination, order/refund workflows, customer service, analytics, conversion, content, promotion coordination, and cross-functional handoffs;
5. explain platform-specific tools as employer skills rather than universal credentials;
6. include CareerOneStop/WIOA, Canada federal/provincial supports, SENA Betowa, and OIT/Cinterfor without promising eligibility, seats, funding, or outcomes;
7. include employer training, internships, supervised ecommerce operations, retail/marketing/logistics bridges, and paid entry-level roles as work-based-learning options;
8. clearly separate official wage benchmarks from current non-government estimates;
9. cover truthful advertising, reviews, endorsements, shipping, returns/refunds, marketplace rules, and escalation boundaries without giving legal advice;
10. cover phishing, MFA, seller-account takeover, payment-change fraud, customer data, least-necessary access, and employer-approved systems;
11. include accessible digital-content and storefront practices without claiming certification;
12. keep AI use low-risk, privacy-preserving, employer-approved, and subject to human verification;
13. preserve plain-language, dysgraphia-friendly structure, descriptive headings, short steps, and reusable checklists;
14. avoid unsupported claims of human certification, professional translation certification, legal review, cybersecurity certification, accessibility certification, accreditation, or guaranteed earnings/employment.
