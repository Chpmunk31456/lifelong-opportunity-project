# Guide 40 — Construction Laborer and Trade Helper

**Current-source evidence record — 2026-08-13**  
**Gate:** Research Helper — PASS

## Research scope

This evidence set supports the controlled Version 2 reconstruction of Guide 40. The target is entry-to-development work as a construction laborer and construction-trade helper. The guide must distinguish general labor/helper duties from independently performing regulated or licensed skilled-trade work, heavy-equipment operation, construction supervision/management, hazardous-material removal, blasting, and other specialist work that requires separate authorization, competence, training, or credentials.

## United States — occupation, duties, wages and outlook

### BLS combined occupation and O*NET laborer anchor

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook groups **Construction Laborers and Helpers** and describes workers who use, supply or hold materials or tools, clean work areas/equipment, prepare sites, load and unload materials, erect or dismantle temporary structures, dig/backfill/compact ground, operate certain hand and power tools, and assist tradesworkers. The combined group includes Construction Laborers (SOC 47-2061) and several craft-specific helper occupations.

Current BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook data report:

- combined-group median annual wage in May 2024: **$46,050** (**$22.14/hour**);
- Construction Laborers median annual wage in May 2024: **$46,730**;
- Helpers, Construction Trades median annual wage in May 2024: **$40,430**;
- 2024 combined employment: about **1,649,100** workers;
- projected combined employment growth, 2024–2034: **7%**; and
- about **149,400 projected openings per year**, on average, across the combined group over the decade.

These figures are national occupational statistics, not promised starting wages. Pay varies materially by occupation subtype, location, union/collective-bargaining coverage, project type, prevailing-wage rules, shift, season, experience and employer.

O*NET **47-2061.00 — Construction Laborers** is the principal detailed occupational anchor for general laborer duties. Its 2026-updated profile describes physical construction-site work, hand/power-tool use, site preparation, trenching, temporary support/scaffolding-related tasks, cleanup and assistance to craft workers. O*NET's current national wage view uses BLS 2025 wage data and reports a Construction Laborers national median of about **$47,120/year ($22.66/hour)**. This newer detailed-occupation wage series should not be silently mixed with the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook's May 2024 combined-group median.

### Education and advancement boundary

BLS states that formal education is not typically required for construction laborers and helpers, although some employers prefer a high school diploma or equivalent. Workers commonly learn on the job, and some construction laborers complete apprenticeship programs. Helpers may later enter skilled trades after obtaining the training and credentials required for the trade.

The controlled guide must not imply that performing helper duties automatically qualifies a learner to act independently as an electrician, plumber, rigger, scaffolder, equipment operator, blaster, hazardous-material worker or other regulated/specialist worker.

Source:
- https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/construction-laborers-and-helpers.htm

## United States — safety and OSHA training controls

Construction work is safety-critical. OSHA's Part 1926 construction standards address general safety and health provisions, PPE, scaffolds, fall protection, equipment, excavations, concrete/masonry, demolition and other hazards that can apply depending on the task and site. The guide should direct readers to employer/site-specific instructions and applicable standards rather than treating a general course as authorization for every construction task.

OSHA's Outreach Training Program offers 10-hour and 30-hour construction classes, but OSHA explicitly states that the Outreach program is **voluntary**, does **not** satisfy training requirements imposed by specific OSHA standards, and is **not a certification or license**. Some states, municipalities, employers, unions or project owners may nevertheless require an Outreach card as a condition of work. The 10-hour course is aimed at worker hazard awareness; the 30-hour course is more appropriate for supervisors or workers with some safety responsibility.

Sources:
- https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/
- https://www.osha.gov/training/outreach/
- https://www.osha.gov/training/outreach/construction

## United States — apprenticeship, public workforce and free-first pathways

Apprenticeship.gov identifies **Construction Laborers** among high-demand construction apprenticeship occupations. Registered Apprenticeship can provide paid work experience, mentorship, progressive wage increases, related instruction and a portable credential. Availability is controlled by employers and sponsors and varies by occupation and location, so the guide must direct readers to the live Apprenticeship Finder rather than promise a seat.

CareerOneStop, sponsored by the U.S. Department of Labor, provides American Job Center and training locators. American Job Centers offer free employment assistance, and the WIOA-Eligible Training Program Finder can help eligible job seekers identify training that may qualify for Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act support. Eligibility and funding are conditional; directory presence is not a funding guarantee.

## Canada — occupation, requirements, wages and trade-progression boundary

Statistics Canada's NOC 2021 classifies comparable work under **NOC 75110 — Construction trades helpers and labourers**. The unit group includes workers who assist skilled tradespersons and perform laboring activities at construction sites, quarries and surface mines.

Government of Canada Job Bank states that general construction-labourer experience may be required for some helper roles; certain pipeline work may require prior seasonal experience; traffic-control and rigging certificates may be required for some tasks; and on-the-job training may be provided. Job Bank also warns that certification, licensing or apprenticeship registration can be required depending on the specific occupation and jurisdiction. The controlled guide must therefore avoid presenting one universal Canadian entry rule.

Job Bank's current national wage page for NOC 75110 reports approximately:

- **CAD $18.25/hour low**;
- **CAD $25.00/hour median**; and
- **CAD $40.00/hour high**.

The page says the wage values were updated November 19, 2025 and are based on Statistics Canada Labour Force Survey data for the 2023–2024 reference period. Job Bank's 2025–2027 outlooks vary substantially by province and region, so the guide should use the live regional outlook tool instead of claiming one nationwide outlook rating.

### Canada — Construction Craft Worker / Red Seal progression

The Red Seal Program maps **Construction Craft Worker** to NOC 75110 and lists it as a designated Red Seal trade in multiple provinces. This creates a legitimate progression pathway for some workers, but it does not mean every generic NOC 75110 helper job requires Red Seal status. Provincial/territorial apprenticeship and certification authorities remain authoritative for entry, certification and examination rules.

Source:
- https://red-seal.ca/eng/trades/const-craft-work.shtml

### Canada — apprenticeship funding locator

Canada currently lists apprenticeship and skilled-trades supports including the Canada Apprentice Loan (up to **CAD $4,000** in interest-free loans for eligible apprentices) and employer/organization programs under the Canadian Apprenticeship Strategy. Eligibility, designated-trade rules, provincial/territorial participation and program availability must be verified before relying on funding.

Source:
- https://www.canada.ca/en/services/jobs/training/support-skilled-trades-apprentices/funding-opportunities.html

## Colombia — free public training, safety and employment pathways

SENA's 2026 public training announcements describe tens of thousands of **free** places across auxiliary, operator, technical and technologist levels, while enrollment windows and locations change by cohort. Betowa is the current SENA program/offer platform and should be used to verify live availability before a learner makes plans.

A current occupation-adjacent example is SENA's **Mampostería** operator program, listed as a 1,296-hour in-person program. Its competency set includes preparing construction materials, non-structural masonry, environmental protection, occupational safety and health, and scaffold-related work subject to technical and work-at-height requirements. Course locations and enrollment windows are variable; the existence of the program is not a guarantee of an open seat in a learner's city.

For work at height, SENA maintains a dedicated training page and describes separate training levels. Work-at-height participation is a high-risk safety domain; the guide must instruct readers to verify the current employer/site and Colombian legal requirements rather than assume one generic construction credential covers all tasks.

SENA's Agencia Pública de Empleo is a public, free employment-intermediation service. It can be used as a Colombia job-search and employer-demand locator without implying that a particular vacancy remains open or that selection is guaranteed.

## Employer support and work-based learning controls

The Version 2 guide should explicitly tell readers to ask employers, unions, apprenticeship sponsors, contractors and public workforce systems about:

- paid onboarding and supervised task qualification;
- employer-paid PPE and safety training where applicable;
- Registered Apprenticeship or trade-apprenticeship pathways;
- paid trainee/helper routes;
- tuition assistance or reimbursement;
- tool/equipment policies and required personal purchases; and
- advancement into a skilled trade only after meeting that trade's training, apprenticeship, licensing or certification requirements.

Any reimbursement conditions should be obtained in writing before spending money. No employer benefit, union access, apprenticeship seat, credential, promotion or job offer should be presented as guaranteed.

## Accessibility and physical-demand boundary

BLS describes the work as physically demanding and often outdoors, sometimes at heights or in confined spaces. The guide should discuss realistic task demands without implying that disability automatically excludes someone from construction careers. Learners should evaluate the essential functions of a specific job and may explore accommodation, adaptive equipment, adjacent roles, phased training or public vocational-rehabilitation support where applicable. The guide must not make individualized medical or legal determinations.

Source:
- https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/construction-laborers-and-helpers.htm

## AI, privacy and cybersecurity boundary for reconstruction

AI may be presented only as a secondary learning/administrative aid—for example, explaining terminology, creating practice questions, helping organize a study plan, or drafting non-sensitive résumé bullets that the learner verifies. AI must not replace site safety instructions, construction drawings/specifications, equipment manuals, qualified supervision, hazard assessments, fall-protection plans, excavation controls, lockout/tagout procedures, trade calculations, or required competent/qualified-person decisions.

The guide should warn learners not to upload customer/project data, site plans, access-control information, security details, incident reports, proprietary drawings, personal data, credentials, or other protected information to unapproved public AI systems.
