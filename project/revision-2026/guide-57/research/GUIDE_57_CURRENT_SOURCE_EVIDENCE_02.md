## Research purpose

This evidence package refreshes the legacy guide with current authoritative material for medical-equipment repair/service work, including occupational outlook, education and entry pathways, medical-device safety and servicing boundaries, cybersecurity, Canada mapping, and Colombia training/tecnovigilance context.

The guide must distinguish equipment service and maintenance from clinical practice and must not imply that a general repair title authorizes remanufacturing, design changes, unapproved firmware/configuration changes, or other activities that alter a medical device's intended use or safety/performance specifications.

## United States — servicing versus remanufacturing

### U.S. Food and Drug Administration — Remanufacturing and Servicing Medical Devices

Source: FDA, **Remanufacturing and Servicing Medical Devices**. Updated February 2, 2026.  
URL: https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/quality-and-compliance-medical-devices/remanufacturing-and-servicing-medical-devices

Controlled boundary:

- FDA distinguishes **servicing** from **remanufacturing**.
- Servicing generally means repair, preventive maintenance, or routine maintenance intended to return a finished device to the original equipment manufacturer's safety and performance specifications and original intended use.
- Remanufacturing involves processing, conditioning, renovating, repackaging, restoring, or other activity that significantly changes a finished device's performance or safety specifications or intended use.
- A worker or employer must not assume that calling an activity "repair" makes it servicing if the activity materially changes device performance, safety, specifications, or intended use.
- Poor-quality servicing can contribute to malfunctions and adverse events.
- The FDA Quality Management System Regulation (QMSR) became effective **February 2, 2026**, incorporating ISO 13485:2016 by reference into device current good manufacturing practice requirements for regulated manufacturers.

The guide should describe QMSR as manufacturer/regulatory context, not as a blanket certification requirement for every repair employee.

### FDA — Final Guidance on Remanufacturing Medical Devices

Source: FDA, **Remanufacturing of Medical Devices — Guidance for Industry, Food and Drug Administration Staff, and Third Party Entities**. Final guidance, May 2024.  
URL: https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/remanufacturing-medical-devices

Use:

- Reinforces the need to determine whether activities are servicing or remanufacturing based on what the activity does to the finished device, not merely the business's label for the work.
- Supports a fail-closed instruction: escalate engineering/regulatory questions before performing modifications outside authorized service procedures.

## Medical-device cybersecurity

### FDA — Cybersecurity in Medical Devices

Source: FDA Digital Health Center of Excellence, **Cybersecurity**.  
URL: https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/digital-health-center-excellence/cybersecurity

Relevant current context:

- FDA updated its final premarket medical-device cybersecurity guidance on **June 27, 2025**.
- Connected medical devices can have software vulnerabilities and require lifecycle cybersecurity risk management.
- Servicing personnel may encounter firmware, service software, network interfaces, credentials, logs, removable media, configuration files, and vendor remote-access mechanisms.

### FDA — Postmarket Management of Cybersecurity in Medical Devices

Source: FDA, **Postmarket Management of Cybersecurity in Medical Devices**.  
URL: https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/postmarket-management-cybersecurity-medical-devices

Guide controls derived from this context:

- Do not install unapproved firmware/software or make undocumented network/configuration changes.
- Use only authorized service tools, accounts, and access methods.
- Protect credentials, service keys, device logs, patient data, and configuration backups.
- Follow employer/OEM change control and vulnerability-response procedures.
- Escalate suspected compromise or abnormal device/network behavior through approved incident-response channels.
- Generative AI must not receive protected patient information, credentials, proprietary service data, device secrets, or confidential configuration material unless an explicitly approved system and use case permits it.

These are safety/security controls, not a claim that every repairer is a cybersecurity engineer.

## Colombia — SENA pathway

### SENA — Mantenimiento de Equipo Biomédico

Official SENA normogram sources list **Mantenimiento de Equipo Biomédico** as a **Tecnólogo** program with a **24-month** duration.

Useful official references:

- SENA Resolución 472 de 2014: https://normograma.sena.edu.co/compilacion/docs/resolucion_sena_0472_2014.htm
- SENA Resolución 1843 de 2013: https://normograma.sena.edu.co/compilacion/docs/resolucion_sena_1843_2013.htm

A more recent SENA Circular 22 de 2024 lists **MANTENIMIENTO DE EQUIPO BIOMÉDICO** as a **Tecnólogo (TG)**, **Presencial**, associated with the **Centro de Automatización Industrial** in Caldas:

https://normograma.sena.edu.co/compilacion/docs/circular_sena_0022_2024.htm

Controlled interpretation:

- Colombia has an official public-training pathway directly aligned to biomedical-equipment maintenance.
- Historical/normative listings do not guarantee that a cohort is open today. Readers must verify current availability, admission requirements, center, schedule, delivery mode, and current program status through SENA/Betowa before paying or relocating.
- A SENA program listing is not itself authorization to ignore employer, manufacturer, health-facility, electrical-safety, metrology, radiation, or medical-device regulatory requirements.

## Colombia — medical-device technovigilance

### INVIMA — Programa Nacional de Tecnovigilancia

Source: Instituto Nacional de Vigilancia de Medicamentos y Alimentos (INVIMA), **Programa Nacional de Tecnovigilancia**.  
URL: https://www.invima.gov.co/productos-vigilados/dispositivos-medicos/programa-nacional-de-tecnovigilancia

Current controls:

- Tecnovigilancia is Colombia's post-market surveillance system for identifying, qualifying, evaluating, managing, and preventing adverse events/incidents associated with medical devices.
- INVIMA describes local and institutional responsibilities for reporting and managing device-related events and incidents.
- The official material explicitly notes that information can be captured through **corrective-maintenance reports for biomedical equipment** when patient or operator health has been implicated.
- INVIMA lists an active 2026 technovigilance training schedule, confirming the program remains operational/current.

Guide consequence:

A repairer should not conceal, erase, or privately "fix and forget" a device incident that may have affected patient/operator safety. Follow the employer's technovigilance, safety-event, quality, manufacturer, and regulatory reporting pathway.

## Editorial and safety controls for the 2026 English master

The revised guide must:

1. Separate **medical equipment repair/service** from clinical diagnosis or treatment decisions.
2. Preserve the FDA servicing/remanufacturing boundary.
3. Tell readers not to bypass OEM procedures, safety interlocks, calibration requirements, change control, or employer authorization.
4. Address electrical, mechanical, battery, pressure, radiation/laser, biological/infection, and network/cybersecurity hazards where applicable to the equipment being serviced.
5. Require documented preventive maintenance, calibration/verification, parts/service records, and escalation according to employer/OEM/regulatory procedures.
6. Treat firmware/software/configuration changes as controlled technical changes, not casual repair steps.
7. Protect patient information, device credentials, logs, service keys, configuration files, and proprietary manuals.
8. Include current U.S. wage/outlook data without promising earnings.
9. Present Canada's mapping as a broader occupational-family comparison, not automatic credential equivalence.
10. Present SENA's biomedical-equipment maintenance pathway and INVIMA technovigilance without claiming that U.S./Canadian credentials transfer automatically to Colombia.
11. Include free-first funding locators and explicit scam/spending-pause checks.
12. Preserve accessibility and reasonable-accommodation guidance.
13. Make no claim of independent human certification, professional translation certification, legal/regulatory approval, manufacturer authorization, accreditation, licensure approval, employment guarantee, or earnings guarantee.
