## Research boundary

This register supports the controlled 2026 revision of Guide 23. It distinguishes property and community-association management from activities that may require real-estate, strata, condominium, community-association, legal, accounting, trust-account, building-trade, safety, or other jurisdiction-specific authority. Requirements vary by country, state/province, municipality, employer, property type, and assigned duties. Learners must verify current rules before paying for training or accepting regulated responsibilities.

## United States — occupation, work, education, and income

### O*NET / U.S. Department of Labor

**Source:** O*NET OnLine, occupation 11-9141.00, *Property, Real Estate, and Community Association Managers*  
**URL:** https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-9141.00  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

O*NET describes this occupation as planning, directing, or coordinating the selling, buying, leasing, and governance activities of commercial, industrial, or residential real estate. The occupation includes managers of rented or leased housing and community associations such as homeowners' and condominium associations. Duties can include resident and owner communications, contracts, budgets, maintenance, rule administration, records, vendor coordination, inspections, collections, leasing, and operational oversight, depending on the job.

**Current official wage reference:** O*NET's national wage view incorporates BLS 2025 wage data and reports a U.S. median of **$69,990 per year / $33.65 per hour**, with approximately **$41,010** at the 10th percentile and **$139,680** at the 90th percentile. These are occupation-level statistical estimates, not guaranteed individual earnings.

## United States — licensing and regulated-activity boundary

The BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook notes that licensing requirements vary by state. In most states, property managers must have either a property-management licence or a real-estate broker licence when their duties fall within regulated real-estate activity; buying or selling real estate requires a real-estate licence, and some states require community-association managers to hold a real-estate or other credential. Public-housing roles may have additional certification requirements.

The guide must therefore avoid implying that a generic property-management course authorizes brokerage, leasing, trust-account handling, eviction practice, legal representation, or community-association management everywhere. Learners should verify the state real-estate commission or other applicable regulator and the employer's assigned duties before paying for a licence-preparation program.

## Colombia — propiedad horizontal pathway and authority boundary

### Ley 675 de 2001

**Primary official source:** Secretaría del Senado, Ley 675 de 2001  
**URL:** https://www.secretariasenado.gov.co/senado/basedoc/ley_0675_2001.html  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

**Official legal-system cross-check:** SUIN-Juriscol  
**URL:** https://suin-juriscol.gov.co/viewDocument.asp?id=1665811  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

Ley 675 de 2001 governs Colombia's **régimen de propiedad horizontal**. The statutory framework assigns governance and administration functions among the assembly of owners, the board/council where applicable, and the administrator. The administrator's authority derives from the law, the building's governing documents, valid decisions of the competent governing bodies, and the specific appointment or contract; it is not unlimited professional authority.

The Colombia edition must distinguish property-horizontal administration from real-estate brokerage, legal advice, accounting/audit authority, licensed technical work, public-notary functions, and governmental registry authority. It should tell learners to verify the current law, building regulations, contracts, insurance requirements, municipal rules, and any sector-specific obligations before assuming responsibility.

### SENA / public employment and skills pathways

**Source:** SENA Agencia Pública de Empleo (APE), current property-horizontal administrator demand examples  
**Verified:** 2026-08-10

Current SENA APE vacancy descriptions for property-horizontal administration include functions such as customer/resident service, collection of administration fees under applicable rules, supervision of security/maintenance/cleaning services, implementation of property-horizontal programs, contracting/coordination of services, administrative meetings, and inventory control. These are useful labor-market task examples, not a universal legal scope of practice.

SENA's public employment services are free. Learners in Colombia should use **SENA / Betowa** and the **Agencia Pública de Empleo** to search for current free or low-cost offerings in administration, accounting/bookkeeping, customer service, digital records, conflict management, facilities support, occupational safety, and related skills, and to inspect current employer demand.

**Important research limitation:** no current dedicated SENA/Betowa "administración de propiedad horizontal" training course was verified in this research session. The guide must therefore not advertise a specific SENA property-horizontal course as currently available. Availability should be rechecked directly in SENA systems at application time.

## Latin America — country-specific pathway

Condominium, co-ownership, horizontal-property, tenancy, consumer, labor, tax, data-protection, fire-safety, building, and real-estate rules differ materially across Latin America. A credential or scope of authority in Colombia does not automatically transfer to Mexico, Brazil, Chile, Peru, Argentina, Central America, or another jurisdiction.

The revised guide should direct learners to the competent national, state/provincial, municipal, housing, condominium, real-estate, tax, labor, privacy, and safety authorities for the country where the work will be performed. Public technical institutes, employer training, national employment services, and official scholarship programs should be checked before commercial programs.

## Privacy, cybersecurity, access control, and financial handling

Property managers may hold sensitive resident, owner, applicant, employee, visitor, vendor, bank, payment, identity, lease, complaint, camera/access-control, emergency-contact, and building-security data. The guide should teach data minimization, least-privilege access, secure payment practices, strong authentication, approved record-retention procedures, incident escalation, and careful vendor/system access.

Publicly observable property information does not make resident or owner data unrestricted. Workers should follow applicable privacy laws, contracts, association policies, employer security rules, and payment-card/banking controls. They should never alter financial, access-control, lease, incident, or compliance records to conceal errors or disputes.

## AI use — controlled safety position

AI may assist with study plans, checklists, plain-language explanations, first drafts of routine communications, non-authoritative summaries, maintenance categorization, and training exercises. It must not be presented as an authoritative source for legal rights, eviction, lease interpretation, fair-housing decisions, disability accommodation, applicant screening, rule enforcement, emergency/safety decisions, budgets, trust-account treatment, vendor legal compliance, or governing-document interpretation.

High-impact decisions must be verified against authoritative documents and current law and escalated to the appropriately authorized manager, board, lawyer, accountant, regulator, licensed contractor, safety professional, or other specialist when required. Sensitive resident, owner, applicant, payment, access-control, or security data should not be uploaded to external AI tools unless the organization has explicitly authorized that use and applicable privacy, confidentiality, security, contractual, retention, and vendor requirements are satisfied.
