## Editorial reconstruction review

The English Version 2.0 candidate is a controlled reconstruction rather than a cosmetic rewrite of the legacy July 2026 package. It contains 22 numbered sections and directly addresses the expanded opportunity standard.

### Occupation scope and title accuracy — PASS

The candidate distinguishes CNC operator, setup operator/technician, machinist, and CNC/CAM programmer rather than treating them as interchangeable. It describes drawing interpretation, workholding, tooling, offsets, machine operation, manual machining, dimensional inspection, troubleshooting, documentation, and escalation boundaries in occupation-appropriate language.

The BLS occupation is represented as machinists rather than incorrectly assigning tool-and-die-maker scope to all readers.

### Safety and scope boundaries — PASS

The candidate includes machine guarding, moving/rotating equipment, entanglement, chip/workholding hazards, metalworking fluids, setup/maintenance/troubleshooting boundaries, and explicit stop/escalate conditions. It does not instruct readers to defeat guards, interlocks, lockout/tagout controls, quality controls, or employer/manufacturer procedures.

It avoids machine-specific step-by-step hazardous operating instructions and correctly frames PPE as task-dependent, including the entanglement risk that gloves can create around rotating machinery.

### Entry, training, funding, and free-first pathways — PASS

No funding, scholarship, reimbursement, apprenticeship, or placement is promised.

### Compensation evidence — PASS

The candidate separates official and non-government evidence:

- BLS machinist-specific May 2024 median: **$56,150/year**;
- BLS machinist-specific 2024–2034 employment change: approximately **0%**;
- combined machinists/tool-and-die-makers projected openings: about **34,200/year**;
- Indeed July 20, 2026 CNC Machinist average: approximately **$28.06/hour**, explicitly labeled a **non-government market estimate**;

The candidate does not convert these figures into guaranteed personal income or a Latin America benchmark.

### Credential boundaries — PASS

NIMS is described as an industry credentialing pathway, not a government license or universal employer requirement. The candidate includes the current NIMS policy boundary that credentials earned on or after June 30, 2026 are subject to a five-year validity/renewal cycle, while older credentials are not retroactively placed under that new rule.

Canada Red Seal is described as a national trade standard/endorsement framework administered through provincial and territorial apprenticeship/certification authorities rather than as one national licensing office.

### Canada accuracy — PASS

The candidate identifies Machinist as Red Seal NOC 72100, notes the national harmonization recommendation of four training levels and 7,200 total apprenticeship hours, and warns that implementation varies by jurisdiction.

It correctly states the current Canada Apprentice Loan as up to C$4,000 interest-free per period of technical training for eligible apprentices in designated Red Seal trades and does not present the former Apprenticeship Incentive Grant or Apprenticeship Completion Grant as open 2026 grants.

### Colombia and Latin America — PASS

It identifies APE as a free public employment service and treats individual vacancies as examples rather than national standards.

Broader Latin America coverage is explicitly jurisdiction-bound and avoids exporting U.S., Canadian, Colombian, or NIMS rules to other countries.
