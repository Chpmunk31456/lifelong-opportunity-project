## Research purpose and control boundary

This evidence record refreshes the legacy July 2026 source notes before English Version 2.0 reconstruction. It separates official occupational, safety, training, funding, and credential sources from a clearly labeled non-government compensation estimate. It does **not** treat any credential, course, apprenticeship, or guide text as a license, statutory authorization, independent human certification, accreditation, legal advice, safety approval, or guarantee of employment or income.

Jurisdiction-specific rules, employer requirements, apprenticeship registration, credential validity, funding eligibility, and training availability can change. The English master must tell readers to verify current requirements with the responsible authority, employer, training provider, or funding program before paying, enrolling, relocating, or relying on a credential.

## 1. United States occupation scope, work, education, pay, and outlook

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Occupational Outlook Handbook

Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, **Machinists and Tool and Die Makers**.  
URL: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/production/machinists-and-tool-and-die-makers.htm  
Source page last modified: August 28, 2025; verified for this controlled revision August 11, 2026.

Controlled facts suitable for Guide 33:

- Machinists set up and operate equipment to produce precision metal parts, instruments, and tools.
- Work commonly includes reading blueprints, sketches, CAD/CAM files, setting up and operating manual and CNC machine tools, adjusting cutting tools and workpieces, monitoring feeds and speeds, turning/milling/drilling/grinding, and verifying finished parts against requirements.
- BLS says machinists typically need a high school diploma to enter the occupation and are typically trained on the job; some learn through training or apprenticeship programs, vocational schools, or community and technical colleges.
- Median annual wage for **machinists** was **$56,150 in May 2024**. The lowest 10 percent earned less than **$38,100**, and the highest 10 percent earned more than **$78,760**.
- For the combined machinists/tool-and-die-makers profile, BLS reports 2024–2034 overall employment projected to decline 2 percent, but **machinists specifically are projected at 0 percent change** over 2024–2034.
- BLS projects about **34,200 openings per year**, on average, for machinists and tool and die makers over the decade, primarily from replacement needs.
- The English master must avoid presenting the combined occupation’s $57,700 figure as the machinist-specific median; the machinist-specific figure is $56,150.

### O*NET OnLine — Machinists 51-4041.00

Source: O*NET OnLine, **Machinists (51-4041.00)**.  
URL: https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/51-4041.00  
Page indicates: Updated 2026; verified August 11, 2026.

Controlled facts:

- O*NET describes machinists as workers who set up and operate machine tools to produce precision parts and instruments from metal and may fabricate or modify parts used to repair machine tools or industrial machines.
- Reported job titles include CNC Machinist, Gear Machinist, Maintenance Machinist, Manual Lathe Machinist, Production Machinist, and Tool Room Machinist.
- This supports explaining that “machinist” and “CNC machine operator” overlap but are not always identical jobs; employers may separate operator, setup, programmer, inspector, and machinist responsibilities.

## 2. U.S. apprenticeship and work-based learning

### Apprenticeship.gov occupation/industry evidence

Sources:  
- Apprenticeship.gov, **Occupation Finder: Machinists**, https://www.apprenticeship.gov/apprenticeship-occupations/listings?occupationCode=51-4041.00  
- Apprenticeship.gov, **Advanced Manufacturing**, https://www.apprenticeship.gov/apprenticeship-industries/advanced-manufacturing  
Verified August 11, 2026.

Controlled interpretation:

- The Occupation Finder page for O*NET 51-4041.00 currently states that the specific “Machinists” occupation entry is **not currently approved for use in a Registered Apprenticeship Program**.
- However, Apprenticeship.gov’s Advanced Manufacturing page lists **CNC Machine Operator** and **Precision Machinist** among high-demand apprenticeship occupations.
- Therefore Guide 33 must not make the inaccurate blanket statement “Machinist is a federally approved Registered Apprenticeship occupation.” It may instead direct readers to Apprenticeship.gov and state that apprenticeship pathways exist in advanced manufacturing under occupation titles such as CNC Machine Operator and Precision Machinist, while exact occupation approval and program availability must be checked for the specific program and jurisdiction.
- BLS also states that some machinists learn through apprenticeship programs, and directs readers to Apprenticeship.gov or state apprenticeship offices for opportunities.

## 3. U.S. training funding, scholarships, and employer support

### Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act (WIOA)

Sources:  
- U.S. Department of Labor, **Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act**, https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/wioa  
- U.S. Department of Labor, **Workforce Development Solutions**, https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/employers/workforce-development-solutions  
- U.S. Department of Labor, **TEGL 08-19**, https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/advisories/training-and-employment-guidance-letter-no-08-19  
Verified August 11, 2026.

Controlled facts:

- WIOA is designed to help job seekers access employment, education, training, and support services and to help employers hire and retain skilled workers.
- DOL states that eligible individuals can qualify for **Individual Training Accounts (ITAs)** to pay for training, subject to local eligibility and an eligible training provider/program.
- WIOA permits several training mechanisms, including occupational skills training, on-the-job training, incumbent-worker training, workplace training combined with related instruction, skill upgrading/retraining, and customized training.
- Guide 33 should tell readers to ask their local American Job Center/workforce board whether a machining/CNC program is on the state Eligible Training Provider list and whether tuition, tools, transportation, supportive services, or work-based training may be covered. It must not promise eligibility or payment.

### CareerOneStop Scholarship Finder

Source: CareerOneStop, **Scholarship Finder**, https://www.careeronestop.org/Toolkit/Training/find-scholarships.aspx  
Verified August 11, 2026.

Controlled use:

- CareerOneStop provides a searchable scholarship database and warns users to confirm current sponsor deadlines and eligibility.
- Manufacturing-related awards can appear in the database, including scholarships connected with manufacturing education.
- Guide 33 may recommend this as a scholarship discovery tool but must not promise that a particular scholarship is open or applicable to machinist/CNC training without current sponsor verification.

### Employer support

BLS and DOL evidence supports a practical “earn while learning” path through employer training, work-based learning, apprenticeship where available, incumbent-worker training, or customized training. The guide should advise readers to ask employers about paid training, tuition reimbursement, tool/PPE allowances, shift differentials, credential reimbursement, and structured progression from operator to setup/machinist/programming responsibilities. These benefits are employer-specific and must be verified in writing.

## 4. U.S. safety evidence

### OSHA machine guarding

Sources:  
- OSHA, **Machine Guarding — Overview**, https://www.osha.gov/machine-guarding/  
- OSHA, **Machine Guarding eTool — General Requirements**, https://www.osha.gov/etools/machine-guarding/introduction/general-requirements  
Verified August 11, 2026.

Controlled facts:

- Moving machine parts can cause severe injuries including crushing, amputations, burns, and blindness.
- OSHA states that machine parts, functions, or processes that may cause injury must be safeguarded; 29 CFR 1910.212 addresses general machine guarding requirements.
- Point-of-operation hazards, rotating parts, flying chips/sparks, and other mechanical hazards require appropriate safeguards.
- Guide 33 must not encourage bypassing guards, interlocks, lockout/tagout procedures, manufacturer instructions, or employer safety rules for setup, cleaning, measuring, chip removal, maintenance, troubleshooting, or programming.

### OSHA metalworking fluids

Sources:  
- OSHA, **Metalworking Fluids — Overview**, https://www.osha.gov/metalworking-fluids  
- OSHA, **Evaluating Exposure**, https://www.osha.gov/metalworking-fluids/exposure-evaluation  
Verified August 11, 2026.

Controlled facts:

- Metalworking fluids can expose workers through skin contact and inhalation of mist/aerosol and can contribute to skin, eye, nose, throat, and respiratory problems when poorly controlled or maintained.
- OSHA recommends controls such as obtaining SDS information, selecting less hazardous fluids where possible, maintaining fluids and machines properly, using splash controls and appropriate ventilation/exhaust, and following applicable PPE and work-practice controls.
- The guide should emphasize training and employer procedures rather than giving machine-specific operating instructions that could create a safety hazard.

## 5. Industry credentials — NIMS

Sources:  
- National Institute for Metalworking Skills (NIMS), **Credentialing**, https://www.nims-skills.org/credentialing  
- NIMS, **Machining Credentials**, https://www-d9.nims-skills.org/machining-credentials  
- NIMS, **How do I earn the Machining Level I credential?**, https://www.nims-skills.org/faq/220  
- NIMS, **Renewal Policy**, https://www.nims-skills.org/renewal-policy  
Verified August 11, 2026.

Controlled facts and boundaries:

- NIMS offers machining credentials covering areas such as Measurement, Materials & Safety; CNC Mill Operations; CNC Mill Programming, Setup & Operations; CNC Lathe Operations; CNC Lathe Programming, Setup & Operations; milling, turning, grinding, EDM, and newer advanced CNC/CAM roles.
- NIMS states that most credentials combine a knowledge exam with a performance evaluation.
- NIMS’ March 12, 2026 FAQ states that Machining Level I is made up of multiple credentials and individuals may earn combinations of those credentials.
- NIMS’ current renewal policy states that credentials earned on or after **June 30, 2026** are valid for five years unless renewed under the policy; credentials earned before that date are not retroactively placed under that new expiration rule.
- Guide 33 may present NIMS as an optional industry-recognized skills-validation pathway. It must not call NIMS a government license, imply it is universally required, or suggest that a credential substitutes for employer qualification, apprenticeship/journeyperson requirements, or jurisdictional authorization.

- These figures are official Canadian labour-market estimates tied to NOC 72100 and must be labeled by currency, reference period, and source date rather than presented as guaranteed pay.

### Red Seal — Machinist

Sources:  
- Red Seal, **Machinist**, https://red-seal.ca/eng/trades/machinist.shtml  
- Red Seal, **Machinist Occupational Standard**, https://red-seal.ca/eng/trades/machinists/overview.shtml

Verified August 11, 2026.

Controlled facts:

- Machinist is a Red Seal trade, NOC **72100**, with designation in multiple provinces and territories.
- The Red Seal occupational description includes conventional, portable, and CNC machines such as lathes, mills, grinders, drills, boring machines, EDM, and related equipment.
- The national harmonization recommendation identifies **4 levels** of technical training and **7,200 total training hours**, while implementation varies by province/territory.
- Apprenticeship registration and certification are administered by provincial/territorial authorities; Guide 33 must direct readers to the applicable authority rather than treating Red Seal information as a single national licensing process.

Controlled facts:

- The Canada Apprentice Loan currently offers up to **C$4,000 in interest-free loans per period of technical training** for eligible apprentices in designated Red Seal trades; Quebec has an alternate support system.
- Canada’s apprenticeship funding page also identifies employer/organization supports, including Apprenticeship Service funding that can provide up to **C$10,000** to eligible employers for hiring certain new first-year apprentices, subject to program rules.
- Eligible apprentices may also have access to Employment Insurance during technical training and provincial/territorial supports.
- The former Apprenticeship Incentive Grant and Apprenticeship Completion Grant are no longer open for new progression/completion dates after March 31, 2025; Guide 33 must not present those former grants as currently available 2026 funding.

## 8. Latin America coverage boundary

There is no single Latin American licensing, apprenticeship, wage, or qualification system for machinists/CNC operators. Guide 33 should therefore:

- use Colombia/SENA as a concrete regional pathway without implying it applies elsewhere;
- tell readers in Mexico, Central America, the Caribbean, South America, and Brazil to verify local technical institutes, national training services, labor ministries, employer-sponsored training, unions/industry associations, and apprenticeship or dual-education systems;
- distinguish manufacturer/vendor training and private certificates from government-recognized occupational qualifications;
- avoid converting U.S. or Canadian wages into a supposed regional salary benchmark;
- emphasize that industrial safety, labor rules, electrical/lockout procedures, environmental requirements, credential recognition, and apprenticeship arrangements are jurisdiction-specific.

## 10. Responsible AI, privacy, cybersecurity, and automation boundary

Machining increasingly involves CAD/CAM, CNC controls, networked manufacturing systems, automated inspection, digital work instructions, and sometimes AI-assisted programming or process optimization. The guide may encourage learners to understand these tools, but it must preserve the following controls:

- treat AI-generated G-code, feeds/speeds, setup instructions, dimensional interpretations, tooling choices, and process recommendations as unverified until checked through qualified workplace procedures;
- never upload employer-controlled drawings, customer files, export-controlled data, proprietary geometry, process parameters, credentials, or personal data to public AI systems without explicit authorization;
- do not bypass CNC controller security, machine interlocks, access controls, network segmentation, approved software, change-management, or inspection procedures;
- use simulation, dry-run/single-block controls, first-article inspection, qualified supervision, and employer/manufacturer procedures where applicable rather than treating AI output as production-ready;
- present automation as changing the mix of skills rather than guaranteeing either job loss or job growth. BLS specifically notes that machinists will still be needed to set up, monitor, and maintain CNC and related systems even as technology improves productivity.

## 11. Accessibility and realistic entry planning

The occupation can involve standing, repetitive motion, close visual inspection, precision measurement, noise, chips, coolant/mist exposure, material handling, and rotating machinery. Accessibility needs are individual and workplace-specific. Guide 33 should encourage readers to evaluate essential job functions, training-lab accessibility, PPE compatibility, schedule/shift requirements, and reasonable-accommodation processes rather than self-excluding based only on a diagnosis or disability label.

Remote-only expectations should be handled carefully: hands-on machining and CNC operation are predominantly site-based. Some adjacent progression paths — CAD/CAM programming, estimating, process planning, quality documentation, applications support, technical training, or certain manufacturing-engineering functions — may contain more computer-based work but are not automatically remote.
