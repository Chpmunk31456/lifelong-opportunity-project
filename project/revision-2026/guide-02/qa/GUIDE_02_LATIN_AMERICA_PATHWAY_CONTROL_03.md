# Guide 02 Latin America pathway control 03

**Guide:** 02 — Peer Support Specialist  
**Controlled revision:** 2026  
**Scope:** Latin America regional treatment for the English revision baseline  
**Status:** Evidence control; not a credential, accreditation, clinical standard, legal opinion, or independent certification

## Purpose

This control prevents the Guide 02 revision from presenting a United States-style Peer Support Specialist credential as though it were a single transferable occupation across Latin America. The English master must distinguish the general peer-support model from country-specific job titles, training rules, health-system roles, and employer requirements.

## Regional evidence baseline

### World Health Organization

WHO's 2021 normative technical package, **Peer support mental health services: Promoting person-centred and rights-based approaches**, describes peer support as a recovery-oriented, person-centred and human-rights-based service model. It is part of WHO's broader community mental-health guidance and is available in Spanish and Portuguese as well as English.

Source: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240025783

WHO's companion community mental-health guidance states that countries should develop services that protect legal capacity, reduce coercion, support recovery, and connect mental-health systems with housing, education, employment, and social-protection sectors.

Source: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240025707

**Editorial rule:** WHO establishes a defensible regional *service model*, not a single Latin American occupational license or credential. The guide must not convert WHO guidance into a claim that a person is formally qualified to practise as a nationally recognized Peer Support Specialist in any specific country.

## Country examples and limits

### Colombia

The existing Guide 02 official-source research already records Colombia-specific community mental-health pathways and Ministry of Health materials. Those pathways may be presented as relevant entry points for community and psychosocial-support work, but not as evidence of a national U.S.-style peer-support certification unless an official Colombian source explicitly establishes one.

**Required wording direction:** describe Colombia as a country where community mental-health, psychosocial, recovery, prevention, and territorial support roles may overlap with peer-support functions. Tell readers to verify current employer, EPS/IPS, local health-secretariat, SENA, and professional requirements for the exact role.

### Mexico

A current official local-government example shows the Municipality of Chihuahua operating a men's support group in 2026 that explicitly describes participants as sharing experiences and accompanying one another "entre pares," with mental-health professionals present.

Source: https://www.municipiochihuahua.gob.mx/CCS/Prensa/Un_espacio_para_dialogo_y_acompa%C3%B1amiento%3A_Invita_Municipio_a_Grupo_de_Apoyo_para_Hombres

**Editorial rule:** this is evidence that peer-oriented support practices exist in Mexico; it is not evidence of a national Peer Support Specialist credential, national wage series, or standardized occupational pathway.

## Latin America editorial requirements for Guide 02

The English master must:

1. Explain that peer support is a model of support grounded in lived experience, mutuality, recovery, dignity, and appropriate boundaries.
2. State that formal titles, certification, registration, supervision, and scope differ by country, health system, employer, and program.
3. Avoid implying automatic portability of U.S. or Canadian credentials into Latin America.
4. Use WHO guidance as the regional conceptual baseline where country-specific occupational regulation is absent or unclear.
5. Include Colombia-specific pathways where verified, while clearly separating adjacent training from a formal Peer Support Specialist credential.
6. Use country examples only to illustrate service models or pathways; do not infer national licensing, salary, or accreditation from local programs.
7. Do not invent a Latin America-wide salary range. Country compensation must be sourced separately and labeled by occupation match and methodology.
8. Encourage readers to verify current local requirements with official ministries of health, public employment services, recognized training bodies, and prospective employers.

## Safety and accessibility controls

The revised guide must also make clear that peer workers are not substitutes for licensed clinicians when clinical assessment, diagnosis, prescribing, emergency intervention, or other regulated services are required. Lived experience should never be presented as an obligation to disclose private trauma or health information to employers or the public. Accessible alternatives should be offered for readers who cannot safely or comfortably disclose personal history.

## Gate decision

**PASS for regional editorial integration.** There is sufficient official evidence to include a Latin America section based on WHO's peer-support model plus country-specific examples and verified Colombia pathways, provided the final guide preserves the credential, wage, scope, and portability limitations above.

This PASS is limited to editorial evidence intake. It does not establish publication readiness, factual completeness for every Latin American country, accessibility conformance, professional accreditation, or independent human review.
