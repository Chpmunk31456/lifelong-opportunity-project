- Colombia/Latin America text continues to prohibit inventing a directly comparable peer-support wage when one has not been verified.

### 3. Credential and scope controls — PASS

The translation does not convert frameworks or adjacent learning into licenses or mandatory credentials. It preserves these material limits:

- SAMHSA competencies are a practice framework, not a universal U.S. national license.
- U.S. state, Tribal, payer and employer requirements must be verified locally.
- Canada NOC classification does not establish identical requirements for every peer-support role.
- Colombia community mental-health and mutual-help materials do not establish a U.S.-style nationwide Certified Peer Support Specialist credential.
- WHO guidance is treated as an international conceptual framework, not a portable national occupational credential.
- Regulated clinical professions remain outside the peer-support role unless the worker separately holds the required qualification and acts within that authorized scope.

### 4. Regional-language control — PASS for intake

The translation uses broadly understandable neutral Latin American Spanish rather than country-specific slang. Where English institutional or legal terms are proper names or have no safe one-to-one regional equivalent, the official English name is retained or the Spanish explanation is qualified. Examples include BLS, SAMHSA, Job Bank, NOC, Apprenticeship.gov, Registered Apprenticeship, SENA and Servicio Público de Empleo.

Terms requiring focused terminology review before publication include:

- `peer support` / `apoyo entre pares`;
- `peer worker` / `trabajador(a) par` or `persona trabajadora par`;
- `self-disclosure` / `autorrevelación`;
- `recovery` / `recuperación`;
- `behavioral health` / `salud conductual`;
- `work-based learning` / `aprendizaje basado en el trabajo`;
- `apprenticeship` / `aprendizaje` where legal systems differ; and
- `scope` / `alcance` or `ámbito de actuación` depending on sentence context.

These terms are not automatically defective; they are flagged for consistency and regional-naturalness review.

### 5. Accessibility and readability intake — PASS

The Spanish file retains short headings, lists, direct reader instructions, explicit warnings, and plain-language explanations. The text avoids encoding-dependent decorative symbols in substantive instructions and preserves Markdown heading/list semantics suitable for later DOCX/PDF generation.

Detailed sentence-level readability, screen-reader output, DOCX style mapping, PDF tag/render behavior, and alternative-text requirements remain future gates.

A complete external-link re-test must still be run on the trilingual publication candidate before release.
