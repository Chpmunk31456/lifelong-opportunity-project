# Guide 02 — Pathways, training, apprenticeship, and income research 02

- **Guide:** 02 — Peer Support Specialist
- **Research date:** 2026-08-06
- **Scope:** United States, Canada, and Colombia, with Latin America treatment limited to evidence that can be verified without inventing a national credential or wage category
- **Control:** Source-backed editorial research for the controlled revision. This file is not independent human certification, accreditation, legal advice, medical advice, professional translation certification, or accessibility certification.

## Editorial rules established by this research

1. Treat **Peer Support Specialist** as a role whose credentialing and reimbursement rules can vary by jurisdiction and employer. Do not imply that one national U.S. credential, one Canadian credential, or one Colombian credential applies everywhere.
2. Separate **official occupational wage proxies** from **direct market estimates**. A proxy must be labeled as a proxy rather than presented as an exact peer-support wage.
3. Treat lived experience as relevant to many peer-support roles, but do not pressure readers to disclose diagnoses, treatment history, disability status, substance-use history, or other sensitive information beyond what a lawful employer or credentialing process actually requires.
4. Training examples must be described as training or pathway resources unless the issuing body explicitly states that completion confers a regulated credential.
5. Apprenticeship language must be conditional. Readers should verify active programs and sponsors in their own jurisdiction rather than being told that an apprenticeship is universally available.
6. Scholarships and employer-paid training must be presented as opportunities to search for and verify, not as guaranteed benefits.

## United States

### Official occupation and income evidence

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) includes **peer support specialists** as an example title within the broader **Community Health Workers** occupation (SOC 21-1094). BLS states that peer support specialists have experienced a mental health or substance-use disorder and are in recovery while supporting people facing similar challenges.

For Community Health Workers, BLS reports:

- 2024 median annual wage: **$51,030**
- 2024 median hourly wage: **$24.54**
- Lowest 10 percent: below **$37,930** annually
- Highest 10 percent: above **$78,560** annually
- Typical entry education: high school diploma or equivalent
- Typical post-entry preparation: short-term on-the-job training
- 2024–2034 projected employment growth: **11%**
- BLS explicitly notes that some community health workers participate in apprenticeships or other hands-on training programs and that some states require certification.

**Required label in Guide 02:** These BLS figures are an **official broader-occupation proxy**, not a direct national wage survey exclusively for peer support specialists.

Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook, Community Health Workers. Last modified August 28, 2025; accessed 2026-08-06.
https://www.bls.gov/ooh/community-and-social-service/community-health-workers.htm

### Current non-government market estimate

ZipRecruiter reports a U.S. average for **Peer Support Specialist** of approximately **$41,023/year ($19.72/hour)** as of July 16, 2026. It reports a majority range of about **$35,000–$45,000** annually, with a reported 90th-percentile figure of about **$52,000**.

**Required label in Guide 02:** This is a **non-government job-market estimate derived from job-posting and third-party data**, not an official wage statistic and not a guaranteed salary. It should be shown beside, not blended into, the BLS proxy.

Source: ZipRecruiter, Peer Support Specialist Salary, data current July 16, 2026; accessed 2026-08-06.
https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Salaries/Peer-Support-Specialist-Salary

### Certification and reimbursement caution

Medicaid policy demonstrates that peer-support provider qualifications can be state-specific. For example, CMS approved Utah State Plan Amendment **UT-26-0004** on April 30, 2026, modifying provider qualifications for peer support specialist services effective March 1, 2026.

**Revision consequence:** Guide 02 must direct U.S. readers to verify their state's behavioral-health/Medicaid authority and credentialing rules. It must not state that one U.S. credential automatically qualifies someone everywhere.

Source: Medicaid.gov, State Plan Amendments, Utah UT-26-0004; accessed 2026-08-06.
https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/medicaid-state-plan-amendments

### Apprenticeship, employer support, and funding pathway

BLS confirms that some workers in the broader Community Health Worker occupation participate in apprenticeships. Guide 02 should therefore include a verification workflow rather than promise a specific program:

1. Search Apprenticeship.gov and state workforce/apprenticeship portals for **peer support**, **behavioral health**, **recovery support**, and **community health worker**.
2. Confirm that the sponsor is active and that the program applies to the reader's state and target employer.
3. Ask behavioral-health employers whether required peer-support training, examination fees, continuing education, supervision, or onboarding are employer-paid or reimbursable.
4. Check state workforce agencies, American Job Centers, community colleges, and eligible-training-provider lists for WIOA or other workforce funding where applicable.
5. Do not describe an employer reimbursement policy, scholarship, or workforce grant as available unless the current program page confirms eligibility.

## Canada

### Official occupational and income evidence

Canada's Job Bank maps **Peer Support Worker** to **NOC 42201 — Social and community service workers**. The national wage page, updated November 19, 2025 and using 2023–2024 Labour Force Survey data, reports:

- Canada low wage: **C$19.00/hour**
- Canada median wage: **C$26.00/hour**
- Canada high wage: **C$36.06/hour**
- **83.6%** of workers in the occupation receive at least one non-wage benefit

The Job Bank summary also reported **313 advertised jobs** when the page was captured and states that prospects vary by province and territory.

Sources: Government of Canada Job Bank; pages modified June 2, 2026; accessed 2026-08-06.
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/24973/ca
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/24973/ca

### Requirements and training caution

Job Bank states that for NOC 42201, completion of a college or university program in a social-service, psychology, or health-related discipline is usually required, but previous work or volunteer/support experience may replace formal education for some occupations in the unit group. Provincial regulatory requirements may apply to some social-service-worker roles.

**Revision consequence:** This is a broad NOC group. Guide 02 must not convert these group-level requirements into a claim that every Canadian peer-support job requires the same diploma or professional licence.

Source: Government of Canada Job Bank, Peer Support Worker requirements; accessed 2026-08-06.
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/24973/ca

### Peer-support practice guidance

The Mental Health Commission of Canada's **Guidelines for the Practice and Training of Peer Support** describe structured peer support and the knowledge, values, and abilities expected in training. The Commission itself warns that the resource was published in **2013** and that its data may be out of date.

**Revision consequence:** Use the guidelines for enduring practice principles, but explicitly date them and do not present them as a current universal Canadian certification standard.

Source: Mental Health Commission of Canada; accessed 2026-08-06.
https://mentalhealthcommission.ca/resource/guidelines-for-the-practice-and-training-of-peer-support/

### Funding and employer-supported learning

Federal funding examples show that peer-support training can be funded through specific programs, but these are program-specific rather than universal entitlements. A Health Canada-funded campus peer-support pilot announced in 2022 included training and certification for student peer supporters. More recent federal mental-health funding in 2026 also supports training through named recipient organizations.

**Revision consequence:** Guide 02 may tell readers to check employers, colleges, provincial workforce programs, community mental-health organizations, and current federal/provincial grant-funded initiatives for funded training. It must not imply that a historic pilot remains open.

Sources:
https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/news/2022/05/health-canada-provides-2m-to-support-mental-health-project-for-post-secondary-students.html
https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/news/2026/05/stronger-mental-health-supports-when-emergencies-strike.html

## Colombia and broader Latin America

### Colombia pathway evidence

Colombia's Ministry of Health describes **Salud Mental Comunitaria** and currently lists 2025 guidance for community mental-health devices, psychological first aid, and territorial integrated health networks. Community devices include **Centros de Escucha**, **Zonas de Orientación Escolar**, and **Zonas de Orientación Universitaria**, among other settings.

**Revision consequence:** Guide 02 can explain that Colombia has real community mental-health and psychosocial-support pathways, but it must not relabel these structures as a nationally regulated U.S.-style “Peer Support Specialist” credential.

Source: Ministerio de Salud y Protección Social, Salud Mental Comunitaria; accessed 2026-08-06.
https://www.minsalud.gov.co/salud/publica/salud-mental/Paginas/salud-mental-comunitaria.aspx

### Free/low-cost adjacent training

SENA's Betowa catalog currently returns multiple mental-health training options, including:

- **Acciones de prevención en salud mental** — 40 hours; complementary training; virtual and/or in-person offerings depending on current availability
- **Acciones básicas para la atención de una persona con afectación de la salud** — 40 hours; complementary virtual training
- **Cuidado de la salud mental en los entornos de vida** — 48 hours; complementary training with role-specific prerequisites on the cited offering

**Required label in Guide 02:** These are **adjacent training pathways**, not evidence of a national Peer Support Specialist credential. Availability, modality, location, prerequisites, and enrollment periods can change.

Sources: SENA Betowa; accessed 2026-08-06.
https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta?search=salud+mental

### Colombia income treatment

No authoritative national Colombian wage series specific to the English-language title **Peer Support Specialist** was identified in this research pass. The controlled edition therefore must **not invent a COP salary band** or silently substitute psychologist, social worker, or nursing wages.

A future Colombia compensation section may use clearly labeled current job-posting evidence for genuinely comparable community mental-health or peer-support roles, but only after title, duties, location, employment type, and date are verified.

## Latin America editorial treatment

Across Latin America, occupational titles, formal recognition, funding mechanisms, and community mental-health structures differ materially by country. The guide should:

- use country-specific official health, training, and employment portals;
- distinguish volunteer peer-support activity from paid employment;
- avoid converting a course certificate into a regulated professional credential;
- verify whether work involving clinical assessment or treatment is reserved to licensed professionals;
- describe compensation only where comparable role evidence exists.
