## Review controls

### 1. Core role terminology — PASS

The three editions preserve the role as peer support rather than clinical practice.

Controlled concepts remain aligned:

- `peer support` → Spanish `apoyo entre pares` → Portuguese `apoio entre pares`;
- `peer worker` → Spanish contextual forms such as `persona trabajadora par` / `trabajador(a) par` → Portuguese `trabalhador de apoio entre pares` or equivalent contextual phrasing;
- `lived experience` → Spanish `experiencia vivida` → Portuguese `experiência vivida`;
- `recovery` → Spanish `recuperación` → Portuguese `recuperação`;
- `self-disclosure` → Spanish `autorrevelación` → Portuguese `autorrevelação`;
- `mutuality` → Spanish `mutualidad` → Portuguese `mutualidade`;
- `behavioral health` → Spanish `salud conductual` → Portuguese `saúde comportamental`.

None of these translations converts the role into psychotherapy, diagnosis, licensed casework, or another regulated clinical function.

### 2. Credential and scope language — PASS

All three editions preserve the high-impact limitations that:

- SAMHSA core competencies are a practice framework, not a universal U.S. national license;
- state, Tribal, payer, program, and employer requirements must be verified locally in the United States;
- Canada Job Bank's NOC 42201 mapping is a broader occupational classification and does not impose identical requirements on every peer-support role;
- Colombia community mental-health and mutual-help materials do not create a U.S.-style nationwide Certified Peer Support Specialist credential;
- WHO guidance is an international conceptual resource and does not create a portable national occupational credential; and
- peer-support experience does not itself qualify a worker for regulated professions such as counseling, psychology, social work, or nursing.

### 3. Apprenticeship and work-based-learning terminology — PASS with controlled contextualization

The English source distinguishes `apprenticeship` from the broader concept of `work-based learning`.

Spanish uses contextual terms including `aprendizaje`, `aprendizaje basado en el trabajo`, and the official proper name `Registered Apprenticeship` where a direct legal equivalence would be unsafe. The text explicitly warns that the word `aprendizaje` may describe systems that do not map directly to U.S. apprenticeship structures.

Portuguese uses `aprendizagem profissional` for apprenticeship contexts and `aprendizagem baseada no trabalho` for the broader concept, while retaining `Registered Apprenticeship` as the official U.S. program label where appropriate. The text likewise warns against assuming legal-system equivalence.

This treatment is intentionally descriptive rather than asserting cross-border credential portability.

### 4. Income and numeric/date parity — PASS

The three editions preserve the controlled distinctions and values already accepted by automated parity QA:

- BLS Community Health Workers remains a clearly labeled **official occupational proxy**, not a dedicated peer-support wage series;
- May 2024 BLS median remains **US$51,030 / US$24.54 per hour** in English, with locale-appropriate numeric punctuation in Spanish/Portuguese;
- projected BLS growth remains **11% for 2024–2034**;
- ZipRecruiter remains explicitly **non-government market data** at **US$41,023/year / US$19.72/hour as of August 7, 2026**;
- Canada Job Bank remains the broader **NOC 42201** grouping with **C$19.00/C$26.00/C$36.06** national low/median/high values, table update **November 19, 2025**, and **2023–2024** reference period; and
- Colombia/Latin America text continues to prohibit inventing a directly comparable national peer-support wage where none has been verified.

Locale-specific punctuation (`51,030` versus `51.030`, `24.54` versus `24,54`) is treated as correct localization, not numeric disagreement.

### 5. Funding, scholarships, employer support, and free-first framing — PASS

All editions retain the same decision sequence:

1. start with official/public/free learning;
2. determine the exact credential or employer requirement;
3. search public workforce and employer-paid funding before paying personally;
4. compare recognized public, nonprofit, technical, college, and work-based options; and
5. pay only when a verified next step is established.

Regional pathways remain represented for the United States, Canada, Colombia, and broader Latin America without implying that a listed funding route is automatically available or that all training is credential-qualifying.

### 6. Accessibility and inclusion — PASS

The three editions preserve coverage of accessible documents, screen-reader compatibility, captioning, interpreters, quiet testing environments, scheduling flexibility, ergonomic or assistive technology, mobility access, accessible transportation, language support, and disability-related accommodations.

The language directs readers to verify separate accommodation processes without encouraging unnecessary disclosure of medical or disability information.

### 7. Privacy, cybersecurity, and ethical AI — PASS

All editions preserve the core controls:

- use only employer-approved systems and AI tools;
- do not put protected health information, confidential participant data, case notes, credentials, background-check information, or proprietary employer material into a public AI service unless explicitly authorized;
- use multifactor authentication where required or available;
- minimize collected/recorded data; and
- do not fabricate services, participant contacts, credentials, clinical conclusions, or documentation.

### 8. Natural-language and regional-readability review — PASS

The Spanish edition reads as broadly understandable neutral Latin American Spanish and avoids country-specific slang. Proper names such as SAMHSA, BLS, Job Bank, NOC, SENA, Servicio Público de Empleo, Apprenticeship.gov, and Registered Apprenticeship are retained where translating them could imply a false official local name.

The Portuguese edition uses Brazilian Portuguese vocabulary, agreement, accentuation, number formatting, and reader-directed phrasing. Proper names and foreign institutional terms are retained where necessary for source accuracy.

Both translations preserve short headings, direct instructions, lists, warnings, and plain-language explanations rather than reproducing English syntax mechanically.

### 9. Safety and non-guarantee framing — PASS

No edition promises:

- employment;
- a guaranteed wage;
- universal credential transfer;
- free training;
- automatic recognition of a foreign credential; or
- suitability of the occupation for every reader.

The three editions preserve the instruction to verify high-impact requirements before paying, borrowing, relocating, accepting employment, or signing repayment/service agreements.

### 10. Assurance-language control — PASS

No edition claims independent human certification, professional translation certification, accessibility certification, accreditation of the guide itself, legal review, medical review, or financial advice.

Automated and AI-assisted QA is represented only as internal project quality control.
