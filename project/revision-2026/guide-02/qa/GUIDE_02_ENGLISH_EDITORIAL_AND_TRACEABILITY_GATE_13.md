## 1. Editorial and natural-readability review

Reviewed the complete English working master for spelling, grammar, punctuation, sentence construction, heading consistency, tone, and avoidable machine-like phrasing.

Result: **PASS at working-master level.**

Observations:

- The guide consistently uses direct reader-facing language and avoids promises of employment, free training, credential portability, or guaranteed wages.
- Long-form caution language is concentrated where the reader is making high-impact decisions (payment, credentialing, privacy, crisis escalation, employer repayment), rather than repeated mechanically in every section.
- Terminology remains consistent around `peer support specialist`, `peer worker`, `peer support worker`, `credential`, `certification`, `official proxy`, `market estimate`, `work-based learning`, and `employer-supported learning`.
- The text distinguishes clinical authority from peer-support scope and avoids representing peer work as psychotherapy, diagnosis, medication management, legal advice, or emergency clinical response.
- The text avoids representing adjacent Colombia training as a formal U.S.-style Peer Support Specialist credential.

### United States occupational proxy and outlook

Claim family: BLS identifies peer support specialists as a type of Community Health Worker; Community Health Workers had a May 2024 median wage of $51,030 ($24.54/hour) and projected employment growth of 11% from 2024 to 2034.

Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook — Community Health Workers.
URL: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/community-and-social-service/community-health-workers.htm
Live revalidation: 2026-08-07.
Result: **SUPPORTED AS AN OFFICIAL PROXY.**

Control: The working master visibly states that BLS does not provide a dedicated peer-support-only wage series and does not represent the Community Health Worker wage as an exact Peer Support Specialist wage.

## 5. Scope, safety, and ethical-AI controls
