## Control status

- **Purpose:** Controlled factual-source intake for the English revision.
- **Date checked:** 2026-08-06.
- **Scope:** United States, Canada, and Colombia official/public-interest sources relevant to role definition, training, practice, reimbursement context, and community pathways.

## United States

### SAMHSA — Core Competencies for Peer Workers in Behavioral Health Services

Source: https://www.samhsa.gov/substance-use/recovery/peer-support-workers/core-competencies

Controlled use in Guide 02:

- Treat peer support as recovery-oriented, person-centered, voluntary, relationship-focused, and trauma-informed.
- Present competencies as a training and workforce-development framework, not as a universal license or credential.
- State clearly that state, payer, program, and employer requirements can differ.
- Preserve lived experience as a defining feature of peer work while avoiding language that pressures readers to disclose private health information during a job search.

Source status checked 2026-08-06; SAMHSA page reports last update 2026-03-24.

### CMS/Medicaid — Peer Support Services FAQ

Source: https://www.medicaid.gov/federal-policy-guidance/2024-06-05/165641

Controlled use in Guide 02:

- Explain that Medicaid/CHIP coverage can support peer services, but implementation and qualification requirements are jurisdiction- and program-specific.
- Do not imply that Medicaid coverage creates a single national certification requirement or guarantees reimbursement to an individual worker.

Published 2024-06-05; rechecked 2026-08-06.

## Canada

### Mental Health Commission of Canada — Peer Support

Source: https://mentalhealthcommission.ca/what-we-do/access/peer-support/

Controlled use in Guide 02:

- Define peer support as a supportive relationship grounded in shared lived experience.
- Note that peer support may be delivered in peer-run organizations, workplaces, schools, and healthcare settings.
- Present peer support as complementary to clinical care rather than as a substitute for regulated clinical practice.

### Mental Health Commission of Canada — Guidelines for the Practice and Training of Peer Support

Source: https://mentalhealthcommission.ca/resource/guidelines-for-the-practice-and-training-of-peer-support/

Controlled use in Guide 02:

- Use the guidelines as a practice/training reference and roadmap, not as a current universal Canadian credential standard.
- Explicitly label the resource as older guidance (published 2013) and verify province-, territory-, employer-, and program-specific requirements separately.

Rechecked 2026-08-06; the MHCC resource itself warns that its 2013 data may be out of date.

## Colombia

### Ministerio de Salud y Protección Social — Salud Mental Comunitaria

Source: https://www.minsalud.gov.co/salud/publica/salud-mental/Paginas/salud-mental-comunitaria.aspx

Controlled use in Guide 02:

- Use Colombia's community mental-health framework to explain locally relevant community-support environments and pathways.
- Point readers to current Ministry materials including community-based mental-health rehabilitation and community mental-health devices.
- Do not equate Colombia's community mental-health framework with a U.S.-style peer-support certification unless a Colombian authority explicitly establishes such a credential.
- Where discussing paid employment, instruct readers to verify the precise employer role, required education/credential, scope of practice, and any health-sector requirements before applying.

Rechecked 2026-08-06; the Ministry page currently lists community mental-health materials including 2025 guidance and territorial integrated-health-network resources.

## Revision requirements generated from this intake

1. Separate **peer support**, **peer recovery support**, and regulated clinical roles; never imply that a peer worker is a therapist or licensed clinician unless separately qualified.
2. Explain that lived experience is central to many peer-support roles while protecting readers' privacy and emphasizing voluntary, context-appropriate disclosure.
3. For the United States, distinguish national SAMHSA practice frameworks from state/program certification and Medicaid qualification rules.
4. For Canada, distinguish national guidance from provincial, territorial, employer, and program requirements; label the 2013 MHCC training guidelines as an older source.
5. For Colombia, frame pathways through current community mental-health and social/community-support systems without inventing an equivalent national peer-support credential.
6. Funding, training, scholarship, apprenticeship, employer-support, and income sections still require separate controlled evidence before publication.
7. Current non-government income estimates must be labeled as market estimates, dated, and kept distinct from official labor/wage data or broader occupational proxies.
