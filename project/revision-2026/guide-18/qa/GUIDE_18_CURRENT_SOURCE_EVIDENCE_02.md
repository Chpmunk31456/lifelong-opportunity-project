# Guide 18 — Current-source evidence 02

**Guide:** 18 — Legal Administrative Assistant and Legal Office Coordinator  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-09  
**Stage:** Research Helper  
**Status:** **PASS**

## Research rule

This evidence set refreshes the legacy Guide 18 publication baseline using current or currently applicable sources. Official occupational, government, education, workforce, and professional-responsibility sources are primary. Current private salary estimates are included only as clearly labeled market supplements and never replace government wage statistics.

## 1. United States occupation definition — O*NET

**Source:** O*NET OnLine, `43-6012.00 — Legal Secretaries and Administrative Assistants`  
https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/43-6012.00

**Current state:** O*NET page marked **Updated 2026**.

Controlled findings:

- The occupation performs secretarial duties using legal terminology, procedures, and documents.
- It may prepare legal papers and correspondence such as summonses, complaints, motions, and subpoenas.
- It may assist with legal research.
- Reported titles include Legal Administrative Assistant, Legal Coordinator, Legal Office Support Assistant, Legal Practice Assistant, Legal Secretary, Litigation Secretary, and related titles.

**Editorial control:** “may assist with legal research” must not be expanded into independent legal analysis, legal advice, representation, or unsupervised practice of law.

## 2. United States wage, employment, entry, and outlook — BLS

**Source:** U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook — Secretaries and Administrative Assistants  
https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/secretaries-and-administrative-assistants.htm

Controlled findings for **Legal secretaries and administrative assistants (SOC 43-6012)**:

- May 2024 median annual wage: **USD 54,140**.
- Employment in 2024: **156,300**.
- Projected employment in 2034: **147,300**.
- Projected 2024–2034 change: **-6%**, or **-9,000** positions.
- BLS states that high school graduates comfortable with word-processing and spreadsheet programs usually qualify for entry-level secretary/administrative-assistant positions, while legal and medical secretaries may need additional training in industry-specific terminology.

**Control:** the broader all-secretaries median of USD 47,460 is not the legal-secretary wage and must not replace the specific USD 54,140 figure.

**Source:** IRS, `IR-2026-55` / updated Section 127 educational-assistance FAQs, April 20, 2026  
https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-updates-frequently-asked-questions-about-section-127-educational-assistance-programs

Controlled findings:

- Under a qualifying Section 127 educational-assistance program, benefits up to **USD 5,250** may be excluded from an employee's gross income for calendar years 2025 and 2026, subject to the applicable rules and the employer's written plan.

**Control:** USD 5,250 is a tax-rule ceiling for qualifying benefits, not a promise that an employer offers this amount or that a particular course is reimbursable.

## 9. Unauthorized-practice and supervision boundary

**Source:** American Bar Association guidance discussing Formal Opinion 506 and Model Rules 5.3 / 5.5  
https://www.americanbar.org/news/abanews/aba-news-archives/2023/06/guidance-legal-assistants/

Controlled findings:

- Nonlawyer assistants can perform delegated functions under appropriate policies, training, and supervision.
- Whether a nonlawyer may answer a specific legal question depends on the question and what the applicable jurisdiction treats as the practice of law.
- Improper delegation can create ethical and client-protection problems.

**Mandatory editorial boundary:** the guide must clearly state that legal administrative support does not itself authorize independent legal advice, legal strategy, representation, fee-setting, establishment of an attorney-client relationship, or any task reserved by the applicable jurisdiction to an authorized lawyer or other specifically authorized professional. Local law and employer supervision control.

## 10. Generative AI and client confidentiality

**Source:** American Bar Association, Formal Opinion 512 guidance on lawyers' use of generative AI  
https://www.americanbar.org/news/abanews/aba-news-archives/2024/07/aba-issues-first-ethics-guidance-ai-tools/

Controlled findings:

- The ABA identifies competence, confidentiality, communication, supervision, candor, and fees among the professional-responsibility issues implicated by generative AI.
- Confidential client information requires protection when AI tools are used.

**Guide 18 control for nonlawyer staff:** administrative staff should use only employer-approved AI systems and workflows; must not put confidential client, prospective-client, matter, court, privileged, work-product, identity, credential, or internal law-office information into unapproved/public AI tools; and must not treat AI output as legal authority or a substitute for lawyer review.
