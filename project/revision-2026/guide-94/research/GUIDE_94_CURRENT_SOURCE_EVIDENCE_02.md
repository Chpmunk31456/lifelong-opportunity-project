## Research conclusion
Civil Engineering Technician has a direct current U.S. occupational benchmark and strong international technical comparisons. The controlled 2026 reconstruction can proceed without a research blocker if it preserves the boundary between technician/technologist support work and jurisdictionally reserved engineering approval, signing, sealing, or professional-practice authority.

- **United States primary benchmark:** O*NET-SOC **17-3022.00 — Civil Engineering Technologists and Technicians**.
- **Canada primary comparison:** NOC **22300 — Civil engineering technologists and technicians**.
- **Colombia primary comparison:** CUOC **31121 — Técnicos en construcción y arquitectura**, within group 3112 Técnicos en ingeniería civil.
- **Brazil training/occupation comparison:** **Técnico em Edificações**, associated with CBO **3121-05 / 312105 — Técnico de obras civis** in the MEC technical-course catalogue.
- **Chile progression comparison:** ChileValora **P-4100-3123-001-V04 — Jefe(a) de Obra**, active through **2028-12-30**, with a recognized education linkage to **Técnico en Obras Civiles**.
- **Peru training comparison:** SENCICO offers **Edificaciones**, a **3-year** professional technical program, plus related construction programs.

## United States — occupation, duties, preparation, and apprenticeship
O*NET 17-3022.00 is updated in **2026**. It describes applying civil-engineering theory and principles to planning, design, construction, maintenance, inspection, testing, documentation, quantities, budgets, field surveys and related technical work under engineering staff or physical scientists.

Current task and detailed-work evidence supports a revised guide covering:
- reading drawings, plans and structural specifications;
- calculating dimensions, areas, quantities and material requirements;
- drafting detailed dimensional drawings and layouts;
- estimating costs and developing project budgets;
- field survey and site-condition data collection;
- materials and structure testing;
- site inspection and conformance checks;
- construction documentation and reporting;
- CAD/civil-design, GIS and terrain/model workflows;
- project files, records, version/revision control and data quality;
- coordination with engineers, contractors and technical personnel.

O*NET places the occupation in **Job Zone Three — Medium Preparation Needed**. Current respondent education signals include postsecondary certificates, some college and associate degrees; BLS states an associate degree, preferably in civil engineering technology, is the typical entry education.

O*NET lists **Civil Engineering Technician (Nof)** as a U.S. Department of Labor-approved Registered Apprenticeship title associated with the occupation. An approved apprenticeship title does not guarantee a live opening in a particular location; readers must verify current openings through Apprenticeship.gov or local workforce services.

## United States — official wages and outlook
Two current federal views use different reference years and must not be blended without labels:

### Current O*NET/BLS wage series
O*NET's current 2026 occupation page reports **BLS 2025 wage data**:
- median hourly wage: **$31.23**;
- median annual wage: **$64,950**;
- 10th percentile: **$44,220/year**;
- 90th percentile: **$98,980/year**.

This is the newest official wage benchmark found in the controlled research review.

### BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook
BLS OOH reports May 2024 median pay of **$64,200/year** (**$30.86/hour**), employment of about **64,900** in 2024, projected growth of **2%** from 2024–2034, and about **5,500 openings per year** on average over the decade.

The revised guide should use the newer 2025 O*NET/BLS wage figure for current pay context while preserving the BLS OOH projection period and explicitly identifying each source year.

## United States — current non-government market estimates
Current market estimates vary by methodology and should be labelled as non-government context, not official statistics:

- ZipRecruiter, **2026-07-27**, reports an estimated U.S. average of **$52,811/year** (**$25.39/hour**) for the title Civil Engineering Technician, with a stated 25th–75th percentile range of roughly **$42,500–$57,500**.
- Salary.com, **2026-08-01**, reports an estimated U.S. average of **$60,968/year** for Civil Engineering Technician, with a 25th–75th percentile range of **$54,129–$67,570**.

The spread itself is useful evidence that title-specific market estimates are methodology-sensitive. These figures must never replace the official BLS/O*NET occupational benchmark or be presented as guaranteed compensation.

## Technician versus licensed/professional engineer boundary
BLS states that civil engineering technologists and technicians help civil engineers and that, because they are not licensed, they **cannot approve designs or supervise the overall project**. This boundary is central to Guide 94.

The controlled guide should distinguish support functions such as calculations, drafting, field data, testing, inspection, estimates, documentation and assigned coordination from jurisdictionally reserved professional engineering functions such as final engineering responsibility, signing/sealing, approval of designs, or representations that require a licensed/registered professional.

Titles and legal scopes differ by country, province/state and specialty. The guide must direct readers to verify local professional-practice rules rather than infer authority from a job title, software skill, diploma or years of experience.

## Canada — NOC 22300
Canada Job Bank maps civil engineering technologists and technicians to **NOC 22300**. The national summary, modified **2026-08-07**, describes technical support/services in structural, municipal, construction design and supervision, highways and transportation, water resources, geotechnical engineering and environmental protection.

Current national wage data, updated **2025-11-19**, use a 2023–2024 reference period:
- low: **C$22.00/hour**;
- median: **C$33.89/hour**;
- high: **C$51.10/hour**.

Current Job Bank requirements distinguish:
- civil engineering technologists: usually a **2- or 3-year** college program in civil engineering technology or a closely related discipline;
- civil engineering technicians: usually a **1- or 2-year** college program in civil engineering technology;
- provincial certification may be available or required for some positions;
- supervised work experience, usually **2 years**, is required before certification;
- Quebec requires membership in the regulatory body to use the title **Professional Technologist**.

Prospects vary by province/territory. The national median must not be represented as a local wage guarantee.

### Canada funding/support boundary
Canada.ca's current student-aid portal provides grants and loans for study at college, university or in a trade. Readers should use provincial/territorial student-aid systems and the federal portal to verify eligibility.

The former federal Apprenticeship Incentive Grant and Apprenticeship Completion Grant are **closed** as of 2026; they must not be advertised as current funding. Canada Apprentice Loans and other skilled-trades supports remain available for eligible designated Red Seal apprenticeships, but Civil Engineering Technician/NOC 22300 should not be assumed to be a Red Seal trade. Readers must verify program eligibility before relying on any apprenticeship support.

### Colombia wage-data warning
OCUPACOL displays a range of **COP $600,000–$6,467,913**, but explicitly warns that its salary data **do not have statistical representativeness** under the applied methodology. The revised guide may mention this only with the warning and should not use it as a reliable current national salary benchmark.

## Brazil — Técnico em Edificações / CBO 3121-05
Brazil's Ministry of Education technical-course catalogue lists **Técnico em Edificações** with a **1,200-hour minimum** and associates it with **CBO 312105 — Técnico de obras civis**. The profile includes building projects, execution planning, budgets, construction technology, maintenance and work in construction companies, design offices, jobsites and laboratories.

The catalogue also identifies relevant continuation routes including BIM, geoprocessing, building construction, works control, roads, construction materials, sanitation, hydraulic works, architecture and civil engineering.

Brazil's catalogue references **Lei nº 5.524/1968** and **Decreto nº 90.922/1985** in the professional-practice context. The revised guide must therefore avoid implying unrestricted engineering authority and should tell readers to verify current professional registration and scope requirements for the work offered.

## Safety controls
OSHA identifies construction as a high-hazard industry. Current federal construction resources identify hazards including:
- falls;
- unguarded machinery;
- struck-by heavy equipment;
- electrocution;
- silica dust and asbestos;
- trenching/excavation;
- cranes and derricks;
- confined spaces;
- highway/work-zone hazards.

Because civil engineering technicians may visit jobsites, inspect work, collect samples or test materials, the controlled guide must include task-specific hazard assessment, site authorization, required PPE, traffic/heavy-equipment separation, excavation/trench boundaries, fall protection, silica controls, heat/environmental controls and stop-work/escalation behavior. The guide is educational and must not substitute for employer/site safety procedures or OSHA/local requirements.

## Responsible AI and digital-data controls
Civil engineering technician work can involve drawings, coordinates, quantities, budgets, inspection findings, specifications and project records. AI may assist with low-consequence drafting, study explanations, templates or synthetic practice data, but must not be treated as authoritative engineering evidence.

NIST AI RMF/Generative AI guidance supports a risk-management approach. The controlled guide should require:
- no upload of protected client/site/design data to unapproved AI services;
- independent verification of calculations, units, quantities, codes and specifications;
- verification of generated formulas/scripts before use;
- preservation of source and revision lineage;
- prohibition on invented test results, inspection observations, measurements, approvals, professional seals/signatures or as-built conditions;
- human/domain review proportional to consequence.
