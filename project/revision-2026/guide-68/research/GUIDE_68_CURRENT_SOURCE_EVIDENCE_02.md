# Guide 68 — Current-Source Evidence 02

**Occupation:** Baker and Pastry Production Worker  
**Review date:** 2026-08-21  
**Gate:** Current-source Research — PASS

## Research scope and occupation boundary

Guide 68 uses **O*NET-SOC 51-3011.00 — Bakers** as the primary U.S. occupational benchmark. O*NET, updated in 2026, defines Bakers as workers who mix and bake ingredients to produce breads, rolls, cookies, cakes, pies, pastries, and other baked goods. Reported titles include Baker, Cake Decorator, Dough Mixer, Mixer, Pastry Chef, and Scaler.

The legacy title **Baker and Pastry Production Worker** is broader and employer-defined. The Version 2 guide must not imply that every baker or pastry-production worker is automatically a pastry chef, executive chef, food-service manager, bakery manager, dietitian/nutritionist, food scientist, or another higher-authority or regulated professional. Actual authority depends on the job description, employer, establishment, jurisdiction, training, and credentials.

## United States — official occupation, wages, outlook, and tasks

### O*NET/BLS 2025 benchmark

O*NET currently reports BLS 2025 national wage data for `51-3011.00`:

- median hourly wage: **USD $17.86**;
- median annual wage: **USD $37,160**;
- 10th percentile annual: **USD $28,120**;
- 25th percentile annual: **USD $32,660**;
- 75th percentile annual: **USD $44,360**;
- 90th percentile annual: **USD $49,020**.

O*NET reports approximately **249,100** Bakers employed in 2024, projected growth in the **5%–6%** range for 2024–2034, and **39,900** projected openings. The BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook reports the same 2024 employment base, projected employment of about **263,200** in 2034, **6%** growth, and about **39,900 openings each year** over the decade.

O*NET task evidence supports coverage of:

- checking raw materials and finished products for quality;
- measuring and weighing ingredients;
- mixing doughs, batters, fillings, and icings;
- setting oven temperatures and baking times;
- operating mixers, ovens, cutters, slicers, wrapping equipment, and other bakery machinery;
- shaping dough and preparing pastries;
- applying glazes, icings, toppings, or decorations;
- monitoring equipment and adjusting time, speed, temperature, humidity, or conveyor settings within training and authority;
- cleaning production equipment and work areas;
- maintaining production, inventory, or receiving records;
- ordering or receiving supplies where delegated;
- developing recipes or presentations when the role includes that responsibility.

O*NET places Bakers in Job Zone 1–2 and states that preparation ranges from very little to some preparation, with job training ranging from a few days to one year depending on the employer and role.

### BLS May 2024 comparison

The BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook reports a **May 2024 median annual wage of USD $36,650** for Bakers. BLS states that bakers typically need no formal educational credential, although employers may prefer or require a high school diploma, and that workers generally learn through on-the-job training. Technical or culinary school and apprenticeship are additional pathways rather than universal requirements.

The May 2024 BLS wage and the 2025 O*NET/BLS wage are different reference periods and must remain date-labeled rather than treated as contradictory.

## Current non-government U.S. pay estimate

A current market estimate may be presented only as a separately labeled non-government signal:

- **Salary.com, August 1, 2026:** average Baker salary **USD $35,203/year**, about **$17/hour**, with a reported 25th–75th percentile range of **$32,953–$37,748**.

Salary.com's estimate must not be represented as a government statistic and should not be averaged with O*NET/BLS values. Employer, location, shift, overtime, union coverage, production scale, specialization, experience, and supervisory duties can materially change pay.

## United States — apprenticeship, training, funding, and scholarships

O*NET lists three example U.S. Registered Apprenticeship titles for Bakers:

- **Baker (Bake Produce)**;
- **Baker (Hotel & Restaurant)**;
- **Cook, Pastry (Hotel & Restaurant)**.

O*NET states that these titles are vetted by industry and approved by the U.S. Department of Labor for use in Registered Apprenticeship programs. This supports presenting apprenticeship as a possible earn-and-learn path, not as a guarantee that an opening exists in a particular location.

A free-first U.S. strategy should include:

- **Apprenticeship.gov** to search actual Registered Apprenticeship opportunities;
- **CareerOneStop Find Training** to locate public and local education/training programs;
- **CareerOneStop WIOA-Eligible Training Program Finder** and local American Job Centers to investigate WIOA-supported training where the individual is eligible;
- **CareerOneStop Scholarship Finder**, which currently searches thousands of scholarships, fellowships, grants, and other awards, with sponsor eligibility and deadlines requiring direct verification;
- employer-paid onboarding, food-safety training, cross-training, apprenticeship, tuition reimbursement, shift-lead development, or equipment training where offered.

Funding, scholarships, apprenticeships, admission, reimbursement, and employment are never guaranteed.

## United States — food-safety and allergen boundary

The FDA's **2022 Food Code**, with the December 2024 supplement, is a **model code** for retail and food-service safety. It is offered for adoption by state, local, tribal, territorial, and federal jurisdictions; it is not one nationwide restaurant or bakery law that automatically imposes identical credential rules everywhere.

Guide 68 must direct readers to verify the controlling health authority and employer requirements before paying for food-handler, certified-food-protection-manager, allergen, or other food-safety training.

Operational coverage should include, as applicable:

- handwashing and hygiene;
- employee illness reporting;
- receiving and storage;
- time/temperature controls;
- prevention of cross-contamination;
- cleaning and sanitizing;
- ingredient and allergen controls;
- labeling and approved recipe/specification use;
- pest control;
- waste handling;
- incident escalation and product disposition according to approved procedures.

A baker must not diagnose allergies, make unsupported medical claims, or guess whether a product is safe for a person with an allergy. Use approved ingredient, recipe, label, and production information and escalate uncertainty according to employer procedure.

## Worker-safety boundary

Bakery and pastry production can involve hot ovens, steam, hot pans, sharp tools, powered mixers, rollers, slicers, conveyors, repetitive motion, flour dust, slippery floors, lifting, awkward loads, early or overnight schedules, and rushed production.

The guide should reinforce training and employer procedures for guarding, lockout/maintenance boundaries, burns, cuts, lifting, spill response, protective equipment, fire/emergency response, and safe cleaning. Production targets never justify bypassing machine guards, sanitation rules, food-safety controls, or other safety requirements.

## Canada — official comparison

The closest Canadian national benchmark is **NOC 63202 — Bakers**. Job Bank describes bakers as preparing bread, rolls, muffins, pies, pastries, cakes, and cookies in retail/wholesale bakeries and dining establishments. Bakers who are supervisors and apprentices are included in the unit group.

Current national Job Bank wage data, updated **November 19, 2025** using the **2023–2024 Labour Force Survey** reference period, reports:

- low: **CAD $15.00/hour**;
- median: **CAD $17.50/hour**;
- high: **CAD $24.81/hour**.

Job Bank states that secondary school is usually required, on-the-job training may be provided, and a three- or four-year baker apprenticeship, a college/other baking program, or several years of commercial baking experience is usually required. Trade certification is available but voluntary in listed provinces and territories, and qualified bakers may pursue the **Red Seal** endorsement after the interprovincial examination.

Canadian requirements remain province-, territory-, employer-, and establishment-specific.

## Colombia — current public SENA pathways

SENA Betowa currently provides several relevant public examples.

### PASTELERIA — Técnico

Current SENA Betowa information lists **PASTELERIA** as a titled (`Titulada`) in-person **Técnico** program with a duration of **2,208 hours**. Competencies include preparing pastry products under production programs and current requirements, hygienizing food-production facilities according to technical procedure and sanitary rules, storage, occupational safety and health, entrepreneurship, communication, digital tools, English, and a practical stage.

The current program page also lists an additional secondary-education certificate requirement. Intake, location, prerequisites, schedule, and availability must be checked directly in Betowa when applying.

### PASTELERIA — virtual complementary training

SENA Betowa also lists **PASTELERIA** as complementary virtual training of **40 hours**. The program includes equipment, ingredients, good manufacturing practices, and pastry preparation concepts. Availability and technical prerequisites must be verified at enrollment.

### PROCESOS BASICOS DE PANADERIA

SENA Betowa lists **PROCESOS BASICOS DE PANADERIA** as a complementary in-person special course of **100 hours**, focused on preparing baked products according to the production program and applicable requirements. It is an example of a shorter public training pathway and does not by itself guarantee employment, promotion, food-safety authorization, or chef status.

No national Colombia salary benchmark is introduced by this research record.

## Latin America pathway

OIT/Cinterfor is an ILO technical service and regional vocational-training knowledge network. Its materials include a guide on integrating occupational safety and health into vocational training for the **gastronomy sector**. Guide 68 should direct readers toward national vocational-training institutions, labor/education ministries, public hospitality/food-production institutes, and OIT/Cinterfor-linked institutions rather than implying that one regional credential applies across Latin America.

When comparing programs, readers should verify recognition where relevant, total cost, uniforms/tools/materials, transportation, practical-stage requirements, food-handler or health-document requirements, schedule, accessibility/support services, and credential portability.

## Authority and professional boundaries to preserve downstream

A baker or pastry-production worker may, when trained and authorized, weigh/mix ingredients, prepare dough and pastry components, operate bakery equipment, monitor baking, decorate products, clean/sanitize work areas, rotate stock, receive supplies, maintain production records, support inventory, and assist with routine production coordination.

The guide must not imply that every worker may independently:

- diagnose allergies, food intolerance, nutrition needs, or medical conditions;
- design therapeutic diets without qualified clinical authority;
- declare food safe when required controls have not been met;
- override health-authority or employer food-safety requirements;
- alter validated recipes, allergen controls, labels, or production specifications where changes require approval;
- bypass machine guards or maintenance/energy-control procedures;
- hire, fire, or formally discipline employees unless delegated;
- sign contracts or approve major purchases unless authorized;
- alter payroll or time records outside approved procedures;
- access unrestricted employee, customer, payment, recipe, supplier, or business data;
- represent the employer to regulators unless formally designated;
- conceal food-safety incidents, injuries, harassment, theft, fraud, or other reportable events.

## Privacy, cybersecurity, accessibility, and responsible AI

The Version 2 guide should include practical controls for bakery POS, ordering, inventory, scheduling, payroll, delivery, loyalty, e-commerce, and production systems:

- use role-based/least-privilege access;
- never share passwords or system credentials;
- follow approved payment-card and customer-data procedures;
- protect employee, payroll, supplier, recipe/specification, sales, and production information;
- verify suspicious vendor, payment, password-reset, QR-code, payroll, or bank-change requests through an approved secondary channel;
- support accessible customer service and workplace practices according to employer procedure and applicable requirements without promising a particular legal outcome.

Public AI systems must not receive payment-card data, payroll or employee records, customer personal information, confidential recipes/formulas, unpublished production or sales data, credentials, incident reports, supplier banking details, or internal investigations without explicit organizational approval.

AI must not independently determine whether food is safe, whether an allergen is present, whether a medical condition exists, whether a legal/regulatory requirement applies, or whether an incident may be ignored. Human review is required for AI-assisted recipes, labels, ingredient/allergen communications, schedules, training materials, customer communications, production plans, and operational decisions.
