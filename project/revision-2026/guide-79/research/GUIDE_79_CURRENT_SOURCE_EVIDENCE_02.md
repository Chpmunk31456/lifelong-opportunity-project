# Guide 79 — Current Source Evidence 02

**Occupation:** Technical Writer and Documentation Specialist  
**Stage:** Current-source Research  
**Research date:** 2026-08-21  
**Controlled branch:** `revision/guide-00-100-2026`

## Research conclusion

Current authoritative evidence supports Guide 79 as **Technical Writer and Documentation Specialist**. The strongest cross-country occupational anchors are:

- United States: **O*NET-SOC 27-3042.00 — Technical Writers**;
- Canada: **NOC 51112 — Technical writers**;
- Colombia: **CUOC 26410 — Autores y otros escritores**, whose occupational denominations explicitly include `Autor técnico`, `Comunicador técnico de manuales`, `Escritor de documentación`, `Escritor técnico`, and `Redactor técnico`.

The evidence supports a guide centered on researching, structuring, drafting, editing, illustrating, revising, controlling and publishing technical material. The guide must distinguish documentation responsibility from engineering approval, legal/regulatory interpretation, medical/scientific authority, safety certification, cybersecurity approval, software-release authority, records-retention authority and certified translation.

## 1. United States — O*NET occupation benchmark

O*NET identifies **27-3042.00 — Technical Writers**, updated in 2026. O*NET defines the occupation as writing technical materials such as equipment manuals, appendices, operating instructions and maintenance instructions, with possible layout work.

Current O*NET tasks include:

- organizing material according to standards for order, clarity, concision, style and terminology;
- maintaining records and files of work and revisions;
- editing or standardizing material prepared by others;
- selecting photographs, drawings, diagrams and charts;
- interviewing production/engineering personnel and researching technical material;
- developing or maintaining online help documentation;
- studying drawings, specifications, mockups and product samples;
- reviewing published material and recommending revisions;
- coordinating technical specifications and subject matter with customers, vendors, executives or publishers.

O*NET places the occupation in **Job Zone Four — Considerable Preparation Needed**. It says most occupations in this zone require a four-year bachelor's degree, but some do not, and considerable related knowledge/experience is typically needed.

O*NET lists example Registered Apprenticeship titles **Technical Content Professional** and **Technical Writer (Nof)**. The existence of approved apprenticeship titles does not guarantee that an active local program is currently accepting applicants.

Sources:
- https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/27-3042.00
- https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/27-3042.00

## 9. Colombia — CUOC 26410

OCUPACOL identifies **CUOC 26410 — Autores y otros escritores**, skill level 4. Its description includes planning, researching, creating, documenting and writing manuals, specifications and other materials for publication or presentation, excluding journalistic/periodical work.

Occupational denominations explicitly include:

- Autor técnico;
- Comunicador técnico de manuales;
- Escritor de documentación;
- Escritor técnico;
- Redactor técnico.

The profile maps to **CIUO 2641 — Autores y otros escritores**, CNO 5121 — Escritores, and CNO 5122 — Editores y Redactores.

OCUPACOL displays a historical/derived salary range of COP 300,000–5,043,006, but its page explicitly states the data **do not have statistical representativeness under the methodology applied**. Therefore this guide will not use that range as a representative current salary benchmark.

Source:
- https://ocupacol.mintrabajo.gov.co/Profile/OccupationalProfile/26410

## 10. Colombia — current SENA learning options

SENA Betowa currently lists **Redacción y ortografía** as a complementary **48-hour** special course. Its competency is to write texts according to writing techniques and communication requirements. Current 2026 offerings have appeared in multiple locations/employer cohorts; cohort, location, eligibility and seat availability must be checked at enrollment time.

Betowa also lists **Habilidades cognitivas en lecto-escritura** as a complementary virtual **48-hour** program focused on effective communication and grammatical/linguistic foundations.

These are useful low-cost/free foundational writing options, but neither is represented as a complete technical-writing qualification. Technical writers also need subject-matter learning, interviewing/research, information architecture, revision control, accessibility and technical publication skills.

## 12. Plain language and audience-centered writing

Digital.gov's current Plain Language Guide Series emphasizes writing for the specific audience, organizing information in a logical order, putting important information first and using clear, short sentences. Those principles fit technical documentation when they are applied without sacrificing technical accuracy or required terminology.

A technical writer must not simplify a safety, legal, engineering, medical or regulatory instruction so aggressively that the meaning changes. When clarity and technical precision appear to conflict, resolve the issue with the responsible subject-matter authority.

Source:
- https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language

## 13. Accessible electronic documentation

Section508.gov's current guidance says federal agencies must ensure electronic documents and digital services are accessible to people with disabilities and provides authoring/testing guidance for Word, PDF, presentations and other formats. Its Word guidance emphasizes meaningful heading styles, navigable structure, image descriptions/alternative text, accessible tables and appropriate testing/remediation.

For Guide 79, practical accessibility habits should include:

- real heading styles and logical structure;
- descriptive links;
- meaningful alternative text for informative images;
- simple/appropriately marked tables;
- language identification;
- readable typography and color use;
- captions/transcripts where documentation includes synchronized media;
- accessible PDF/Word production and testing when those formats are in scope;
- web documentation aligned with applicable WCAG requirements.

A technical writer should not claim an entire document set is legally accessible based solely on an automated checker or visual review. Accessibility requirements vary by jurisdiction, format and implementation, and may require specialist testing.

Sources:
- https://www.section508.gov/create/
- https://www.section508.gov/test/documents/
- https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/

## 14. Privacy and cybersecurity boundary

Technical documentation may include internal architecture, screenshots, credentials, network details, vulnerabilities, customer information, unpublished product functions, configuration examples, security procedures and incident material.

CISA Secure Our World supports practical account-security controls such as phishing awareness, strong passwords/password managers, multifactor authentication and software updates. Guide 79 should additionally require approved repositories, least-necessary access, credential redaction, synthetic examples when appropriate, controlled review and security escalation before sensitive information is published.

The writer documents approved security behavior but does not independently invent security architecture or authorize disclosure of sensitive information.

Source:
- https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world

## 15. Responsible AI boundary

NIST's AI Risk Management Framework is voluntary risk-management guidance; the Generative AI Profile extends it for generative-AI risks. Technical writers may use approved AI tools for low-risk brainstorming, draft restructuring, terminology lists, synthetic examples, readability alternatives or code explanations when policy allows.

Human verification is mandatory for technical accuracy, source truth, safety implications, code/configuration, legal/regulatory language, licensing/provenance, privacy/security, accessibility and publication readiness. Confidential engineering information, proprietary source code, credentials, regulated data or private customer information must not be uploaded to an unapproved AI service.

Sources:
- https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
- https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence
