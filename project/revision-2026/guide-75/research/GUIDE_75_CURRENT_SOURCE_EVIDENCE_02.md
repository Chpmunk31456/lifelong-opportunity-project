# Guide 75 — Current Source Evidence

## Occupation
Marketing Coordinator and Digital Marketing Assistant

## Research date
2026-08-21

## Controlled purpose
This evidence pack supports the English revision of Guide 75. It distinguishes official labour-market evidence from current non-government market estimates and avoids pretending that the mixed title “Marketing Coordinator and Digital Marketing Assistant” maps perfectly to one occupation code in every country.

## United States — official occupation and wage evidence

### O*NET OnLine — Market Research Analysts and Marketing Specialists
Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/13-1161.00

Controlled facts:
- Closest broad U.S. benchmark for many marketing-specialist/coordinator functions: **O*NET-SOC 13-1161.00 — Market Research Analysts and Marketing Specialists**.
- O*NET reports **2025 median wages of $37.87/hour and $78,760/year**.
- Employment: about **941,700 workers in 2024**.
- Projected growth: **7% or higher from 2024 to 2034**; projected openings about **87,200 per year**.
- The occupation is broader and generally more analytical/professional than some entry-level digital-marketing assistant roles. The guide must state that titles and scope vary and must not present this classification as an exact match for every coordinator or assistant job.

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Occupational Outlook Handbook
Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/market-research-analysts.htm

Controlled facts:
- BLS reports a **May 2024 median annual wage of $76,950** for market research analysts and marketing specialists.
- Employment is projected to grow **7% from 2024 to 2034**, with about **87,200 openings per year**.
- Typical entry education for the BLS occupation is a **bachelor’s degree**.
- The guide must distinguish this professional benchmark from marketing-assistant roles where employers may accept certificates, associate-level education, portfolios, internships, customer-service/sales experience, or demonstrated digital skills.

### Current BLS wage-year control via O*NET
Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/13-1161.00

Controlled facts:
- O*NET identifies its current national values as sourced from **BLS 2025 wage data**.
- U.S. annual distribution shown: **$43,390 at the 10th percentile, $78,760 median, $155,480 at the 90th percentile**.
- These are occupation-level official statistics and should not be represented as guaranteed pay for a marketing coordinator or assistant title.

### U.S. funding and work-based-learning locator
Source: https://www.careeronestop.org/LocalHelp/EmploymentAndTraining/find-WIOA-training-programs.aspx

Controlled use:
- CareerOneStop provides state directories of WIOA-eligible training programs.
- Eligibility and funding are not automatic; readers should verify with an American Job Center.
- Relevant searches may include marketing, digital marketing, communications, business, ecommerce, analytics, web content, customer relationship management, and office/digital productivity programs.
- Registered apprenticeship availability for this occupation should not be assumed. Internships, paid assistant roles, employer training, agency trainee roles, campus marketing jobs, nonprofit campaigns, and supervised portfolio projects may be more realistic work-based-learning pathways.

## Canada — official occupation and wage evidence

### Job Bank — Marketing coordinator
Source: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/27114/ca

Controlled facts:
- Job Bank maps “marketing coordinator” to **NOC 11202 — Professional occupations in advertising, marketing and public relations**.
- Canada-wide wages, updated **November 19, 2025**, are **C$20.50 low, C$35.58 median, and C$57.44 high per hour**.
- Reference period is **2023-2024**, sourced from the Statistics Canada Labour Force Survey.
- Job Bank reports **89.1%** of workers in this occupation receive at least one non-wage benefit nationally.
- The NOC scope is broader and more professional than some assistant roles; the guide should not imply identical duties across all marketing titles.

### Government of Canada — training supports
Sources:
- https://www.canada.ca/en/services/jobs/training.html
- https://www.canada.ca/en/services/jobs/training/initiatives/skills-success.html

Controlled use:
- Canada.ca provides national training, student-aid, work-experience, youth internship, skills, and provincial/territorial support gateways.
- Skills for Success includes free assessment/training resources for reading, writing, numeracy, digital skills, problem solving, communication, creativity/innovation, collaboration, and adaptability.
- Eligibility and program availability vary; the guide must not promise funding, admission, or paid placement.

## Colombia — official occupation and training pathways

### OCUPACOL — Ministerio del Trabajo
Sources:
- https://ocupacol.mintrabajo.gov.co/Profile/OccupationalProfile/24312
- https://ocupacol.mintrabajo.gov.co/Profile/OccupationalProfile/24311

Controlled facts:
- OCUPACOL profile **24312** includes digital-content and social-media occupations such as administrador de redes sociales, community manager, gestor de mercadeo digital y redes sociales, content manager, social media manager, and social media strategist.
- Functions include administering web/social content, planning digital strategies, managing campaigns and product/service launches, interacting with online communities, creating/editing digital content, and collecting/analyzing audience and consumption statistics.
- OCUPACOL profile **24311** covers advertising/commercialization professionals and includes planning, coordinating, developing, and organizing advertising and promotion campaigns to support sales objectives.
- These profiles are useful occupation comparisons but do not establish that every entry-level assistant role requires professional-level preparation.

### SENA Betowa — Marketing Digital
Sources:
- https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/marketing-digital?modality=V&programId=155939
- https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/marketing-digital?modality=P&offertype=open&programId=142014

Controlled facts:
- SENA lists **Marketing Digital** as complementary training of **48 hours** in virtual and in-person forms.
- The virtual program states a competency of structuring digital-marketing strategy according to campaign objectives and legal requirements, with content covering digital environment, digital consumer, online communication, and metrics.
- Admission mechanisms are subject to availability and SENA selection procedures; cohorts, locations, dates, and seats change.
- The guide must direct readers to verify live Betowa availability rather than promise a seat, modality, or start date.

## Current non-government market estimates — clearly labelled

These are market estimates, not government wage statistics and not guaranteed compensation.

### United States — Indeed
Source: https://www.indeed.com/career/marketing-coordinator/salaries

- Indeed reported an average base salary of approximately **$57,136/year** for Marketing Coordinators in the United States.
- Based on about **4.3k salaries from job postings over the prior 36 months**, updated **August 2, 2026**.
- Displayed range was approximately **$40,235 to $81,136/year**.
- This market title is narrower and often more junior than the BLS/O*NET professional occupation benchmark, so the figures must not be merged or averaged together.

### Colombia — Indeed
Source: https://co.indeed.com/career/coordinador-de-mercadotecnia/salaries

- Indeed reported an average base salary of approximately **COP 3,042,755/month** for Coordinador/a de mercadotecnia in Colombia, updated **May 25, 2026**.
- The estimate was based on only **2 reported salaries**, so it is too small a sample to treat as representative national compensation.
- The guide should retain the value only as a clearly labelled market estimate with a strong small-sample caution.

## Privacy, accessibility, cybersecurity, advertising, and responsible-AI controls

The English revision should include practical, non-legal-advice boundaries for:

- customer and prospect data minimization;
- consent and preference-management workflows;
- email/SMS unsubscribe and suppression handling through approved systems;
- cookie/tracking and advertising-platform requirements as jurisdiction- and employer-specific matters;
- account security, MFA, phishing awareness, password-management, and role-based access;
- secure handling of CRM exports, audience lists, analytics, customer identifiers, payment-related data, and confidential campaign information;
- verification of unusual payment, invoice, domain, ad-account, or vendor-access requests;
- truthful marketing claims, source verification, and escalation of regulated/health/financial/legal claims to authorized reviewers;
- accessible digital content practices such as meaningful alt text, captions/transcripts where appropriate, readable structure, descriptive links, contrast awareness, and accessible forms/landing pages without claiming certification;
- responsible AI for low-risk brainstorming, drafts, summaries, keyword/grouping ideas, and formatting where approved, with human verification before publication;
- prohibition on entering confidential customer data, credentials, payment data, private contracts, unreleased business information, or protected personal information into unapproved AI tools;
- human review of AI-generated claims, prices, statistics, targeting logic, legal/compliance interpretations, translations, accessibility commitments, and brand-sensitive content.

## Editorial controls for the English revision
The revised master should:

1. distinguish assistant, coordinator, specialist, analyst, and manager responsibility levels;
2. explain that no single occupation code perfectly captures every digital-marketing support role;
3. cover campaign coordination, content, email, social, web, analytics, CRM/automation, ecommerce support, paid-media support, SEO basics, reporting, vendor coordination, and portfolio evidence without implying universal tool requirements;
4. present degree expectations for professional analyst/specialist benchmarks while also showing certificate, portfolio, internship, employer-training, and experience pathways for junior roles;
5. label official wages separately from market-title estimates;
6. include WIOA/American Job Center, Canada training gateways, SENA Betowa, and OIT/Cinterfor without promising eligibility or funding;
7. include work-based-learning pathways while avoiding unsupported claims that a registered apprenticeship is commonly available;
8. explain privacy, consent, platform, advertising, customer-data, cybersecurity, and accessibility responsibilities as employer/jurisdiction-specific controls rather than legal advice;
9. permit responsible AI only with privacy controls and human verification;
10. maintain plain-language, dysgraphia-friendly structure, descriptive headings, short planning steps, reusable checklists, and no unsupported claim of certification.
