## Purpose

This evidence record establishes the current-source foundation for controlled reconstruction of Guide 36. It distinguishes official/public occupational and training evidence from current non-government salary estimates and records the limits of each source. It does not claim that any one occupational code covers every warehouse or inventory-control job title in every country.

## Occupation scope and title control

The Guide 36 title is broader than a single occupational code. For the United States, the closest controlled baseline is O*NET 43-5071.00, **Shipping, Receiving, and Inventory Clerks**. O*NET describes work that verifies and maintains incoming/outgoing shipment and inventory records, checks shipment contents against manifests or orders, records discrepancies, prepares shipping documentation, stores materials, coordinates shipping activity, and uses inventory, ERP, warehouse-management, spreadsheet, barcode/RFID, and related systems.

This supports an English master that treats `Warehouse and Inventory Control Specialist` as an umbrella career title, with adjacent titles including inventory clerk, inventory control specialist, receiving clerk/coordinator, shipping clerk/coordinator, materials control associate, warehouse inventory clerk, stock-control clerk, and related entry-to-mid-level materials-control work. The guide must not imply that all employers use identical duties, physical demands, software, or credential requirements.

Official U.S. source:

- U.S. Department of Labor O*NET OnLine, Shipping, Receiving, and Inventory Clerks (43-5071.00): https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-5071.00
- O*NET page marked **Updated 2026**.

## United States — duties, preparation, training and outlook

## Canada — occupation, requirements and wages

Government of Canada Job Bank maps **Inventory control clerk** to **Purchasing and inventory control workers, NOC 14403**. Job Bank states that these workers process purchasing transactions and maintain inventories of materials, equipment and stock.

Current Job Bank requirements state:

- completion of secondary school is usually required;
- courses in production and inventory management and the ability to operate a computerized inventory system may be required for inventory control workers;
- the occupation is not identified as regulated in Canada in Job Bank's current record.

Current national Job Bank wage data for Inventory Control Clerk / NOC 14403, updated November 19, 2025 and still current on the 2026 Job Bank record, reports:

- low: **CAD $17.50/hour**
- median: **CAD $24.00/hour**
- high: **CAD $36.06/hour**

Reference period is 2023-2024 Labour Force Survey data. The guide must identify this as Canadian official national wage evidence and encourage local/provincial checking before decisions.

Official sources:

## Canada — education funding

Canada Student Grants and Loans remain a possible funding route for eligible post-secondary learners. Canada.ca states that grants generally do not require repayment while loans must be repaid. Eligibility is administered through provinces or territories and depends on the learner and program. The guide must not present this as automatic funding for short private warehouse courses.

Current federal policy note relevant to 2026: starting August 1, 2026, students attending private, for-profit international schools are no longer eligible for federal funding, subject to the transition exception described by Canada.ca. Pending approval, additional restrictions may apply to grants for students at private, for-profit schools in Canada. The guide should therefore direct learners to verify current provincial/territorial eligibility before enrolling.

Official source:

- Canada Student Grants and Loans: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/education/student-aid/grants-loans.html

## Safety and physical-demand control

Warehouse jobs can involve standing, walking, lifting, noise, powered equipment, loading areas, temperature variation, PPE and other facility-specific risks. O*NET identifies physical activity, material handling and protective-equipment use in this occupation family. The revised guide must advise workers to follow employer and jurisdiction-specific safety rules and must not instruct untrained readers to operate forklifts, powered industrial trucks, conveyors, dock equipment, or other machinery.

## Responsible AI and cybersecurity control

The revised guide may recommend AI only for low-risk learning and drafting tasks such as practice interview questions, non-sensitive training summaries, or explanations of general inventory concepts. It must instruct readers not to upload proprietary inventory levels, customer orders, supplier pricing, facility layouts, credentials, security details, shipment schedules, employee data, or other protected information into unapproved public AI systems.

AI-generated quantities, formulas, stock recommendations, reorder calculations, dates, routing suggestions and citations must be verified before operational use.
