# Guide 99 — Current Source Evidence

Food Science Technician

## Research date
2026-08-22

## Controlled purpose
This evidence pack supports the controlled English revision of Guide 99. It distinguishes official labour-market evidence from current non-government market estimates and does not guarantee pay, employment, admission, funding, certification, licensing, or work-based-learning placement.

## United States — official occupation, wage, outlook, and education evidence

### O*NET OnLine — Food Science Technicians
Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/19-4013.00

Controlled facts:
- O*NET-SOC: **19-4013.00 — Food Science Technicians**.
- The occupation supports food scientists and technologists in research, development, quality control, testing, production, and related technical work.
- O*NET reports **2025 median wages of $25.06/hour and $52,130/year**.
- Employment: about **20,400 workers in 2024**.
- Projected growth: **5% to 6% from 2024 to 2034**, faster than average.
- Projected openings: about **3,200 over the projection period** as reported by O*NET.
- Education responses vary materially: O*NET reports new-hire requirements ranging from high-school preparation through associate and bachelor’s degrees. The controlled guide must therefore avoid presenting one universal entry credential.

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Occupational Outlook Handbook
Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/life-physical-and-social-science/agricultural-and-food-science-technicians.htm

Controlled facts:
- BLS groups agricultural and food science technicians for outlook purposes and separately reports food science technician wage data.
- The OOH reports a **May 2024 median annual wage of $49,430 for food science technicians**.
- Work can include laboratories, offices, farms, processing plants, and manufacturing settings depending on assignment.
- Education and training requirements vary by specialty and employer; technical laboratory, science, quality, food-processing, and related postsecondary preparation can be relevant.

### Current BLS wage-year control
Sources:
- https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ocwage.t01.htm
- https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/19-4013.00

Controlled use:
- BLS May 2025 occupational wage results report food science technicians at **$56,660 mean annual pay** and **$27.24 mean hourly pay**.
- O*NET’s current national wage distribution, sourced from BLS 2025 wage data, reports **$39,820 at the 10th percentile, $52,130 median, and $78,820 at the 90th percentile annually**, corresponding to **$19.14, $25.06, and $37.89 per hour**.
- The revised guide should use the 2025 wage data for its principal U.S. wage table and may preserve the OOH’s 2024 figures only when clearly date-labelled.

## Canada — official occupation, wages, requirements, and outlook

### Job Bank — Food technologist / NOC 22100
Sources:
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/2942/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/2942/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/2942/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/outlook-occupation/2942/ca

Controlled facts:
- Job Bank maps the market title **Food technologist** to **NOC 22100 — Chemical technologists and technicians**. The Canadian scope is broader than the U.S. Food Science Technician occupation, so the guide must present it as a comparison rather than an exact equivalence.
- Chemical technologists and technicians provide technical support or may work independently in chemical/biochemical research, industrial chemistry, quality control, and related processing settings.
- Canada-wide wages, updated **November 19, 2025**, are **C$21.00 low, C$29.80 median, and C$47.69 high per hour**, with national reference period 2023–2024 from the Statistics Canada Labour Force Survey.
- Typical requirements: chemical technologists usually require a two- or three-year college program in chemical, biochemical, chemical engineering technology, or a closely related discipline; technicians usually require a one- or two-year college program.
- Certification may be available through professional associations and employer/regulatory expectations can vary by province. The controlled guide must direct readers to current provincial requirements rather than imply universal licensure.
- Job Bank reports national labour demand and supply for NOC 22100 as broadly balanced for **2024–2033**, while short-term prospects vary by province/territory.

## Colombia — occupation and training pathways

### OCUPACOL — Ministerio del Trabajo
Sources:
- https://ocupacol.mintrabajo.gov.co/Profile/OccupationalProfile/31160
- https://ocupacol.mintrabajo.gov.co/Profile/OccupationalProfile/32573

Controlled facts:
- **CUOC 31160 — Técnicos en química industrial** includes the occupational denomination **Técnico químico de alimentos** and related technical chemistry roles.
- **CUOC 32573** includes **Técnico de control calidad de procesamiento de alimentos** among its occupational denominations and links the profile to the food-processing qualification area.
- These are useful Colombian comparators for laboratory, quality-control, and processing-support pathways. They are not perfectly identical to U.S. SOC 19-4013.00, so the guide must avoid false one-to-one equivalence.
- OCUPACOL labour-market indicators may have methodology limitations; the guide should not elevate occupation-page salary displays over official, clearly documented statistical sources.

### SENA Betowa — food quality and processing pathways
Sources:
- https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/control-de-calidad-en-la-industria-de-alimentos?programId=173734
- https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/procesamiento-y-conservacion-de-alimentos?programId=210256
- https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/proteccion-y-conservacion-de-alimentos?programId=70917

Controlled facts:
- **Control de calidad en la industria de alimentos** is listed as a SENA **Tecnólogo** program with **3,984 hours** of training. Competencies include physicochemical analysis, sensory analysis, microbiological content determination, laboratory coordination, production-quality control, environmental protection, and occupational safety.
- **Procesamiento y conservación de alimentos** is listed as a **Tecnólogo** program with **3,984 hours**, covering raw-material preparation, storage, preservation, hygienization, production, quality verification, and related controls.
- **Protección y conservación de alimentos** is available in virtual offerings and covers physical, chemical, and biological contaminants, good manufacturing practices, foodborne illness, and food-quality assurance.
- SENA explicitly states that admission depends on available capacity and the applicable selection process. Live cohorts, locations, modality, dates, and seats can change; readers must verify Betowa before acting.

## Food safety, sanitation, allergen, traceability, and regulatory boundaries

### U.S. FDA — CGMP and preventive controls
Sources:
- https://www.fda.gov/food/guidance-regulation-food-and-dietary-supplements/current-good-manufacturing-practices-cgmps-food-and-dietary-supplements
- https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/fsma-final-rule-preventive-controls-human-food
- https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/fsma-rules-guidance-industry

Controlled use:
- FDA’s 21 CFR Part 117 framework includes current good manufacturing practices, hazard analysis, and risk-based preventive controls for covered human-food facilities.
- CGMP topics include employee hygiene, sanitation, plant/equipment conditions, production/process controls, and allergen cross-contact considerations.
- Food-safety-plan, preventive-control, validation, verification, corrective-action, recall-plan, and qualified-individual responsibilities can belong to specifically authorized personnel. A technician may collect samples, run approved tests, document observations, and escalate deviations but must not self-assign regulatory sign-off authority.
- Requirements depend on food type, facility, jurisdiction, and applicable exemptions. The guide must remain educational and direct consequential compliance decisions to current FDA rules, employer food-safety plans, qualified personnel, and other competent authorities.

## Laboratory, equipment, biological, chemical, and quality-system boundaries

The controlled English revision should require technicians to:
- follow approved sampling plans, methods, calibration, chain-of-custody, sanitation, allergen, PPE, chemical-handling, and waste-disposal procedures;
- distinguish observation and test execution from final regulatory release, product disposition, method validation, or authorized laboratory sign-off;
- record results accurately without fabricating, backdating, deleting, or selectively suppressing data;
- stop and escalate when controls, equipment, samples, environmental conditions, or results fall outside approved limits;
- follow employer and facility procedures for biological hazards, chemicals, hot surfaces, pressurized systems, moving equipment, refrigeration systems, sharp tools, and other task-specific risks;
- never taste, smell, touch, or manipulate unknown or potentially hazardous samples outside approved methods.

## Cybersecurity, data integrity, privacy, and responsible AI

### CISA — Secure Our World
Source: https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world

Controlled use:
- Apply phishing awareness, strong passwords/password managers, multifactor authentication, and software-update practices through employer-approved systems.
- Food laboratories and processing environments can contain formulas, supplier data, batch records, test results, credentials, production data, and regulated records. Technicians should use authorized systems, protect account access, verify unusual requests, and report suspected compromise or manipulation.

### NIST — AI Risk Management Framework and Generative AI Profile
Sources:
- https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
- https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence

Controlled use:
- Generative AI may help with low-risk drafting, study explanations, checklist brainstorming, or formatting when the organization permits it.
- It must not replace approved laboratory methods, scientific review, product-release decisions, regulatory interpretation, hazard analysis, allergen decisions, validation, traceability records, calibration evidence, or authorized quality sign-off.
- Do not enter confidential formulas, proprietary process parameters, personal data, credentials, unpublished test results, regulated records, customer/supplier confidential information, or security-sensitive facility data into an unapproved AI system.
- Consequential claims must be verified against approved laboratory records, current regulatory sources, validated methods, and authorized human reviewers.

## Editorial controls for the English revision
The controlled English master should:

1. anchor the U.S. occupation to **O*NET-SOC 19-4013.00** and clearly distinguish technician support from food scientist/technologist authority;
2. use **2025 BLS/O*NET wage data** as the principal U.S. wage benchmark and date-label older OOH values;
3. present Canada **NOC 22100** as a broader comparator, not an exact occupational equivalent;
4. present Colombia **CUOC 31160 / 32573** as relevant technical and food-quality comparators without false equivalence;
5. include SENA food-quality, processing, conservation, and food-safety pathways with live-availability disclaimers;
6. include WIOA/American Job Center, Canada training supports, Apprenticeship.gov, and OIT/Cinterfor as locators without promising eligibility or availability;
7. label all non-government salary figures as estimates and keep them separate from official statistics;
8. explain food-safety, sanitation, allergen, traceability, sampling, laboratory, chemical, equipment, and data-integrity controls without claiming regulatory authority;
9. require escalation for out-of-specification results, contamination concerns, equipment failures, method deviations, data-integrity issues, or unsafe conditions;
10. distinguish test execution and documentation from product release, final disposition, regulatory certification, method validation, or professional sign-off;
11. include cybersecurity and responsible-AI boundaries for formulas, batch records, laboratory data, supplier/customer information, and credentials;
12. repair the legacy **Action Plan 1 To 6** defect with a clear six-step entry plan that remains practical for adult learners and career changers;
13. use plain language, descriptive headings, short paragraphs, checklists, and dysgraphia-friendly structure;
14. make no unsupported claim of independent human review, certified translation, accreditation, legal/regulatory review, food-safety certification, accessibility certification, guaranteed funding, employment, or income.
