# Guide 72 — Current Source Evidence

## Occupation
Retail Supervisor and Merchandising Coordinator

## Research date
2026-08-21

## Controlled purpose
This evidence pack supports the English revision of Guide 72. It uses current public occupational, wage, and training sources and separates official labour-market evidence from non-government market estimates. It does not guarantee employment, earnings, admission, training funding, apprenticeship placement, promotion, certification, licensing, or any other outcome.

## United States — official occupation benchmark

### O*NET OnLine — U.S. Department of Labor
Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/41-1011.00

Controlled facts:
- O*NET-SOC: **41-1011.00 — First-Line Supervisors of Retail Sales Workers**; page updated in 2026.
- The occupation directly supervises and coordinates retail sales workers. Duties can also include purchasing, budgeting, accounting, and personnel work in addition to supervision.
- Current task evidence includes supervising employees engaged in sales, inventory taking, cash reconciliation, and customer service; assigning duties; preparing work schedules; maintaining records; monitoring sales and service; resolving customer complaints; examining merchandise for correct pricing, display, and functioning; arranging shelves and displays; reviewing sales and inventory reports; reordering merchandise; supporting promotions; and enforcing safety, health, and security rules.
- O*NET supplemental activities also support merchandise-display, pricing-policy, advertising/promotion, inventory, budget, employee-training, and sales-campaign responsibilities. These tasks support the blended title **Retail Supervisor and Merchandising Coordinator** when the employer actually assigns those duties.

### U.S. wage and outlook data
Sources:
- https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/41-1011.00
- https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/41-1011.00

Controlled facts:
- **2025 median wage:** **$23.33/hour** or **$48,520/year**.
- 2025 national wage distribution displayed by O*NET/BLS: approximately **$33,120/year at the 10th percentile** and **$77,080/year at the 90th percentile**; hourly equivalents approximately **$15.92** and **$37.06**.
- Employment: about **1,432,600 workers in 2024**.
- Projected employment change, 2024–2034: **decline of 1% or lower**.
- O*NET displays approximately **125,100 projected job openings for 2024–2034** from growth and replacement needs. The guide should not reinterpret this figure as a guaranteed annual number.
- O*NET reports that about 68% of workers in this occupation are in Retail Trade.

### U.S. entry and training context
Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/41-1011.00

Controlled facts:
- The occupation falls in Job Zone 1–2.
- O*NET reports that a high school diploma or equivalent is common; its education survey displays about **52% high school diploma/equivalent**, **19% associate degree**, and **14% less than high school** among respondents.
- Training can range from a few days to about one year of on-the-job learning depending on employer and responsibility level.
- Retail supervisor roles commonly grow from retail sales, cashiering, stocking, customer service, inventory, or department-lead experience rather than requiring a private career school.

## Canada — official classification, requirements, and wages

### Job Bank — Retail sales supervisors
Sources:
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/1654/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/1654/ca
- https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/1654/ca

Controlled facts:
- Canadian classification: **NOC 62010 — Retail sales supervisors**.
- Job Bank describes the occupation as supervising and coordinating workers in retail sales, visual merchandising, cashiering, shelf stocking/order filling, and other sales-related occupations.
- Employers include retail stores, wholesale businesses that sell to the public, rental-service establishments, and similar businesses.
- Detailed Job Bank requirements state that **completion of secondary school is usually required** and **previous retail sales experience is usually required**.
- Job Bank indicates that this occupation is **not regulated in Canada** in its current records.
- Canada-wide wage data updated November 19, 2025: **C$16.55/hour low, C$22.00/hour median, C$36.06/hour high**.
- Wage reference period: 2023–2024; source: Statistics Canada Labour Force Survey.

Controlled use:

## Colombia — official occupation context

### Ministerio del Trabajo — OCUPACOL
Source: https://ocupacol.mintrabajo.gov.co/Profile/OccupationalProfile/52220

Controlled facts:
- Colombian classification: **CUOC 52220 — Supervisores de tiendas y almacenes**.
- Competence level displayed: **3**.
- OCUPACOL describes workers who supervise and coordinate commercialization of goods and services by sales personnel in stores, supermarkets, large-format retail establishments, wholesale businesses serving the public, and related settings.
- Example occupational titles include **Coordinador de mercadeo**, Supervisor de almacén, Supervisor de promotores de ventas, Supervisor de puntos de venta, Supervisor de supermercado, Supervisor de tienda, Supervisor de vendedores, and Supervisor de ventas.
- Functions include supervising sales and customer service, preparing reports, managing merchandise and inventory, ensuring products are displayed according to promotional plans/campaign protocols, and applying workplace safety procedures.
- This official classification supports both the supervisory and merchandising-coordination dimensions of Guide 72.

### Colombia wage-control note
OCUPACOL may display labour-market indicators, but the controlled revision should not create or infer a representative national wage where the official profile does not provide a statistically representative wage benchmark suitable for publication. Current Colombia pay context should therefore be clearly separated and labelled as non-government market evidence.

## Current non-government market estimates — clearly labelled

These figures are market estimates, not government wage statistics and not guaranteed compensation.

### United States — Indeed
Source: https://www.indeed.com/career/retail-supervisor/salaries

Controlled interpretation:
- Indeed displayed an average U.S. retail-supervisor base salary of approximately **$48,124/year**, with the headline page updated **August 1, 2026**.
- Different sections of the page displayed differing sample-count/range metadata, so the controlled guide should retain only the headline average and update date rather than present internally inconsistent sample/range figures as authoritative.
- This is a non-government market estimate and should be used only as a comparison point beside official O*NET/BLS data and actual current job postings.

### Colombia — Indeed
Source: https://co.indeed.com/career/supervisor-de-tienda/salaries

Controlled interpretation:
- Indeed displayed an average **COP 1,923,218/month** for `supervisor de tienda`, updated **July 21, 2026**.
- The page showed a small sample of **7 salaries**, with a displayed range of approximately **COP 1,300,409 to COP 2,844,312/month**.
- Because the sample is small and the title does not capture every merchandising/supervisory configuration, the figure must be labelled as an illustrative non-government market estimate, not a representative national wage.

## Editorial controls for the English revision
The revised English master should:

1. distinguish first-line supervisory duties from store-manager, district-manager, HR, finance, legal, procurement, corporate-merchandising, safety, and loss-prevention authority;
2. use the merchandising component only where supported by actual duties such as displays, pricing, promotion execution, sales campaigns, stock presentation, and inventory coordination;
3. preserve the U.S. 2025 median wage and 2024–2034 outlook accurately and describe the **125,100** O*NET opening figure without converting it into an unsupported annual claim;
4. use Job Bank’s detailed Canadian entry requirements rather than a coarse occupation-summary education label;
5. avoid inventing an official Colombia wage benchmark; label Indeed values as market estimates with date/sample limitations;
6. include cash/POS, refunds, discounts, payment-card, customer-data, loyalty-account, employee-data, inventory, key/access, and vendor-payment cybersecurity/privacy boundaries;
7. include shrink/fraud/loss-prevention awareness without encouraging profiling, accusation, unsafe confrontation, unauthorized searches, or independent investigation outside policy and law;
8. explain HR boundaries for interviewing, scheduling, coaching, performance documentation, discipline, accommodations, and termination: authority depends on employer policy and applicable law;
9. include workplace safety, emergency, manual-handling, violence-prevention, accessibility, and reasonable-accommodation awareness without claiming professional safety or legal certification;
10. explain responsible AI: do not put customer, employee, payment, credential, investigation, or confidential business data into unapproved AI systems; do not use AI alone for hiring, discipline, termination, fraud accusations, pricing authority, legal decisions, or safety decisions;
11. include CareerOneStop/WIOA, Canada.ca, SENA/Betowa, ILO/Cinterfor, employer training, and work-based-learning routes while preserving eligibility and availability caveats;
12. show realistic low-cost progression from cashier, sales associate, stock/inventory, customer service, department lead, or visual-merchandising support into first-line supervision;
13. preserve accessibility and disability-support planning for both workers and customers;
14. retain the project’s CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license and explicit assurance boundary.
