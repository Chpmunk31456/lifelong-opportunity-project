## Editorial checks

- Title and occupation benchmark are explicit: O*NET-SOC 29-2012.00 — Medical and Clinical Laboratory Technicians.
- Canadian comparisons are deliberately split between NOC 32120 (medical laboratory technologists) and NOC 33101 (medical laboratory assistants and related technical occupations); the guide does not claim exact equivalence.
- Colombia comparisons are deliberately split between CUOC 32120 (Técnicos de laboratorios médicos) and CUOC 53294 (Auxiliares de laboratorio clínico).
- Technician, technologist/scientist, assistant, phlebotomist, pathologist, laboratory director and other authorized clinical roles are clearly distinguished.
- The guide expressly prohibits diagnosis, unauthorized result release, QC override, validated-method changes, data fabrication, identity manipulation, credential misrepresentation and use of unapproved AI with patient/laboratory information.
- CLIA is presented as a U.S. federal quality framework with waived/moderate/high-complexity concepts and personnel requirements; the text avoids giving universal personnel-eligibility or legal advice.
- OSHA bloodborne-pathogen, sharps and laboratory chemical-safety boundaries are presented as employer-procedure-driven controls rather than improvised instructions.
- Quality control, quality assurance, proficiency testing, specimen identity/traceability, calibration, data integrity, LIS/privacy and cybersecurity are occupation-specific and actionable.
- U.S. labour-market figures identify the 2025 O*NET/BLS-linked wage and explicitly state that the national wage/employment series combines clinical laboratory technologists and technicians.
- BLS May 2024 wage is dated separately, avoiding false contradiction.
- Private ZipRecruiter/Glassdoor figures are clearly labelled as noisy title-sensitive market estimates rather than official statistics.
- U.S. education/certification sections distinguish accredited education, national certification, state licensure and employer requirements.
- WIOA and apprenticeship/work-based-learning routes include eligibility/availability caveats.
- Canadian wage, education, certification and regulation sections preserve the MLT/MLA distinction.
- Colombia occupational classifications and the 48-hour SENA sample-collection course are not misrepresented as professional licensure or a full technician qualification.
- Accessibility and dysgraphia-support sections preserve specimen identity, infection control, competency, audit-trail and patient-safety requirements.
- Responsible-AI language allows low-risk learning support while reserving patient interpretation, diagnosis, QC, validation, result release and other consequential decisions to authorized people and validated systems.
- Safe portfolio and résumé sections prohibit real patient/confidential material and unsupported competency claims.
- The legacy action-plan defect is repaired: the English master contains a dedicated **Six-step action plan** with explicit **Step 1** through **Step 6** headings.
- Source and verification links are grouped by jurisdiction/topic and the guide contains an explicit educational/no-guarantee/no-certification notice.
- Authorship, AI-assistance disclosure and CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license statement are present.
- No TODO, placeholder, unsupported human-review claim, guaranteed funding/employment/income claim or obvious publication-only artifact was identified.
