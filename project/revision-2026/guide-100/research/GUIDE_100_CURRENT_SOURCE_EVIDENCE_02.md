**Occupation:** Clinical Laboratory Technician  
**Controlled branch:** `revision/guide-00-100-2026`  
**Research date:** 2026-08-22  
**Gate:** Current-source Research

## Research conclusion

**PASS.** Current official and clearly labelled market sources support a controlled English revision of Guide 100. No research blocker remains.

The revised guide must preserve jurisdictional boundaries carefully. “Clinical laboratory technician,” “medical laboratory technician,” “medical laboratory technologist/scientist,” “medical laboratory assistant,” and Colombia’s technical/auxiliary titles are related but are not interchangeable across countries.

## 1. United States occupation anchor

### O*NET-SOC 29-2012.00 — Medical and Clinical Laboratory Technicians
Official current page:
https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/29-2012.00

O*NET, updated in 2026, defines the occupation as performing routine medical laboratory tests for diagnosis, treatment, and prevention of disease and notes that the worker may operate under supervision of a medical technologist. Current task examples include chemical analysis of body fluids, calibration/maintenance of laboratory equipment, reagent preparation, specimen collection, blood testing, microbiology work, and examination of stained cells.

O*NET places the occupation in **Job Zone Three — Medium Preparation Needed** and identifies associate-degree/vocational preparation as common. Its current education-response data show mixed employer expectations, so the guide must not claim one universal educational route.

O*NET also lists two apprenticeable occupation titles associated with this occupation and directs users to Apprenticeship.gov. Availability is local and must never be promised.

### Current U.S. wage and employment evidence
O*NET’s 2026 occupation page reports BLS-derived **2025 median wages of US$30.26/hour and US$62,930/year** for the combined Clinical Laboratory Technologists and Technicians wage series. It reports:

- employment 2024: **351,200**;
- projected growth 2024–2034: **1%–2% / slower than average**;
- projected annual openings: **22,600**;
- health care and social assistance: **83%** of employment.

Source:
https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/29-2012.00

The BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook separately reports the combined occupation “Clinical Laboratory Technologists and Technicians,” with **US$61,890 median annual wage in May 2024**, 351,200 jobs in 2024, 2% projected growth for 2024–2034, and about 22,600 openings per year. BLS states that technicians sometimes qualify with an associate degree, some states require licensure, and employers may prefer certification.

Source:
https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/clinical-laboratory-technologists-and-technicians.htm

The guide must explicitly label the O*NET/BLS wage series as covering the combined technologist/technician employment series rather than implying a technician-only national median.

## 2. U.S. education, accreditation and certification

### NAACLS
The National Accrediting Agency for Clinical Laboratory Sciences identifies Medical Laboratory Technician (MLT) education as an associate-degree pathway and describes MLT work as routine laboratory testing in blood banking, chemistry, hematology, immunology, microbiology, molecular biology, and/or urinalysis. NAACLS accreditation standards cover pre-analytical, analytical and post-analytical work, safety, quality, laboratory operations and supervised applied learning.

Sources:
https://naacls.org/students/
https://naacls.org/about/

### ASCP Board of Certification
ASCP BOC’s current MLT page describes MLT practice as collecting, processing and performing routine tests. One current U.S. eligibility route requires an associate degree or 60 semester hours plus completion of a NAACLS- or ABHES-accredited MLT program within the required time window. Other routes exist, including U.S. military training and documented clinical experience.

Source:
https://www.ascp.org/boc/explore-credentials/view-all-credentials/MLT

ASCP is a major professional certification pathway, but the guide must not describe ASCP certification as a universal legal requirement. State licensure and employer requirements vary and must be checked separately.

## 3. U.S. CLIA scope and quality boundaries

### CLIA applicability
CDC states that the Clinical Laboratory Improvement Amendments of 1988 (CLIA) establish federal standards applicable to U.S. facilities or sites that test human specimens for health assessment or for diagnosis, prevention, or treatment of disease.

Source:
https://www.cdc.gov/clia/

### Test complexity and personnel controls
CDC explains that nonwaived testing is subject to CLIA quality-system requirements, including proficiency testing, quality control/assessment and personnel requirements. Moderate- and high-complexity testing differ in personnel requirements. Laboratory-developed or manufacturer-modified tests default to high complexity under CLIA rules.

Source:
https://www.cdc.gov/clia/php/test-complexities/index.html

Primary regulation:
https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-493

Personnel subpart:
https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-493/subpart-M

The controlled guide must not attempt to reduce CLIA personnel qualifications to a single simplified credential rule. Test complexity, laboratory certificate, education, experience, role assignment and state law all matter. Readers must verify current CMS/eCFR requirements and employer/state rules for the exact work they intend to perform.

### Quality control
CDC states that CLIA requires laboratories to maintain QC procedures for accuracy and precision throughout the testing process; the Individualized Quality Control Plan (IQCP) is one option for eligible nonwaived testing.

Sources:
https://www.cdc.gov/lab-quality/php/about/index.html
https://www.cdc.gov/lab-quality/php/iqcp/index.html

The guide should teach a technician to follow validated methods, verify controls, preserve original data, document repeats/corrections and escalate failed QC or out-of-range results. It must not teach a trainee to override QC, release results outside authorization, change validated methods, or repeat testing until a preferred result appears.

### Free CLIA learning
CDC offers free clinical-laboratory educational resources, including a basic Introduction to CLIA eLearning course and other OneLab/quality materials.

Source:
https://www.cdc.gov/lab-training/php/courses/introduction-clinical-laboratory-improvement-amendments-of-1988-clia.html

## 4. U.S. occupational safety

### Bloodborne pathogens and sharps
OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1030 applies to occupational exposure to blood or other potentially infectious materials and specifically defines a clinical laboratory. It requires exposure-control measures, engineering/work-practice controls, appropriate PPE, universal precautions, safe sharps handling and suitable specimen containers.

Source:
https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1030

The guide should emphasize specimen containment, PPE, hand hygiene, exposure reporting, sharps controls, no mouth pipetting and no unauthorized recapping/bending of contaminated needles.

### Hazardous chemicals
OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1450 applies to covered laboratory use of hazardous chemicals and requires a Chemical Hygiene Plan, appropriate controls, training and exposure protections.

Source:
https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1450

The guide must direct readers to employer SOPs, SDS information, engineering controls, waste procedures and trained supervision rather than provide improvised chemical-handling recipes.

## 7. Canada — two different comparison pathways

The U.S. technician title does not map cleanly to one Canadian occupation. The controlled guide must present two comparators rather than claim exact equivalence.

### NOC 32120 — Medical laboratory technologists
Job Bank describes NOC 32120 as conducting medical laboratory tests, experiments and analyses to assist diagnosis, treatment, monitoring and prevention of disease.

Current national wages, updated 2025-11-19:

- low: **C$25.13/hour**;
- median: **C$39.00/hour**;
- high: **C$49.00/hour**.

Sources:
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/4187/ca
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/4187/ca

Job Bank’s current requirement page states that a two- or three-year college program in medical laboratory technology is typically required, supervised practical training may be required, registration is required in specified regulated provinces, and CSMLS certification is usually required by employers.

Source:
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/4189/ca

CSMLS describes Canadian MLT education as typically three to four years in an EQual-accredited program and administers national certification. Regulatory status is provincial and can change; readers must verify directly with the provincial regulator and current Job Bank/CSMLS information.

Sources:
https://csmls.org/certification/medical-laboratory-professionals/how-to-become-a-medical-lab-professional/
https://csmls.org/about-us/about-csmls/partners-and-stakeholders/

### NOC 33101 — Medical laboratory assistants and related technical occupations
Job Bank describes NOC 33101 as pre-analysis/pre-testing work such as collecting blood and other specimens, processing samples, preparing reagents, conducting routine tests and maintaining equipment. This is often the closer comparator for a narrower technician/assistant route.

Current national wages, updated 2025-11-19:

- low: **C$19.18/hour**;
- median: **C$27.00/hour**;
- high: **C$36.11/hour**.

Job Bank states that the occupation usually requires postsecondary education/apprenticeship of less than two years or more than six months of on-the-job training, depending on the job.

Sources:
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/4207/ca
https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/4207/ca

CSMLS states that medical laboratory assistant programs are commonly about 9–12 months and offers MLA certification. CSMLS also notes that MLA is not currently a regulated health profession across Canada, while employer requirements still vary.

Source:
https://csmls.org/certification/medical-laboratory-professionals/how-to-become-a-medical-lab-professional/

### Canada training and financial support gateway
Government of Canada’s current training page links student aid, loans/grants, recognized training, employment supports and foreign credential recognition.

Source:
https://www.canada.ca/en/services/jobs/training.html

## 8. Colombia occupation mapping

### CUOC 32120 — Técnicos de laboratorios médicos
OCUPACOL directly identifies **CUOC 32120 — Técnicos de laboratorios médicos**, competence level 3. Titles include Técnico de laboratorio clínico, Técnico de laboratorio médico, Técnico de hematología, Técnico de histología, Técnico de banco de sangre and related technical titles.

OCUPACOL describes functions including collecting/preparing specimens, assisting with chemical analysis of body fluids, analyzing laboratory data/samples under applicable standards, examining stained cells and supporting pathology/autopsy procedures within defined supervision and protocols.

Source:
https://ocupacol.mintrabajo.gov.co/Profile/OccupationalProfile/32120

### CUOC 53294 — Auxiliares de laboratorio clínico
OCUPACOL separately defines **CUOC 53294 — Auxiliares de laboratorio clínico**, competence level 2. Functions include receiving/taking samples, preparing materials, instructing users on specimen collection, preparing culture samples, maintaining records, checking labeling/transport and caring for equipment.

Source:
https://ocupacol.mintrabajo.gov.co/Profile/OccupationalProfile/53294

The guide must keep CUOC 32120 and 53294 distinct and must not imply that either title grants the professional authority of a bacteriologist, pathologist, physician, laboratory director or other separately regulated health professional.

### Current SENA complementary route
SENA Betowa currently lists **Toma de muestras para procesamiento en el laboratorio clínico**, a **48-hour complementary/special course**. The August 2026 Bogotá offering requires the learner to be a technician or auxiliary in nursing plus an institutional presentation letter and selection process. The course supports diagnostic procedures according to delegation, protocols and required technology.

Source:
https://betowa.sena.edu.co/oferta/toma-de-muestras-para-procesamiento-en-el-laboratorio-clinico?location=57011001&modality=P&programId=84606

This is a complementary course, not proof of qualification as a clinical laboratory technician. Availability, location, prerequisites and dates change and must be verified in Betowa.

### Colombia labour-market caution
OCUPACOL’s salary indicator for CUOC 53294 explicitly warns that the underlying data are not statistically representative. It must not be presented as a reliable current national salary benchmark.

The SENA Public Employment Agency can be used for current vacancy intelligence, but individual postings are not national wage statistics.

Source:
https://agenciapublicadeempleo.sena.edu.co/

## 10. Patient privacy, cybersecurity and data integrity

Clinical laboratory work routinely involves patient identifiers, test orders/results and laboratory information systems. The controlled guide must require use of authorized systems, unique accounts, approved storage/transmission, access controls and employer incident-reporting processes.

For U.S. HIPAA-covered laboratories, protected health information must be handled under applicable HIPAA Privacy/Security requirements. The guide is educational and must not attempt to provide legal advice or a universal privacy rule for all jurisdictions.

The core operational rule is broader than any one law: do not copy patient records, images, test results, credentials, instrument exports or proprietary laboratory data into personal email, consumer cloud storage or unapproved AI systems.

## 11. Responsible AI boundary

NIST’s AI Risk Management Framework is a voluntary framework for incorporating trustworthiness considerations into AI design, development, use and evaluation. NIST states that AI RMF 1.0 is under revision and also provides a Generative AI Profile.

Source:
https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework

For this occupation the safe editorial boundary is strict:

AI may assist with low-risk learning, public-information summaries, study questions, drafting non-patient training materials, spreadsheet explanations or workflow brainstorming when organizational policy permits.

AI must **not** be treated as the final authority for patient-specific interpretation, diagnosis, test-result release, critical-value handling, QC acceptance, proficiency-testing answers, calibration decisions, method validation, transfusion compatibility, microorganism identification, pathology conclusions or other regulated/clinically consequential decisions. Patient or confidential laboratory information must not be placed in an unapproved AI system.

## 12. Accessibility and realistic work context

O*NET reports very high importance of exactness/accuracy, daily PPE use for many workers, regular exposure to disease/infections, close visual work and substantial teamwork/contact. BLS notes infectious specimens, biohazard exposure, standing and schedules that may include nights, weekends or holidays.

The guide should describe essential functions without assuming disability exclusion. Potential accommodations may include ergonomic benches/seating where compatible with essential tasks, magnification or accessible displays, non-color-only labeling, written checklists, task sequencing, speech-to-text for non-regulated drafting, accessible software, anti-fatigue measures and schedule adjustments where reasonable and safe. Any accommodation must preserve specimen identity, infection control, data integrity, competency requirements and patient safety.

## 13. Required English-revision controls

The controlled English master must:

1. anchor U.S. scope to O*NET-SOC 29-2012.00 while explaining BLS combined-series statistics;
2. distinguish technician from technologist/scientist, phlebotomist, assistant, pathologist and authorized laboratory leadership;
3. explain CLIA complexity/quality/personnel concepts without giving false universal eligibility advice;
4. distinguish U.S. certification from state licensure and employer requirements;
5. present Canadian NOC 32120 and NOC 33101 as separate comparators;
6. present Colombia CUOC 32120 and CUOC 53294 separately;
7. include safe specimen identity, chain-of-custody/transport, QC, calibration, proficiency-testing, data-integrity, bloodborne-pathogen, sharps and chemical-safety controls;
8. prohibit unauthorized diagnosis, result release, method changes, QC overrides, data fabrication and unapproved AI use;
9. include WIOA, accredited-program, apprenticeship/work-based-learning, Canada training-support, SENA and OIT/Cinterfor locators with eligibility/availability caveats;
10. clearly label private salary data as noisy market estimates;
11. repair and verify the legacy **Action Plan 1 To 6** defect;
12. retain plain-language, dysgraphia-friendly organization and accessibility guidance;
13. contain no unsupported claim of independent human review, legal/regulatory certification, laboratory accreditation, professional licensure review, accessibility certification, guaranteed funding, employment or income.
