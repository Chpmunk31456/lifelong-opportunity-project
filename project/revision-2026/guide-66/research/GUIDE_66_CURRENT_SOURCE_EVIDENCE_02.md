## Research scope and occupation boundary

Guide 66 combines an employer-defined trainee/supervisory title with the broader food-service management occupation. The controlled U.S. benchmark is **O*NET-SOC 11-9051.00 — Food Service Managers**, while the guide must make clear that a restaurant supervisor or management trainee may have less authority, lower compensation, and narrower duties than a fully accountable food service manager.

O*NET describes Food Service Managers as workers who plan, direct, or coordinate activities of an organization or department that serves food and beverages. Reported titles include Food Service Supervisor, Kitchen Manager, Restaurant Manager, Catering Manager, and Food Service Manager. O*NET also identifies **Manager, Food Service** and **Restaurant Manager** as example Registered Apprenticeship titles, which supports presenting apprenticeship as a possible work-based pathway rather than a universal requirement.

### BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook comparison

The BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook reports a **May 2024 median annual wage of USD $65,310** (**$31.40/hour**), 2024 employment of about **352,800**, projected growth of **6%** from 2024–2034, and about **42,000 openings per year**. The 2024 OOH wage and 2025 O*NET wage references have different wage reference periods and must not be presented as contradictory figures.

BLS says food service managers typically need a high school diploma and several years of food-service experience, often gained in jobs such as cook, server, or food-preparation/serving supervisor. Some employers recruit management trainees from hospitality or food-service management programs. BLS also notes that some states and localities require food-safety certification, so the guide must direct readers to verify the actual jurisdiction and employer rather than imply one national credential rule.

### Current non-government U.S. restaurant-supervisor estimates

These estimates describe **Restaurant Supervisor**, not the broader official Food Service Manager occupation, and must remain separately labeled:

- **Salary.com, August 1, 2026:** average **USD $42,306/year**, approximately **$20/hour**; 25th–75th percentile **$37,639–$49,149**.
- **ZipRecruiter, August 5, 2026:** average **USD $54,962/year**, approximately **$26.42/hour**; reported majority range **$45,000–$63,500**.

The two market estimates use different methods and should not be averaged. They illustrate why a trainee/supervisor title should not inherit manager-level official wages automatically.

## United States — food-safety and worker-safety boundary

The FDA states that the **2022 Food Code** is its most recent full Food Code and is a model for retail and food-service safety; state, local, tribal, territorial, and other authorities decide how it is adopted and enforced. The 2024 supplement updates the 2022 Food Code. The model Food Code includes provisions for a certified food protection manager/person in charge, but local adoption and exceptions vary. Therefore, Guide 66 must tell readers to verify the controlling health department and employer requirements.

BLS also identifies common restaurant-management hazards such as slips, falls, cuts, hot equipment, crowded kitchens, long shifts, and stressful customer interactions. A trainee or supervisor must follow employer safety procedures and must not bypass food-safety, injury-prevention, or emergency controls to meet service targets.

## Canada — official comparison

The closest national benchmark is **NOC 60030 — Restaurant and food service managers**. Job Bank describes these workers as planning, organizing, directing, controlling, and evaluating restaurant, bar, cafeteria, and other food-and-beverage-service operations.

Current national Job Bank wage data, updated **November 19, 2025** using the 2023–2024 Labour Force Survey reference period, reports:

- low: **CAD $18.00/hour**;
- median: **CAD $26.00/hour**;
- high: **CAD $48.08/hour**.

Job Bank's current requirements page says a college or other hospitality/food-and-beverage management program is usually required, several years of food-service experience including supervisory experience are required, and responsible beverage service certification is usually required for managers of establishments serving alcoholic beverages. Job Bank states that the occupation itself is not regulated nationally; provincial/territorial and employer-specific requirements still need verification.

## Colombia — current public training pathways

Current SENA Betowa examples support a practical progression into restaurant service and later supervision/management but do not establish a national salary or guarantee promotion:

- **Servicio de restaurante y bar** — titled (`Titulada`) **Técnico**, **2,208 hours**, in-person; listed competencies include food handling under current regulations, serving food and beverages to technical standards, workplace safety, entrepreneurship/business processes, communication, and practical-stage learning.
- **Emprendedor en técnicas de mesa y bar para el servicio de alimentos y bebidas** — complementary/special course, **330 hours**, in-person.

The technical program page also identifies an additional medical-fitness requirement for entry into service workshops and references Colombian food-handling rules. The guide should present current SENA offerings as examples whose location, intake, prerequisites, and availability must be checked in Betowa at the time of application.

No unsupported national Colombia wage figure is introduced by this research record.

## Duties and authority boundaries to preserve downstream

The Version 2 guide should distinguish responsibilities that may be delegated to a trainee/supervisor from those that require formal manager authority. Typical supported activities include shift coordination, assigning duties, monitoring service quality, inventory checks, handling complaints within authority, supporting training, sanitation checks, scheduling support, and escalating incidents.

The guide must not imply that every trainee/supervisor may independently hire/fire staff, alter payroll, sign contracts, approve major purchases, change menu pricing, authorize alcohol service, override food-safety rules, access unrestricted employee/customer/payment information, or make regulatory representations. Those powers depend on employer delegation, role level, and jurisdiction.

## Privacy, cybersecurity, accessibility, and responsible AI

Later editorial stages should explicitly address:

- least-privilege access to POS, scheduling, payroll, HR, reservation, loyalty, delivery-platform, and inventory systems;
- secure handling of customer payment and contact information and employee records;
- no credential sharing or security-control bypass for speed;
- accessibility and accommodation duties according to employer policy and applicable law;
- safe handling of allergen-related information and escalation to approved procedures rather than improvisation;
- no entry of payment-card data, employee records, customer personal data, unpublished business data, incident reports, or other sensitive information into public AI tools without organizational approval;
- human review of AI-assisted schedules, training drafts, menu descriptions, customer communications, and operational summaries.

AI output must never override food-safety rules, labor requirements, accessibility obligations, alcohol-service requirements, or human supervisory judgment.
