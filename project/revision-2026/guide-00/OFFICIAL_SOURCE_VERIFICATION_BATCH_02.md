## Controlled editorial conclusions

1. **Financial-aid categories must remain separate.** Grants, scholarships, earned work-study wages, employer reimbursement, forgivable aid, and loans are not interchangeable.
2. **Eligibility is never implied by listing a source.** Each program depends on jurisdiction, status, income, disability, enrollment, occupation, employer, or other conditions.
3. **Labor-market information is contextual evidence.** Wage and outlook data must show geography, date, unit, and source; they are not guarantees.
4. **Work authorization must be explicit.** Canadian Job Bank information can be useful to international readers, but most Canadian jobs require valid authorization unless the employer recruits foreign candidates.
5. **Recognition terms must not be inflated.** In Colombia, institutional authorization, a program's registro calificado, and acreditación de alta calidad are distinct concepts.
6. **Credit must be labeled as repayable.** Canada Apprentice Loans and ICETEX financing may be useful, but neither may be presented as free funding.
7. **Open or closed status must be date-stamped.** Calls, credit lines, supportive services, and employer incentives can change after publication.
8. **Translated editions must preserve official names.** Provide a plain-language explanation in Spanish or Portuguese without mistranslating the formal name of an institution, system, benefit, or legal status.
