## Scope and evidence rules

This register separates official occupational/labour-market evidence from non-government compensation estimates. Job titles vary substantially by employer and jurisdiction. A facilities coordinator or building-operations assistant is not automatically equivalent to a facilities manager, licensed electrician, plumber, HVAC/refrigeration technician, engineer, fire-systems technician, or other regulated trade. The revised guide must preserve those boundaries.

## United States — official sources

1. **U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook — Administrative Services and Facilities Managers.** Facilities managers had a median annual wage of **$104,690 in May 2024**; BLS projects 4% combined employment growth for administrative services and facilities managers from 2024–2034. This is a **manager-level benchmark**, not a promised coordinator wage.  
   https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/administrative-services-managers.htm

2. **O*NET OnLine — Facilities Managers (11-3013.00).** Use for work activities, knowledge, skills, technology context, and the distinction between coordination/operations support and management-level responsibility.  
   https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-3013.00

3. **Apprenticeship.gov — Construction.** Registered Apprenticeship is a legitimate earn-while-you-learn pathway in construction and skilled trades that can feed building-operations careers. The guide must not imply that every facilities-coordinator job has a registered apprenticeship.  
   https://www.apprenticeship.gov/apprenticeship-industries/construction

4. **Apprenticeship.gov — University of Virginia Facilities Management apprenticeship example (2026).** Confirms that facilities organizations may sponsor registered skilled-trades apprenticeships. This is an example, not a nationwide entitlement or guarantee.  
   https://www.apprenticeship.gov/national-apprenticeship-week/events/uva-skilled-trades-job-fair

5. **CareerOneStop / American Job Centers and WIOA.** Use as the public starting point for locally available workforce services, eligible training, supportive services, and funding screening. Eligibility and approved training vary locally; applicants must verify before enrolling or paying.  
   https://www.careeronestop.org/LocalHelp/AmericanJobCenters/american-job-centers.aspx

6. **U.S. Access Board / ADA accessibility standards.** Building-operations staff should recognize accessibility issues and route technical/legal determinations through the responsible facilities, design, safety, or compliance professionals.  
   https://www.access-board.gov/ada/

## United States — current non-government income research

7. **Salary.com — Facilities Coordinator Salary in the United States.** As of **July 1, 2026**, Salary.com reported an average of **$73,794/year**, with a 25th–75th percentile range of **$65,970–$79,203**. This is a non-government market estimate and should be labeled accordingly; methodology, title matching, geography, experience, and employer size can materially change results.  
   https://www.salary.com/research/salary/listing/facilities-coordinator-salary

8. **PayScale — Facility Coordinator.** PayScale’s 2026 page reports **$23.54/hour average base pay** and an observed total-pay range of roughly **$38,000–$73,000**, based on its salary-profile dataset. The page notes a last salary-profile update of March 1, 2025. Use only as a second non-government comparison, not as an official wage statistic.  
   https://www.payscale.com/research/US/Job=Facility_Coordinator/Hourly_Rate

   https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/summary-occupation/297406/ca

10. **Government of Canada Job Bank — Wages.** National NOC 70012 wages were updated November 19, 2025: **C$25.96 low / C$45.20 median / C$70.67 high per hour**, using Statistics Canada reference data. Treat these as NOC-level management wages rather than guaranteed coordinator pay.  
    https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/297406/ca

    https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/requirements/297406/ca

## Latin America portability rule

There is no single Latin American licensing or wage system. The guide should teach readers to verify, for the country and municipality where they will work: employer-required credentials; electrical, gas, refrigeration/HVAC, fire-protection, elevator, work-at-height, confined-space and other regulated technical boundaries; occupational-safety requirements; and local wage evidence. Training or experience earned in one country does not automatically authorize regulated work in another.

## Funding and free-first rule

The English v2 edition should prioritize: public workforce services; community/technical colleges and public vocational systems; employer-paid onboarding and tuition/credential assistance; registered apprenticeships and union/employer skilled-trades programs where available; SENA and Servicio Público de Empleo pathways in Colombia; and scholarships or grants that the learner verifies in writing before committing money. It must warn readers not to take on debt based on a promised job, wage, certification, or reimbursement that has not been independently verified.

## Safety, privacy, cybersecurity, and AI controls

Facilities work can involve badge/access systems, visitor logs, CCTV, building-management systems, alarms, maintenance records, floor plans, keys, vendor access, and equipment telemetry. Learners should follow employer policy, least-privilege access, approved systems, retention rules, and escalation procedures. AI may assist with drafting checklists, summarizing non-sensitive procedures, training practice, or organizing work orders when employer policy permits, but it must not replace qualified safety judgments, code/regulatory decisions, lockout/tagout procedures, emergency instructions, or authorization for regulated technical work. Sensitive building, identity, access-control, security, or personal data should not be placed in public AI tools without explicit authorization.
