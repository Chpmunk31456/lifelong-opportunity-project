## 1. Occupational scope — United States

### O*NET OnLine — Tellers, 43-3071.00

- Source: https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/43-3071.00
- Current page state reviewed: O*NET marks the occupation as **Updated 2026**.
- Core definition: receive and pay out money and keep records of money and negotiable instruments involved in financial-institution transactions.
- Sample reported titles include **Bank Teller**, **Credit Union Teller**, **Member Services Representative**, **Financial Services Representative**, **Customer Relationship Specialist**, and related front-line banking titles.
- Controlled use: this is the primary occupational mapping for Guide 17. The guide may use “member services representative” when the work is materially teller/front-line financial-service work, but should warn that employers may use the same title for broader call-centre, account-service, lending, or sales duties.

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Occupational Outlook Handbook, Tellers

- Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/tellers.htm
- Current page reviewed in 2026.
- BLS states that tellers process routine transactions such as cashing checks and depositing money at banks or credit unions.
- Typical entry requirement: high school diploma or equivalent; short-term on-the-job training is typical. BLS also notes that an employer may require a background check.
- BLS May 2024 median pay: **$39,340 per year / $18.91 per hour**.
- BLS projects teller employment to decline **13% from 2024 to 2034**, while still projecting about **29,800 openings per year** on average, mainly replacement openings.
- Controlled use: the English v2 guide must state both sides: the occupation is accessible but structurally declining because of digital banking and automation, so workers should build portable customer-service, fraud-awareness, digital-banking, compliance, account-service, and relationship skills rather than treating teller work as a static long-term endpoint.

### BLS May 2025 OEWS occupation structure

- Source: https://www.bls.gov/oes/2025/may/oes_stru.htm
- The May 2025 OEWS structure lists **43-3071 Tellers** under Financial Clerks.
- Current non-government services that republish the May 2025 BLS dataset report a national median of approximately **$43,030/year** and mean of approximately **$42,350/year** for SOC 43-3071. These figures must not be presented as independently verified direct BLS page extraction unless the direct BLS occupation table is captured during downstream traceability QA.
- Controlled use: retain the official OOH May 2024 wage as the directly verified official figure. If the May 2025 values are used later, label the provenance precisely and re-check the direct BLS table first.

## 3. Canada occupational mapping and income evidence

### National Occupational Classification 2021 — NOC 64400

- Source: https://noc.esdc.gc.ca/Structure/NOCProfile?GoCTemplateCulture=en-CA&code=64400&version=2021.0&wbdisable=true
- Title: **Customer services representatives - financial institutions**.
- Example titles include **Bank teller**, **Credit union teller**, **Financial customer service representative**, **Teller trainee**, and related financial-institution customer-service roles.
- Main duties include processing deposits, withdrawals, transfers and other transactions; obtaining information for services; answering account questions; resolving discrepancies; and informing customers about available banking products and services.
- Employment requirements: completion of secondary school is required; a college diploma in business administration may be required; on-the-job training is provided.
- Progression to supervisory roles is possible with experience.
- Controlled use: NOC 64400 is the primary Canadian mapping. Do not substitute NOC 14201, which NOC explicitly lists as an exclusion for banking, insurance and other financial clerks.

### Government of Canada Job Bank — Bank teller

- Source: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/wages-occupation/14136/ca
- Current national wage page modified **2026-06-02**; wages updated **2025-11-19** from the 2023-2024 Labour Force Survey reference period.
- Canada hourly wages: **C$18.00 low / C$22.50 median / C$30.67 high**.
- Job Bank summary states this occupation usually requires a secondary-school diploma or several weeks of on-the-job training.
- Controlled use: use the C$22.50/hour national median as the current official Canadian benchmark, date-stamped and with the reference period disclosed.

## 7. Role boundaries, compliance, privacy, and safety

The English v2 master should distinguish routine front-line banking service from roles that may require additional authority, licensing, employer designation or specialized training.

- Tellers may process routine transactions, answer account questions, identify customer needs, and refer customers to appropriate personnel.
- Do not generalize teller/member-service work into authority to provide investment advice, approve credit, originate regulated mortgage loans, override fraud/AML controls, or make legal/compliance determinations.
- Employers may require identity verification, cash controls, dual control, fraud prevention, suspicious-activity escalation, privacy safeguards, cybersecurity hygiene and strict adherence to institutional procedures.
- The guide must teach escalation: when a transaction, identity, fraud indicator, customer vulnerability, sanctions/AML concern, privacy concern, system warning or policy exception exceeds the worker's authority, stop and use the employer's approved escalation process.

## 8. Accessibility and responsible AI controls for v2 drafting

The v2 master should:

- use short sections, descriptive headings, plain language, examples, checklists and explicit “verify before paying” guidance;
- avoid color-dependent meaning and unexplained abbreviations;
- separate official wage evidence from private market estimates;
- distinguish requirements from examples and opportunities from guarantees;
- tell readers not to paste customer names, account numbers, transaction records, identification documents, authentication data, confidential bank information or other protected information into public AI tools;
- limit AI use to low-risk tasks such as study planning, interview practice, drafting generic learning notes, explaining terminology and generating practice scenarios with fictional data;
- require human verification for policy, compliance, customer-impacting and financial decisions.
