# Guide 17 English v1 → v2 Reconciliation

**Date:** 2026-08-09  
**Controlled status:** RECONCILIATION COMPLETE; English v2 working master not yet frozen  

This record defines what must change between the legacy July 2026 publication package and the controlled v2 English master. It uses the legacy English README and source-review summary as editorial evidence, plus `guide-17-v2-current-source-ledger.md` as the current factual authority for drafting.

It does not claim independent human review, legal review, accreditation, accessibility certification, or regulator approval.

## 1. Legacy state retained as evidence, not authority

The current English README identifies Guide 17 as version 1.0, publication month July 2026, with DOCX and PDF artifacts. It describes the guide broadly as career exploration material and includes an appropriate no-guarantee notice.

The legacy source-review summary contains a useful BLS teller source and shared U.S. links, but it is not sufficient for the controlled v2 standard because it does not establish:

- a current O*NET mapping that explicitly connects “Member Services Representative” to Tellers;
- current Canadian NOC and Job Bank evidence;
- a Colombia/SENA pathway;
- neutral Latin America framing;
- current private-market income estimates separated from official wage evidence;
- detailed funding and scholarship evidence;
- the current teller-specific apprenticeship-status discrepancy;
- explicit privacy, cybersecurity, fraud-escalation and responsible-AI boundaries;
- the controlled 19-section v2 structure;
- claim-level freshness rules for time-sensitive sources.

## 2. Title and occupational mapping

### Retain

- Guide title: **Bank Teller and Member Services Representative**.
- Front-line banking and credit-union focus.

### Correct/strengthen

- Anchor the U.S. occupation to **O*NET 43-3071.00 Tellers**, which currently lists “Member Services Representative” among sample reported titles.
- Explain that employer titles are not standardized. A “member services representative” may be teller-like, but some employers use the same title for broader account service, lending support, contact-centre or sales work.
- Anchor Canada to **NOC 64400 Customer services representatives - financial institutions**. Do not use NOC 14201 as the primary mapping because NOC 64400 explicitly excludes banking, insurance and other financial clerks in 14201.

## 3. U.S. labor-market evidence

### Replace generic or stale wage language with controlled provenance

Use directly verified official BLS OOH evidence for the stable baseline:

- May 2024 median wage: **$39,340/year ($18.91/hour)**.
- 2024 employment: about **347,400**.
- 2024-2034 projection: **-13%**.
- Average annual openings: about **29,800**, primarily replacement openings.

The v2 guide must not hide the decline. It should explain that digital banking, automation, kiosks and enhanced ATMs reduce routine teller demand, while replacement openings and hybrid customer-service roles still create entry opportunities.

### May 2025 OEWS control

The BLS May 2025 occupation structure confirms SOC 43-3071 Tellers. Secondary services that reproduce the May 2025 dataset report a national median around $43,030/year, but that number must remain out of the final “official directly verified” box unless the direct BLS occupation table is captured during traceability QA.

## 4. Private income estimates

Add a clearly separated “current non-government estimates” subsection. At the research date:

- Indeed: **$19.25/hour average**, updated 2026-07-20, based on approximately 15.2k salaries from postings over the prior 36 months.
- ZipRecruiter: **$36,351/year average (~$17.48/hour)**, page state dated 2026-07-27; central reported range about $32,000-$40,000.
- Salary.com: **$37,557/year average**, page state dated 2026-07-01.

These must be labeled estimates with different methods. Do not average them together or present them as government statistics.

## 5. Canada coverage

Add a full Canadian pathway based on NOC 64400 and Job Bank.

Current official national Job Bank benchmark:

- C$18.00/hour low;
- **C$22.50/hour median**;
- C$30.67/hour high;
- wages updated 2025-11-19 from the 2023-2024 Labour Force Survey reference period.

Entry framing: secondary school is normally required; a business administration diploma may be required by some employers; on-the-job training is provided. Requirements vary by employer and province.

## 6. Colombia and Latin America coverage

Add a specific Colombia section using current SENA Betowa evidence, including the Centro de Servicios Financieros and relevant learning such as financial-consumer information/advisory, basic commercial transaction records, and personal income/expense concepts when live offerings are available.

The v2 guide must state that SENA availability, dates, modality and admission are dynamic. Readers should verify the live offer before relying on a course.

For Latin America outside Colombia, use a non-universal framing: bank regulation, credentials, wages, job titles and training systems differ by country. Readers should check the national financial supervisor, education/workforce system and specific employer requirements.

## 7. Funding and free/low-cost learning

Add a free-first sequence:

1. employer onboarding/on-the-job training;
2. public libraries, financial-literacy resources and free spreadsheet/digital-skills practice;
3. SENA where applicable in Colombia;
4. community college or career-school options only after verifying cost, recognition and funding;
5. paid credentials only when a target employer or progression path materially values them.

Funding controls to add:

- FAFSA for eligible U.S. college/career-school programs, including possible grants, work-study and loans; never imply that a short teller course is automatically aid-eligible.
- Scholarships through schools, community groups, employers, state agencies and other verified sources; no guarantee of award.
- WIOA training or work-based learning where the individual and provider/program qualify locally; verify through an American Job Center before paying.
- Employer Section 127 educational assistance where offered: for 2025 and 2026, up to $5,250 may be excludable from gross income under a compliant plan. Employer participation and course eligibility are not automatic.

## 8. Apprenticeship correction

Do not use a simple statement such as “bank teller apprenticeships are available” or “teller is not an apprenticeship occupation.” Current Apprenticeship.gov pages conflict:

- the teller Occupation Finder page says 43-3071.00 is **not currently approved** for Registered Apprenticeship use;
- the Financial Services industry page lists **Bank Teller** among high-demand apprenticeship occupations;
- a 2026 National Apprenticeship Week event described a teller apprenticeship with a credit union.

V2 wording must disclose this discrepancy and instruct readers to verify a specific program’s registration and occupation approval before relying on Registered Apprenticeship status.

## 9. Role boundaries and compliance

Expand the guide beyond generic customer-service language.

Teller/member-service workers may:

- process routine deposits, withdrawals, payments and transfers;
- balance drawers and transaction records;
- answer routine account questions;
- explain institution-approved products and services within their training;
- identify needs and refer customers to authorized specialists.

They should not be described as automatically authorized to:

- approve loans or credit;
- originate regulated mortgage loans;
- provide investment or legal advice;
- override fraud, sanctions, AML, privacy or authentication controls;
- make compliance determinations outside assigned authority.

The guide should teach escalation rather than improvisation whenever identity, transaction, fraud, privacy, sanctions/AML, system-warning or policy-exception concerns exceed authority.

## 10. Cybersecurity, privacy and fraud awareness

Add practical front-line safeguards:

- protect customer account and identity information;
- follow approved authentication and access procedures;
- do not share passwords, authentication factors or confidential records;
- recognize social engineering and unusual transaction patterns;
- use only approved systems and communication channels;
- escalate suspected fraud or account takeover;
- never paste customer information into public AI systems.

## 11. Responsible AI

Allow low-risk uses such as:

- interview practice;
- study plans;
- terminology explanations;
- fictional customer-service scenarios;
- generic writing practice.

Prohibit or warn against using public AI tools for customer-identifying information, account data, transaction records, authentication data, internal procedures, suspicious-activity details, or confidential institution information. AI output must not replace institution policy, human judgment or authorized compliance review.

## 12. Accessibility and natural readability

The English v2 master should use:

- short paragraphs and descriptive headings;
- plain-language definitions before acronyms;
- scannable bullets and checklists;
- explicit country labels;
- visible distinction between “required,” “may be required,” “recommended,” and “optional”;
- direct warnings before costs, sensitive-data handling and jurisdiction-specific decisions;
- no color-only meaning and no reliance on decorative symbols for critical instructions.
