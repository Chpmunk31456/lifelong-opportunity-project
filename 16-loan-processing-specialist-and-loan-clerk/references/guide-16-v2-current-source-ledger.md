## 4. Mortgage licensing and scope boundary — United States

### Consumer Financial Protection Bureau — Regulation H / SAFE Act
- CFPB Regulation H states that an individual who engages in the business of a residential mortgage loan originator generally must register through the NMLSR and obtain/maintain the applicable state license unless an exclusion applies.
- CFPB Appendix C explains that independent-contractor loan processors or underwriters can fall within state mortgage-loan-originator licensing requirements. It also gives examples where a state is not required by the SAFE Act to license an employee who performs **only clerical or support duties** under qualifying direction, supervision, and instruction and does not offer or negotiate rates or terms.
- **Editorial control:** Guide 16 must not imply that every processor is unlicensed, that every processor must hold an MLO license, or that a training course itself confers legal authority. Readers considering residential mortgage processing must verify their employment model, activities, state law, NMLS/SAFE Act requirements, and employer compliance before performing regulated work.
- **Primary sources:**
  - https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1008/103/
  - https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1008/c/
  - https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1008/23/
- **Accessed:** 2026-08-09.

## 7. United States funding and low-cost training routes

### Federal Student Aid / FAFSA
- The 2026–27 FAFSA process provides access to federal student aid for eligible college, career-school, and trade-school students, including federal grants such as Pell Grants, work-study, and federal student loans.
- FAFSA submission is free. States, schools, and some private aid providers also use FAFSA information to determine eligibility for their aid programs.
- **Editorial control:** Guide 16 must say eligibility, award amounts, participating programs, and deadlines vary; FAFSA is not itself an award or guarantee of funding.
- **Source:** https://studentaid.gov/articles/fafsa-student-steps/
- **Accessed:** 2026-08-09.

### WIOA / Eligible Training Providers
- U.S. Department of Labor guidance for WIOA Title I recognizes multiple training types, including occupational skills training, on-the-job training, incumbent-worker training, skill upgrading/retraining, and customized training, subject to program and participant eligibility.
- State Eligible Training Provider Lists are part of the WIOA training-provider framework.
- **Editorial control:** Guide 16 may direct readers to an American Job Center to ask whether a relevant financial-services, office-technology, compliance, customer-service, or related program is locally eligible. It must not promise WIOA payment.
- **Source:** https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/advisories/training-and-employment-guidance-letter-no-08-19
- **Accessed:** 2026-08-09.

### Employer educational assistance — Internal Revenue Service
- IRS release IR-2026-55, dated April 20, 2026, states that qualifying benefits under a section 127 employer educational assistance program are excluded from an employee’s gross income up to **$5,250 for calendar years 2025 and 2026**, subject to the statutory/program rules.
- **Editorial control:** Present this as a U.S. tax rule for qualifying employer plans, not as a universal employer benefit. Readers should verify their employer plan and current tax rules.
- **Source:** https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-updates-frequently-asked-questions-about-section-127-educational-assistance-programs

## 9. Broader Latin America pathway control

There is no single region-wide credential or licensing regime for loan-processing support work across Latin America. Guide 16 should therefore:
1. describe transferable skills such as document verification, spreadsheet use, records accuracy, customer communication, financial terminology, privacy, fraud awareness, and workflow tracking;
2. direct readers to official vocational-training, public-employment, banking-regulator, consumer-finance, and employer sources in their own country;
3. use Colombia/SENA as a concrete example where relevant, without presenting Colombia’s programs or regulatory framework as regional rules; and
4. avoid invented salary equivalencies across currencies or countries.

## 10. Scholarships and free/low-cost learning editorial standard

The v2 guide should prioritize:
- free official learning and employer-provided training before paid credentials;
- community-college or recognized vocational programs where the user has verified total cost, recognition, completion terms, refund/withdrawal terms, and job relevance;
- school, community foundation, workforce, employer, union/association, and local-government scholarships or grants only after current eligibility is verified;
- avoiding any provider that promises employment, licensing, income, or guaranteed financing.

No specific scholarship should be described as currently available unless its official current application page and eligibility window have been verified during the publication cycle.

## 11. Required v2 claim controls

The English v2 reconstruction must:
- distinguish official occupational statistics from private salary estimates;
- identify dates/reference periods for wage figures;
- distinguish loan processing/clerical support from regulated mortgage-loan-originator activity;
- avoid promising employment, wages, funding, admission, licensing, certification, promotion, or apprenticeship availability;
- state that requirements differ by employer, jurisdiction, product, and employment model;
- include privacy, cybersecurity, fraud-prevention, fair-lending, accessibility, and responsible-AI safeguards appropriate to financial records;
- require human verification for consequential decisions and prohibit entering confidential borrower data into unapproved AI systems;
- use neutral, plain-language, accessible prose; and
- make no claim of independent human certification, translation certification, accreditation, legal review, or regulator endorsement unless separately documented.
