## Required v2 substantive corrections and additions

### 1. Occupational scope and boundaries

Anchor the U.S. occupational discussion to **O*NET-SOC 43-4131.00 — Loan Interviewers and Clerks**, updated 2026. Explain that relevant reported titles include Loan Clerk, Loan Processor, Mortgage Loan Processor, Mortgage Processor, and related titles, while actual duties vary materially by employer and lending product.

The guide must distinguish processing, verification, documentation, and clerical support from regulated mortgage-loan-originator activities. It must not imply that the title “loan processor” alone determines licensing status.

For Canada, use **NOC 14201 — Banking, insurance and other financial clerks** as the broad occupational mapping. Statistics Canada explicitly includes `loan clerk - financial sector` and `mortgage clerk`; Canada Job Bank uses the same NOC for its loan-clerk profile.

Do not treat U.S. O*NET/SOC and Canadian NOC classifications as legally or statistically interchangeable.

### 2. U.S. mortgage licensing safeguard

Add a prominent SAFE Act/CFPB boundary. The v2 master must explain in plain language that residential mortgage licensing depends on the activities performed, employment relationship, supervision, state law, and applicable federal rules.

CFPB Regulation H and Appendix C show that independent-contractor processors/underwriters performing covered residential-mortgage processing activities may require state mortgage-loan-originator licensing, while certain employees performing only clerical/support duties under qualifying supervision are examples for which a state is not required by the SAFE Act to impose that licensing requirement.

The guide must therefore instruct readers to verify the applicable state/NMLS rules and employer compliance requirements before performing regulated work. It must not provide legal advice or promise that a course confers authority to originate loans.

### 3. Income evidence

Replace stale, unsupported, or generic salary statements with evidence that is clearly classified and dated:

- **Official U.S. occupational evidence:** BLS May 2025 national OEWS for Loan Interviewers and Clerks — employment 164,790; mean hourly wage $25.25; mean annual wage $52,520; median hourly wage $24.05.
- **Current non-government estimate:** ZipRecruiter Loan Processor estimate as of July 19, 2026 — average $44,308/year and $21.30/hour, clearly labeled as private/non-government and title-specific.
- **Canada official/public evidence:** Canada Job Bank NOC 14201 national median C$25.33/hour, with the wage table updated November 19, 2025 and underlying reference period identified.

The guide must explain that these sources use different occupational scopes, methodologies, currencies, and reference periods. No figure is a guaranteed entry wage. Do not convert U.S. wages into a purported Latin American salary benchmark.

### 4. Education and entry pathway

Present loan processing as a role that may be entered through secondary education plus employer training, vocational or community-college coursework, banking/financial-services experience, customer service, office administration, or related pathways depending on employer and jurisdiction.

Prioritize practical skills that transfer across lending environments:

- accurate document review and data entry;
- spreadsheet and office-productivity tools;
- customer communication;
- financial terminology;
- basic credit/lending workflow knowledge;
- privacy and secure records handling;
- fraud awareness and escalation;
- deadline/workflow tracking; and
- professional writing and numerical accuracy.

Avoid portraying a paid private credential as universally required.

### 5. Funding and free-first pathways — United States

The v2 master must tell readers to verify eligibility before spending money and prioritize free, public, or employer-funded routes. Include, where applicable:

- the **2026–27 FAFSA** as the free application used to access federal aid for eligible college, career-school, and trade-school programs, including grants, work-study, and federal loans;
- **WIOA/American Job Center** services and eligible training-provider pathways, with local participant/program eligibility clearly stated;
- **employer educational assistance**, noting the IRS 2026 exclusion of up to **$5,250** for qualifying Section 127 benefits and that employer plans are optional and plan-specific;
- **Registered Apprenticeship** as a paid work-and-learning model, while making clear that a loan-processing apprenticeship is not guaranteed to exist in every area; and
- employer onboarding, cross-training, public-library learning, recognized community-college noncredit courses, and other genuinely free/low-cost foundations before paid credentials.

### 6. Scholarships and employer support

Add a verification-first scholarship and employer-support strategy rather than naming stale awards. Readers should check current official school, workforce-board, community-foundation, employer, association, union, and government scholarship/grant pages and record eligibility, deadlines, total cost, renewal conditions, repayment/service commitments, and withdrawal consequences before accepting funds.

Where employer reimbursement is available, verify in writing what costs are covered, grade/completion requirements, repayment obligations after resignation or termination, tax treatment, and whether preapproval is required.

### 7. Colombia pathway

Use current SENA evidence carefully. SENA Resolution 3779 of December 9, 2025 approved adjacent financial-sector programs including:

- **Tecnología en Operaciones Fiduciarias y Servicios Financieros** — code 123206;
- **Tecnología en Gestión de Seguridad Social, Prestaciones Sociales y Servicios Financieros** — code 123205; and
- **Tecnología en Gestión Administrativa y Comercial de Seguros y Riesgos** — code 123204.

Label these as adjacent financial-services pathways, not as proof of a dedicated national loan-processor credential. Tell readers to verify current SENA/BETOWA offerings, delivery location/modalities, admission requirements, and fit before enrollment.

Include SENA’s Agencia Pública de Empleo as a free Colombia employment resource without promising placement.

### 8. Broader Latin America

Do not invent a region-wide license, credential, salary, or training requirement. Instead, instruct readers to verify:

- the national banking/financial regulator and consumer-finance rules in the country where they will work;
- public vocational-training institutions and public employment services;
- employer-specific bank, credit-union/cooperative, fintech, mortgage, microfinance, or lending-company requirements; and
- privacy/data-protection obligations applicable to borrower records.

Use Colombia as an example, not as a regional rule.

### 9. Canada pathway

Use Statistics Canada NOC 14201 and Canada Job Bank as the official/public baseline. State that Job Bank currently describes the broad occupation as usually requiring a secondary-school diploma or several weeks of on-the-job training, and that prospects vary by province/territory.

Encourage readers to verify provincial requirements, employer training, bilingual/language expectations where relevant, and current Job Bank wage/outlook data before making a decision.

### 10. Accessibility and human readability

The v2 English master must use:

- descriptive headings;
- short paragraphs and manageable lists;
- plain-language explanations of lending and mortgage terminology;
- meaningful link text;
- consistent heading hierarchy;
- text alternatives/descriptions where visuals are ever introduced;
- Unicode/UTF-8 without accidental BOM or replacement-character corruption; and
- wording that works for career changers, older learners, neurodivergent readers, and readers using assistive technology.

Avoid unexplained acronyms, decorative clutter, dense legalistic phrasing, and false certainty.

### 11. Privacy, cybersecurity, fraud, fair treatment, and responsible AI

Strengthen the legacy controls because loan files can contain highly sensitive identity, income, employment, banking, credit, property, and other personal data.

The v2 master must state that readers should:

- use only employer-approved systems and secure channels for borrower information;
- follow least-privilege, authentication, clean-desk/screen, retention, and incident-reporting rules established by the employer;
- verify unusual payment, identity, bank-account, wire, document, or contact changes through approved channels;
- never upload confidential borrower/employer data into public or unapproved AI tools;
- use AI for practice, drafting, organization, or learning only within employer policy and with appropriate human review;
- not use AI output as the sole basis for credit, eligibility, compliance, fraud, adverse-action, or other consequential determinations; and
- escalate suspected discrimination, unfair treatment, security incidents, or fraud through the appropriate employer/compliance channel.

The guide must not turn educational discussion into individualized legal, lending, or credit advice.

### 12. Career progression

Describe plausible progression without guarantees: more complex processing, closing, servicing, quality/control functions, compliance support, underwriting support, team lead/supervision, operations analysis, or—only after meeting separate requirements—licensed loan-origination paths.

Any path involving licensing, underwriting authority, credit decisions, or management responsibilities must be framed as employer/jurisdiction dependent.

### 13. Publication and review claims

Do not state or imply:

- independent human certification or linguistic validation;
- accreditation or regulator endorsement;
- legal review;
- guaranteed employment, wage, promotion, funding, apprenticeship, admission, or licensing outcome; or
- source equivalence that has not been verified.

Continue the project’s AI-assistance disclosure and keep editorial responsibility explicit.
